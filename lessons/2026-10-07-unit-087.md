### 1. 📘 Unit 087 — superlatives: *the + -est* / *the most …*

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** Of everything on the roadmap, what worries you most?
> **You:** Talent. It's **the biggest risk** we're carrying into 2027 — bigger than budget, bigger than
> infrastructure.
> **CFO:** Bigger than the vendor lock-in?
> **You:** Yes. Vendor lock-in is a real concern, but talent is **the hardest** gap **to** close and **the most
> expensive** one to get wrong.
> **CEO:** And the AI pivot itself?
> **You:** Honestly, it's **the best decision** this board **has ever made** — and, long term, **the most
> important** one **in** the company's history.

Notice a new shape sitting one step beyond last week's comparatives. *Bigger than* ranked talent **above** one
other thing. But *the biggest risk*, *the hardest gap*, *the most expensive one*, *the best decision*, *the most
important one* don't compare two things — they pick **one** out of a **whole set** and put it at the **top** (or
bottom) of the group. Two signals mark the pattern: the word **the** in front (never dropped), and either
**-est** on a short adjective (*big → biggest*) or **the most** before a long one (*the most important*). Watch
also what follows: *in* the company's history, and *the best … we've ever made* — the superlative's natural
companions.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

A superlative ranks **one** thing at the extreme of a **group of three or more** (or a whole category). The
frame is always **the + superlative**:

| Adjective | Superlative | Example |
|---|---|---|
| short (1 syllable) | *the + -est* | *the **fastest** route · the **cheapest** option* |
| ends in *-y* | *the + -iest* | *risky → the **riskiest** bet · easy → the **easiest** win* |
| long (2+ syllables) | *the most + adj* | *the **most important** call · the **most expensive** mistake* |
| irregular | memorise | *good → **the best** · bad → **the worst** · far → **the furthest***  |

- **The is obligatory:** *It's **the** best option*, never *"It's best option."*
- **Spelling shifts** mirror the comparative: *big → the biggest* (double the consonant), *large → the largest*
  (just *-st*), *easy → the easiest* (*-y → -iest*).
- **Never combine** *-est* and *most*: *the **most biggest*** ❌ — pick one route, not both.
- **What follows the superlative:**
  - **in** + a place or group: *the biggest company **in** the world / **in** the industry*.
  - **of** + a time span or set: *the best quarter **of** the year · the worst **of** the three options*.
  - a **clause**, often with *ever* + **present perfect**: *the best deal we'**ve ever** closed*.
- **One of the + superlative + plural noun:** *It's **one of the biggest risks** we face* — "among the top," not
  "the single top."

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why does this structure exist?** Comparatives (*-er … than*, *as … as*) line up **two** things. But leaders
constantly need to rank **one** thing against **everything else**: the *single* biggest risk, the *best* option
on the table, the *most* important decision of the year. The superlative is the only tool that says "top of the
whole set." It carries weight precisely because it closes the field — *a big risk* is one of many; *the biggest
risk* is **the** one to watch.

**Decision rule vs the rival (the comparative):** count how many things are in play.

| You're comparing… | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **two** things (A vs B) | comparative + *than* / *as … as* | *Our model is **faster than** theirs.* |
| **one vs a whole group** (3+) | superlative + *the* | *Ours is **the fastest** model **on the market**.* |

Minimal pairs — same facts, different scope:
- *Talent is **a bigger** risk **than** budget.* → ranks talent above **one** rival (budget).
- *Talent is **the biggest** risk **in** the portfolio.* → ranks talent above **everything** — the whole set.

So the choice isn't about degree, it's about **how many** you're measuring against. Two → comparative. Many →
superlative. Reach for the superlative when you want to **close the discussion**: *this is the one.*

**PT→EN mapping:**
- *o maior / o mais importante* → **the biggest / the most important**. The Portuguese **o/a** maps to English
  **the** — so, unlike many structures, the article is *present* in both. The trap is dropping it anyway out of
  nervousness, or doubling the marking.
- *o melhor do mercado* → **the best in the market** (place/group → *in*), **not** *"of the market."*
  Portuguese *do mundo / do mercado* tempts you toward *of*, but for a place or category English wants **in**.
- *a melhor decisão que já tomamos* → **the best decision we've ever made** — note the **present perfect** after
  *ever*, not the simple past.

**Top Brazilian-speaker errors:**
1. ❌ *"It's **the most biggest** risk."* → ✅ *"It's **the biggest** risk."* Portuguese has no double marking,
   but the slip comes from treating *most* as a safe booster. One route only: short adjective → *-est*; long
   adjective → *the most*. Never both.
2. ❌ *"It was **best** decision the board ever made."* → ✅ *"It was **the best** decision…"* A superlative
   **always** carries *the* (even though Portuguese *melhor* can appear bare in speech, English can't drop it).
3. ❌ *"We're **the biggest player of** the market."* → ✅ *"…**the biggest player in** the market."* Use **in**
   for a place or group (*in the world, in the industry, in the room*); save **of** for a time span or an
   explicit set (*the best of the year, the best of the three*).

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. **Talent is the biggest risk we're carrying into 2027.**
   *— superlative, not comparative: it tops the whole risk set, not just one rival. "a bigger risk than X" would
   only rank it against one thing.*
2. **The AI pivot is the best decision this board has ever made.**
   *— "the best" + ever + present perfect ("has ever made") — the natural superlative pattern for "across all
   time up to now."*
3. **Long term, it's the most important call in the company's history.**
   *— long adjective → "the most important" (never "the importantest"); "in … history" uses in for a
   span-as-group, and the obligatory "the" opens it.*
4. **Talent is the hardest gap to close and the most expensive one to get wrong.**
   *— two superlatives ranking one item at the extreme of the set; "the most expensive" (long adj → most), "the
   hardest" (short adj → -est). The "to"-phrase (to close / to get wrong) is a common superlative tail.*
5. **This is one of the biggest bets in our innovation portfolio.**
   *— "one of the + superlative + plural" = among the top, not the single top; note the plural "bets" after it.*
6. **Q3 was the strongest quarter of the year for adoption.**
   *— "of the year": a time span takes of, where a place would take in. "the strongest" ranks it against all four
   quarters.*
7. **It's the fastest route to market, but not the cheapest.** *(everyday / strategy blend)*
   *— two superlatives, two short adjectives → -est; each closes the field ("faster than" would only beat one
   alternative).*
8. **That café has the best coffee in the neighbourhood.** *(everyday)*
   *— irregular "the best"; "in the neighbourhood" = place/group → in, not "of the neighbourhood."*

### 8. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

- Ask your tutor to run a **"top of the set" drill**: they name a category from your world (*risk, opportunity,
  hire, investment, mistake*) and you answer in one superlative sentence that closes the field — *"Talent is
  **the biggest** risk **in** the portfolio," "That acquisition was **the best** decision we'**ve ever** made."*
  Force the obligatory *the*, the right *in/of*, and the *ever* + present perfect tail.
- Then a **comparative-vs-superlative flip**: the tutor gives you two items (comparative) and then a whole group
  (superlative) for the same idea — *"faster than their model"* → *"the fastest model on the market."* Doing
  the flip out loud trains the one decision that matters: two things vs the whole set.

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 086):**
1. **as** — *"as accurate **as** theirs."* The equality frame *as … as* always takes **as**; Portuguese
   *quanto/que* wrongly tempts you toward *than*, but *than* belongs only to the *-er / more* comparatives.
2. **the same as** — *"roughly **the same as** last year's."* *The same* is followed by a **noun** (*the same
   **budget** as*), never an adjective — *the same **as*** is the fixed join.
3. **"Ours is not as good as theirs."** — the *not as … as* frame concedes the gap **politely**, the same
   meaning as "worse than" but without the blunt declaration of defeat.

**Spaced review:**
- **Unit 080:** ✅ *"The last rollout went **well**."* After the **action verb** *went* you need the **adverb**
   *well*, not the adjective *good* — *"went good"* is the classic interference slip. (Contrast: after a linking
   verb like *look*, the adjective returns — *"the numbers look good."*)
- **Unit 055:** ✅ *"The board hasn't **carried it out** yet."* *Carry out* is **separable**, and a **pronoun**
   object (*it*) **must** sit **between** the verb and the particle. A noun can go either side (*carried out the
   plan / carried the plan out*), but *it* has no choice — *"carried out it"* is wrong.

**Recognition drill:**
1. **"the most biggest" → "the biggest"** — never combine *-est* and *most*. Short adjective *big* takes *-est*
   alone; *most* is only for long adjectives (*the most expensive*).
2. **(b)** *Talent is **the biggest** risk in the portfolio* — ranking one thing against a **whole group** (3+)
   is the superlative's job; the comparative *(a)* only beats one named rival.
3. **in** — *"the biggest AI player **in** the market."* Use *in* for a place or group; *of* would be for a time
   span or explicit set (*the best **of** the year*). Portuguese *do mercado* tempts *of*, but it's *in* here.
4. Two fixes: add the obligatory **the** (*"**the** best decision"*) and, more naturally, use **present
   perfect** with *ever* — *"the best decision the board **has ever** made."* A superlative can't drop *the*.

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

Esta unidade é sobre **eleger um item no topo (ou no fundo) de um grupo inteiro** — não comparar dois, mas
destacar **um** contra **todos**. A forma é sempre **the + superlativo**: adjetivo curto → **the + -est** (*the
biggest risk*, *the fastest route*), adjetivo longo → **the most + adjetivo** (*the most important call*), e os
irregulares *the best / the worst*. A regra de decisão contra o comparativo é só contar: **dois** elementos →
comparativo (*bigger than budget*); **um contra o conjunto todo** → superlativo (*the biggest risk in the
portfolio*). Depois do superlativo vêm os companheiros naturais: **in** para lugar/grupo (*the best in the
world*), **of** para período/conjunto (*the best quarter of the year*), e *ever* + **present perfect** (*the
best decision we've ever made*).

As armadilhas são três. (1) Nunca junte *-est* e *most*: *"the **most biggest**"* ❌ → *the biggest* ✅ — escolha
**um** caminho. (2) O **the** é obrigatório, mesmo que no português falado *melhor* apareça sem artigo: *"It was
best decision"* ❌ → *the best decision* ✅. (3) Preposição: use **in** para lugar ou grupo — *"the biggest player
**in** the market"* ✅ — e guarde **of** para tempo ou um conjunto explícito (*the best **of** the year*); o
*"do mercado"* do português puxa para *of*, mas aqui é *in*. Se travar entre comparar dois e destacar um, releia
`lessons/2026-10-06-unit-086.md` (as … as) e volte a este arquivo.
