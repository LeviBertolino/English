# 🔁 Review Week 15 — Units 100 to 105

*Consolidation through contrast. ~15 minutes, read-only. The last review (Week 14) closed on Unit 098; the units since then form two clean families — **relative clauses made shorter / sharper** (100–101) and **adjectives vs everything that competes with them** (102–105). The job this week is not to re-learn each rule but to tell the rivals apart under pressure.*

---

## 1. 📒 Recap — one line per unit

- **Unit 100 — *-ing* / *-ed* clauses (reduced relative clauses):** drop *who is / that was* and use a participle — *-ing* for who **does** it (*the team **leading** the pivot*), *-ed* for who **gets** it done (*the budget **approved** by the board*).
- **Unit 101 — relative clauses with prepositions & quantifiers:** the preposition can slide to the end (*the vendor **we signed with***) and never doubles; attach quantity with *of whom / of which* (*the directors, **all of whom** approved · a budget, **most of which** is committed*).
- **Unit 102 — *-ing* / *-ed* adjectives:** *-ing* = the **cause** of the feeling (*the meeting is **boring***); *-ed* = the **person feeling** it (*I'm **bored***).
- **Unit 103 — adjective word order + linking verbs:** stack *opinion → size → age → origin → type* (*a **bold three-year strategic** plan*); after *be / seem / sound / look / feel* use a plain **adjective** (*the strategy **sounds solid***, not *solidly*).
- **Unit 104 — adjectives vs adverbs:** manner adverbs usually add *-ly* (*quick → quickly*), but *fast / hard* add nothing, *hardly* means *quase não*, and *good* → *well* after an action verb (*she presented **well***).
- **Unit 105 — *so* vs *such*:** *so* + adjective/adverb (*so important · so fast*); *such* + (adjective +) **noun** (*such a bold bet*); both build the result pattern *so/such … that*.

---

## 2. 🗺️ Confusion map — when each rival wins

### A. The *-ing* vs *-ed* split shows up **twice** — keep the two jobs separate

| You are building… | Form | Test | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| a **reduced relative clause** (Unit 100) — shortening *who/that + be* | *-ing* = active (does it) | Can you expand to *who is **—ing***? | *the team **leading** the pivot* = the team that is leading |
| | *-ed* = passive (gets it done) | Can you expand to *which was **—ed** by…*? | *the risks **raised** in the review* = risks that were raised |
| an **adjective of feeling** (Unit 102) | *-ing* = the **cause** | Does it *cause* the feeling in others? | *the roadmap is **exciting*** |
| | *-ed* = the **experiencer** | Is it a person *feeling* it? | *I'm **excited** about the roadmap* |

> The trap: both units use *-ing* and *-ed*, but Unit 100 is about **who acts vs who is acted on**, while Unit 102 is about **what causes a feeling vs who has it**. *The board **confused** by the numbers* (feeling) ≠ *the board **confusing** the numbers* (nonsense). Expand the clause to check which family you're in.

### B. Adjective or adverb? — the verb decides (Units 103 ↔ 104)

| After this kind of verb… | Use | Why | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **linking** verb — *be, seem, sound, look, feel, taste, become* | **adjective** | describes the **subject**, not the action | *the strategy **sounds solid*** / *you look **tired*** |
| **action** verb — *go, perform, present, work, move, run* | **adverb** | describes **how** the action is done | *the quarter went **well*** / *she presented **well*** |

> *good* vs *well* is the border post: **good** is an adjective (linking verbs + nouns — *a good plan, it looks good*); **well** is the adverb for action verbs (*it went well*). And beware the overcorrection reflex: a linking verb takes the plain adjective — ❌ *sounds solidly*, ❌ *seems ambitiously*.

### C. *so* vs *such* — is there a noun in the phrase? (Unit 105)

| Structure | Pick | Example |
|---|---|---|
| + adjective **alone** / adverb | **so** | *so **important** · so **fast*** |
| + (adjective +) **noun** | **such** | *such **a bold bet** · such **talented people*** |
| + bare quantity | **so much / so many** | *so **much** pressure · so **many** risks* |
| result clause | both | *it was **so** tight **that** … / **such a** tight deadline **that** …* |

> The Portuguese *uma decisão **tão** importante* word order is exactly what produces the classic ❌ *a **so** important decision*. A noun in the phrase → **such** (and keep the *a/an*).

## 3. 🧪 10 recognition exercises

*Mostly contrast items — choose the right structure between two or three rivals. Answers with explanations at the end. No peeking.*

1. **(100 vs 102)** The directors ___ by the numbers asked for a rewrite. → *(a)* **confusing** *(b)* **confused**
2. **(102)** It was a ___ quarter, and honestly the whole team felt ___ afterwards. → fill both: *excite___ / exhaust___*.
3. **(103 vs 104)** Choose: *"The pilot results look ___ , so the team performed ___ ."* → *good / well* — place each.
4. **(104)** Spot the error: *"We need to move fastly before a competitor locks in the partnership."*
5. **(105)** Choose: *"It was ___ a bold bet that even the CFO hesitated."* → *so / such*
6. **(101)** Fix: *"The fund **in which we invested in** returned 3x."*
7. **(104 false friend)** True or false — *"The board **hardly** discussed the AI budget"* means the board discussed it intensely.
8. **(103 word order)** Reorder: *"a / strategic / bold / three-year / plan"*.
9. **(100 vs 102)** Choose: *"The risks ___ in the review changed the roadmap."* → *(a)* **raising** *(b)* **raised**
10. **(105 vs 104)** Choose: *"The timing was ___ tight that we pushed the launch."* → *so / such* — and say why the rival fails here.

## 4. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula — 3 themes worth drilling with your tutor

1. **The adjective/adverb border (Units 103–104).** Ask your tutor to feed you a mix of linking-verb and action-verb sentences about your quarter (*"the numbers look ___", "the rollout went ___"*) and have you supply *good* vs *well* / adjective vs adverb on the spot. This is the contrast most likely to slip in live executive speech.
2. **so vs such under result pressure (Unit 105).** Role-play describing a high-stakes decision — *"it was **such a** risky bet **that** …"* / *"the window was **so** narrow **that** …"*. Force the *… that …* result clause every time, since that's where the *so/such* choice is most exposed.
3. **Tightening sentences with reduced relatives (Units 100–101).** Practice re-saying a long clause two ways: *"the team that is leading the pivot"* → *"the team leading the pivot"*, and *"the vendor that we signed with"* → keep the preposition at the end. Great for sounding more fluent and less translated in board updates.

## 5. ✅ Gabarito

1. **(b) confused** — a reduced relative = *the directors **who were** confused*; the directors *experience* the feeling (Unit 102) and are also the ones *acted on* by the numbers (Unit 100) → *-ed* either way. *Confusing* would mean the directors cause confusion in others.
2. **exciting / exhausted** — the quarter *causes* the feeling → *-ing*; the team *has* the feeling → *-ed* (Unit 102).
3. **look good / performed well** — *look* is a linking verb → adjective *good*; *performed* is an action verb → adverb *well* (Units 103–104).
4. **❌ fastly → ✅ fast** — *fast* is already an adverb and takes no *-ly* (Unit 104). *"move fast"*.
5. **such** — *a bold bet* is a noun phrase, so *such* (+ keep *a*); *so* is forbidden before a noun (Unit 105). The result clause *… that …* is correct.
6. **✅ *the fund **(which) we invested in*** (or formal *in which we invested*)** — the preposition appears **once**; ❌ *in which … in* doubles it (Unit 101).
7. **False** — *hardly* is a false friend meaning *quase não / barely*. The sentence says they **barely** discussed it. "Discussed intensely" would need *discussed it **hard** / at length* (Unit 104).
8. **a bold three-year strategic plan** — opinion (*bold*) → age/period (*three-year*) → type (*strategic*) → noun (Unit 103).
9. **(b) raised** — the risks *get raised* (passive) → *-ed* clause; *raising* would mean the risks are doing the raising (Unit 100).
10. **so** — *tight* is an adjective standing **alone** (no noun), so *so*; *such* fails because there's no noun phrase for it to attach to (Units 104–105). *"so tight that we pushed the launch."*

## 6. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**O que já deve estar sólido.** As orações relativas reduzidas (Unit 100) — trocar *who is / that was* por particípio: *-ing* para quem **faz** (*the team leading*), *-ed* para quem **sofre** a ação (*the budget approved*). E a preposição no fim da relativa sem duplicar (Unit 101): *the vendor **we signed with***, nunca *in which we invested **in***. Essas duas deixam seu inglês mais enxuto e menos "traduzido" em updates para o board.

**O que provavelmente ainda escorrega.** Três armadilhas de interferência do português. (1) ***-ing* vs *-ed* de sentimento** (Unit 102): *-ante* em PT → *-ing* em inglês (*frustrante → frustrating*); *-ado/-ido* → *-ed* (*frustrado → frustrated*). Nunca *"I'm boring in this call."* (2) **adjetivo vs advérbio** (Units 103–104): depois de verbo de ligação (*sounds, looks, seems*) use **adjetivo** (*sounds solid*, não *solidly*); depois de verbo de ação use **advérbio** (*went well*, não *went good*); e cuidado com *hardly* (= *quase não*) e *fastly* (não existe). (3) ***so* vs *such*** (Unit 105): o português *uma decisão **tão** importante* gera o erro clássico *"a **so** important decision"* — se há **substantivo** na frase, é **such** (*such an important decision*). Se tiver só adjetivo/advérbio, é **so**. **Reler se precisar:** `lessons/2026-10-25-unit-105.md` (so/such) e `lessons/2026-10-24-unit-104.md` (adjetivo vs advérbio) são os dois mais confundíveis desta semana.
