### 1. 📘 Unit 052 — "get"

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** Where are we on the AI platform decision?
> **You:** Almost there. Let's **get aligned** on the budget before Thursday. Once the
> CFO **gets** the final numbers, she'll move fast.
> **CEO:** And the board?
> **You:** The vision finally **got approved** yesterday, so we're clear. This quarter I
> even **got to present** the 2027 roadmap to them directly.
> **CEO:** Good. Things were **getting complicated** for a while.
> **You:** They were. But I'll **get to** the boardroom early Thursday and walk them
> through it. Text me if anything changes before you **get home** tonight.

Notice one small verb doing many jobs. *Get **aligned*** = *become* aligned. *Get**s**
the numbers* = *receive* them. *Got **approved*** = a change that *happened* (a soft
passive). *Got **to** present* = *had the chance* to. *Get **to** the boardroom* =
*arrive*. *Get **home*** = *arrive home*. In Portuguese those are five different verbs —
*ficar, receber, conseguir, chegar* — and English packs them all into **get**. That's
today's whole lesson: one word, several meanings, each with a clear pattern.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

**get** changes meaning depending on **what follows it**:

| Pattern | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **get + adjective** | *become* / change into a state | *get **ready**, get **aligned**, get **worse**, get **nervous*** |
| **get + noun** | *obtain / receive* | *get **the budget**, get **an email**, get **approval*** |
| **get to + place** | *arrive at / reach* | *get **to the office*** *(but* get **home / here / there** *— no* to*)* |
| **get + past participle** | informal passive — a change *happens* | *get **approved**, get **done**, get **promoted**, get **paid*** |
| **get to + verb** | *manage to / have the chance to* | *I **got to present** to the board* |

**Key facts:**
- **The core meaning is *change*.** With an adjective, *get* = *become*: you move
  **into** a new state (*get ready* = you weren't ready, now you are).
- **get to a place = arrive there.** *Get to the meeting* = reach/arrive at it. But
  *home, here, there, abroad* take **no *to*** — *get **home***, not *get to home*.
- **get + past participle** is the everyday, spoken passive of a *change*: *the budget
  **got approved*** = it moved from unapproved to approved. Focus is on the **event**.
- **get to + verb** (with an *action* after *to*) = you *managed / had the opportunity*:
  *I **got to** meet the CEO* = I had the chance to.
- *get* is **natural, not slang** — executives use it constantly. Avoiding it (with
  *become / receive / arrive*) sounds stiff and over-formal.

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why does this structure exist?** English needs a light, everyday verb for the idea of
**changing into a state** or **coming to have something** — without reaching for a heavy
word like *become, obtain, receive, arrive*. That verb is **get**. It's the difference
between the **process/moment of change** and the **finished state**: *get* takes you
across the line; *be* describes where you already are. Portuguese has no single word for
this, so it spreads the job across *ficar, receber, conseguir, chegar* — and that's
exactly where the interference starts.

**Decision rule #1 — get vs be (change vs state, the core seam).** This is the one that
matters most.

| | **get** (the change) | **be** (the state) |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | *become* — you move **into** the state | you are **already** in the state |
| Example | *"Let's **get aligned** on the budget."* (we aren't yet) | *"We **are aligned** on the budget."* (already agreed) |
| Example | *"The board **got nervous**."* (it changed) | *"The board **is nervous**."* (current mood) |

Minimal pair — same adjective, different verb, different meaning:
- *"**Get ready** for the board meeting."* = *become* ready, do the preparation.
- *"**Be ready** for the board meeting."* = *already be* in the ready state when it starts.

**Decision rule #2 — get to (a place) vs arrive.** *Get to X* = *arrive at / reach X*.
The trap is the preposition:
- ✅ *"I'll **get to** the office by eight."* / ✅ *"I'll **arrive at** the office by eight."*
- ❌ *"I'll **arrive to** the office."* — *arrive* takes **at/in**, never *to*; *get*
  takes **to**. And *home/here/there* drop the preposition entirely: *"get **home**"*.

**Decision rule #3 — get + past participle vs be + past participle.** Both look passive,
but *get* highlights the **event/change**; *be* highlights the **resulting state**:
- *"The budget **got approved** yesterday."* — the moment it happened (a change, with a
  time).
- *"The budget **is approved**."* — its current status (a state, right now).

**PT→EN mapping — where Portuguese betrays Levi.** *Get* fractures into several
Portuguese verbs, and translating each one back with its "own" word sounds unnatural:
- *"**ficar** cansado / nervoso / pior"* → ✅ *"**get** tired / nervous / worse"*, not
  *"**stay** tired"* nor the stiff *"**become** tired"*.
- *"**chegar** ao escritório"* → ✅ *"**get to** the office"*, ❌ *"**arrive to** the office"*.
- *"**receber** um e-mail / **conseguir** o orçamento"* → ✅ *"**get** an email / **get**
  the budget"*.
- *"**consegui** apresentar ao board"* → ✅ *"I **got to** present to the board"* (had the
  chance), ❌ *"I **got present**"* / ❌ *"I **could** present"* (which loses the "I
  actually did it" meaning).

**Top Brazilian-speaker errors:**
- ❌ *"When did you **arrive to** the office?"* → ✅ *"When did you **get to** the office?"*
  (or *"**arrive at**"*) — *chegar a* pulls you to the wrong preposition.
- ❌ *"The forecast **stayed worse** after the call."* / ❌ *"…**became** worse."* → ✅
  *"The forecast **got worse**."* — *ficar* = *get* for a change of state; *stay* means
  "remained", the opposite idea.
- ❌ *"I want to **get done this** today."* → ✅ *"I want to **get this done** today."* —
  with *get + past participle* the object goes **in the middle**: *get* **it** *done*.

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. *"Let's **get aligned** on the AI budget before the board meeting."*
   *— get + adjective = reach the state of alignment (we aren't there yet). "Be aligned" would claim we already agree — a different, riskier statement to the CEO.*
2. *"Once the CFO **gets** the final numbers, she'll move fast."*
   *— get + noun = receive/obtain; PT "receber os números". "Once the CFO receives" is correct but stiff — get is what executives actually say.*
3. *"I need to **get to** the boardroom before nine."*
   *— get to + place = arrive at/reach it; chegar. Never "arrive to the boardroom" — arrive takes* at*, get takes* to.
4. *"The AI vision finally **got approved** yesterday."*
   *— get + past participle = the change happened (with a time,* yesterday*). "Is approved" would give the current status, not the moment it flipped.*
5. *"This quarter I **got to present** our 2027 strategy to the CEO."*
   *— get to + verb = had the opportunity; PT "consegui apresentar". Not "I got present"; not "I had to present" (that's obligation, the opposite of a privilege).*
6. *"Adoption is **getting complicated** as we scale."*
   *— get + adjective for a gradual change in progress; ficar. "Is complicated" = a fixed state;* getting *= the state is worsening right now.*
7. *"I **got tired** of waiting for the elevator this morning."*
   *— everyday life:* ficar cansado *= get tired (the change), never "stayed tired" / "became tired".*
8. *"Text me when you **get home** tonight."*
   *— everyday life: get + home = arrive home, with* no *"to". "Get to home" is the classic slip.*

### 8. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

- Ask your tutor to run a **"ficar / conseguir / chegar" swap drill**: they say a
  sentence in Portuguese from your world (*"O board ficou nervoso"*, *"Consegui aprovar o
  orçamento"*, *"Cheguei atrasado à reunião"*, *"Recebi o feedback do CEO"*) and you
  answer instantly in English with **get** (*"The board got nervous", "I got the budget
  approved", "I got to the meeting late", "I got the CEO's feedback"*). Goal: stop
  reaching for *become / arrive to / receive* and let *get* become the reflex.
- Then a **change-vs-state round** on the core seam: have your tutor throw an adjective
  from an executive setting (*ready, aligned, clear, complicated, worse*) and you say
  **both** — *"get ___"* (the change) and *"be ___"* (the state) — in a real sentence,
  explaining out loud which one you'd use before a board meeting and why. This is the
  distinction that makes your English sound native instead of translated.

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 051 — preposition + -ing):**
1. ❌ *"without **to see**"* → ✅ *"We can't decide **without seeing** the numbers."* —
   after any preposition the verb is *-ing*; there is no *to* and no bare verb.
2. ***to presenting*** — *"I look forward **to presenting** our AI vision to the board."*
   In *look forward to*, the *to* is a **preposition** (test: *"look forward to **it**"*
   works), so it takes *-ing*, not the infinitive. *"Look forward to present"* is the
   classic email error.
3. ***interested in leading*** — *interested* pairs with the preposition *in*, and after
   a preposition an action becomes *-ing*. The little word before the verb is the
   trigger: preposition *in* → *-ing*; there is no *"interested to lead"* pattern for
   naming the activity.

**Spaced review:**
- **(Unit 045)** ***to align*** — *"I called the offsite **to align** the leadership
  team."* When **you are the actor** stating your purpose, it's *to* + verb (*why* you
  called it). *"For aligning"* names the event's **function** (what the offsite is *for*),
  which is why the same idea flips form depending on whether a person or the event is the
  subject.
- **(Unit 024)** ***spoken … was written*** — *"Have you **spoken** to the CEO? The 2027
  vision **was written** before I joined."* Both slots need the **past participle**: the
  first because *have* (present perfect) demands it (*"Have you **spoke**"* is the classic
  slip), the second because it's a **passive** (*was* + participle). *Speak* and *write*
  are irregular, so the participle differs from the past simple (*spoke / wrote*).

**Recognition drill:**
1. ❌ *"arrive **to** the office"* → ✅ *"When did the team **get to** the office?"* **or**
   ✅ *"…**arrive at** the office?"* — *get* takes *to*; *arrive* takes *at/in*; *"arrive
   to"* exists in neither (it comes straight from *chegar a*).
2. ***had gotten nervous*** — *"By the time I finished, the whole board **had gotten
   nervous**."* The phrase *"by the time I finished"* marks a **change** that built up
   during the demo, so you want *get* (become). *"Was nervous"* would just report a state
   with no sense of it developing — grammatical, but it loses the "it grew on them" meaning.
3. ***got to present*** — *"This quarter I **got to present** our roadmap to the CEO."*
   *Get to + verb* = *had the opportunity / managed to* (matches *"a real privilege"*).
   *Have to present* would mean an **obligation** — the opposite feeling. The little word
   *to* is the same, but *get*/*have* flip the meaning from privilege to duty.
4. ***got worse*** — *"The market **got worse** after the earnings call."* *Ficar (pior)*
   = *get* for a **change of state**. *"Became worse"* is technically correct but stiff
   and unnatural in speech; *"stayed worse"* means *remained* (it was already bad and
   didn't change) — the opposite of the change you're describing.

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**A regra e quando usar.** *Get* é o verbo "coringa" do inglês para a ideia de **mudança
de estado** e de **passar a ter algo** — sem precisar dos pesados *become, obtain,
receive, arrive*. O sentido central é **mudança**: *get + adjetivo* = *ficar / tornar-se*
(*get ready, get aligned, get worse*); *get + substantivo* = *receber / conseguir* (*get
the budget, get an email*); *get to + lugar* = *chegar a* (*get to the office* — mas *get
**home / here / there***, sem *to*); *get + particípio* = uma passiva informal, a mudança
que **aconteceu** (*got approved, got done*); *get to + verbo* = *conseguir / ter a chance
de* (*I got to present to the board*). O par decisivo é **get × be**: *get* atravessa a
linha (a mudança), *be* descreve onde você já está (o estado) — *get aligned* (ainda não
estamos) × *be aligned* (já concordamos).

**As armadilhas.** (1) *Get* se parte em vários verbos do português (*ficar, receber,
conseguir, chegar*), e traduzir cada um pelo "seu" verbo soa artificial: diga *"get
tired"* (não *stayed/became tired*), *"get to the office"* (não *arrive **to***), *"get
the budget"* (não só *receive*). (2) *Chegar a* → *get **to***, nunca *"arrive to"*
(*arrive* pede *at/in*). (3) Com *get + particípio*, o objeto vai **no meio**: *"get **it**
done"*, não *"get done it"*. (4) *Got to + verbo* = privilégio/oportunidade (*consegui*),
o oposto de *have to* (obrigação) — não confunda os dois. Se ainda embolar o *to* de
lugar × o *to* de infinitivo, releia `lessons/2026-09-01-unit-051.md` (preposition +
-ing), que mostra o outro lado do *to* traiçoeiro.
