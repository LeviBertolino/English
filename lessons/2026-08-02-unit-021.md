### 1. 📘 Unit 021 — is done / was done (the passive 1)

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** Where do things stand on the AI programme?
> **You:** The budget **was approved** at the last board meeting, and the roadmap
> **is being finalised** now. The first two pilots **were launched** in June, and the
> results **are reviewed** every fortnight by the steering committee.
> **CEO:** Who signed off on the vendor?
> **You:** That decision **was made** by the CFO — I only recommended the shortlist.

Notice: the sentences are built around **what happened to things** — the budget, the
roadmap, the pilots, the results — not around who did it. *was approved, were launched,
are reviewed, was made.* The doer is either obvious, unimportant, or added at the end
with **by** (*by the CFO*). That is the whole job of the passive.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

The passive is always **the verb `be` + past participle** (*done, made, approved,
launched, reviewed*). You change the tense by changing **`be`** — the participle never
moves.

| Tense | Active | Passive (**be** + participle) |
|---|---|---|
| Present simple | *We **review** the results.* | *The results **are reviewed**.* |
| Past simple | *We **approved** the budget.* | *The budget **was approved**.* |

| Number | Present | Past |
|---|---|---|
| Singular | *is* + participle (*is made*) | *was* + participle (*was made*) |
| Plural | *are* + participle (*are made*) | *were* + participle (*were made*) |

**Key facts:**
- **Active vs passive — same event, flipped viewpoint.** Active starts from the **doer**
  (*The board approved the budget*). Passive starts from the **thing acted on**
  (*The budget was approved* — by the board). The object of the active becomes the
  subject of the passive.
- **The participle is fixed; the tense lives in `be`.** *is approved* (present),
  *was approved* (past). Get `be` right and the tense is right.
- **The doer is optional.** Add it only if it matters, with **by**: *The decision was
  made **by the CFO**.* If it's obvious or irrelevant, leave it out — that's usually the
  reason you chose the passive at all.
- **Regular participle = -ed; irregular ones must be learned** (*make → made, take →
  taken, give → given, build → built, do → done, write → written*).

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why does the passive exist?** Because sometimes the **thing done matters more than the
doer** — or the doer is obvious, unknown, or better left unnamed. The passive lets you
put the important thing (*the budget, the results, the decision*) in the spotlight at the
front of the sentence, and push the doer offstage (or drop it entirely). Active can't do
that: it forces the doer to the front. In executive language this is everywhere — process
descriptions (*"the results are reviewed every fortnight"*), reporting outcomes without
finger-pointing (*"a mistake was made"*), and describing decisions where the **what**
outranks the **who** (*"the budget was approved"*).

**Decision rule — active or passive?** Ask two questions:
1. **Who cares about the doer?** If the doer is the point (*The **CFO** made that call*),
   go active. If the doer is obvious, unknown, or unimportant (*The budget **was
   approved***), go passive.
2. **What do I want at the front of the sentence?** The passive lets you lead with the
   thing acted on — useful when that's your topic (*"The roadmap is being finalised"*).

**Minimal pairs — same event, the choice shifts the focus:**
- *The board **approved** the budget.* → the story is about **the board** (who acted).
  vs. *The budget **was approved**.* → the story is about **the budget** (what happened
  to it); who did it may not even be stated.
- *Someone **stole** my laptop.* → clumsy, vague doer up front.
  vs. *My laptop **was stolen**.* → natural; the doer is unknown, so drop it.
- *We **make** the decisions here.* (emphasis on **we**) vs. *The decisions **are made**
  here.* (emphasis on **the process** — impersonal, procedural).

**PT→EN mapping — where the interference bites.**
- Portuguese has a clean passive with *ser* + particípio: *"foi aprovado", "é revisado",
  "foram lançados"* → *was approved, is reviewed, were launched.* This maps almost
  directly — good news.
- **The `se` trap.** Portuguese loves the impersonal *se*: *"aprovou-**se** o orçamento",
  "revisam-**se** os resultados", "vende-**se**".* English does **not** have this — you
  render it with the **passive**: *the budget **was approved**, the results **are
  reviewed**, it **is sold**.* Do not translate *se* with a reflexive or with *"one"*
  by reflex.
- **`by` ≠ `for`.** The doer in a passive takes **by**, never *for*: *approved **by** the
  board*, not *approved ~~for~~ the board*. Portuguese *por* is the right instinct here —
  trust *por → by*.

**Top Brazilian-speaker errors:**
- ❌ *The budget **was approve** last week.* → ✅ *The budget **was approved** last week.*
  — *the passive needs the **past participle** (approv**ed**), not the bare base; `be`
  carries the tense but the participle must still be complete.*
- ❌ *The results **are review** every fortnight.* / *The results **review** every
  fortnight.* → ✅ *The results **are reviewed** every fortnight.* — *without `be` +
  participle there is no passive; "the results review" wrongly says the results do the
  reviewing.*
- ❌ *The decision was made **for** the CFO.* → ✅ *The decision was made **by** the CFO.*
  — *the agent of a passive is introduced with **by**; "for the CFO" means it was done
  on his behalf, a different meaning.*

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. The AI budget **was approved** at the last board meeting. — *passive: the budget is the
   topic and who approved it (the board) is obvious, so it's dropped — focus on the
   outcome, not the actor.*
2. The roadmap **is** currently **being reviewed** by the steering committee. — *present
   passive with the doer kept, because naming the committee adds real information about
   ownership.*
3. The first two pilots **were launched** in June. — *past passive: the pilots are the
   subject; who ran them is irrelevant to the point (that they went live).*
4. Every investment case **is assessed** against the same three criteria. — *present
   passive for a standing **process** — impersonal and procedural, exactly what the
   passive is for.*
5. That decision **was made** by the CFO, not by me. — *passive with an explicit **by**
   because here the doer is precisely the point — you're assigning it away from yourself.*
6. Our data **is** still **being migrated** to the new platform. — *present-progressive
   passive (be + being + participle) for an in-progress process; the migrator doesn't
   matter.*
7. A mistake **was made** in the forecast, and we've already corrected it. — *the classic
   agent-hiding passive: report the fact without naming who erred.*
8. My laptop **was stolen** at the airport, so I lost the offline deck. — *everyday life:
   the doer is unknown, so the passive is the only natural choice.*

### 8. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

- Ask your tutor to run a **"describe the process" drill**: you explain, entirely in the
  passive, how a decision moves through your company — *"Each case **is assessed**, the
  shortlist **is prepared**, the recommendation **is sent** to the board, and the budget
  **is approved**."* This trains the impersonal, procedural passive that dominates
  governance and reporting language.
- Then a **"who's on stage?" contrast**: give your tutor the same event twice — once you
  want the **doer** in focus (*"The CFO made that call"*), once you want the **outcome**
  in focus (*"That call was made at CFO level"*). Have them push you to justify each
  choice, so active-vs-passive becomes a deliberate framing decision, not an accident.

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 020)**
1. **signed** — ✅ *"We **signed** the vendor contract **last quarter**."* — *"last
   quarter" is a finished, named time → past simple; a named past time bans the present
   perfect. (Drop the time word and mean only "it's signed, we can proceed" → "We've
   signed the vendor contract".)*
2. ✅ *"I've **finished** the diagnostic."* (present perfect) — *no date is given and the
   point is the **result now** (it's ready); the bare past simple "I finished" would leave
   the listener waiting for "…when?".*
3. ❌ *"When have you launched…?"* → ✅ *"**When did** you **launch** the AI assistant?"* —
   *"when" asks for a specific finished point, and the present perfect refuses any specific
   past time, so the question is past simple.*

**Spaced review**
- **(Unit 014)** **was finishing** — ✅ *"When the vendor called, I **was finishing** the
  investment model."* — *the continuous shows the call caught you **mid-task**; "I
  finished" would mean you completed it **after** the call — a sequence, not an
  interruption.*

**Recognition drill**
1. ❌ *"was sign"* → ✅ *"The vendor contract **was signed** last quarter."* — *the passive
   is `be` + **past participle**; `be` (was) carries the tense, but the participle
   (sign**ed**) must still be complete — "was sign" is only half the form.*
2. Both are correct; the choice is about **focus**. (b) *"The results **are reviewed**…"*
   is better for describing a **standing procedure** — impersonal, the process is the
   point, the reviewer is obvious. (a) active is better only if you specifically want
   **the committee** in the spotlight (who does it). — *for governance/process language,
   the passive is the default.*
3. ✅ *"The budget **was approved** in the meeting."* — *Portuguese impersonal **se**
   (aprovou-se) has no English twin; render it with the **passive**, not with "one
   approved…", which sounds stilted and wrong.*
4. ❌ *"recommended **for** me… approved **for** the board"* → ✅ *"The shortlist was
   recommended **by** me and approved **by** the board."* — *the doer (agent) of a passive
   takes **by**; "for me/for the board" would mean it was done **on their behalf**, a
   completely different meaning.*

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**A regra e quando usar.** A voz passiva é sempre **`be` + particípio passado**
(*is done, was approved, were launched*) — você muda o tempo mexendo em **`be`**
(*is/are* = presente, *was/were* = passado), e o particípio nunca muda. Use a passiva
quando **o que aconteceu importa mais do que quem fez**: o agente é óbvio, desconhecido,
irrelevante, ou melhor não citar. Ela coloca a **coisa afetada** (o orçamento, os
resultados, a decisão) na frente da frase e joga o autor para o fim com **by**
(*by the CFO*) — ou some com ele. É a linguagem natural de processos e governança:
*"os resultados **são revisados** a cada quinze dias", "o orçamento **foi aprovado**".*
Regra de decisão: se o **autor** é o ponto, use ativa; se a **coisa** ou o **processo**
é o ponto, use passiva.

**As armadilhas.** (1) O particípio precisa estar **completo**: *"was **approved**"*,
nunca *"was approve"* — o `be` carrega o tempo, mas o particípio ainda tem de estar
inteiro. (2) A armadilha do **se** português: *"aprovou-se, revisam-se, vende-se"* **não**
existe em inglês — traduza com a **passiva** (*was approved, are reviewed, it is sold*),
nunca com "one approved…" nem com reflexivo. (3) O agente entra com **by**, não *for*:
*approved **by** the board* (o instinto do *por* português está certo; *"for the board"*
significa "em nome do board", outro sentido). Se ainda embolar quando escolher ativa ou
passiva, releia `lessons/2026-08-01-unit-020.md` (foco no resultado) para reforçar a ideia
de "o que importa é a coisa, não a data/o autor".
