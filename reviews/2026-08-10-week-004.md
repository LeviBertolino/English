# 🔁 Review Week 004 — Units 23 to 28

This week split cleanly into two blocks. First, the **machinery under every
sentence**: *be / have / do* as helpers (Unit 023) and the **past-simple vs
past-participle** shapes those helpers demand (Unit 024). Then the payoff block —
**four different ways English talks about the future**: the present continuous and
present simple pointed forward (Unit 026), *going to* (Unit 027), and *will / shall*
(Unit 028). Sitting between them, *used to* (Unit 025) handles the mirror image —
a **past** habit that has since stopped. This is not new grammar; the whole point
of Review Week is making the **choices between** these forms automatic, because in
a real board update or a vision presentation they collide in a single breath —
*"We used to approve pilots one by one; now we're moving to an AI-first model, and
I'll walk the board through it on Thursday."* Read time: ~15 minutes.

(Note: there is no Unit 022 lesson to recap — that day of the cycle was Review
Week 003, so the count jumps from 021 straight to 023.)

## 1. 📋 Recap — one line per unit

- **Unit 023 — be / have / do (present & past).** The three verbs that are **both
  main verbs and helpers**. The one rule that governs everything: you add **do /
  does / did** to build a **negative or question in the present/past simple — and
  only there**. The moment another helper is present (*be, have, can, will*),
  **do disappears** and that helper does the job.
- **Unit 024 — regular & irregular verbs.** Every verb has three shapes: **base /
  past simple / past participle**. Regular verbs hide the split (*reviewed /
  reviewed*); irregular verbs expose it (*took ≠ taken*). The choice is decided by
  the **word in front**: a helper (*have / was*) → **participle**; nothing → **past
  simple**.
- **Unit 025 — I used to…** A **past habit or state that is no longer true** —
  *"AI decisions used to land on my desk"* (they don't now). Past-only; after
  *did / didn't* it drops the *-d* (**didn't use to**). Beware the false twin
  **be used to doing** = *to be accustomed to* (present, ongoing).
- **Unit 026 — present tenses for the future.** Two present tenses point forward:
  **present continuous** for a **personal arrangement** already on the calendar
  (*I'm presenting on Thursday*), **present simple** for a **timetable** fixed by
  the clock/agenda (*The offsite starts at nine*).
- **Unit 027 — I'm going to (do).** One shape, two jobs: an **intention / decision
  already made** (*we're going to invest in AI*) and a **prediction from present
  evidence** (*look at the run-rate — we're going to overshoot*).
- **Unit 028 — will / shall.** The future you **decide at the moment of speaking**,
  plus **offers, promises, and refusals** (*I'll handle it · I'll have the numbers
  by nine · the vendor won't move on price*). *Shall I…? / Shall we…?* survive as
  offers and proposals.

## 2. 🗺️ Confusion map — when each form wins

The week turns on **four decisions**. Master these four reflexes and the whole
zone — the helper system and the future quartet — falls into place.

### Decision A — do I add *do/does/did*, or is a helper already there? (Unit 023)

Ask one question before **every** negative or question: *is there already an
auxiliary in this sentence?*

| Situation | Helper to use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Plain **present simple** main verb | add **do / does** | ***Does** the roadmap **cover** 2027?* |
| Plain **past simple** main verb | add **did** | ***Did** the board **approve** it?* |
| A form of **be** already there | use **be**, no *do* | ***Are** we ready?* |
| **have** (perfect) already there | use **have**, no *do* | ***Have** you decided?* |
| A modal (**can/will/should**) already there | use the modal, no *do* | ***Can** you present?* |

After *does / did*, the main verb goes back to its **base form**: *Does she **lead***
(not *leads*), *Did they **approve*** (not *approved*). The tell for a Brazilian
speaker: Portuguese has **no do-support**, so the instinct is to drop the helper
(*"You work here?"*) or grab the wrong one (*"Is you work here?"*). Reflex to build:
**plain verb + question/negative → insert do/does/did.**

### Decision B — past simple or past participle? (Unit 024)

Look at the **word directly in front** of the verb slot:

| What's in front | Shape to use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Nothing** (the verb is the plain past action) | **past simple** | *The board **took** the decision on Monday.* |
| **have / has / had** (perfect) | **past participle** | *The board **has taken** the decision.* |
| **is / was / been** (passive) | **past participle** | *The decision **was taken** last week.* |

Regular verbs make this invisible (*approved / approved*); it only bites on
irregulars, where *took ≠ taken*, *wrote ≠ written*, *did ≠ done*. There is **no
rule** that generates *taken* from *take* — this one is pure memorisation.

### Decision C — *used to do* vs the past simple vs *be used to doing* (Unit 025)

Two different rivals hide under one form.

| Meaning | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A **single** finished past event | **past simple** | *We **approved** the pilot in March.* |
| A **repeated** past habit / state, now over | **used to + base** | *We **used to approve** each pilot one by one.* |
| **Accustomed to** something (present, ongoing) | **be used to + -ing** | *I**'m used to** long board meetings.* |

Decision rule: if you can naturally add *"…but not anymore"*, it's **used to**. If
you mean *"it feels normal to me now"*, it's **be used to**. The two look alike and
mean opposite things — *I used to have long meetings* (they stopped) ≠ *I'm used to
long meetings* (I'm fine with them).

### Decision D — the future quartet: *will · going to · present continuous · present simple* (Units 026–028)

This is the heart of the week. All four point at the future; each answers a
**different question**. Ask them in order:

| Ask yourself… | If yes → use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Is it a **fixed timetable** (clock/agenda decides)? | **present simple** | *The offsite **starts** at nine.* |
| Is it a **personal arrangement** already booked (time + place)? | **present continuous** | *I**'m presenting** to the board on Thursday.* |
| Is it an **intention / decision already made** (no slot yet), or a **prediction from evidence**? | **going to** | *We**'re going to** double down on AI.* / *At this run-rate we**'re going to** overshoot.* |
| Is the decision being made **right now, as you speak** — or an offer/promise/refusal? | **will** | *"The report's late." → "**I'll** finish it tonight."* |

The line Brazilians most often cross: reaching for **will** for a plan already
made. If it was decided **before** you opened your mouth, it is **going to** or the
**present continuous**, never *will*. Reserve *will* for the decision taken *in the
moment* — and for offers (*Shall I…?*), promises (*I'll have it by nine*), and
refusals (*it won't move*).

**The overlap you can stop worrying about:** *going to* and the present continuous
often both fit a future plan. The nuance — *going to* = the **decision**, present
continuous = the **booked slot** — but if you use either for a genuine plan, you'll
be understood. The real errors are **will-for-a-plan** and **present-simple-for-my-
own-arrangement** (*"I chair the review at nine tomorrow"* ❌ → *"I'm chairing"*).

## 3. 🧪 10 recognition exercises (contrast-focused)

Answers and one-line explanations in section 5 — retrieve first.

1. **Helper check.** *"___ the CFO have the final numbers before we present?"* —
   **Does**, **Is**, or **Has**? And once you pick it, does *have* stay *have* or
   become *has*?

2. **Two helpers fighting.** Fix if needed: *"Do you can join the strategy sync
   tomorrow?"* — one word has to go. Which, and why?

3. **Shape after a helper.** *"The board has ___ a final position on the AI
   budget."* — **took** or **taken**? Name the word that forces the choice.

4. **Passive shape.** Fix if needed: *"The roadmap **was wrote** before the
   offsite."* — what's wrong, and what does *was* demand?

5. **used to vs be used to.** *"After a year in the role, I ___ presenting to the
   board — it doesn't rattle me anymore."* — **used to** or **am used to**? What
   would the other one mean?

6. **used to vs past simple.** Same event, one occasion: *"We ___ (approve) the
   first AI pilot in March 2024."* — **used to approve** or **approved**? Why?

7. **The future quartet — arrangement vs timetable.** *"The keynote ___ (start) at
   nine, but I ___ (chair) the innovation review straight after."* — which tense
   in each blank, and what decides it?

8. **will vs going to (the big one).** *"Look at the adoption curve — this ___
   (be) a much bigger rollout than we scoped."* — **will be** or **'s going to
   be**? Point to the word that gives it away.

9. **will vs going to — the plan trap.** *"It's already decided and in the deck —
   next quarter we ___ (increase) the AI budget by 20%."* — **will increase** or
   **'re going to increase**? Why not *will*?

10. **will — the on-the-spot offer.** *"The vendor just emailed a blocker." → "No
    problem, I ___ (call) them now."* — which future form, and why is *"I call
    them now"* wrong here even though Portuguese would allow the present?

## 4. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

Bring the **most confusable contrasts**, not the individual forms:

- **The future-quartet drill (Decision D) — the highest-value round.** Ask your
  tutor to describe an executive scenario and have you pick the right future form
  on the fly: a booked board slot (*present continuous*), a settled strategic
  decision with no date yet (*going to*), a prediction from a chart they show you
  (*going to*), and a problem they drop on you mid-sentence that you must react to
  (*will* — *"I'll handle it"*). Then have them try to **trap** you into *will* for
  a plan you clearly made earlier, and you catch it and switch to *going to* /
  present continuous. The reflex to build: **decided before I spoke → not *will*.**

- **The *used to* line (Decision C).** Role-play a "how the function has changed"
  interview: your tutor asks *"How did innovation governance work when you joined?"*
  and you answer in **used to** (*"We used to approve each pilot separately; there
  didn't use to be a governance layer"*), then contrast with **be used to** for how
  it feels now (*"I'm used to defending the portfolio to the CFO every quarter"*).
  Fight the Portuguese *costumava / costumo* pull that blurs the two.

- **The helper reflex (Decisions A & B).** Have your tutor fire rapid yes/no
  questions at you about your roadmap so you must build questions and negatives at
  speed — catching yourself before dropping *do/does/did* on a plain verb, and
  before saying *"has took"* / *"was wrote"* on an irregular. Low-glamour, but it's
  the wiring the future forms all sit on.

## 5. ✅ Gabarito — exercises

1. **Does** — and *have* stays **have** (*Does the CFO **have** the numbers?*). Here
   *have* is a **main** verb (possession), so present simple needs **does**, and
   after *does* the verb returns to its base — not *has*, not *Has the CFO…*.
2. Drop **do**: *"**Can** you join…?"* *can* is already the helper; *do* never sits
   in front of a modal.
3. **taken.** *has* (perfect) forces the **past participle**; *has took* is wrong.
4. **was written.** After *be* (here *was*) the passive demands the **past
   participle**, not the past simple *wrote*.
5. **am used to** — *be used to + -ing* = accustomed to it now, present and ongoing.
   *used to present* would mean you did it habitually in the past and **stopped**.
6. **approved.** A single, dated occasion → **past simple**. *used to approve* is
   for a **repeated** habit, which one March event isn't.
7. **starts** / **'m chairing.** The keynote runs on a **timetable** → present
   simple; chairing the review is **your arrangement** → present continuous.
8. **'s going to be.** A **prediction from present evidence** — the giveaway is
   *"Look at the adoption curve"*; visible evidence → *going to*, not *will*.
9. **'re going to increase.** The decision was made **before** speaking (*already
   decided and in the deck*) → *going to*. *will* is only for a decision made **as
   you speak**.
10. **I'll call** them now. A decision made **on the spot** in reaction to the news
    → *will*. Portuguese uses the present for such offers (*"Eu ligo pra eles"*),
    but English **must** use *will* there.

## 6. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**O que deve estar sólido.** A espinha da semana são duas coisas. Primeiro, a
**mecânica dos auxiliares**: você adiciona **do/does/did** só para fazer pergunta
ou negativa no presente/passado simples — se já existe outro auxiliar (*be, have,
can, will*), o *do* some (Unidade 023); e depois de um auxiliar (*have / was*) o
verbo irregular vira **particípio** (*taken, written*), nunca *took / wrote*
(Unidade 024). Segundo, e mais valioso, o **quarteto do futuro**: *timetable* →
presente simples (*starts at nine*); **compromisso agendado** → presente contínuo
(*I'm presenting on Thursday*); **decisão/intenção já tomada** ou **previsão pela
evidência** → *going to*; **decisão na hora**, ofertas, promessas e recusas →
*will* (Unidades 026–028). Some a isso o *used to*, que é o **espelho no passado**:
hábito ou estado que **não é mais verdade** (Unidade 025).

**As armadilhas para vencer.** A número um é usar **will para um plano já
decidido** — se você resolveu **antes** de falar, é *going to* ou presente
contínuo, nunca *will*; guarde o *will* para a decisão tomada **enquanto você
fala** e para ofertas (*Shall I…?*). A segunda é confundir **used to do** (hábito
que parou) com **be used to doing** (estar acostumado, presente) — o *costumava* vs
*estou acostumado* do português. E a terceira, mais silenciosa: derrubar o
auxiliar em perguntas (*"You work here?"* → **Do you**…?) e dizer *"has took" /
"was wrote"* nos irregulares. Se algo aqui ainda vacila, releia **`lessons/
2026-08-08-unit-027.md` (going to)** e **`lessons/2026-08-09-unit-028.md`
(will / shall)** — é ali que o quarteto do futuro se decide.
