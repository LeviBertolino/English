### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** Why are we still debating the AI roadmap in October?
> **You:** Honestly, **I wish we had decided this in Q2.** We'd be executing by now.
> **CEO:** So what's holding us back today?
> **You:** Visibility. **I wish I knew** how fast our competitors are really moving — we're guessing.
> **CFO:** The board keeps reopening the budget line every meeting.
> **You:** Exactly. **I wish they wouldn't keep reopening it** — we can't plan on a moving number.
> **CEO:** And the talent gap?
> **You:** **I wish we had two more senior ML leads.** With the team we have, we're stretched thin.
> **CEO:** I hope we close that gap next quarter.
> **You:** So do I — but *hope* is for things that still might happen. Right now I just **wish** we were further along.

Notice the shape. Every *wish* here is followed by a **past** form — *I wish we **had** decided, I wish I **knew**, I wish we **had** two more leads* — yet nobody is talking about the past. That past tense is doing the exact same job it did in yesterday's unreal conditionals: it marks the thing as **not true now**, something you regret or long for. And watch the last line: *wish* and *hope* are not the same word. *Hope* is for what might really happen; *wish* is for what isn't so.

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

*wish* opens a two-part sentence, and the second part goes one step "back", just like the *if*-part of an unreal conditional:

| You want to… | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| change a **present state / fact** | *wish* + **past simple** | *I wish I **knew** the numbers.* (I don't know them) |
| change a present state with *be* | *wish* + **were** (all subjects) | *I wish I **were** further along.* |
| regret a **past** thing | *wish* + **past perfect** (*had* + done) | *I wish we **had decided** earlier.* (we didn't) |
| want someone/something to **change their behaviour** | *wish* + **would** + base verb | *I wish the board **would decide**.* |

**Key contrasts built into the forms:**

- *wish* + **past simple** = a situation **right now** you'd change: *I wish I **had** more runway.* (I don't, now)
- *wish* + **would** = you want a **repeated action or behaviour to stop/start** — usually someone else's, often with mild frustration: *I wish they **wouldn't keep** changing the scope.*
- You normally **don't** say *I wish I would* for your own actions — for yourself use *wish* + past (*I wish I had more time*), because *would* is about wanting *another* party to change.

**Contractions:** *would* → *'d* (*I wish they'd decide*); *would not* → *wouldn't*.

The one rule that mirrors yesterday: after *wish*, the clause is **past in form but present in meaning** — don't use the present tense (❌ *I wish I know*).

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why does this structure exist?**
*wish* lets you name the **gap between reality and what you want** — regret, longing, or frustration about a situation that is **not the way you'd like it**. In executive talk it's the honest, non-defensive way to admit a constraint (*I wish we had more data*), to flag a behaviour that's blocking you (*I wish the board would commit to a number*), or to own a missed window (*I wish we had moved in Q2*) — without blaming anyone or pretending the problem isn't real. The past tense after *wish* is the quiet signal: **this is contrary to fact — I'm telling you what *isn't* so.**

**Decision rule #1 — *wish* vs *hope* (the one that trips everyone up):**
This is the single most important distinction, because Portuguese blurs it. Ask: **is this still realistically possible, or is it already contrary to fact / very unlikely?**

| | Word | Followed by | Use when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **real possibility (future)** | *hope* | present / *will* | it still **might happen** |
| **contrary to fact / unlikely (now)** | *wish* | **past** | it **isn't so**, and you regret/long for it |

Side-by-side minimal pairs:

- *I **hope** we **win** the enterprise deal.* → it's still open; a real future possibility.
- *I **wish** we **had won** the enterprise deal.* → we **lost** it; regret about a done fact.

- *I **hope** the board **approves** the budget next week.* → the vote is coming, outcome genuinely open.
- *I **wish** the board **would approve** the budget.* → you're frustrated they keep stalling; you doubt it.

Say *"I wish"* for something that's actually on the table and you sound defeatist about a live opportunity; say *"I hope"* for something already lost and you sound out of touch. The word choice broadcasts whether you think it's still winnable.

**Decision rule #2 — *wish* + past vs *wish* + would:**
Both are "wishes," but they point at different things. Ask: **am I wishing a *state* were different, or wishing someone would *act* differently?**

- **State now → past simple:** *I wish we **had** a bigger AI team.* (the size of the team — a fact)
- **Behaviour I want changed → would:** *I wish the board **would stop** reopening the budget.* (a repeated action, usually by someone else, often annoying)

A useful tell: if you could add *"...but they keep doing it"* or *"...but they won't"*, you want **would**. If it's simply *"...but that's how things are right now,"* you want the **past simple**.

**PT→EN mapping:**
Portuguese expresses *wish* mainly with **"Eu queria que…" / "Eu gostaria que…" / "Quem me dera que…"** + imperfect subjunctive, and regret about the past with **"Eu queria ter…" / "Quem me dera que tivéssemos…"**:
- *"**Queria que** o conselho **decidisse** logo."* → *I **wish** the board **would decide** soon.*
- *"**Queria** mais dados."* / *"Quem me dera ter mais dados."* → *I **wish** I **had** more data.*
- *"**Queria ter** decidido no Q2."* → *I **wish** we **had decided** in Q2.*

Where it betrays you — **two classic traps**:
1. Portuguese uses the **same verb (*querer / gostaria*)** for both "wish" and polite "would like," and uses **"espero"** for "hope." Brazilians routinely swap *wish* and *hope*: they say *"I wish we win the deal"* meaning *"I hope we win."* In English, *wish* + **present** is simply wrong, and *wish* for a live future possibility sends the wrong message.
2. Because *"queria que"* takes the **subjunctive**, the instinct is to reach for a special form — but English just uses the **plain past** after *wish* (*I wish I knew*, not *I wish I know* and not *I wish I would know*).

**Top typical Brazilian-speaker errors:**

1. ***wish* + present tense** (the biggest one):
   - ❌ *I wish I **know** more about our competitors' roadmap.*
   - ✅ *I wish I **knew** more about our competitors' roadmap.*
2. ***wish* used where English needs *hope*** (live future possibility):
   - ❌ *I **wish** the board **approves** the budget next week.* (the vote is really coming)
   - ✅ *I **hope** the board **approves** the budget next week.*
3. ***was* instead of *were*** after *wish* (same as *if I were*):
   - ❌ *I wish I **was** further along with the AI strategy.*
   - ✅ *I wish I **were** further along with the AI strategy.*

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. **I wish I knew how fast our competitors are really moving on AI.**
   *Executive. State now → past simple (*knew*). You don't have the visibility, and it's a present fact you'd change — not a future hope.*
2. **I wish we had decided the roadmap in Q2 — we'd be executing by now.**
   *Executive. Regret about the **past** → past perfect (*had decided*). The decision didn't happen; this owns the missed window without blaming anyone.*
3. **I wish the board would commit to a single budget number.**
   *Executive. Wanting a **behaviour** to change → *would*. You could add "…but they keep reopening it"; that tell means *would*, not the past simple.*
4. **I wish we had two more senior ML leads.**
   *Executive. Present **state** (team size) → past simple (*had*). Compare "I hope we hire two leads" — that's the still-possible future; this is the gap right now.*
5. **I hope we close the talent gap next quarter — but right now I wish we were further along.**
   *Executive. The contrast in one line: *hope* for the live future, *wish* + *were* for the contrary-to-fact present. *were*, never *was*.*
6. **I wish the market gave us a clearer signal before we commit the innovation budget.**
   *Executive. Present state of the world → past simple (*gave*). Not "I wish the market will give" — *wish* never takes the present/future.*
7. **I wish I had more time this weekend — I'd finally rest.**
   *Everyday. Present shortage → past simple (*had*), with the unreal *would* ('d) in the second half, exactly like yesterday's conditionals.*
8. **I wish my neighbour wouldn't park across my driveway every morning.**
   *Everyday. A repeated, annoying **behaviour** of someone else → *wouldn't*. The "every morning" + "but they keep doing it" feel is the signal for *would*.*

### 8. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

1. Ask your tutor to run a **"wish vs hope" sorting drill**: they throw you a mix of scenarios — some still open (*"the enterprise deal is on the table"*), some already lost or clearly contrary to fact (*"the Q2 window is gone," "you don't have the data"*) — and you must respond on the spot with *"I hope…"* (+ present) for the live ones and *"I wish…"* (+ past) for the contrary-to-fact ones, until the choice is instant.
2. Then a **"frustration round" for *wish* + would**: the tutor plays a stakeholder with an annoying repeated behaviour (the board reopening the budget every meeting, a vendor who keeps missing deadlines, a peer who reschedules every sync) and you push back diplomatically with *"I wish they would / wouldn't…"*, keeping *would* for behaviour and the plain past for states.

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 095):**
1. The ***if*-part** is wrong: ❌ *If we **would double**…* → ✅ *If we **doubled** the budget, we'd ship twice as fast.* No *would* after *if* — the *if*-part takes **past simple**.
2. ***approves / will*** — *"If the board **approves** the budget, we **will** start in Q1."* Sign-off is realistically expected, so it's a **real** conditional (present + *will*), not the hypothetical past + *would*.
3. *"If I **were** you, I **wouldn't** (or *I'd*)…"* — **were** for every subject in an unreal condition, and **would/wouldn't** keeps it advice rather than a command. *(e.g. "If I were you, I wouldn't lock us into one vendor.")*

**Spaced review:**
- **Unit 089:** ✅ *"We **have already submitted** the budget, and the board **never rejects** a proposal we've prepped this well."* Middle-position adverbs go **after** the auxiliary (*have **already** submitted*) and **before** a single main verb (*never rejects*) — not after the object.
- **Unit 066:** ***None*** — *"How much runway…? — **None**."* *None* stands alone, replacing "no runway," and answers a **quantity** question. *No* can't stand alone (it needs a noun: *no runway*), and *no one* is only for **people**, not an amount.

**Recognition drill:**
1. ❌ *I wish I **know**…* → ✅ *I wish I **knew** the real cost…*. After *wish*, the clause is **past in form, present in meaning** — never the present tense.
2. ***hope / win*** — *"I **hope** we **win** it."* The deal is still open (a real future possibility), so *hope* + present, not *wish* + past.
3. ***would / stop moving it*** — *"I wish she **would stop** moving it."* It's a **repeated behaviour** you want changed ("keeps pushing… every week"); the "but she keeps doing it" tell means *wish* + **would**, not the past simple of a state.
4. ❌ *I wish I **was**…* → ✅ *I wish I **were** the one presenting…*. In professional English, *wish* takes **were** for every subject (same rule as *if I were*).

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

*wish* serve para nomear a **distância entre a realidade e o que você gostaria** — arrependimento, desejo ou irritação com algo que **não está do jeito que você queria**. A forma espelha a condicional irreal de ontem: depois de *wish*, a oração vai um passo "para trás" — **passado simples** para um estado presente que você mudaria (*I wish I **knew*** = não sei, agora), **past perfect** (*had* + particípio) para arrependimento do passado (*I wish we **had decided** in Q2*), e ***were*** para todos os sujeitos com o verbo *be* (*I wish I **were** further along*). A regra de ouro: depois de *wish*, nunca use o presente (❌ *I wish I know*).

Dois tropeços vêm direto do português. Primeiro, **wish ≠ hope**: português usa *querer/gostaria* e *esperar* de forma embaralhada, mas em inglês *hope* é para o que **ainda pode acontecer** (*I hope we win the deal* — aberto) e *wish* é para o que **não é verdade agora** ou já se perdeu (*I wish we had won* — perdemos). Dizer *wish* para uma oportunidade viva soa derrotista. Segundo, *wish* + **would** é para quando você quer que **alguém mude um comportamento repetido** (*I wish the board **would** commit to a number* — "mas vivem reabrindo"), enquanto o passado simples descreve um **estado** atual (*I wish we **had** more data*). O teste: se cabe "…mas eles continuam fazendo isso," use *would*; se é só "…mas é assim que está agora," use o passado simples.
