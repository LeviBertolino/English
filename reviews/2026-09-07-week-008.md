# 🔁 Review Week 008 — Units 51 to 56

*One week, one big idea: **English carries meaning in light, everyday verbs + a
small word** — a gerund after a preposition, `get`/`do`/`make`/`have` + a noun,
a verb + a particle. Portuguese collapses most of these into one heavy verb
(*fazer, ficar, ter*), so the trap all week is the same: sounding "translated"
instead of native. This review is about picking the right light verb / particle
under pressure. ~15 min, read-only.*

---

### 2. 📋 Recap — one line per unit

- **Unit 51 — Preposition + -ing** — after **any preposition** (*in, of, before, without, about, at, to-as-preposition*), a verb must wear its noun costume: **-ing** (*"interested in **investing**"*, *"before **deciding**"*, *"without **approving**"*). The trigger is the preposition, not the verb.
- **Unit 52 — get** — the light verb of **change** and **acquiring**: *get ready* (become ready), *get aligned* (become aligned), *get to the meeting* (arrive), *get approved* (passive — receive approval). *get* = the moment you cross the line; *be* = where you already are.
- **Unit 53 — do and make** — **make** produces a **result** you can then hold (*make a decision, make a profit, make an offer*); **do** performs an **activity** (*do the research, do the analysis, do business*). Portuguese *fazer* hides the split.
- **Unit 54 — have** — **have + activity noun** is the natural way to say *do / experience* an event (*have a meeting, have a look, have a call, have breakfast*). This *have* takes *having* and never *have got*; possession-*have* does the opposite.
- **Unit 55 — Phrasal verbs (object position)** — the spoken, boardroom default (*roll out, carry out, put off, call off, turn down*) beats the formal Latinate rival in a live room. With a **pronoun** object the particle **must split**: *"put **it** off"*, never *"put off it"*.
- **Unit 56 — Phrasal verbs 2 (the particle has meaning)** — the little word is the reusable part: *out* = finish/remove/make-visible, *in* = enter/submit/include. Same verb, different particle = different reality (*pull out* = withdraw vs *pull off* = succeed; *work out* = solve vs *work up* = draft).

### 3. 🗺️ Confusion map — which light verb / word carries the meaning?

The whole week is one question: **you have an idea — which small English machine
delivers it?** Here is when each wins.

| You want to say… | Reach for… | Unit | Signal / test | Anchor example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| an **action** sitting after a preposition | **-ing** (gerund) | 51 | a preposition is in front (*in, of, before, without, at*) | *"committed to **scaling**"*, *"before **committing**"* |
| **become** / cross into a state | **get** + adj/participle | 52 | you're **not there yet**; it's a change | *"Let's **get aligned**."* (we aren't yet) |
| you're **already** in the state | **be** + adj | 52 | no change, just describing now | *"We **are aligned**."* |
| you **produced a result** (now you hold it) | **make** + noun | 53 | at the end there's a **new thing** | *"We **made a decision**."* |
| you **performed an activity** (effort, no new object) | **do** + noun | 53 | it names **work done**, no product | *"We **did the analysis**."* |
| you **hold / experience an event** | **have** + activity noun | 54 | you can say *"having"*; can't use *have got* | *"Let's **have a call** at four."* |
| you **own / possess** something | **have (got)** | 54 | no *"having"*; *have got* works | *"We've **got** a strong team."* |
| the natural spoken verb in a meeting | **phrasal verb** (verb + particle) | 55 | you're speaking, not writing a memo | *"Let's **roll out** the tool."* |
| the meaning shading a single verb can't give | **the right particle** | 56 | *out* = finish/remove/reveal; *in* = enter/submit | *"**work out** the ROI"* vs *"**work up** a draft"* |

**The four traps that catch Brazilian speakers most:**

1. **The `to` disguise (Unit 51).** Not every `to` takes a base verb. In
   *"committed **to** investing"* and *"look forward **to** presenting"*, `to` is a
   **preposition** → force **-ing**. Test: can a **noun** follow it? *"committed to
   **the plan**"* works → preposition → *"committed to **investing**"*. Compare
   *"I want to **invest**"* (real infinitive → base verb).
2. **get vs be (Unit 52).** Portuguese *ficar* covers both the change and the
   state, so Brazilians reach for *be* when they mean *become*. *"We are aligned"*
   claims agreement **already exists**; *"Let's get aligned"* says we **still need
   to reach it**. Also *"get **to** the office"* (arrive), never *"get **in** the
   office"* for arriving.
3. **do vs make = the *fazer* trap (Unit 53).** One Portuguese verb, two English
   ones. Default errors: *"make the research / make a course"* (❌ → **do**) and
   *"do a decision / do a mistake"* (❌ → **make**). Ask: **new thing → make;
   activity → do.**
4. **The pronoun splits the phrasal verb (Unit 55).** With a noun, either order
   is fine (*"put off the launch"* / *"put the launch off"*). With a **pronoun**,
   it **must** split: *"put **it** off"* — never *"put off it"*. Portuguese gives no
   warning here.

### 4. 🧪 10 recognition exercises (contrast — pick the right structure)

*Answers with explanations at the end. Force yourself to choose before scrolling.*

1. *"The board is interested in ___ (invest) more aggressively next year."* → **to invest** / **investing** / **invest**?
2. *"We can't sign ___ (see) a real reference customer first."* → **without seeing** / **without to see** / **without see**?
3. *"Let's ___ on the number before we walk into the room."* (we haven't agreed yet) → **be aligned** / **get aligned** / **have aligned**?
4. *"By Friday the whole leadership team ___ on the AI strategy."* (state — it's settled) → **gets aligned** / **is aligned** / **is getting aligned**?
5. *"We ___ a decision on the vendor after we ___ the analysis."* → **made / did** / **did / made** / **made / made**?
6. *"The unit ___ a strong profit this quarter, and the team ___ really well."* → **made / did** / **did / made** / **made / made**?
7. *"I'll ___ at the deck tonight and send notes."* → **have a look** / **do a look** / **make a look**?
8. *"We ___ three AI vendors shortlisted — I'm confident in the list."* → **are having** / **have** / **are making**?
9. *"The launch date slipped, so we had to ___ ."* → **put it off** / **put off it** / **off put it**?
10. *"The cheaper vendor is gone — we ___ them ___ after the security review."* (eliminated the option) → **ruled … out** / **rolled … out** / **pulled … off**?

### 5. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula (with your tutor)

Pick the contrasts that felt shakiest and turn them into live practice:

1. **The *fazer* split (Units 53–54).** Ask your tutor to fire rapid prompts —
   *"a decision, the research, a profit, a meeting, a look, a mistake, business,
   breakfast"* — and you supply **make / do / have** with no thinking time. This
   is pure collocation memory; only speed drills fix it.
2. **get vs be, live (Unit 52).** Role-play a pre-board huddle: your tutor plays
   the CFO. Every time you mean *"we still need to reach X"* you must say
   **get** (*get aligned, get approved, get ready*); every time you mean *"we're
   already there"* you must say **be**. Have them catch every slip into *be*
   where you meant *become*.
3. **Sound native, not translated (Units 55–56).** Take five formal sentences
   (*postpone, cancel, implement, reduce, reject*) and have your tutor make you
   re-say each as a **phrasal verb** in a meeting register (*put off, call off,
   carry out / roll out, cut back on, turn down*) — then swap the object for a
   **pronoun** and force the split (*"put it off"*).

### 6. ✅ Gabarito (answers + one-line why)

1. **investing** — after the preposition *in*, the verb takes **-ing** (Unit 51).
2. **without seeing** — *without* is a preposition → **-ing** (Unit 51); *"without
   to see"* is the classic direct-translation error.
3. **get aligned** — you **haven't** agreed yet, so you need the **change** verb;
   *be aligned* would falsely claim agreement already exists (Unit 52).
4. **is aligned** — *"by Friday… it's settled"* describes the **finished state**, so
   **be**, not the change verb (Unit 52).
5. **made / did** — a decision is a **result** you now hold (**make**); the analysis
   is an **activity** (**do**) (Unit 53).
6. **made / did** — a profit is a **produced result** (**make a profit**); *"did
   well"* is fixed — performance as **activity** (**do well**) (Unit 53).
7. **have a look** — *have + activity noun* is the idiomatic way to say *look*;
   *do/make a look* don't exist (Unit 54).
8. **have** — this is **possession** (you own the shortlist), so plain **have**; you
   can't say *"are having"* for possession (Unit 54).
9. **put it off** — with a **pronoun** object the particle **must split**; *"put off
   it"* is ungrammatical (Unit 55).
10. **ruled … out** — *rule out* = eliminate an option; *roll out* = launch, *pull
    off* = succeed — wrong meanings from the wrong particle (Unit 56).

### 7. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**O que já deve estar sólido:** depois de **qualquer preposição**, o verbo vira
**-ing** (*"interested in **investing**", "before **deciding**"*) — Unidade 51. E a
lógica dos verbos "leves": **get** é a **mudança** (*get aligned* = ainda vamos nos
alinhar), **be** é o **estado** (*be aligned* = já alinhados) — Unidade 52.

**O que provavelmente ainda precisa de treino** — tudo que o português resolve com
**uma** palavra e o inglês com **duas**: (1) o *fazer* que se parte em **make**
(resultado: *make a decision, make a profit*) vs **do** (atividade: *do the
research, do well*) — Unidade 53; (2) **have + substantivo de atividade** para
eventos (*have a meeting, have a look*), que aceita *having* e nunca *have got* —
Unidade 54; (3) os **phrasal verbs**, que soam nativos numa reunião (*roll out, put
off, call off*) e que **obrigam a partícula a se separar quando o objeto é
pronome** (*"put **it** off"*, nunca *"put off it"*) — Unidade 55; e (4) a
**partícula que carrega o sentido** (*out* = terminar/remover/revelar, *in* =
entrar/entregar), onde trocar a palavrinha muda tudo (*pull out* = sair vs *pull
off* = conseguir) — Unidade 56.

**Reler se sobrar tempo:** **Unidade 53 (do/make)** e **Unidade 52 (get)** — são as
duas armadilhas de interferência mais persistentes desta semana.
