### 1. 📘 Unit 060 — the possessive 's (the board's decision) and 's vs of

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** I want a single line for the board tomorrow. Where do we stand?
> **You:** **The board's** own numbers back us up. **The CFO's** model shows the AI bet paying
> back inside **two years' time**, and **this quarter's** results already point that way.
> **CEO:** And the risk?
> **You:** **The vendor's** roadmap slips sometimes, and **our competitors'** move is faster than
> we'd like. But **the company's** position is still the strongest in the market.
> **CEO:** Good. Put **today's** version in front of them, not last week's.

Notice the little **'s** doing all the work before any rule. *The board's numbers*, *the CFO's
model*, *this quarter's results*, *the company's position*, *today's version* — English glues an
apostrophe + s to the **owner** and puts it **in front of** the thing owned. Portuguese does the
opposite: *"os números **do** board"*, *"a posição **da** empresa"* — owner **after**, joined by
*de*. That flip, and knowing when English wants *'s* and when it wants *of*, is today's lesson.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

You attach **'s** to the **owner**, and the owner comes **first**:

| Owner | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| singular noun / name | owner **+ 's** | *the CFO**'s** model*, *Sofia**'s** deck* |
| plural noun ending in **-s** | owner **+ '** (apostrophe only) | *the investors**'** expectations*, *our competitors**'** move* |
| irregular plural (no -s) | owner **+ 's** | *the people**'s** feedback*, *the children**'s** books* |
| time expressions | time **+ 's / '** | *today**'s** meeting*, *two years**'** time* |

**Key facts:**
- The owner goes **before** the thing owned: *the board's decision* = *a decisão do board* — the
  order is **reversed** from Portuguese.
- **Singular → 's; plural already ending in -s → just the apostrophe.** *The investor's report*
  (one investor) vs *the investors' report* (several). The apostrophe **position** carries the
  meaning.
- Names ending in *-s* usually take **'s** in speech: *"James's office"*, *"the boss's call"*.
- **'s ≠ is/has here.** In *"the board's decision"* the *'s* means **belonging to**, not *is* or
  *has*. Context tells them apart: *"the board's meeting"* (of the board) vs *"the board's met"*
  (has met).

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why does 's exist?** English has two ways to show possession — **'s** and **of** — and it
doesn't spread them evenly. *'s* is the natural, default form for **people, animals,
organizations, and time**: the owner is a *someone* (or a *when*), and English puts that owner up
front with a quick *'s*. *of* is the fallback for **things** — inanimate objects, parts, abstract
relationships — where *'s* would sound odd. Portuguese has only **de** for all of it, so Levi has
one tool where English has two, and no built-in signal for which to reach for.

**Decision rule — 's vs of. Ask: is the owner a person / group / time, or a plain thing?**

| Owner is… | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| a person or name | **'s** | *the CEO's calendar*, *Sofia's model* |
| an organization / group | **'s** (of also possible) | *the board's decision*, *the company's strategy* |
| a time period | **'s** | *this quarter's results*, *a week's notice* |
| a thing / object / part | **of** | *the top **of** the page*, *the end **of** the report* |

Minimal pair — same two nouns, the choice changes the register (and sometimes the sense):
- *"the **board's** decision"* ✅ (natural — the board is a group of people)
- *"the decision **of** the board"* — grammatical but **heavier, more formal/legalistic**; in a
  live meeting the *'s* version is what a native says.
- *"the **report's** cover"* → sounds off (a report is a thing) → ✅ *"the cover **of** the
  report."*

**PT→EN mapping — where *de* betrays Levi.** Portuguese builds every possessive with *de* +
owner, **after** the noun: *a decisão **do** board*, *a estratégia **da** empresa*, *os
resultados **deste** trimestre*. Translating *de* straight gives *"the decision **of** the
board"*, *"the results **of** this quarter"* — understandable but unnatural, because English
wants **'s** for people, groups and time. Two extra traps: Portuguese never uses an apostrophe,
so Levi tends to **drop the 's entirely** (*"the CEO calendar"*), and he can't hear where the
apostrophe goes on a **plural** owner (*investors' vs investor's*).

**Top typical Brazilian-speaker errors:**

1. ❌ *"the strategy **of** the company", "the decision **of** the board"* → ✅ *"the **company's**
   strategy", "the **board's** decision"* — for people, groups and organizations, English defaults
   to **'s**, not the direct-from-Portuguese *of*.
2. ❌ *"the **CEO** calendar", "the **CFO** approval"* (no apostrophe) → ✅ *"the **CEO's**
   calendar", "the **CFO's** approval"* — Portuguese has no *'s*, so it's easy to drop it entirely;
   English needs it to mark the owner.
3. ❌ *"the results **of this quarter**", "we gave **one week of** notice"* → ✅ *"**this quarter's**
   results", "we gave **one week's** notice"* — **time** expressions take *'s* too, not *of*.

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. *"**The board's** decision on the AI budget won't come before Thursday."*
   *— 's for a group of people; "the decision of the board" is the heavier, translated-from-*de* version.*
2. *"**The CFO's** model shows payback inside two years."*
   *— person → 's, owner first; the classic slip is dropping it: "the CFO model" ✗.*
3. *"**This quarter's** results already point in the right direction."*
   *— time expression → 's; "the results of this quarter" is the direct-from-Portuguese error.*
4. *"We need to respect **our competitors'** speed to market."*
   *— plural owner ending in -s → apostrophe only (competitors'), not "competitor's" (that's one rival).*
5. *"**The company's** position is the strongest in the market."*
   *— organization → 's; natural and light, where "the position of the company" would sound formal.*
6. *"They gave us **a week's** notice before pulling out of the deal."*
   *— time + 's again; "one week of notice" is a common interference error.*
7. *"Have you seen **Sofia's** deck? It's on **the last page of** the report."*
   *— everyday mix: Sofia (person) → 's; "the report" (a thing) → of. Both rules in one line.*
8. *"That's **my daughter's** school, right at **the end of** the street."*
   *— everyday: daughter (person) → 's; the street (a thing/place) → of.*

### 8. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

1. Ask your tutor to fire Portuguese possessive phrases from your world — *"a decisão do board",
   "a estratégia da empresa", "o modelo do CFO", "os resultados deste trimestre", "a expectativa
   dos investidores"* — and you flip each one into English **owner-first with 's** on the spot
   (*"the board's decision"*), never the translated *"the decision of the board"*. The goal is to
   reverse the word order automatically.
2. Then a **plural-apostrophe round**: have them mix singular and plural owners — *one investor /
   several investors, a competitor / our competitors, the employee / the employees* — and you say
   the full phrase, landing the apostrophe in the right place (*investor's* vs *investors'*). This
   is the one you can't hear, so drill it out loud until the position feels obvious.

2. **me** — *"between the CEO and **me**."* After a preposition it's **always** the object form;

3. *"**She** trusts **him** on the numbers."* — *she* is the **doer** before the verb → subject;

**Spaced review:**
- *(Unit 053 — do / make)* *"We need to **make** a decision this week, but first the team has to
  **do** the analysis."* — a decision is a **result you produce** → *make*; analysis is an
  **activity/task** → *do*. Swapping them (*"do a decision / make the analysis"*) is the classic
  *fazer* slip.
- *(Unit 037 — would like)* *"**Would you like me to** present the AI roadmap to the board?"* —
  English offers to do something with *would like + me to*. *"Would you like that I present"* is a
  word-for-word map of *"gostaria que eu apresentasse"* and isn't English.

**Recognition drill:**
1. **the board's decision** — both are grammatical, but the board is a **group of people**, so the
   *'s* form is the natural default; *"the decision of the board"* is heavier and more formal, the
   kind of thing that reads well in a contract, not in a live meeting.
2. **investors'** — *"the **investors'** feedback"* (apostrophe **after** the -s, because the owner
   is plural). *"investor's"* would mean **one** investor's feedback — the apostrophe position is
   the whole difference.
3. ❌ → ✅ *"I'll send you **this quarter's** results by Friday."* — a **time** expression takes
   *'s*; *"the results of this quarter"* is the direct-from-*deste-trimestre* interference error.
4. *"The **CEO's** calendar is full, so the **CFO's** approval will have to wait."* — people take
   *'s* (owner first). *"the CEO calendar"* drops the apostrophe (Portuguese has none) and *"the
   approval of the CFO"* keeps the *de*-order English avoids for people.

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

Para dizer que algo **pertence** a alguém, o inglês cola **'s** no dono e coloca o dono **na
frente** da coisa — o inverso do português. *"A decisão do board"* vira *"the **board's**
decision"*; *"a estratégia da empresa"* vira *"the **company's** strategy"*. A regra de escolha:
para **pessoas, grupos/organizações e tempo**, use **'s** (*the CFO's model*, *this quarter's
results*, *a week's notice*); para **coisas/objetos**, use **of** (*the top **of** the page*, *the
end **of** the report*). Dono no singular → **'s**; dono no plural já terminado em -s → só o
apóstrofo: *investor's* (um) × *investors'* (vários) — a **posição** do apóstrofo muda o sentido.

As armadilhas vêm do *de* único do português. (1) Traduzir *de* direto gera o pesado *"the
decision **of** the board"* / *"the results **of** this quarter"* — o natural é **'s**. (2) Como o
português não tem apóstrofo, é fácil **esquecer o 's** e dizer *"the CEO calendar"* em vez de *"the
CEO's calendar"*. (3) Tempo também pede *'s*: *"os resultados deste trimestre"* → *"**this
quarter's** results"*, não *"of this quarter"*. Se a diferença entre dono e a coisa possuída ainda
embolar com os pronomes possessivos (*mine/yours/hers*), releia `lessons/2026-09-08-unit-058.md`.
