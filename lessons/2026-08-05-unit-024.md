### 1. 📘 Unit 024 — Regular and irregular verbs (past simple vs past participle)

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CFO:** Has the board **taken** a final position on the AI budget?
> **You:** Not yet. They **reviewed** the numbers on Monday and **asked** for one
> more scenario. The revised model was **built** last week and **sent** to them
> yesterday.
> **CFO:** So nothing has been **decided**?
> **You:** The direction is **set** — we've **chosen** the platform. Only the
> funding **remains** open.

Notice, before any rule, that two kinds of verb are doing two different jobs.
*reviewed, asked, decided, remains* are **regular** — you just add **-ed**.
*taken, built, sent, set, chosen* are **irregular** — no *-ed*, each with its own
shape. And look **where** the irregular ones land: after *has / been / was / is*
(*has taken, been decided, was built, is set*). That's the whole point of this
unit — the form a verb takes depends on **who stands in front of it**.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

Every verb has three shapes you need: the **base**, the **past simple**, and the
**past participle**.

| Type | base | past simple | past participle | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Regular** | work | work**ed** | work**ed** | past = participle, both **-ed** |
| **Regular** | decide | decid**ed** | decid**ed** | (add **-d** after -e) |
| **Irregular** | take | **took** | **taken** | three different shapes |
| **Irregular** | write | **wrote** | **written** | three different shapes |
| **Irregular** | build | **built** | **built** | past = participle (but not -ed) |
| **Irregular** | set | **set** | **set** | all three the **same** |

**Where each shape is used** — this is the load-bearing part:

| Shape | Used for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **past simple** | a finished past action **on its own** (Unit 020) | *The board **took** a decision.* |
| **past participle** | after **have / has / had** (perfect) | *The board **has taken** a decision.* |
| **past participle** | after **be** (passive, Unit 021) | *A decision **was taken**.* |

For **regular** verbs the two shapes are identical (*reviewed / reviewed*), so
you never notice the difference. The **only** place the choice bites you is with
**irregular** verbs, where *took ≠ taken*, *wrote ≠ written*, *did ≠ done*.

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why does this distinction exist?** English marks *"the board did this"* (a past
event) differently from *"the board has done this"* / *"this was done"* (a result
or a passive). For regular verbs one form covers both jobs, so the language hides
the split. Irregular verbs expose it — and because *have done* and *is done* are
exactly the structures you drilled all week (Units 016–021), a wrong participle
now breaks a sentence you otherwise built correctly.

**Decision rule — past simple or past participle?** Look at the **word directly in
front** of the verb slot:
- **A helper is there** — *have / has / had* (perfect) or *is / was / been*
  (passive) → use the **participle**: *taken, written, built, chosen, done*.
- **Nothing is there** — the verb is the plain past action itself → use the
  **past simple**: *took, wrote, built, chose, did*.

Minimal pairs — same verb, the helper flips the shape:

| Meaning | ✅ Correct | ❌ Wrong | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| plain past action | *The board **took** the decision on Monday.* | *has took* here | no helper → **past simple** *took* |
| result now (perfect) | *The board **has taken** the decision.* | *has took* | *has* → needs **participle** *taken* |
| passive | *The decision **was taken** last week.* | *was took* | *was* → needs **participle** *taken* |
| plain past action | *We **wrote** the proposal in March.* | *have wrote* here | no helper → **past simple** *wrote* |
| result now (perfect) | *We **have written** the proposal.* | *have wrote* | *have* → needs **participle** *written* |

**PT→EN mapping.** Good news first: Portuguese **has the same split** — *fez*
(pretérito) vs *feito* (particípio), *escreveu* vs *escrito*, *tomou* vs
*tomado*. So the **concept** is already yours; *"foi feito"* = *"was done"*,
*"tem escrito"* ≈ *"has written"*. The trap is purely **vocabulary and reflex**:
(1) you must **memorise** each English irregular form — there's no rule that
generates *taken* from *take*; and (2) under pressure the ear accepts *"has
took"* because it *sounds* close, exactly as *"tem tomado"* would never slip in
Portuguese. Train the front-word check, not a translation.

A second, opposite trap: **over-regularising**. Portuguese lets you feel a verb
is "normal", so the instinct is to bolt *-ed* onto an irregular verb — *buyed,
taked, writed, breaked*. English simply doesn't: those verbs are *bought, took,
wrote, broke*, and you have to know them by heart.

**Top Brazilian-speaker errors:**
- ❌ *The board **has took** the decision.* → ✅ *The board **has taken** the
  decision.* — *after* has *you need the **participle** (*taken*), not the past
  simple (*took*).*
- ❌ *The budget **was wrote** last week.* → ✅ *The budget **was written** last
  week.* — *passive =* be *+ **participle**;* wrote *is the past simple and can't
  follow* was.
- ❌ *We have **buyed** the licenses already.* → ✅ *We have **bought** the
  licenses already.* — *never add* -ed *to an irregular verb; the form is* bought.

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. **The board has taken a clear position on the AI roadmap.**
   *has* → participle *taken*, not *took*: the perfect needs the participle, and
   this verb splits the two shapes.*
2. **The revised model was built by the data team and sent to the CFO.**
   *Two passives (*was built*, *[was] sent*) — both* be *+ participle;* built *and*
   sent *happen to double as the past simple, which is why they feel "safe".*
3. **We took that decision last quarter, and we haven't revisited it since.**
   *First verb has no helper → past simple* took*; second has* haven't *→
   participle* revisited *(regular, so invisible).*
4. **The 2027 vision was written before I joined, so I've rewritten it.**
   *Passive* was written *and perfect* have rewritten *— both demand the
   participle* written; *"was wrote / have wrote" is the classic slip.*
5. **The vendor gave us a demo, but no contract has been signed yet.**
   *Plain past* gave *(no helper) vs* has been signed *(perfect + passive stacked)
   → participle* signed*; the front word decides each one.*
6. **Have you spoken to the CEO about the innovation portfolio?**
   *Perfect question → participle* spoken*, not* spoke; *"Have you spoke…" is a
   very common error.*
7. **I broke my glasses this morning, so I've ordered a new pair.**
   *Everyday life:* broke *is the past simple (no helper);* have ordered *(regular)
   is the perfect — the irregular one is where the trap hides.*
8. **The costs have risen sharply, and the forecast was revised twice.**
   *have risen *(irregular participle* risen*, not* rose*) +* was revised *(regular
   passive) — the perfect and the passive both pull the participle.*

### 8. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

1. Ask your tutor to run a **"three-forms" fire drill** on the verbs you actually
   use at work — *take, make, give, choose, write, build, send, run, break,
   rise, speak, hold, meet, set* — calling the base and having you snap back
   *"took / taken"*, *"chose / chosen"*, *"rose / risen"*. The goal is reflex:
   the participle must arrive without thinking, so it's ready the moment a *have*
   or a *was* appears.
2. Then a **rewrite-in-two-frames** exercise: your tutor gives a plain past
   sentence about your world (*"The board took the decision on Monday"*) and you
   flip it into the perfect (*"The board has taken…"*) and the passive (*"The
   decision was taken…"*) on the spot — training the front-word check that
   turns *took* into *taken* under pressure.

2. Drop **do**: ✅ *"**Can** you present tomorrow?"* — *can* is already the helper,
   and English never puts *do* in front of a modal.

   **existence** is *there is/are*, not *have*; *have* is only for possession.

**Spaced review**
- **(Unit 017)** **been** — *"The CTO has **been** to the summit."* She's back, so
  it's a finished, came-back experience; **gone** would mean she is **still
  there** and couldn't be "back with a report".
- **(Unit 001)** *"The roadmap **is** ready, but the stakeholders **are** not
  aligned yet."* — singular *roadmap* → **is**; plural *stakeholders* → **are**.

**Recognition drill**
1. **taken** — *"The board has **taken** a final decision."* The word *has*
   forces the **participle**; *took* is the bare past simple and can't follow a
   perfect helper.
2. ❌ *was wrote* → ✅ *"The strategy memo **was written** before the offsite."* —
   passive is *be* + **participle** (*written*); *wrote* is the past simple.
3. **chose … chosen** — *"We **chose** the platform in March, and we've **chosen**
   it ever since."* First slot has no helper → past simple *chose*; second has
   *we've* → participle *chosen*.
4. ❌ *Have you spoke* → ✅ *"Have you **spoken** to the CFO about the headcount
   yet?"* — the perfect needs the participle *spoken*, not the past simple *spoke*.

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**A regra e quando usar.** Todo verbo tem três formas: **base** (*take*),
**passado simples** (*took*) e **particípio** (*taken*). Nos verbos **regulares**
o passado e o particípio são iguais (*review → reviewed → reviewed*), então a
diferença fica escondida. Nos **irregulares** ela aparece — e é aí que mora o
erro. A decisão é simples: olhe a **palavra na frente** do verbo. Se há um
auxiliar — *have/has/had* (present perfect) ou *is/was/been* (passiva) — use o
**particípio** (*taken, written, spoken, chosen, done*). Se não há nada, é uma
ação passada solta e você usa o **passado simples** (*took, wrote, spoke, chose,
did*). É a mesma lógica das Unidades 016–021: *have done* e *is done* pedem o
particípio.

**As armadilhas.** O português **tem** essa mesma divisão (*fez* vs *feito*,
*escreveu* vs *escrito*), então o **conceito** já é seu — o problema é (1)
**decorar** cada forma irregular do inglês (não há regra que gere *taken* de
*take*) e (2) o ouvido aceitar *"has took"* / *"was wrote"* porque *soa* parecido.
Evite os dois erros clássicos: nunca use o passado simples depois de auxiliar
(*"has took"* → **has taken**, *"was wrote"* → **was written**, *"have spoke"* →
**have spoken**); e **nunca** cole *-ed* em verbo irregular (*"buyed / taked /
writed"* → **bought / took / written**). Se a diferença passado-vs-particípio
embolar, releia `lessons/2026-08-01-unit-020.md` (present perfect vs past simple)
e `lessons/2026-08-02-unit-021.md` (a passiva).
