### 1. 📘 Unit 051 — Preposition + -ing

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** Are we ready to move on the AI platform?
> **You:** I'm **interested in investing**, but I want the numbers tight **before
> committing**. We shouldn't sign **without seeing** a real reference customer.
> **CEO:** The board is nervous about spend.
> **You:** They're **good at spotting** risk — that's their job. But I'm **tired of
> waiting** for perfect data. Instead of **hiring** a big team now, I'd start small.
> **CEO:** So what do you need from me?
> **You:** Back me. I'm **committed to delivering** one flagship bet, and I **look
> forward to showing** the board a working prototype next quarter.

Notice the shape before any rule. Every bolded verb sits **right after a little
word** — *in, before, without, at, of, to* — and every one of them ends in **-ing**:
*interested **in investing**, before **committing**, without **seeing**, good **at
spotting**, tired **of waiting**, committed **to delivering**, look forward **to
showing**. That word before the verb is a **preposition**, and after a preposition
English uses **-ing**, never a plain infinitive. Portuguese does the opposite
(*antes de **decidir***, *sem **ver***), and that mismatch is today's whole lesson.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

When a verb comes **straight after a preposition**, it goes into the **-ing** form.

| Preposition | + verb in -ing | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **in** | investing | *interested **in investing*** |
| **at** | spotting | *good **at spotting** risk* |
| **of** | waiting | *tired **of waiting*** |
| **before / after** | committing / launching | *before **committing***, *after **launching*** |
| **without** | seeing | *without **seeing** the data* |
| **instead of** | hiring | *instead of **hiring** now* |
| **for** | delivering | *responsible **for delivering*** |
| **to** *(preposition!)* | delivering / showing | *committed **to delivering***, *look forward **to showing*** |

**Key facts:**
- **Preposition + -ing, always.** Any preposition — *in, on, at, of, for, about,
  before, after, without, instead of* — is followed by **-ing**: *"good at
  **leading**"*, *"before **deciding**"*, *"without **approving**"*.
- **It doesn't matter if the subject is a noun or a verb.** After *in* you can put a
  thing (*interested **in AI***) or an action (*interested **in investing***) — an
  action becomes **-ing**.
- **The dangerous *to*.** *To* is usually the infinitive marker (*want **to** go*),
  but in some fixed phrases *to* is a **preposition** — and then it takes **-ing**:
  *look forward **to** seeing*, *committed **to** delivering*, *used **to** working*,
  *object **to** paying*. Test: can you put *"it"* after the *to*? If yes (*look
  forward to **it***), *to* is a preposition → use **-ing**.
- **No conjugation after the preposition.** The verb never changes for tense or
  person here — it's just the raw *-ing* form.

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why does this structure exist?** A preposition needs a **noun-like thing** after
it — you say *"good at **chess**"*, *"interested in **AI**"*. When the "thing" you
want to name is actually an **action**, English can't drop a bare verb in there; it
turns the verb into a noun-like *-ing* form (a *gerund*): *"good at **negotiating**"*,
*"interested in **investing**"*. So *-ing* is how English fits a verb into a slot that
grammar reserves for a noun. That's the entire logic — after a preposition, the action
must wear its "noun costume", and the costume is *-ing*.

**Decision rule #1 — preposition + -ing vs to-infinitive (the core seam).** Both
attach a second verb, but they're triggered by different things:

| | **preposition + -ing** | **to + infinitive** |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | a **preposition** sits in front (*in, of, before, without, at, for*) | a **verb/adjective** that wants *to* (*want, decide, need, hope; ready, able*) |
| Shape | *-ing* | *to* + base verb |
| Example | *"I'm **interested in investing**."* | *"I **want to invest**."* |
| Example | *"We agreed **before signing**."* | *"We agreed **to sign**."* |

Minimal pairs — the trigger word decides the form:
- *"I'm **keen to start**."* (adjective *keen* + **to**) vs *"I'm **interested in
  starting**."* (preposition *in* + **-ing**). Same idea, different trigger word,
  different form.
- *"We decided **to postpone**."* (verb *decide* + **to**) vs *"We're thinking
  **about postponing**."* (preposition *about* + **-ing**).

**Decision rule #2 — the *to* that is really a preposition.** A handful of common
executive phrases end in *to* that is a **preposition**, so they take **-ing**, not a
bare verb. Learn them as blocks:
- *look forward **to** hearing / seeing / showing*
- *committed **to** delivering*
- *used **to** working* (= accustomed to)
- *object **to** paying*, *the key **to** winning*, *close **to** finishing*
- Test again: if *"…to **it**"* sounds right (*look forward to **it***, *committed to
  **it***), the *to* is a preposition → **-ing**.

**PT→EN mapping — where Portuguese betrays Levi.** Portuguese puts the **infinitive**
after a preposition, so a word-for-word translation drops the *-ing* every time:
- *"**antes de decidir**"* → ✅ *"before **deciding**"*, ❌ *"before **to decide**"* /
  ❌ *"before **decide**"* (PT infinitive *decidir* pulls you to a bare/`to` verb).
- *"**sem aprovar**"* → ✅ *"without **approving**"*, ❌ *"without **approve**"*.
- *"**interessado em investir**"* → ✅ *"interested in **investing**"*, ❌ *"interested
  in **invest**"* / ❌ *"interested **to** invest"*.
- *"**em vez de contratar**"* → ✅ *"instead of **hiring**"*, ❌ *"instead of **hire**"*.
- The *look forward to* trap is the classic email killer: *"Aguardo **poder falar** com
  você"* → ✅ *"I look forward to **speaking** with you"*, ❌ *"I look forward to
  **speak** with you"* (here *to* is a preposition, not an infinitive).

**Top Brazilian-speaker errors:**
- ❌ *"We can't decide **without to see** the numbers."* → ✅ *"…**without seeing** the
  numbers."* — after any preposition the verb is *-ing*; there is no *to*.
- ❌ *"I look forward **to hear** from the board."* → ✅ *"I look forward **to hearing**
  from the board."* — *look forward to* ends in a **preposition** *to* → *-ing*.
- ❌ *"I'm **interested to invest** in the platform."* → ✅ *"I'm **interested in
  investing** in the platform."* — *interested* pairs with the preposition *in*, so the
  action becomes *-ing*. (*"Interested to hear"* exists, but *"interested in + action"*
  is the everyday pattern.)

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. *"I'm **interested in investing**, but I want the numbers tight first."*
   *— preposition* in *+ -ing; the action* invest *fills a noun slot, so it wears the -ing costume — never "interested in invest".*
2. *"We shouldn't sign **without seeing** a real reference customer."*
   *— after* without *the verb is always -ing;* "without to see" / "without see" *are the direct-from-Portuguese slips.*
3. *"I want the numbers tight **before committing** to the spend."*
   *—* before *+ -ing for the earlier action; PT "antes de comprometer" tempts "before to commit", but English needs* committing.
4. *"The board is **good at spotting** risk — that's their job."*
   *—* good at *+ -ing; the whole phrase* good at + action *takes -ing, like* skilled at, experienced in.
5. *"I'm **committed to delivering** one flagship bet."*
   *— the dangerous* to*: here it's a preposition (test: "committed to* it*"), so the verb is* delivering*, not "to deliver".*
6. *"I **look forward to showing** the board a working prototype."*
   *— the #1 email trap;* look forward to *ends in a preposition → -ing. "Look forward to show" is wrong in every business email.*
7. *"**Instead of hiring** a big team now, I'd start small."*
   *—* instead of *+ -ing; PT "em vez de contratar" → "instead of hiring", never "instead of hire".*
8. *"Thanks **for coming** early — let's start **after finishing** our coffee."*
   *— everyday life; both* for *and* after *are prepositions, so both verbs are -ing (*coming*,* finishing*).*

### 8. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

- Ask your tutor to run a **"preposition trigger" fire drill** from your world: they
  throw a starter — *"before …", "without …", "interested in …", "responsible for …",
  "good at …", "instead of …"* — and you finish it with an *-ing* verb about a real
  decision (*"before committing to the budget", "without seeing the pilot data",
  "responsible for delivering the roadmap"*). Goal: the *-ing* becomes automatic the
  instant a preposition lands, so the Portuguese infinitive never leaks through.
- Then a **sign-off round** targeting the *to* trap: have your tutor prompt the close
  of an executive email or board note, and you produce the correct
  *"I look forward to **hearing** / **presenting** / **discussing** …"* and
  *"we're committed to **delivering** …"* live. This is the exact phrase that trips up
  most Brazilian executives in writing — drill it until *"look forward to hear"* never
  surfaces again.

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 049 — see/hear somebody do vs doing):**
1. ***saw … hesitate*** — *"I **saw the CFO hesitate** before she signed."* Perception
   verbs take the **bare** verb (a complete, single flash of action), and — like
   *make/let* — the *to* never appears; *"saw the CFO **to** hesitate"* is the reflex
   error.
2. ***presenting*** — *"When I walked in, I heard the CEO **presenting** the vision."*
   The *-ing* form marks the action **in progress**: you caught a slice of it as you
   entered. *Present* (base) would mean you heard the **whole** presentation start to
   end, which clashes with just *walking in* on it.
3. The middle slot is an **object**, so it's an object form — *him*, not *he*; and with
   a noun it's just the noun (*adoption*): *"We saw **adoption** climbing all quarter."*
   You *hear **him** arriving*, never *"hear **he** arriving"*.

**Spaced review:**
- **(Unit 044)** ***ask … to ring-fence*** — *"I'll **ask the CFO to ring-fence** the
  money."* The pattern is **verb + object (who) + to + base verb**. English uses this
  object-plus-*to* frame, **not** a *that*-clause, so *"ask that the CFO ring-fences"*
  is wrong — Portuguese *"pedir que o CFO proteja"* is the trap.
- **(Unit 020)** ***have narrowed / ran*** — *"We **have narrowed** the shortlist to
  three, and we **ran** the reference calls two weeks ago."* *Have narrowed* = present
  perfect, a result that matters **now** with no date attached. *Ran* = past simple,
  because *"two weeks ago"* is a **finished, dated** past time — the specific time
  forces the switch out of the present perfect.

**Recognition drill:**
1. ❌ *"…without **to test** it first."* → ✅ *"We approved the pilot **without testing**
   it first."* — after the preposition *without* the verb must be *-ing*; there is no
   *to* and no bare verb.
2. ***to presenting*** — *"I look forward **to presenting** our AI vision to the board."*
   In *look forward to*, the *to* is a **preposition** (test: *"look forward to **it**"*
   works), so the verb takes *-ing*, not the infinitive. *"Look forward to present"* is
   the classic email error.
3. ***to postpone*** vs ***postponing***. After the verb *decide* you use the
   **infinitive**: *"We **decided to postpone** the launch."* But after *"thinking
   **about**"* the trigger is the **preposition** *about*, so it's *-ing*: *"We're
   **thinking about postponing** the launch."* Same second verb, different trigger word
   (verb *decide* → *to*; preposition *about* → *-ing*).
4. ***interested in leading*** — *"I'm **interested in leading** the innovation
   portfolio."* *Interested* pairs with the preposition *in*, and after a preposition an
   action becomes *-ing*: not *"interested **to** lead"* (wrong pattern for naming the
   activity) and not *"interested in **lead**"* (a preposition can't be followed by a
   bare verb).

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**A regra e quando usar.** Depois de uma **preposição** — *in, on, at, of, for, about,
before, after, without, instead of* — o verbo em inglês vai para a forma **-ing**:
*"interested **in investing**"*, *"before **committing**"*, *"without **seeing**"*,
*"good **at spotting**"*, *"instead of **hiring**"*. A lógica é simples: a preposição
precisa de algo tipo substantivo depois dela, então a ação "veste a fantasia de
substantivo", que é o *-ing*. Não confunda com o *to* + infinitivo, que é acionado por
um **verbo ou adjetivo** (*want **to** invest*, *decided **to** postpone*, *ready **to**
go*), não por uma preposição. O gatilho é a palavra que vem **antes**: preposição → *-ing*;
verbo/adjetivo que pede *to* → infinitivo.

**As armadilhas.** (1) O português usa **infinitivo** depois de preposição (*antes de
decidir*, *sem aprovar*, *interessado em investir*), então a tradução literal derruba o
*-ing*: diga *"before **deciding**"*, *"without **approving**"*, *"interested in
**investing**"* — nunca *"before to decide"* nem *"without approve"*. (2) O *to*
traiçoeiro: em *look forward **to***, *committed **to***, *used **to***, o *to* é uma
**preposição**, então vem *-ing* — *"I look forward **to hearing** from you"*, jamais
*"to hear"* (o erro nº 1 em e-mails executivos). Teste rápido: se *"…to **it**"* soa
certo, é preposição → use *-ing*. Se ainda embolar preposição-+-ing × infinitivo,
releia `lessons/2026-08-25-unit-044.md` (verb + object + to), o vizinho que mostra o
lado do *to*-infinitivo.
