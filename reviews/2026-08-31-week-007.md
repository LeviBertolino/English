# 🔁 Review Week 007 — Units 44 to 49

*One week, one deep idea: **what shape does the second verb take?** Every unit
below answers the same question in a different frame — plain `to`, `for`, `-ing`,
or bare. This review is about telling them apart under pressure. ~15 min,
read-only.*

---

### 2. 📋 Recap — one line per unit

- **Unit 44 — Verb + object + to…** — *want / tell / ask / expect / advise + **who** + **to** do*. English points at the person, then uses `to` + verb — never a *that*-clause (*"I want **you to** decide"*, not *"I want that you decide"*).
- **Unit 45 — `to` for purpose (I went there to…)** — to say **why** you do something, use bare **`to` + verb** (*"I flew to Boston **to close** the deal"*). Portuguese *para* splits into English **`to` + verb** vs **`for` + noun**.
- **Unit 46 — Preposition + -ing** — after **any preposition** (*in, on, of, before, about, to-as-preposition, instead of*), a verb must be **-ing** (*"committed to **investing**"*, *"before **committing**"*).
- **Unit 47 — Adjective + to-infinitive** — after an adjective, the verb is **`to` + base** (*"hard **to predict**"*, *"ready **to scale**"*, *"the first **to move**"*).
- **Unit 48 — Make & Let (+ base verb)** — *make* (force) and *let* (allow) take **object + bare verb, no `to`** (*"let me **explain**"*, *"the board made us **rethink**"*).
- **Unit 49 — See/hear somebody do vs doing** — perception verbs (*see, hear, watch, notice, feel*) take **object + bare verb** (whole, completed action) **or object + -ing** (action in progress). The shape changes the meaning.

---

### 3. 🗺️ Confusion map — the second verb's four shapes

The whole week is one decision tree. After the first verb (or adjective, or
preposition), the next verb takes **one of four shapes**. Here is when each wins:

| The second verb looks like… | Use it when… | Unit | Signal words | Anchor example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **`to` + base** (infinitive) | after **want/tell/ask/expect/advise + a person**; after an **adjective**; to state a **purpose (why)** | 44, 45, 47 | want you *to*, ready *to*, hard *to*, (I came) *to* close | *"I need you **to** back me."* / *"It's hard **to** predict."* / *"I flew **to** close it."* |
| **`for` + noun** (not a verb!) | purpose expressed as a **noun**, not an action | 45 | for the meeting, for lunch, for aligning (noun-like -ing) | *"I'm here **for** the board meeting."* |
| **-ing** | right after a **preposition** (*in/on/of/before/about/instead of/ to-as-preposition*) | 46 | interested *in*, committed *to*, before, instead of, look forward *to* | *"Committed **to investing**."* / *"Before **committing**."* |
| **bare base** (no `to`) | after **make / let**; after **see/hear/watch/notice/feel** for a **completed** action | 48, 49 | make us, let me, saw her, heard them | *"Let me **explain**."* / *"I saw her **leave**."* |
| **-ing** (perception) | after **see/hear/watch/feel** for an action **in progress** | 49 | saw … -ing, heard … -ing | *"I saw adoption **climbing**."* |

**The two traps that catch Brazilian speakers most:**

1. **The `to` disguise (Unit 46 vs 44/47).** Not every `to` is an infinitive
   marker. In *"committed **to** investing"* and *"look forward **to** presenting"*,
   `to` is a **preposition** → force **-ing**. Test: can you put a **noun** after
   it? *"committed to **the plan**"* works → so it's a preposition → *"committed to
   **investing**"*. Compare *"I want to **invest**"* (real infinitive, no noun test
   → base verb).
2. **`make/let` drop the `to`, `want/tell/ask` keep it (Unit 48 vs 44).** Same
   surface (verb + person + verb), opposite rule. *"I want you **to** decide"* but
   *"I'll let you **decide**"* / *"That made us **rethink**"*. Portuguese hides this
   because *"deixe-me explicar"* and *"quero que você decida"* don't warn you.

### 4. 🧪 10 recognition exercises (contrast — pick the right shape)

*Answers with explanations at the end. Force yourself to choose before scrolling.*

1. *"The CEO asked me ___ (lead) the AI roadmap."* → **to lead** / **leading** / **lead**?
2. *"We're focused on ___ (scale) the platform, not on chasing trends."* → **to scale** / **scaling** / **scale**?
3. *"Let me ___ (walk) you through the two scenarios."* → **to walk** / **walking** / **walk**?
4. *"I flew to Boston ___ (close) the vendor deal."* → **to close** / **for close** / **for closing**?
5. *"It's ___ (hard) ___ (predict) the market that far out."* → **hard predicting** / **hard to predict** / **hard for predict**?
6. *"The disappointing numbers ___ me question the whole roadmap."* → **made** / **let** / **let me to**?
7. *"I ___ the board debate it for an hour, then move on."* (I perceived the **whole** hour of debate) → **heard … debate** / **heard … to debate** / **heard … debating**?
8. *"Before ___ (commit) the budget, we benchmarked two more vendors."* → **to commit** / **committing** / **commit**?
9. *"I'm here ___ the offsite; I came ___ align the leadership team."* → **for / to** / **to / for** / **for / for**?
10. *"The board expects us ___ (deliver) a single flagship bet."* → **to deliver** / **delivering** / **deliver**?

### 5. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

Three contrasts from this week are the most confusable in real speech — bring
these to your tutor:

1. **The `to`-preposition vs `to`-infinitive split (Units 44/47 vs 46).** Ask
   your tutor to fire rapid prompts mixing *"committed to ___"*, *"want you to
   ___"*, *"look forward to ___"*, *"ready to ___"* and make you pick **-ing** or
   **base** on the spot. This is the single highest-value drill of the week.
2. **make/let vs want/tell (Unit 48 vs 44).** Role-play a strategy sync where you
   must delegate: *"I'll **let** the team **run** the pilot, but I **need** the CFO
   **to** ring-fence the budget."* Alternate forcing/allowing (bare) with
   requesting (with `to`) in the same breath.
3. **Purpose `to` vs `for` (Unit 45).** Have your tutor ask *"Why did you…?"* and
   *"What's the … for?"* rapid-fire, so you reflexively answer *"**to** close the
   deal"* (action) vs *"**for** the board review"* (noun).

### 6. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**O que já deve estar sólido:** a ideia central da semana — que **o segundo verbo
muda de forma** conforme o que vem antes. Depois de *make* e *let*, verbo **puro,
sem `to`** (*"let me explain"*, *"the board made us rethink"*). Para dizer o
**porquê** de uma ação, **`to` + verbo** (*"I flew to close the deal"*). Depois de
adjetivo, **`to` + verbo** (*"hard to predict"*, *"ready to scale"*). E o padrão
*want/tell/ask + pessoa + `to`* (*"I want you to decide"*) — sem *that*-clause, que
é a tradução direta de *"quero que você decida"*.

**O que provavelmente ainda precisa de treino:** a **armadilha do `to`**. Nem todo
`to` pede infinitivo. Em *"committed **to** investing"*, *"look forward **to**
presenting"*, *"focused **on** scaling"*, o `to`/`on` é **preposição** → verbo em
**-ing**, nunca infinitivo. Teste rápido: se cabe um **substantivo** depois
(*"committed to the plan"*), é preposição → use **-ing**. A segunda cilada é
misturar *make/let* (sem `to`) com *want/tell* (com `to`), porque em português
*"deixe-me explicar"* e *"quero que você decida"* não avisam a diferença. Se só der
tempo de reler dois arquivos, releia **Unit 046 (preposition + -ing)** e **Unit 048
(make & let)** — são os dois que mais se confundem entre si e com o resto.

### ✅ Gabarito — respostas dos 10 exercícios

1. **to lead.** *ask + person + `to` + verbo* (Unit 44). *"asked me leading/lead"* está errado — depois de *ask + pessoa* o verbo leva `to`.
2. **scaling.** *focused **on*** → *on* é preposição → **-ing** (Unit 46). *"focused on to scale"* é impossível.
3. **walk.** *let + objeto + verbo puro*, sem `to` (Unit 48). *"let me to walk"* nunca.
4. **to close.** Propósito (por quê) = **`to` + verbo** (Unit 45). *"for close"* é impossível (nunca *for* + verbo puro); *"for closing"* mudaria para leitura de substantivo/função, não a intenção da viagem.
5. **hard to predict.** Adjetivo + **`to` + verbo** (Unit 47). *"hard predicting"* e *"hard for predict"* estão errados.
6. **made.** O sujeito *"the numbers"* **forçou/causou** a reação → *make*. *let* seria *permitir*, o que não faz sentido aqui (Unit 48). *"let me to"* nunca leva `to`.
7. **heard … debate** (verbo puro). Você percebeu a ação **inteira/completa** (uma hora, do início ao fim) → forma **base** (Unit 49). *"debating"* daria "peguei um trecho no meio"; *"to debate"* nunca aparece com verbos de percepção.
8. **committing.** *Before* é **preposição** → **-ing** (Unit 46). *"Before to commit"* / *"before commit"* estão errados.
9. **for / to.** *for* + **substantivo** (*the offsite*); *to* + **verbo** para o propósito (*align*) (Unit 45). É a divisão exata do *para* português.
10. **to deliver.** *expect + objeto + `to` + verbo* (Unit 44). *"expect us delivering/deliver"* está errado; e nunca *"expect that we deliver"*.
