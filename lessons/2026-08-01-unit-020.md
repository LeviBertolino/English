### 1. 📘 Unit 020 — I have done vs I did (present perfect vs past simple)

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** Where are we on the AI strategy?
> **You:** We've **finished** the diagnostic — the findings are on your desk. I
> **presented** the first draft to the board last Tuesday, and they **asked** for a
> sharper cost case.
> **CEO:** And the vendor shortlist?
> **You:** Done. We've **narrowed** it to three. We **shortlisted** them in June,
> **ran** the reference calls two weeks ago, and I've **already sent** you the scorecard.

Notice the two pasts side by side, doing different jobs. **We've finished / we've
narrowed / I've already sent** — no date attached; what matters is the **result now**
(it's ready, it's on your desk). **I presented / they asked / we shortlisted / we
ran** — each nailed to a **finished past time** (*last Tuesday, in June, two weeks
ago*). The moment a specific past time appears, English drops the present perfect and
switches to the past simple.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

Two tenses, two shapes — the difference is whether the past is **linked to now** or
**closed off in the past**.

| | Present perfect | Past simple |
|---|---|---|
| Form | **have / has + past participle** | **-ed / irregular past** |
| Example | *I **have presented*** / *she **has gone*** | *I **presented*** / *she **went*** |
| Time frame | a period **up to now** (unfinished) | a **finished** period, done and dated |
| Says | the **result / relevance now** | **when** it happened |

| | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Present perfect** | subject + **have/has** + participle | *We **have approved** the budget.* |
| **Past simple** | subject + **past form** | *We **approved** the budget on Monday.* |

**Key facts:**
- **Present perfect = no specific past time.** *"I've spoken to the CFO"* — you don't
  say *when*; the point is that it's done and relevant now.
- **Past simple = a specific finished time**, stated or understood: *yesterday, last
  week, in 2024, two years ago, on Monday, when I joined*. The moment one of these
  appears, you **must** use the past simple.
- **A finished-time word and the present perfect can't share a sentence.** *"I've done
  it yesterday"* ❌ → *"I did it yesterday"* ✅.
- ***When…?* is always past simple.** The question asks for a specific point, so:
  *"**When did** you present it?"* ✅, never *"When have you presented it?"* ❌.

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why do these two exist, and why is this the unit that ties the last five together?**
Units 15–19 built the present perfect (result now, *just/already/yet*, *ever/never*,
*how long*, *for/since*). Units 11–14 built the past simple (finished events, dated on
a timeline). This unit is the **referee**: given a real past action, which one do you
reach for? The whole decision turns on **one question — is there a finished past time
in play, spoken or understood?**

**The decision rule.** Ask: *"Am I saying WHEN it happened, or just THAT it happened?"*
- **WHEN (a finished moment)** → past simple. *"I presented it **on Tuesday**."*
- **THAT / the result now (no moment named)** → present perfect. *"I've presented it"*
  (it's done, you can act on it).

Follow-on rule: a conversation often **opens** in the present perfect (the news,
the result) and then **zooms in** with the past simple once a time is fixed:
> *"We've launched the AI assistant."* (news, result now)
> *"We launched it three weeks ago, and adoption jumped in the first week."* (now dated)

| | Present perfect (*have done*) | Past simple (*did*) |
|---|---|---|
| Time | period up to **now**, unfinished | **finished**, closed |
| Named moment? | **no** — never with *yesterday/ago/in 2024* | **yes** — that's its whole job |
| Focus | the **result / relevance now** | **when** / the event itself |
| Cues | *just, already, yet, ever, never, so far, for, since, this week* | *yesterday, last…, ago, in 2024, then, when* |

**Minimal pairs — same event, meaning shifts with the tense:**
- *"I've **spoken** to the CFO."* → done, and it bears on now (you have his answer).
  vs. *"I **spoke** to the CFO **yesterday**."* → dated report of a finished event.
- *"The board **has approved** the AI budget."* → it's approved; you can spend now.
  vs. *"The board **approved** the AI budget **last quarter**."* → history, on a timeline.
- *"Have you **ever presented** to this board?"* → any time in your life up to now.
  vs. *"**Did** you **present** to them **at the March offsite**?"* → one fixed occasion.

**"This week / today / this year" is the tricky middle.** These periods **include
now**, so they're **unfinished** — present perfect: *"I've had three board calls **this
week**"* (the week isn't over). But *"last week"* is finished → past simple.

**PT→EN mapping — this is where Levi loses points.**
- Portuguese *"eu apresentei / eu fiz"* (pretérito perfeito) is used for **both**
  English tenses. So your instinct gives you the **past simple** every time — fine when
  a time is named, wrong when you needed the result-now present perfect: *"Já terminei o
  diagnóstico"* is **"I've finished the diagnostic,"** not "I finished the diagnostic"
  (with no date).
- **False friend alert:** Portuguese *"tenho feito / tenho apresentado"* looks exactly
  like *"I have done / I have presented"* — **but it isn't the same tense**. Portuguese
  *"tenho apresentado ao board ultimamente"* means a **repeated/ongoing habit lately**,
  closer to *"I've been presenting to the board lately."* A single finished action —
  *"eu terminei"* — is English *"I have finished,"* **never** *"I have been finishing."*
  Don't map the two *tenho + particípio* forms straight across.

**Top Brazilian-speaker errors:**
- ❌ *I've **presented** the roadmap **yesterday**.* → ✅ *I **presented** the roadmap
  **yesterday**.* — *a named finished time (yesterday) bans the present perfect; use the
  past simple.*
- ❌ *When **have** you **approved** the budget?* → ✅ *When **did** you **approve** the
  budget?* — *"when" asks for a specific point, so it's always past simple.*
- ❌ *We **finished** the diagnostic — it's ready for you.* (meaning: done, relevant now,
  no date) → ✅ *We've **finished** the diagnostic.* — *no time is named and the point is
  the result now, so English wants the present perfect; the bare past simple sounds like
  a date is missing.*

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. We've **finished** the AI diagnostic — the findings are ready for you. — *present
   perfect: no date, the point is the result available now; a bare "we finished" would
   leave the listener waiting for "…when?".*
2. I **presented** the first draft to the board **last Tuesday**. — *"last Tuesday" is a
   finished, named time → past simple; you cannot say "I've presented … last Tuesday".*
3. The board **hasn't approved** the AI budget **yet**. — *"yet" = up to now, period
   still open → present perfect; the decision's absence matters right now.*
4. We **shortlisted** the vendors **in June** and **ran** the reference calls **two weeks
   ago**. — *both dated (in June, two weeks ago) → past simple throughout; "ago" always
   forces it (carried from Unit 019).*
5. **Have** you **ever taken** a board through a full AI investment case? — *"ever" =
   any time up to now, no specific occasion → present perfect (from Unit 017).*
6. **When did** you **sign off** on the innovation portfolio? — *"when" demands a
   specific point → past simple; "When have you signed off?" is impossible.*
7. I've **had** three C-Level syncs **this week**, and it's only Wednesday. — *"this
   week" includes now → unfinished period → present perfect; if it were "last week" it
   would flip to "I had".*
8. She's **left** the room — you'll have to brief her later. vs. She **left** the room
   **at four**. — *everyday life: "has left" = she's gone and it affects now; "left at
   four" pins the finished moment → past simple.*

### 8. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

- Ask your tutor to run a **"news then details" drill**: you announce a real result in
  the present perfect (*"We've launched the internal AI assistant", "The board has
  approved the pilot"*), and they immediately push for specifics (*"When? How? Who
  signed off?"*), forcing you to flip to the past simple (*"We launched it three weeks
  ago", "They approved it at the June offsite"*). This trains the natural
  present-perfect-opens / past-simple-zooms-in rhythm executives use in updates.
- Then a **"trap the date" exercise**: have your tutor feed you sentences with a finished
  time word already baked in (*"…yesterday", "…in 2024", "…two quarters ago"*) and catch
  yourself before reaching for *"I've…"* — say the past simple out loud. Have them also
  bait the *"When have you…?"* error so you reflexively answer with *"When did you…?"*.

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 019)**
1. **since** — ✅ *"We've worked with this vendor **since** 2024."* — *"2024" is a
   **start point**, not a length, so it takes **since**; **for** would need a duration
   (for two years).*
2. ✅ Finished/dated: *"I **signed** with them two years **ago**."* (ago + past simple).
   ✅ Still running: *"We've **worked** with them **for** two years."* (for + present
   perfect). — *the Portuguese "há" splits into **ago** (over) and **for** (ongoing).*
3. ❌ *"I've joined … three years ago"* → ✅ *"I **joined** the board three years
   **ago**."* — *ago marks a finished, dated event and forces the **past simple**; it
   can never sit with the present perfect.*

**Spaced review**
- **(Unit 013)** ✅ *"When the CEO arrived, I **was presenting** the roadmap."* — *the
  continuous shows the action was **already in progress** when he arrived; "I presented"
  would mean I started **after** he walked in — two events in sequence, not an
  interruption.*

**Recognition drill**
1. ❌ *"I've sent … yesterday evening."* → ✅ *"I **sent** the board deck **yesterday
   evening**."* — *a named finished time (yesterday evening) bans the present perfect;
   the time word wins and pulls the verb into the past simple.*
2. **has invested** — ✅ *"The company **has invested** heavily in AI **this year**."* —
   *"this year" **includes now**, so the period is **unfinished** → present perfect; had
   it been "last year," it would flip to "invested".*
3. **signed** — ✅ *"We **signed** the vendor contract **last quarter**."* — *"last
   quarter" is finished and dated → past simple. To mean only "it's signed, we can
   proceed" with **no date**, drop the time word: **"We've signed the vendor
   contract."***
4. ❌ *"When have you launched…?"* → ✅ *"**When did** you **launch** the AI assistant?"*
   — *"when" asks for a specific finished point, and the present perfect refuses any
   specific past time, so the question must be past simple.*

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**A regra e quando usar.** Esta é a unidade que junta tudo: dada uma ação passada real,
qual tempo usar? A decisão gira em **uma pergunta — há um tempo passado terminado em
jogo (dito ou subentendido)?** Se você diz **quando** aconteceu (*yesterday, last week,
in 2024, two years ago, on Monday*), é **past simple** (*"I presented it on Tuesday"*).
Se você diz apenas **que** aconteceu, sem datar, e o que importa é o **resultado agora**,
é **present perfect** (*"I've presented it"* → está feito, pode agir). Uma conversa
costuma **abrir** no present perfect (a notícia, o resultado) e depois **fechar o zoom**
no past simple assim que uma data entra: *"We've launched it" → "We launched it three
weeks ago."* Cuidado com *this week / today / this year*: como **incluem o agora**, são
períodos **não terminados** → present perfect; *last week* já é fechado → past simple.

**As armadilhas.** (1) Palavra de tempo terminado **proíbe** o present perfect: *"I've
done it **yesterday**"* está errado → *"I **did** it yesterday"*. (2) *When…?* é
**sempre** past simple: *"**When did** you approve it?"*, nunca *"When have you
approved…?"*. (3) A interferência do português: *"eu apresentei / fiz"* serve para os
dois tempos ingleses, então seu instinto entrega o past simple sempre — e falha quando
faltava o present perfect de resultado (*"Já terminei"* = *"I've finished,"* sem data).
(4) Falso amigo: *"tenho apresentado ultimamente"* **não** é *"I have presented"* — é um
hábito repetido, mais perto de *"I've been presenting lately"*; uma ação única terminada
(*"eu terminei"*) é *"I have finished"*, jamais *"I have been finishing."* Se o contraste
ainda embolar, releia `lessons/2026-07-30-unit-018.md` (how long — present perfect) e
`lessons/2026-07-24-unit-012.md` (past simple: o rival do evento datado).
