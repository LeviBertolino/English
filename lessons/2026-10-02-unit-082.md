### 1. 📘 Unit 082 — *so* and *such*

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** Why is the board hesitating on the AI budget?
> **You:** Because it's **such a big** number, and the timing feels **so** aggressive.
> **CFO:** It's **so** expensive that we'd freeze two other bets.
> **You:** True — but it's **such an** opportunity that waiting a year could cost us the market. We have
> **so much** data sitting unused and **so many** teams ready to build.
> **CEO:** So you're telling me the risk of *not* moving is **so** high it outweighs the cost?
> **You:** Exactly. It's **such a** rare window that I'd rather overcommit than miss it.

Watch the split before any rule. When the strong word stands **alone** after *is / feels / looks* —
*so aggressive, so expensive, so high* — it's **so**. When a **noun** shows up — *a big number, an
opportunity, a window* — it flips to **such** (*such a big number, such an opportunity*). And both of
them love a *that*-clause that delivers the consequence: *so expensive **that** …*, *such a window
**that** …*. That consequence is the whole reason these words exist.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

The choice is decided by **one question: is there a noun right after?**

| Pattern | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **so** + adjective / adverb (alone) | **so** | *It's **so** expensive.* · *She presented **so** well.* |
| **such** + (adjective +) **noun** | **such** | *It's **such a** risk.* · *They're **such** bold moves.* |
| **so much / so little** + uncountable noun | **so** | ***so much** pressure · **so little** time* |
| **so many / so few** + countable plural | **so** | ***so many** options · **so few** people* |
| **such a lot of** + any noun | **such** | ***such a lot of** data* |

**The two consequence frames (this is where they earn their keep):**

| Frame | Example |
|---|---|
| **so** + adjective + **that** … | *The number is **so** big **that** the board froze.* |
| **such** (+ adj) + noun + **that** … | *It's **such a** big number **that** the board froze.* |

**Key facts:**
- ***a / an* lives with *such*, never with *so*.** *such **a** rare window* ✅ — *~~so a rare window~~* ❌.
  The article *a/an* is the giveaway that a noun is coming, so the word must be *such*.
- **Singular countable noun → *such a / such an*.** *such **a** risk*, *such **an** opportunity*.
- **Plural or uncountable noun → *such* (no article).** *such **bold moves***, *such **good timing***.
- ***that* is optional and usually dropped in speech**: *"It's so late (that) we should stop."* The
  consequence meaning stays even when *that* disappears.

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (the core section)

**Why do these two words exist?** Both mean roughly *"very"* — but they do something *very* can't: they
**point forward to a result**. *"It's very expensive"* just rates the price. *"It's **so** expensive
**that** we'd freeze two bets"* rates it **and tells you the consequence**. *so / such* are *very* with a
dramatic arrow attached — the emphasis word of cause-and-effect. That's why they dominate persuasive,
board-level speech: you're not describing, you're arguing *therefore*.

**Decision rule — so vs such (the only hard part):** look at the word **immediately after**.
- adjective or adverb, standing alone → **so**: *so high, so quickly, so aggressive.*
- a noun (with or without an adjective in front) → **such**: *such a cost, such a rare window, such moves.*

| ✅ *The window is **so** narrow.* | *narrow* is an adjective standing alone → **so** |
| ✅ *It's **such a** narrow window.* | now *window* (a noun) is there → **such a** |

Same idea, two packagings. The noun is the switch.

**so vs such vs very — the emphasis ladder:**

| | neutral | emphatic, no result | emphatic + result |
|---|---|---|---|
| adjective alone | *It's **very** costly.* | *It's **so** costly.* | *It's **so** costly **that** we paused.* |
| with a noun | *It's a **very** big ask.* | *It's **such a** big ask.* | *It's **such a** big ask **that** we paused.* |

Use **very** to simply rate something; reach for **so / such** when you want weight *and* a "therefore."

**PT→EN mapping.** This is where Portuguese sets the traps:
- *"tão"* + adjetivo → **so**: *"tão caro"* → *"**so** expensive."* Direct and safe.
- *"tão"* + substantivo (*"uma ideia tão boa"*, *"um risco tão grande"*) → English flips to **such**:
  *"**such a** good idea", "**such a** big risk."* Portuguese keeps *tão*; English **cannot** keep *so*
  once the noun appears. This is the #1 slip.
- *"tanto / tanta"* (uncountable) → **so much**: *"tanta pressão"* → *"**so much** pressure."*
- *"tantos / tantas"* (countable) → **so many**: *"tantas opções"* → *"**so many** options."* Portuguese
  uses one family (*tanto*); English splits it by countable vs uncountable.

**Top Brazilian-speaker errors:**
1. ❌ *"It's so a big opportunity."* / *"It was a so good meeting."* → ✅ *"It's **such a** big
   opportunity."* (The article *a* means a noun is coming → *such*, never *so*.)
2. ❌ *"There was so much risks."* / *"so many pressure."* → ✅ *"so **many** risks" · "so **much**
   pressure."* (Countable plural → *many*; uncountable → *much*.)
3. ❌ *"It's a such rare window."* → ✅ *"It's **such a** rare window."* (Order is *such + a*, not
   *a + such* — *such* comes first and pulls the article in behind it.)

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. **The AI budget is so large that it crowds out every other investment this year.**
   *— `so` + adjective (`large`) standing alone, then a `that`-consequence. No noun after `so`, so `such`
   would be wrong.*
2. **It's such a strategic opportunity that I'd protect it over the cost cuts.**
   *— noun (`opportunity`) is present → `such a`; `so a strategic opportunity` is impossible.*
3. **We have so much untapped data that the real cost is leaving it idle.**
   *— `data` is uncountable → `so much` (not `so many`); again a `that` delivers the payoff.*
4. **There are so many competing priorities on the roadmap right now.**
   *— `priorities` is countable plural → `so many` (not `so much`).*
5. **The board moved so fast on approval that legal barely kept up.**
   *— `so` modifies the adverb `fast` (recap of Unit 081 — `fast` takes no `-ly`); adverb alone → `so`.*
6. **Positioning us as an AI-first player is such a bold bet.**
   *— adjective + noun (`bold bet`) → `such a`; the `a` is the signal to switch from `so`.*
7. **The queue was so long that I nearly missed my connection.** *(everyday)*
   *— `long` alone after `was` → `so`; the `that`-clause is the consequence.*
8. **They're such nice people — we felt welcome instantly.** *(everyday)*
   *— plural noun (`people`) → `such` with **no** article; `such a nice people` would be wrong.*

### 8. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

- Ask your tutor to feed you bare adjectives and noun phrases at random (*"expensive" … "a big risk" …
  "aggressive" … "an opportunity"*) and drill the instant switch: adjective alone → *"so expensive"*,
  noun present → *"such a big risk."* Keep going until the article *a/an* automatically triggers *such*
  without a half-second of thought — that reflex kills the *"so a…"* slip for good.
- Then build **consequence sentences** about a real decision you're facing: *"It's such a large bet that
  …", "The market is moving so fast that …", "We have so much upside that …"*. Practising *so/such …
  that …* live trains you to argue *cause → therefore* in meetings, which is where these words pay off.

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 081):**
1. **hardly** — *"the team **hardly** touched the backlog"* = almost nothing got done. *hard* would mean
   *with effort* — the opposite message.
2. **lately** — *"Adoption has grown **lately**"* (= recently). *late* means *not on time / after the
   expected moment*, which makes no sense about growth.
3. **well** / **well** — *"The pilot went **well**"* (adverb of *good*, describing the verb *went*) and
   *"I'm **well**, thanks"* (adjective meaning *healthy*, after the linking verb *am*). Same word, two jobs.

**Spaced review:**
- **~1 week (Unit 075):** *"Our CFO**,** who joined in May**,** already backs the budget."* We already
   know **which** CFO (we have one), so *who joined in May* is **extra** information → fenced by two
   commas. And in a comma'd (non-defining) clause *that* is banned — it must be *who*.
- **~1 month (Unit 048):** **Let** me **explain** the scenario. *let* takes *object + **base verb*** with
   **no *to*** — *let me explain*, never *let me to explain*. (*make* behaves the same.)

**Recognition drill:**
1. **such** — *an aggressive timeline* has the noun *timeline* (and the article *an*) → *such an*.
2. **so** — here *aggressive* stands **alone** after *is* (no noun follows) → *so*. The noun in item 1 is
   what flips the choice; drop the noun and *such* becomes *so*.
3. **so much** — *data* is uncountable, so *much*, not *many*.
4. Wrong: *"a so important decision"* — *so* can't sit before *a + noun*. ✅ *"It was **such an**
   important decision that we brought in the board."* (adjective + noun → *such an*).

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

*so* e *such* são os primos "dramáticos" de *very*: além de intensificar, eles **apontam para uma
consequência** (*so expensive **that** we paused* = tão caro **que** paramos). A escolha entre os dois
depende de **uma só pergunta: vem um substantivo logo depois?** Se vier **adjetivo/advérbio sozinho** →
**so** (*so expensive, so fast*). Se vier **substantivo** (com ou sem adjetivo) → **such** (*such a risk,
such a rare window, such bold moves*). O artigo *a/an* anda com *such*, nunca com *so* — e a ordem é
*such a*, não *a such*. Para quantidades: incontável → **so much** (*so much pressure*), contável no
plural → **so many** (*so many options*).

A armadilha é o português *tão*. Com adjetivo, *"tão caro"* vira *"so expensive"* — tranquilo. Mas
*"uma ideia tão boa"*, *"um risco tão grande"* **não** viram *"so a good idea"*: assim que o substantivo
aparece, o inglês troca para *such a* (*such a good idea, such a big risk*). É o erro nº 1. E *tanto/
tanta/tantos* se divide em dois no inglês: *so much* (incontável) × *so many* (contável). Decore o
gatilho: viu *a/an* ou um substantivo → **such**; só adjetivo → **so**.
