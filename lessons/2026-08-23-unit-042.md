### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** Where do you land on the two AI vendors?
> **You:** I'd **suggest running** a short bake-off before we commit. We can't
> **risk locking** ourselves in for three years.
> **CFO:** Agreed. But I **keep asking** myself about the ROI on the pilots.
> **You:** Fair. I **recommend prioritizing** the two flagship bets and
> **postponing** the rest to Q3.
> **CEO:** Have you **finished preparing** the board deck?
> **You:** Almost. Let's just **stop debating** the vendor and decide today.

Notice the shape before any rule. After *suggest, risk, keep, recommend, postpone,
finish, stop*, the next verb is **not** an infinitive — it wears **-ing**:
*suggest **running***, *risk **locking***, *finish **preparing***. Portuguese would
put a plain infinitive here (*sugiro **rodar**, evito **travar***), and that reflex
is exactly the trap. These verbs simply demand the *-ing* form — it's a **fixed
pattern**, not a choice of meaning.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

Certain verbs, when followed by **another verb**, require that second verb in the
**-ing** form (the *gerund* — an *-ing* form acting like a noun, "the activity").

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| verb + **-ing** | *I **enjoy leading** the transformation.* |
| negative inside | *She **suggested not rushing** the decision.* |
| after the subject's own tense | *We **considered acquiring** the startup.* / *We'**ve stopped funding** it.* |

**Verbs that take -ing (memorise this core set):**

> **enjoy · finish · stop · suggest · recommend · consider · avoid · keep (on) ·
> mind · risk · admit · imagine · postpone / put off · give up · involve · miss ·
> can't help · it's (no) worth**

**Key facts:**
- The verb before it carries the tense; the *-ing* verb never changes for time:
   *"We **avoid delaying**" / "We **avoided delaying**" / "We'll **avoid
   delaying**"* — only the first verb moves.
- To make the *-ing* verb **negative**, put *not* right before it: *"The board
   **suggested not announcing** yet."*
- After a **preposition**, English also uses *-ing* (*"interested **in
   partnering**", "good **at delegating**", "before **deciding**"*) — same *-ing*
   reflex, worth noticing.
- A few verbs take **either** form with little change: *start, begin, continue,
   like, love, prefer, hate* (*"We started **hiring** / to **hire**"*).

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why does this pattern exist?** Because some verbs in English simply **grammatically
select** an *-ing* complement — the *-ing* form behaves like a **noun** naming the
activity: *"I recommend **prioritizing**"* ≈ *"I recommend **the prioritizing of**…"*.
There's no deep meaning switch to reason about; the pattern is **lexical** — it lives
with the verb, and you learn it verb by verb, exactly like you learned *have to* takes
a base verb. For a senior leader this shows up constantly, because your highest-value
verbs are in the *-ing* camp: *suggest, recommend, consider, avoid, risk, postpone,
keep, finish*.

**Decision rule #1 — -ing verbs vs to-infinitive verbs (the whole game).** The choice
is **not** about meaning — it's about **which verb comes first**. Two lists to keep
apart:

| Verb + **-ing** | Verb + **to** + base |
|---|---|
| enjoy, finish, stop, suggest, recommend, consider, avoid, keep, mind, risk, postpone, admit, imagine | want, decide, plan, hope, promise, offer, agree, expect, refuse, learn, manage, afford, need |
| *We **considered acquiring** them.* | *We **decided to acquire** them.* |
| *I **recommend prioritizing**.* | *I **want to prioritize**.* |

Same idea, opposite pattern — *"suggest **doing**"* but *"want **to do**."* When you
reach for a second verb, the question is never *"what do I mean?"* but *"which verb is
in front, and which list is it on?"*

**Decision rule #2 — *stop doing* vs *stop to do* (the one real meaning trap).** A
handful of verbs allow **both** forms with a **different meaning**, and *stop* is the
one you'll use in meetings:
- *"We **stopped funding** the pilot."* → we **quit** the activity; the funding
   ended. (stop + **-ing** = end the action) ✅
- *"On the way to the board, I **stopped to check** the numbers."* → I **paused in
   order to** do something else. (stop + **to** = purpose) ✅

*Stop **debating** the vendor* = end the debate. *Stop **to debate** the vendor* =
interrupt what you're doing so you can debate. Very different — pick by whether you
mean *"quit it"* (-ing) or *"pause in order to"* (to).

**PT→EN mapping — where Portuguese betrays Levi.** Portuguese uses a plain
**infinitive** after these verbs, usually with a preposition: *"sugiro **rodar**",
"recomendo **priorizar**", "evito **atrasar**", "terminei **de preparar**"*. The
literal reflex produces *"suggest **to run**", "recommend **to prioritize**", "avoid
**to delay**", "finish **to prepare**"* — all **wrong**. English forbids the infinitive
here and demands *-ing*. Rule of thumb: when the Portuguese verb is *sugerir,
recomendar, evitar, considerar, terminar, parar de, continuar, arriscar, adiar* →
the English second verb almost always ends in **-ing**.

**Top Brazilian-speaker errors:**
- ❌ *"I **suggest to hire** a Head of AI."* → ✅ *"I **suggest hiring** a Head of
   AI."* — *suggest* takes *-ing*, never *to*. (Same for *recommend*: *"recommend
   **hiring**"*.)
- ❌ *"We should **avoid to delay** the launch."* → ✅ *"We should **avoid
   delaying** the launch."* — *avoid, risk, postpone, consider* are all *-ing* verbs.
- ❌ *"Have you **finished to prepare** the deck?"* → ✅ *"Have you **finished
   preparing** the deck?"* — *finish* + *-ing*; the *de preparar* → *to prepare*
   reflex is the culprit.

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. *"I'd **suggest running** a short bake-off before we commit to a vendor."*
   *— suggest is an -ing verb: "suggest running", never "suggest to run".*
2. *"We can't **risk locking** ourselves into a three-year contract."*
   *— risk + -ing; the -ing names the activity you're avoiding. Not "risk to lock".*
3. *"I **recommend prioritizing** the two flagship bets and **postponing** the rest."*
   *— recommend and postpone both take -ing; one verb, two -ing complements chained.*
4. *"The CFO **keeps questioning** the ROI on the pilots."*
   *— keep + -ing = does it repeatedly/persistently; "keeps to question" is impossible.*
5. *"The board **suggested not announcing** before the audit sign-off lands."*
   *— to negate the -ing verb, put "not" right before it: "suggested not announcing".*
6. *"Let's **stop debating** the vendor and make the call today."*
   *— stop + -ing = end the activity (quit the debate). "Stop to debate" would mean pause in order to debate — the opposite.*
7. *"We **considered acquiring** the startup, but we **decided to build** in-house."*
   *— minimal contrast: consider takes -ing, decide takes to. The verb in front picks the pattern.*
8. *"I **enjoy cycling** on weekends — it's how I switch off before Monday's board prep."*
   *— everyday life: enjoy + -ing. "Enjoy to cycle" is a classic slip from "gosto de pedalar".*

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 041 — So am I / Neither do I):**
1. ***"So am I."*** — a positive statement with *am* → echo the auxiliary and
   **invert** it before *I*. *"So I am"* is only correct as emphatic confirmation
   ("yes, so I am"), not as *"me too."*
2. ***"Neither have we."*** — you're agreeing with a **negative** (*hasn't*), so the
   sign is **Neither**, and you echo *have*. *"Me too"* is wrong because it agrees only
   with **positives**; the auxiliary stays **positive** after *neither* (the *neither*
   already carries the "no"), so never *"Neither haven't we."*
3. ***"So did we."*** — *backed* is past simple with **no auxiliary**, so you borrow
   **did**; positive statement → **So**. Not *"So has we"* (that needs a perfect,
   *has backed*), and the pronoun after *did* is *we*, not the ungrammatical *"has
   we."*

**Spaced review:**
- **(Unit 035)** ***mustn't*** — *"You **mustn't** brief the market yet."* It's
   **forbidden**; *don't have to* would say it's merely *not necessary* (optional) —
   the opposite meaning, and a dangerous one before a market announcement.
- **(Unit 010)** ***were*** — *"There **were** three open risks on the budget."*
   *Three risks* is **plural**, so it's *there were*, never *there was three*; *there
   was* is only for a singular thing.

**Recognition drill:**
1. ***revising*** — *"The board suggested **revising** the timeline."* *suggest* is an
   *-ing* verb; *"suggested to revise"* is ungrammatical (the *sugerir + infinitivo*
   reflex).
2. ***to check*** means she **paused in order to** check — *stop* + **to** = purpose.
   *"stopped **checking** the numbers"* would mean she **quit** an ongoing checking
   activity (stop + *-ing* = end the action) — a different event entirely.
3. **Wrong: *to hire*** → ✅ *"I **recommend hiring** a Head of AI this quarter."*
   *recommend* takes *-ing*, not *to* — same family as *suggest*.
4. ***to launch*** — *"We **decided to launch** the pilot next month."* *decide* is a
   **to-infinitive** verb, not an *-ing* verb. Lesson: the pattern is chosen by **which
   verb comes first**, not by meaning — you must know the list.

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**A regra e quando usar.** Alguns verbos, quando seguidos de **outro verbo**, exigem
esse segundo verbo na forma **-ing** (o *gerúndio*, que funciona como um substantivo —
"a atividade"): *enjoy, finish, stop, suggest, recommend, consider, avoid, keep, mind,
risk, postpone, admit, imagine*. Ex.: *"I **suggest running** a pilot", "We **avoid
delaying** the launch", "Have you **finished preparing** the deck?"*. Não é escolha de
sentido — é um **padrão fixo** que mora no verbo, aprendido verbo a verbo. Só o
**primeiro** verbo carrega o tempo (*avoid / avoided / will avoid*); o *-ing* não muda.
E para negar o *-ing*, coloque *not* antes dele (*"suggested **not** announcing"*).

**As armadilhas.** (1) A cilada nº 1 é o **infinitivo do português**: *"sugiro rodar",
"recomendo priorizar", "evito atrasar", "terminei de preparar"* viram, por reflexo,
*"suggest **to** run", "recommend **to** prioritize", "avoid **to** delay", "finish
**to** prepare"* — todos **errados**; o inglês exige *-ing*. (2) O padrão é decidido
por **qual verbo vem antes**, não pelo sentido: *suggest/consider/avoid* pedem *-ing*,
mas *want/decide/plan/afford* pedem *to* (*"decided **to** build"*). Guarde as duas
listas. (3) O único contraste de **sentido** real é *stop*: *stop **doing*** = parar a
atividade (*"stop debating"* = encerrar o debate); *stop **to do*** = fazer uma pausa
**para** algo (*"stopped to check"* = parou para conferir). Se a mecânica de auxiliar/
concordância ainda embolar, releia `lessons/2026-08-22-unit-041.md` (So am I / Neither
do I).
