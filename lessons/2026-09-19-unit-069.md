### 1. 📘 Unit 069 — both / both of · either / either of · neither / neither of

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** We have two finalists for the AI platform. Which one?
> **You:** **Both vendors** are strong, honestly. **Both of them** cleared security.
> **CEO:** So it doesn't matter which we pick?
> **You:** **Either option** works — **either of them** would ship on time. But **neither vendor**
> is cheap, and **neither of the contracts** gives us an exit clause.
> **CFO:** So what's the real decision?
> **You:** It's **not** platform vs platform. **Neither the price nor the tech** separates them —
> it comes down to **which one** the team will actually adopt.

Notice these words come in threes for **two things**: **both** = the two together (2 of 2),
**either** = one *or* the other, doesn't matter which (1 of 2), **neither** = not one *and* not the
other (0 of 2). And like yesterday's *all / most*, each grabs **of** the moment it points at *the
contracts* or a pronoun (*them*). That's today's whole lesson.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

All three talk about **exactly two** things. Same two-shape rule as Unit 068 (bare noun vs *of the*):

| Word | Meaning (of two) | + bare noun | + *of the* / *of* + pronoun |
|---|---|---|---|
| **both** | the two together | *both **vendors*** | *both **of the** vendors* · *both **of them*** |
| **either** | one or the other | *either **option*** | *either **of the** options* · *either **of them*** |
| **neither** | not one, not the other | *neither **vendor*** | *neither **of the** vendors* · *neither **of them*** |

Verb agreement — the trap:

- **both** → always **plural**: *both options **work***, *both of them **are** ready*.
- **either / neither** → grammatically **singular** by the book: *neither vendor **is** cheap*,
  *either option **works***. (With *of + plural*, spoken English often goes plural — *neither of
  them are ready* — but singular is the safe, formal default.)

The correlative pairs (linking two parts of a sentence):

- **both … and …** → *both fast **and** cheap*
- **either … or …** → *either scale **or** kill it*
- **neither … nor …** → *neither the price **nor** the tech* (note: **nor**, not *or*)

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (the core section)

**Why do these three words exist?** English has one set of words reserved for the **two-item**
decision — the everyday executive situation of *this vendor or that one, plan A or plan B*. They
carry a precision *all / some / none* can't: not "how many out of a crowd" but "which of the two,
and does it matter." **Both** = take the two. **Either** = take one, your choice, both are fine.
**Neither** = reject the two.

**Decision rule — count the options first.** *Both / either / neither* are **only** for **two**. The
moment you have **three or more**, you must switch to *all / any / none*:
- Two finalists → *neither vendor* fits our budget.
- Five finalists → *none of the vendors* fits our budget. (*Neither* would be wrong.)

**Either / neither vs the rival choice.** These two are mirror images and the meaning flips
completely:

| Say | Meaning | When you'd say it |
|---|---|---|
| *Either plan works.* | both are acceptable — pick one | you're comfortable, delegate the call |
| *Neither plan works.* | both are rejected — pick again | you're blocking, sending it back |

And *either* vs *both* in the affirmative:
- *Both are open on Friday* = the two are open. *Either is open on Friday* = pick whichever, one of
  the two will be open — a very different promise to make to a CEO.

**PT→EN mapping.** Brazilian Portuguese uses *ambos / os dois* for *both* and *nenhum dos dois* for
*neither* — and, crucially, PT builds a **double negative**: *"**Nenhum** vendor é barato"* /
*"Não gosto **de nenhum** dos dois."* English does the opposite: **neither is already negative**, so
the verb stays **positive**: *"**Neither** vendor **is** cheap"* (never *"neither vendor isn't
cheap"*). Same trap you met in Unit 066 (no / none / not any). And *nem … nem …* maps to
*neither … **nor** …* — the second word is **nor**, not *or*.

**Top Brazilian-speaker errors:**
1. ❌ *"Neither of the vendors **aren't** cheap."* → ✅ *"**Neither** of the vendors **is** cheap."*
   *Neither* already carries the negative — don't add a second one. (PT's *nenhum … não* interference.)
2. ❌ *"Neither the price **or** the tech separates them."* → ✅ *"Neither the price **nor** the tech
   separates them."* The partner of *neither* is **nor**, not *or*.
3. ❌ *"We tested three tools and **neither** worked."* → ✅ *"…and **none** of them worked."*
   *Neither* is only for **two**; three or more needs *none / any*.

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. **Both vendors** cleared the security review.
   *→ Two vendors, taken together → plural verb (*cleared*). Bare noun, so no *of*.*
2. **Both of them** would ship on time, so cost becomes the tiebreaker.
   *→ Pronoun *them* → *of* is obligatory. *Both them* is ungrammatical; only *both of them* works.*
3. **Either option** works — pick whichever the team prefers.
   *→ *Either* = one or the other, both acceptable. You're delegating the call, not blocking it.
   Singular verb (*works*).*
4. **Neither vendor** gives us a clean exit clause.
   *→ *Neither* = 0 of 2, and it's already negative → verb stays positive (*gives*), no second "not".*
5. **Neither of the two roadmaps** survived contact with the budget.
   *→ *of the* points at *the two roadmaps* specifically. Singular *survived* is the safe default.*
6. We can **either scale the pilot or kill it** — there's no middle path.
   *→ *either … or …* correlative, linking two clear alternatives. Classic executive framing.*
7. The strategy has to be **both ambitious and credible** to the board.
   *→ *both … and …* joins two required qualities. Not one or the other — the two at once.*
8. I checked three shortlisted tools and **none of them** met the bar.
   *→ **Three** tools, so *none of them*, not *neither*. This is the two-vs-many boundary in action.*

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 068 — all / most / none of):**
1. ✅ *"**Most** companies underestimate AI cost, but **most of** our pilots came in on budget."*
   First is general (bare plural → no *of*); second points at *our pilots* (specific → *most of*).
2. ✅ Not quite the same. *"None of the pilots"* judges **these specific** pilots (the ones you have);
   *"No pilot is ready"* is a broader, general-sounding claim about pilots as a category. *Of the*
   localises it to your set.
3. ✅ *"None of the leaders **agree**…"* Standard modern usage takes a **plural** verb when *none of +
   plural* follows (agree with *leaders*). (*agrees* survives only in very formal writing.)

**Spaced review:**
- *(Unit 062 — a / an)* ✅ *"I need **an** hour with the numbers and **a** one-page summary."*
  Choice is by **sound**, not spelling: *hour* starts with a silent *h* → vowel sound → *an*;
  *one-page* starts with a *w* sound → *a*.
- *(Unit 041 — So am I / Neither do I)* ✅ *"**Neither have I.**"* You agree with a **negative**
  (*I haven't…*), so it flips to *Neither + auxiliary + I*, reusing the auxiliary *have* — not *do*,
  because the original used *have* (*haven't seen*).

**Recognition drill:**
1. ✅ *"**Both** options are viable, and **either of** them could ship by Q1."* *Both* + bare noun
   (*options*) → no *of*; *either* + pronoun (*them*) → *of* obligatory.
2. ✅ *"**Neither** the CFO **nor** the CEO **was** convinced…"* Two errors: *or* → *nor* (partner of
   *neither*), and drop the second negative — *neither* is already negative, so the verb is positive
   (*was*, not *wasn't*).
3. ✅ *"**None of the platforms** passed."* You have **four**, and *neither* is only for **two**.
   Three or more always switches to *none / any*.
4. ✅ *"Either plan is fine"* = **both** are acceptable, pick whichever — you're clearing them to
   proceed. *"Neither plan is fine"* = **both** are rejected — you're blocking and sending it back.
   Opposite messages to the CEO.

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

*Both / either / neither* são o trio reservado para **exatamente duas** coisas — a decisão executiva
de sempre: este fornecedor ou aquele, plano A ou plano B. **Both** = os dois juntos (*both vendors*,
verbo no **plural**); **either** = um ou outro, tanto faz (*either option works*); **neither** =
nenhum dos dois (*neither vendor*). Seguem a mesma regra de ontem: substantivo puro sem *of*, mas
*of* obrigatório antes de *the/these* ou de pronome (*both of them*, *neither of the contracts*).
Assim que forem **três ou mais**, troque para *all / any / none* — *neither* não serve.

As ciladas são de interferência do português. Primeira: o português faz **dupla negativa**
(*nenhum … não*), mas *neither* **já é negativo**, então o verbo fica **positivo**: *"neither vendor
**is** cheap"*, nunca *"neither vendor isn't"*. Segunda: *nem … nem …* vira *neither … **nor** …* —
o par de *neither* é **nor**, não *or*. E cuidado com o verbo: por padrão formal, *either* e
*neither* pedem verbo no **singular** (*neither option works*), mesmo que a fala corrente às vezes
use o plural com *of + plural*.
