# 🔁 Review Week 009 — Units 58 to 63

*The "little words that carry ownership, roles, and reference" week.* Six units, one big
theme: the small grammatical words English forces you to get exactly right — which shape of a
pronoun to use, where the apostrophe goes, and which article (or none) sits in front of a noun.
These are precisely the words Portuguese handles differently, so they're where a fluent-sounding
sentence quietly slips. This week is about **choosing between rivals**, not re-learning forms.

*(No standalone Unit 057 lesson exists in this cycle — Review Week 008 fell on that day — so the
week runs 58→63, six units instead of seven.)*

---

## 2. 📋 Recap — one line per unit

- **Unit 058 — Possessive pronouns (mine / yours / hers):** the ownership word that **stands
  alone**, with no noun after it (*"That deck is **mine**"*), vs the adjective *my/your/her* that
  needs a noun. Plus **whose** to ask who owns something.
- **Unit 059 — Subject vs object pronouns (I/me, he/him, they/them):** the **same person** takes
  one shape when they **do** the action (*I present*) and another when they **receive** it or sit
  after a small word (*send it to **me***, *works for **me***).
- **Unit 060 — The possessive 's (the board's decision) & 's vs of:** glue **'s** to the **owner**,
  owner **first** — the flip of Portuguese *"os números do board"*. Apostrophe position
  (*investor's* vs *investors'*) changes the meaning.
- **Unit 061 — Reflexive pronouns (myself / themselves) & each other:** the action **loops back**
  onto the doer (*we set **ourselves** a target*) or just **emphasises** (*I'll present it
  **myself***) — and *each other* for the **A ↔ B** back-and-forth. English uses these on **far
  fewer** verbs than PT.
- **Unit 062 — a / an (the indefinite article):** English **forces** *a/an* before a singular
  countable noun (*"She's **a** strategist"*), where PT drops it. Choice is by **sound**, not
  spelling (*an hour*, *a one-page summary*).
- **Unit 063 — the (the definite article) & the zero article:** *the* points at the **specific,
  shared** thing (*the board*, *the budget*); **no article** marks the **general idea** (*AI is
  reshaping our industry*), where PT keeps *"a IA"*.

## 3. 🗺️ Confusion map — when each rival wins

The week splits into **two battlegrounds**. Keep them separate in your head.

### Battleground A — which shape of the pronoun?

| You want to… | Use | Test / signal | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| put an owner **before a noun** | possessive **adjective** — *my, your, her, our* | a noun comes right after | *"**my** deck", "**our** strategy"* |
| name an owner with **no noun after** | possessive **pronoun** — *mine, yours, hers, ours* | the word ends the phrase | *"That deck is **mine**."* |
| the person **does** the action | **subject** — *I, he, she, we, they* | it's before the verb, the doer | *"**She** trusts the model."* |
| the person **receives**, or sits **after** *to/for/with/between* | **object** — *me, him, her, us, them* | after verb or preposition | *"Send it to **me**." / "between the CEO and **me**"* |
| subject **and** object are the **same** person | **reflexive** — *myself, herself, themselves* | action bounces back onto the doer | *"We set **ourselves** a target."* |
| just **emphasise** who | **reflexive** (emphatic) | you could delete it and the sentence still works | *"I'll present it **myself**."* |
| **A ↔ B**, mutual, back-and-forth | **each other** | two (or more) parties acting on each other | *"They don't agree with **each other**."* |
| ask **who owns** it | **whose** | ownership question (≠ *who's* = who is) | *"**Whose** model is this?"* |

### Battleground B — 's vs of, and which article?

| You want to… | Use | Signal | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| show ownership by a **person/organisation** | **'s** (owner first) | living/organisational owner | *"the board**'s** decision", "the CFO**'s** model"* |
| link two **things** / long phrase | **of** | inanimate, or a heavy modifier follows | *"the top **of** the funnel", "the pace **of** change"* |
| several owners (plural + owns) | **s'** (apostrophe after the s) | plural owner | *"our competitors**'** move"* |
| a **first-mention**, one-of-many, countable thing | **a / an** | not yet specific; by **sound** | *"We need **a** vendor." / "**an** hour"* |
| a **specific, shared** thing (unique / already known) | **the** | you both know which one | *"**the** budget", "**the** vendor we chose"* |
| a **general idea** / uncountable in general | **no article (zero)** | the whole concept, not one instance | *"**AI** is reshaping the industry." / "give me **advice**"* |

**The single sharpest test of the week:** for *I* vs *me*, drop the other name and say it alone.
*"Send it to Sofia and I"* → *"Send it to I"* ❌ → so it's **me**. This kills the classic
overcorrection *"between the CEO and I."*

## 4. 🧪 10 recognition exercises (mostly contrast — ~6 minutes)

Answers and one-line explanations at the end (section 7). Retrieve first.

1. **(mine vs my)** *"That three-year roadmap is ___ ; the budget model is Sofia's."* — *mine*
   or *my*?
2. **(I vs me — the killer test)** *"The board asked the CFO and ___ to co-present the AI bet."*
   — *I* or *me*? State the test.
3. **(subject vs object, both slots)** *"___ built the model, so the board trusts ___ on the
   numbers."* — fill for *she* (doer) then *her* (receiver).
4. **('s vs of)** Choose the natural one for a live meeting: *(a)* "the decision of the board"
   or *(b)* "the board's decision"? — and say why.
5. **(apostrophe position)** You mean **several investors**. *"We can't ignore the ___
   expectations."* — *investor's* or *investors'*? What would the other one mean?
6. **(reflexive vs each other)** *"The CEO and the CFO rarely agree with ___ on the pace."* —
   *themselves* or *each other*? What would the wrong one say?
7. **(the PT reflexive overuse trap)** *"Eu me sinto confiante sobre o plano."* → in English:
   *"I feel ___ confident"* or *"I feel confident"*? Pick one.
8. **(a vs an — by sound)** *"Give me ___ hour with the numbers and ___ one-page summary."* —
   supply both articles.
9. **(a vs the — first mention vs known)** *"We evaluated ___ vendor last week; ___ vendor
   starts on Monday."* — fill both blanks and say what flipped.
10. **(the vs zero article — general vs specific)** *"___ innovation wins when it's disciplined,
    and ___ innovation team we just hired proves it."* — fill both; name the rule for each.

## 5. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula (3 themes worth practising)

1. **The *I* vs *me* / "between you and me" reflex.** Ask your tutor to feed you fast sentences
   with two people in the object slot (*"Send the deck to Sofia and ___"*, *"This stays between the
   CEO and ___"*) so the *drop-the-other-name* test becomes automatic under pressure — this is the
   error most likely to surface in a real board conversation.
2. **Articles in a general-vs-specific pitch.** Role-play framing the company to the board:
   practise switching between the **general** claim (*"**AI** is reshaping our industry"* — no
   article) and the **specific** one (*"**the** AI strategy we agreed on"* — *the*). This is exactly
   the *"a IA" / "a inovação"* interference that marks a Brazilian speaker.
3. **Ownership at speed: 's vs of.** Have the tutor challenge you to restate slow "of" phrases
   the natural owner-first way in real time (*"the roadmap of the vendor"* → *"the vendor's
   roadmap"*), which is how fluent executives sound crisp in meetings.

## 6. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

Esta semana foi sobre **as palavrinhas** que o inglês exige com precisão e que o português trata
de outro jeito. Dois campos: **(A) qual forma do pronome** — *my* (antes do substantivo) vs *mine*
(sozinho); *I/he/she* quando **faz** a ação vs *me/him/her* quando **recebe** ou vem depois de
*to/for/with/between*; *myself/themselves* quando a ação **volta** para o próprio sujeito; e *each
other* para o **A ↔ B** recíproco. **(B) posse e artigos** — o *'s* cola no **dono**, que vem
**antes** (*the board's decision*, o inverso de *"a decisão do board"*); e o artigo: *a/an* para
algo **novo/um entre vários** (escolhido pelo **som**, não pela grafia — *an hour*, *a one-page*),
*the* para o **específico e já conhecido**, e **nenhum artigo** para a **ideia geral**.

O que provavelmente ainda precisa de trabalho: **(1)** o *"between the CEO and I"* — a
hipercorreção; o certo é **me** (teste: tire o outro nome). **(2)** o **excesso de reflexivo** vindo
do PT — *"eu me sinto"* → *"I feel confident"*, **sem** *myself*; o inglês usa reflexivo em muito
menos verbos. **(3)** o artigo na **ideia geral** — *"**A** inovação vence"* vira *"**Innovation**
wins"* (sem artigo), e não *"**The** innovation wins"*. Se for reler dois arquivos, comece por
**Unit 059** (I/me) e **Unit 063** (the / zero article) — são os dois que mais aparecem numa
conversa real com o board.
