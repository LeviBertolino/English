### 1. 📘 Unit 039 — It… (the empty subject)

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** Where do you land on the AI budget?
> **You:** **It's critical to** move now — **it's** clear the market is going
> AI-first. But **it's** risky **to** rush the vendor choice.
> **CEO:** How long before we see a return?
> **You:** **It took us** two quarters to prove the ROI on the first pilot, so
> realistically **it'll be** mid-2027. **It's** worth staging it.
> **CEO:** Fine. **It's** almost four now — let's close here.

Notice the pattern before any rule. Look at every sentence-opening *It*: *It's
critical to move*, *It's clear*, *It's risky to rush*, *It took us two quarters*,
*It'll be mid-2027*, *It's almost four*. None of these *It*s points at a real
thing — you can't ask *"what is it?"*. It's an **empty subject**, a placeholder
that English **requires** in the subject slot even when there's nothing concrete
to put there.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

English sentences **must have a subject**. When there's no real one — for weather,
time, distance, or to carry a *to*-clause — English uses a dummy **it** to fill the
gap. It means nothing; it just holds the seat.

| Job of *it* | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **It's + adjective + to** + verb | *It's* + adj + *to* + base | ***It's important to*** align the board. |
| **It's + adjective + that** + clause | *It's* + adj + *that*… | ***It's clear that*** the market is shifting. |
| **It takes** (time / effort) | *It takes* (sb) time *to*… | ***It took us*** two quarters to prove ROI. |
| **Time / date** | *It's* + time | ***It's*** almost four. / ***It's*** Monday. |
| **Distance** | *It's* + distance | ***It's*** ten minutes to the boardroom. |
| **Weather** | *It's* + weather | ***It's*** raining. / ***It's*** cold. |

**Key facts:**
- The subject *it* is **never optional** in English. *"É importante…"* has no
  visible subject in Portuguese; the English version still needs one: ***It's**
  important…*, never *"Is important…"*.
- **It's + adjective + to + verb** is the workhorse pattern: *It's **hard to**
  scale, **easy to** overpromise, **worth** piloting, **too early to** commit.*
- *It takes + (person) + time + to + verb*: *"**It takes** a team six months **to**
  migrate."* The effort/time goes after *takes*; the action goes in the *to*-clause.
- This dummy *it* is **not** the pointing *it* (*"I read the report — **it** was
  sharp"*). There, *it* replaces a real noun. Here, *it* replaces **nothing**.

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why does this structure exist?** Because English grammar has one iron rule
Portuguese doesn't: **every sentence needs a subject in front of the verb.** When
the real "doer" is a whole idea (*to align the board*), a fact (*that the market is
shifting*), or nothing at all (weather, the clock), English parks a meaningless
**it** in the subject slot and pushes the real content to the back. It lets you
lead with your judgement — *It's critical…, It's clear…, It's too early…* — which
is exactly how executives frame priority, certainty, and timing.

**Decision rule #1 — *It* vs *There* (the two dummy subjects — do not confuse
them).** Both fill an empty subject slot, but they do **opposite jobs**:

| **There + be** = something *exists* | **It + be** = *describing / identifying / empty subject* |
|---|---|
| *"**There's** a risk in the rollout."* → a risk **exists** | *"**It's** risky to rush the rollout."* → *rushing* **is** risky (describes) |
| *"**There are** three open items."* → they **exist**, we're listing them | *"**It's** three o'clock."* → the empty-subject clock |
| *"**There's** a lot **to** decide."* → decisions exist | *"**It's** hard **to** decide."* → deciding **is** hard |

Decision rule: introducing/counting that something **exists** → ***There***. Saying
**what something is like**, or filling the seat for time/weather/*to*-clauses →
***It***. Minimal pair: *"**There's** a problem"* (a problem exists) vs *"**It's** a
problem"* (this situation = problematic).

**Decision rule #2 — *It's important to align…* vs *To align… is important*.** Both
are grammatical, but English overwhelmingly prefers the **it**-first version. Front
a long *to*-clause and the sentence feels top-heavy and formal:
- ✅ *"**It's** essential **to** benchmark two more vendors."* (natural)
- ⚠️ *"**To** benchmark two more vendors **is** essential."* (stiff, rarely used)

So the reflex to build is: *It's + adjective + to…*, not *To… is + adjective*.

**PT→EN mapping — this is where Portuguese betrays Levi the most.** Portuguese is a
**null-subject** language: it lets the verb stand with **no subject at all**. English
never does. So the single biggest interference error here is **dropping the *it***:
- *"É importante alinhar o board"* → ✅ ***It's** important to align the board*,
  ❌ *"Is important to align the board."*
- *"Está chovendo"* → ✅ ***It's** raining*, ❌ *"Is raining."*
- *"Faz três anos que…"* → ✅ ***It's been** three years since…*, ❌ *"Makes three
  years…"* / *"Has three years…"*.
- *"Demorou dois trimestres"* → ✅ ***It took** two quarters*, ❌ *"Delayed two
  quarters"* / *"Took two quarters"* with no subject.
Also watch **"É + adjetivo + que"**: *"É claro que o mercado mudou"* → ***It's**
clear that the market has shifted*, never *"Is clear that…"*.

**Top Brazilian-speaker errors:**
- ❌ *"**Is important to** move now on the AI budget."* → ✅ *"**It's important to**
  move now on the AI budget."* — English requires the subject *it*; Portuguese
  hides it, English can't.
- ❌ *"**Take** three months to onboard a new AI lead."* → ✅ *"**It takes** three
  months to onboard a new AI lead."* — the empty subject *it* + *takes*, not a bare
  verb.
- ❌ *"**Has** two years since we refreshed the strategy."* / *"**Makes** two
  years…"* → ✅ *"**It's been** two years since we refreshed the strategy."* — for
  elapsed time English uses *It's been…*, not *has* or *makes* (a copy of *faz*).

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. *"**It's critical to** align the board before we commit the AI budget."*
   *— Empty-subject "It" + adjective + to-verb; this is how you front a priority. "To align the board is critical" would sound stiff and unnatural.*
2. *"**It's clear that** the market is moving toward AI-first players."*
   *— "It's + adjective + that" frames a judgement as fact; drop the "It" and it stops being English ("Is clear that…" is the classic PT slip).*
3. *"**It took us** two quarters to prove the ROI on the first pilot."*
   *— "It takes/took (sb) time to…" for effort; the empty "It" is obligatory — "Took us two quarters" with no subject is wrong.*
4. *"**It's too early to** pick a single vendor — let's keep two in play."*
   *— "It's too early to + verb" hedges timing; the dummy "It" carries the whole to-clause that follows.*
5. *"**There's** a real risk in rushing, but **it's** worth piloting first."*
   *— One sentence, both dummies: "There's" introduces the risk (it exists); "it's worth" describes the action. Swapping them breaks the meaning.*
6. *"**It's been** three years since we last refreshed the strategy."*
   *— Elapsed time takes "It's been…", never "has" or "makes" — the direct copy of "faz três anos".*
7. *"**It's** almost four — **it's** a ten-minute walk to the boardroom, so let's move."*
   *— The empty "It" for the clock and for distance; neither "It" points at a real thing, both are grammatically required.*
8. *"**It's** raining hard, so the offsite golf is off — **it's** freezing too."*
   *— Everyday weather: English forces a subject even when nothing is doing the raining; Portuguese "Está chovendo" has none.*

### 8. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

- Ask your tutor to run a **"lead with your judgement" drill**: they name a topic
  from your world — *"the AI budget," "the vendor choice," "the 2027 vision," "the
  timeline"* — and you must open with *It's + adjective + to…* / *It's clear that…*
  / *It's too early to…* (*"It's critical to move now," "It's clear that the market
  is shifting," "It's too early to commit"*). Goal: make the empty *It* automatic so
  you never drop it in front of the board.
- Then a **"There vs It" snap drill**: your tutor reads a prompt and you choose the
  right dummy out loud — *"three open risks" → "**There are** three open risks"*;
  *"deciding is hard" → "**It's** hard to decide"*; *"a lot to align" → "**There's**
  a lot to align"*; *"rushing is risky" → "**It's** risky to rush."* This locks in
  the *exists* (There) vs *describes / empty-subject* (It) boundary.

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 038 — the imperative):**
1. **Remove *to*** → *"**Let's align** on the vision before the board call."*
   *Let's* is always followed by the **base verb**; *"Let's to align"* wrongly
   inserts an infinitive *to*.
2. ***"Don't forget the ask."*** — the negative imperative is **Don't + base
   verb**, with **no *-s***; *"don't forgets"* wrongly puts a present-tense *-s* on
   a base verb.
3. ***"Could you send me the updated numbers?"*** — with a **senior**, the polite
   request is the safe register; the bald imperative *"Send me…"* can read as a
   **cold order / demand**. (A bald imperative is fine for your own team, steps,
   directions or offers — just not for a favour up the chain.)

**Spaced review:**
- **(Unit 032)** ***mustn't*** — *"you **mustn't** forward them to anyone outside
  the board."* The figures are confidential, so forwarding is **forbidden**. Pick
  *don't need to* and you'd say forwarding is merely **optional** — the opposite
  message.
- **(Unit 011)** ***went*** — *"I **went** to the board with three options."*
  *"go"* is **irregular** (go → went), so you **never** add *-ed*; *"goed"* doesn't
  exist.

**Recognition drill:**
1. **Add *It's* at the start** → *"**It's** essential to benchmark two more vendors
   before we sign."* English requires a subject in front of the verb; Portuguese
   *"É essencial…"* hides it, but English can't drop it.
2. **There's / It's** → *"**There's** a lot of uncertainty in the forecast"*
   (uncertainty **exists** — introducing it) vs *"**It's** hard to forecast in this
   market"* (**describing** the act of forecasting with the empty *It* + adj + to).
   *There* = something exists; *It* = what it's like / empty subject.
3. **Two fixes** → *"**It takes** about six months to migrate the whole platform."*
   Add the empty subject *It*, and change *take* → *takes* (third person after
   *it*).
4. ***"It's been two years since the last strategy refresh."*** — elapsed time takes
   **It's been…**. *"Makes two years"* is a word-for-word copy of *"faz dois anos"*;
   *"Has two years"* copies *"tem dois anos"* — both are Portuguese structures, not
   English.

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**A regra e quando usar.** Toda frase em inglês **precisa de um sujeito** antes do
verbo — mesmo quando não há um "dono" real da ação. Nesses casos o inglês usa um
**it vazio** (dummy subject), um sujeito que não significa nada, só ocupa o lugar.
Ele aparece em quatro trabalhos: (1) *It's + adjetivo + to + verbo* — a estrutura
mais útil para você: *"**It's** critical **to** align the board"*, *"**It's** too
early **to** commit"*; (2) *It's + adjetivo + that* — *"**It's** clear **that** the
market is shifting"*; (3) *It takes + (alguém) + tempo + to…* para esforço/duração:
*"**It took us** two quarters to prove the ROI"*; (4) tempo, data, distância e clima:
*"**It's** almost four"*, *"**It's** raining"*. Serve para você **liderar com o seu
julgamento** — prioridade, certeza, timing — que é como um executivo enquadra as
coisas.

**As armadilhas.** (1) A cilada nº 1 é **apagar o *it***: o português é língua de
sujeito nulo (*"É importante…", "Está chovendo…"*), o inglês **nunca** é — diga
*"**It's** important…"*, *"**It's** raining"*, jamais *"Is important…"*. (2) *It* ×
*There*: **There + be** = algo **existe** (*"**There's** a risk"* — existe um
risco); **It + be** = **descreve** ou é sujeito vazio (*"**It's** risky to rush"* —
apressar é arriscado). Existe/conta → *There*; como é / clima / hora / oração com
*to* → *It*. (3) Tempo decorrido é *"**It's been** two years since…"*, nunca
*"Makes two years"* (cópia de *faz*) nem *"Has two years"* (cópia de *tem*). (4)
Esforço/duração é *"**It takes** six months to…"*, com o *it* e o *-s* — não o verbo
solto. Se o contraste *There* × *It* ainda embolar, releia
`lessons/2026-08-14-unit-033.md` (there is / there are).
