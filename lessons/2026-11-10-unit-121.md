### 1. 📘 Unit 121 — *in · at · on* for position (where something is)

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** Where are you running the strategy session?
> **Levi:** **In** the boardroom on the 14th floor — everyone's already **in** the building.
> **CEO:** Good. Is the AI investment case **on** the agenda?
> **Levi:** It's **at** the top of the agenda. The three scenarios are **on** slide four, and the full model is **in** the appendix.
> **CFO:** Will Marta be there, or is she still **at** the offsite?
> **Levi:** She's dialling in — she's **at** the airport now, boarding in twenty minutes.
> **CEO:** And the board? Who's **on** the board these days?
> **Levi:** Two new directors joined **in** Q3. I'll put their names **on** the last slide.

Three tiny words, three different pictures. **in** puts something **inside** a space or area — *in the boardroom, in the building, in the appendix, in Q3*. **at** fixes it at a **point** or an **event** — *at the top, at the offsite, at the airport*. **on** lays it on a **surface, a line, or a list** — *on the agenda, on slide four, on the board, on the last slide*. Portuguese reaches for the same *em / no / na* in almost all of these (*na sala, na agenda, no quadro, no aeroporto*), which is exactly why English feels like it keeps changing the rule on you. It isn't — each word draws a different shape.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

Picture the noun as a **box, a point, or a surface**. That picture chooses the word.

| Word | The picture | Use it for | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **in** | **inside** a 3-D space or a bounded area | rooms, buildings, cities/countries, documents, groups | *in the boardroom · in London · in the report · in the market · in a meeting* |
| **at** | a **point** / a specific spot / an **event** | precise locations, addresses, events you attend | *at the office · at the airport · at the offsite · at the top · at a conference* |
| **on** | **on a surface**, a line, or a **list** | surfaces, pages, screens, lists, lines, membership | *on the agenda · on page 3 · on the slide · on the board · on the call · on the roadmap* |

**Key anchors (memorise these, the rest follow):**
- **in** = enclosed / surrounded: *in the room, in the car, in Brazil, in the document, in the team.*
- **at** = a dot on the map or a scheduled event: *at the door, at reception, at the station, at the board meeting.*
- **on** = resting on a line or surface, or named in a list: *on the table, on the wall, on page 10, on the agenda, on the committee.*

**Two useful sub-rules:**
- **at** vs **in** for an event + its building: you are **at** an event (*at the conference, at the meeting*) but **in** the room/building that holds it (*in the conference hall, in the office*). Both can be true at once.
- **on** for being a **member** of a named body or a **list**: *on the board, on the committee, on the shortlist, on the agenda* — the group is treated as a line/list you sit on, not a box you're inside.

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why do these three words exist — and why only three?** English refuses to say "location" with one neutral word. It forces you to choose a **shape**: is the thing *inside* something (**in**), *at a precise point or event* (**at**), or *on a surface / in a list* (**on**)? The shape carries meaning. "It's **in** the agenda" and "it's **on** the agenda" are not the same sloppiness of the same idea — one is wrong because an agenda is a **list** (a surface you add lines to), not a container you climb inside. Choosing the box is choosing the picture, and the picture is the message.

**Decision rule #1 — `at` vs `in` (point/event vs inside):**

| | **at** | **in** |
|---|---|---|
| picture | a **point** on the map, or an **event** | **inside** a space/area |
| answers | *where, precisely? / which event?* | *inside what?* |
| example | *"I'm **at** the airport."* (the location/point) | *"I'm **in** the terminal."* (inside the building) |

Minimal pair — same place, different lens:
- *"She's **at** the offsite."* → she's attending the event / is at that location.
- *"She's **in** São Paulo."* → she's inside that area/city.
Both can be true: *"She's **at** the conference, **in** Lisbon."* — **at** the event, **in** the city.

**Decision rule #2 — `on` vs `in` (list/surface vs container):**

| | **on** | **in** |
|---|---|---|
| picture | a **surface / line / list** | a **container / enclosed space** |
| example | *"It's **on** the agenda."* · *"on** page 3"* · *"on** the board"* | *"It's **in** the report."* · *"in** the boardroom"* · *"in** the budget"* |

Minimal pair that flips the meaning:
- *"It's **on** the agenda."* → it's a listed item we'll discuss.
- *"It's **in** the agenda (document)."* → rare; only if you mean *printed inside the agenda document*. For a discussion item, English wants **on**.

**PT→EN mapping.** This is the hardest preposition set for Brazilians because **Portuguese uses *em* (→ *no / na*) for all three**: *na sala* (**in** the room), *na reunião* (**at/in** the meeting), *na agenda* (**on** the agenda), *no quadro* (**on** the board), *no aeroporto* (**at** the airport), *na empresa* (**at** the company). The direct translation *em → in* works for genuine containers (*in the room, in the report, in Brazil*) but **betrays you** for points/events (needs **at**) and for lists/surfaces (needs **on**). The fix is to stop translating *em* and instead ask *is this a box, a point, or a surface?*

**Top 3 typical Brazilian-speaker errors:**
- ❌ *"The AI case is **in** the agenda."* → ✅ *"The AI case is **on** the agenda."* — an agenda is a **list** (surface), not a container. Same for *on the roadmap, on the shortlist, on the list.*
- ❌ *"I'll meet you **in** the airport."* (meaning the location/point) → ✅ *"I'll meet you **at** the airport."* — *at* for the point/meeting place; keep *in* only when you truly mean inside the building (*in the terminal*).
- ❌ *"He sits **in** the board."* → ✅ *"He sits **on** the board."* — membership of a named body is **on** (*on the board, on the committee*), never *in*.

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. **The AI investment case is at the top of the agenda.**
   *— `at` for a precise **point** (the top), `on` would own "the agenda" as a list; here we pin the item to a spot on that list → `at the top of`.*
2. **The three scenarios are on slide four, and the full model is in the appendix.**
   *— `on` a **surface** (a slide), `in` a **container** (the appendix is a bounded section). Two shapes in one breath — that's the whole unit.*
3. **Two new directors joined the board in Q3.**
   *— `in` for a **bounded time-space** (a quarter is a container of time); note you *join the board* but sit *on* it — see #5.*
4. **She's at the airport now, boarding in twenty minutes.**
   *— `at` = the location as a **point** you're at, not the building you're inside; if you meant physically inside you'd say `in the terminal`.*
5. **I'd like you on the steering committee for the AI programme.**
   *— membership of a named body = `on` (surface/list), never `in`. The classic PT trap: *na comissão* pulls you toward "in".*
6. **Everything we agreed is on page three of the deck.**
   *— `on` a **page** (a surface); Portuguese *na página* tempts "in", but a page is something you write **on**.*
7. **Let's talk about it in the meeting, not on the call.**
   *(everyday/work)* — *`in` the meeting* = inside that event/room; *`on` the call* = connected on the line. Different pictures, both idiomatic — "on the call / on the phone / on the line" are fixed **on** expressions.*
8. **The keys are on the table in the kitchen.**
   *(everyday)* — `on` the **surface** (table), `in` the **room** (kitchen). The cleanest illustration of surface-vs-container with no executive noise.*

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 119):**
1. **(b)** *"My CFO, who sits on the board, is travelling."* — the **commas** (non-defining) add extra info about the one CFO you already have in mind. Version (a), with no commas (defining), implies several CFOs and singles out the one on the board.
2. **which** — *"…, **which** gave the team breathing room."* This is sentence-*which*, pointing at the whole act of pushing the launch, not at a noun. *that* can't open a comma clause; *what* is the Portuguese *o que* interference and is never used here.
3. **which** — *"The partnership, **which** we signed in Q1, …"*. After a comma (non-defining) you **never** use *that*; it's *which* for things, *who* for people. Your instinct to carry *que → that* is the trap.

**Spaced review:**
- *Unit 114:* *"We're **still** negotiating … and we haven't signed **yet**."* — *still* = the situation **continues** (middle position, after *are*); *yet* = expected but **not done** (end position, in a negative). Swap them and you break both the position and the meaning.
- *Unit 091:* *"Can you send **it to me** before the call?"* — when the **thing** is a pronoun (*it / them*), English forbids *"send me it"* and forces **shape B**: *thing + to + person*. ✅ *send it to me* / *send them to her*.

**Recognition drill:**
1. *"…**at** the training centre, and I'll see you **in** reception"* — **at** the centre (a point/location where the event happens); **in** reception (inside that bounded area). Either *"at reception"* (the point) is also fine, but with "see you ___" meaning *inside the reception area*, **in** fits. Key: the centre is a point/venue → *at*; the area you wait inside → *in*.
2. *"…is **on** the agenda for Thursday."* — an agenda is a **list** (a surface you add lines to), not a container → **on**, never *in*. Same family: *on the roadmap, on the shortlist.*
3. *"…be **on** that committee … be **in** the room…"* — membership of a named body = **on** (a committee is a list you sit **on**); a physical space you're inside = **in** (the room is a container).
4. **Both wrong:** *"I'll meet the vendor **at** the airport … on page two of the folder."* — *the airport* is a **point/meeting place** → **at** (use *in* only for *in the terminal*, inside the building); *page two* is a **surface** → **on**.

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

Em inglês, "onde está a coisa" não tem uma palavra neutra: você é obrigado a escolher uma **forma**. **in** = *dentro* de um espaço ou área fechada (*in the boardroom, in the report, in São Paulo, in Q3*). **at** = um **ponto** no mapa ou um **evento** ao qual você comparece (*at the airport, at the offsite, at the top of the agenda, at the meeting*). **on** = sobre uma **superfície, uma linha ou uma lista** (*on the agenda, on page 3, on the slide, on the board, on the call*). A pergunta que resolve quase tudo é: *isto é uma caixa, um ponto, ou uma superfície/lista?*

A armadilha é que o português usa **o mesmo *em* (no / na)** para os três: *na sala* (in), *na reunião* (at/in), *na agenda* (on), *no quadro* (on), *no aeroporto* (at). Traduzir *em → in* funciona só para recipientes de verdade (*in the room, in the report, in Brazil*) e te trai nos outros dois casos. Os três erros que mais pegam executivos brasileiros: (1) *"**in** the agenda"* → o correto é **on** the agenda (agenda é lista/superfície); (2) *"**in** the airport"* quando é ponto de encontro → **at** the airport (deixe *in* só para "in the terminal"); (3) *"sits **in** the board"* → **on** the board (participação em um órgão nomeado é sempre **on**: *on the board, on the committee*). Pare de traduzir *em* e desenhe a figura primeiro. Reler se ainda pesar: **`lessons/2026-11-03-unit-114.md`** (*still/yet*) e **`lessons/2026-10-11-unit-091.md`** (dois objetos / regra do pronome).
