### 1. 📘 Unit 034 — should (giving advice and recommendations)

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** The board is nervous about the AI spend. What's your read?
> **You:** I think we **should** slow the smaller pilots and protect the two
> flagship bets. We **shouldn't** spread the budget across ten experiments.
> **CEO:** And the messaging?
> **You:** We **should** position the company as an AI-first player, but we
> **should** be careful not to over-promise on timelines. **Should** we bring the
> CFO into this conversation earlier?
> **CEO:** Yes — good idea.

Notice the pattern before any rule. Every *should* here is a **recommendation** —
the right thing to do, in your judgment. It is **not** an order (*must*) and **not**
an external rule (*have to*). It's advice: *"this is what I'd do."* Portuguese
says this with *"deveria / deveríamos"* — and that conditional ending is where
the interference starts.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

*should* is a modal. Like *must* and *can*, it takes the **base verb** and never
changes for *he/she/it*.

| | should + base verb | |
|---|---|---|
| Positive | **should** | *We **should** protect the flagship bets.* |
| Negative | **shouldn't** (should not) | *We **shouldn't** over-promise.* |
| Question | **Should** + subject…? | ***Should** we brief the CFO first?* |

**Key facts:**
- Base verb only: *should **present***, *should **be***, never *should to
  present* / *should presents* / *should presenting*.
- Same form for everyone: *I / you / he / we / they* **should** — no *-s*.
- *ought to* means almost the same as *should*, but it **keeps the *to***: *"We
  **ought to** review the portfolio."* (a bit more formal; *should* is the
  everyday choice).
- Softeners are common and native: *"**I think** we should…"*, *"**Maybe** we
  should…"*, *"We should **probably**…"* — they make advice sound like a
  suggestion, not an order.

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why does *should* exist?** English needs a way to give **advice or a
recommendation** — to say *"this is the right/best thing to do"* — that stops
**short of an obligation**. *should* is the voice of judgment and opinion: *"In my
view, this is what we ought to do."* It leaves room to disagree. That's exactly
why it's the executive's default verb: you **recommend** a direction to the board;
you rarely **command** it.

**Decision rule #1 — *should* vs *must* / *have to* (advice vs obligation).** This
is the confusion that matters most, and it builds directly on Unit 032.

| | *should* | *must* / *have to* |
|---|---|---|
| Force | advice, recommendation | obligation, necessity |
| Who decides | **your judgment** — it's the best option | a **rule / authority / hard need** |
| Room to say no | yes, it's a suggestion | no, it's required |
| Example | *We **should** hire a Head of AI.* (I recommend it) | *We **have to** file the compliance report.* (the regulator requires it) |

Minimal pair — same sentence, very different weight:
- *"We **should** present the roadmap to the board."* → it's a **good idea**;
  someone could still say *"actually, let's wait."* ✅
- *"We **have to** present the roadmap to the board."* → it's **required** (the
  board mandated it); there's no choice. ✅

Decision rule: **is it my recommendation, or a hard requirement?** Recommendation
→ *should*. Requirement / rule / no-choice → *have to* (or *must* for a strong
personal/internal insistence). If you'd naturally add *"in my opinion"*, it's
*should*.

**Decision rule #2 — *shouldn't* vs *mustn't* (bad idea vs forbidden).** The
negatives split the same way, and mixing them is a real executive risk:
- *"We **shouldn't** rush the launch."* → it's a **bad idea**, not advisable, but
  not banned. ✅
- *"We **mustn't** share these numbers outside the board."* → it is
  **forbidden**. ✅

Say *shouldn't* when you mean *"unwise"*; say *mustn't* only when you mean
*"prohibited."* Downgrading a prohibition to *shouldn't* can sound dangerously
casual to a board.

**PT→EN mapping — the conditional trap.** Portuguese advice uses the conditional:
*"deveríamos priorizar", "você deveria apresentar", "não deveríamos prometer
demais."* The clean mapping is *"deveria / deveríamos"* → **should**:
*"Deveríamos priorizar as apostas grandes"* → *"We **should** prioritize the big
bets."* Where it betrays Levi: (1) Portuguese folds everything into one word
(*deveria*), but English needs *should* + **base verb**, with **no *to***:
*"should **prioritize**"*, never *"should **to** prioritize."* (2) There's no
*-s*: *"he **should** present"*, never *"he should presents."*

**Top Brazilian-speaker errors:**
- ❌ *"We should **to** consolidate the AI stack."* → ✅ *"We **should
  consolidate** the AI stack."* — *should* is a modal; it takes the **base verb**
  with **no *to*** (the *deveria + infinitivo* reflex adds a phantom *to*).
- ❌ *"The CFO should **approves** the budget first."* → ✅ *"The CFO **should
  approve** the budget first."* — no *-s* on the verb after *should*, ever.
- ❌ *"We **should** file the tax report by Friday"* (when the law requires it) →
  ✅ *"We **have to** file the tax report by Friday."* — a legal/external
  requirement is *have to*, not the softer *should* (advice).

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. *"I think we **should** position the company as an AI-first player."*
   *— A recommendation softened with "I think"; it's your judgment, not an order — so should, not have to.*
2. *"We **shouldn't** spread the innovation budget across ten small bets."*
   *— "Unwise / not advisable," not "forbidden" — so shouldn't, not mustn't.*
3. *"The board **should** see the risk analysis before it votes."*
   *— Advice about the best sequence; if the bylaws required it, you'd switch to "has to see."*
4. *"**Should** we bring the CFO into the AI investment discussion earlier?"*
   *— A question asking for a recommendation; "Should we…?" invites agreement, unlike "Do we have to…?" which asks about a rule.*
5. *"We **should probably** re-forecast before the offsite."*
   *— "probably" is a native softener that turns firm advice into a gentle suggestion — very common in executive speech.*
6. *"You **should** present the vision, not just the roadmap, to the C-suite."*
   *— Personal advice to a peer; base verb after should, no "to present".*
7. *"We **ought to** review the innovation portfolio quarterly."*
   *— "ought to" ≈ should but keeps the "to"; slightly more formal, good for written policy.*
8. *"You look exhausted — you **should** take the afternoon off."*
   *— Everyday life: classic friendly advice, the textbook use of should.*

### 8. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

- Ask your tutor to run a **"should vs have to" sorting drill**: they throw mixed
  situations at you — *"the regulator requires the filing," "in your opinion the
  pilots are too many," "the board bylaws demand a vote," "you think the vendor
  deal is a mistake"* — and you answer fast with *"We have to…"* (requirement) or
  *"We should…"* (your recommendation), saying each aloud so the advice/obligation
  split becomes automatic.
- Then a **board-recommendation drill**: role-play advising the CEO on the AI
  portfolio using only *should / shouldn't*, layered with native softeners
  (*"I think we should…", "we should probably…", "maybe we shouldn't…"*) — the
  goal is to sound like you're **recommending** a direction, not **ordering** it,
  which is the register a senior leader lives in.

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 033 — there is / there are):**
1. ***There's*** — *"**There's** a meeting with the CFO tomorrow."* The
   Portuguese *"tem uma reunião"* misleads you into *has/have*; existence is
   *there is / there are*, **never** *have*.
2. ***are*** — *"There **are** two vendor contracts and one sign-off still
   pending."* Agreement looks at the **first noun after** the verb (*two
   contracts*, plural), not at *there*.
3. ***There's*** — *"**There's** a problem with the forecast."* You're
   **introducing** it (new information). *"It's a problem…"* wrongly assumes the
   problem is **already known** and you're just describing it.

**Spaced review:**
- **(Unit 027)** ***are going to overshoot*** — *"Look at the burn rate — we**'re
  going to** overshoot the AI budget by Q3."* *"Look at…"* points at **evidence
  you can see now**, and evidence-based predictions take *going to*, not *will*.
- **(Unit 004)** ❌ *"You are presenting the 2027 vision at the offsite?"* → ✅
  *"**Are you** presenting the 2027 vision at the offsite?"* — English forms the
  question by **inverting** (*to be* before the subject); Portuguese asks with
  intonation alone, keeping statement order, and that reflex is the trap.

**Recognition drill:**
1. **Drop *to*** — *"We **should reallocate** the budget toward the flagship
   bets."* *should* is a modal followed by the **base verb**; *should to* is never
   correct (the *deveria + infinitivo* interference).
2. **Drop the *-s*** — *"The lead **should report** the metrics to me every
   Friday."* No *-s* after a modal, even for *he/she/it*; *should reports* is
   impossible.
3. ***have to*** — *"We **have to** file the compliance report by Friday."* A
   **regulator's requirement** is an obligation, not advice; *should* would wrongly
   imply it's merely *recommended* and therefore optional.
4. ***mustn't*** — *"You **mustn't** share these figures externally."* Sharing is
   **forbidden**; *shouldn't* would downgrade a prohibition to *"unwise,"*
   dangerously understating the risk to the board.

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**A regra e quando usar.** *should* é o verbo do **conselho e da recomendação** —
diz *"esta é a melhor coisa a fazer, na minha opinião"*, sem chegar a ser uma
obrigação. Equivale ao *"deveria / deveríamos"* do português: *"Deveríamos
priorizar as apostas grandes"* → *"We **should** prioritize the big bets."* É o
verbo natural do executivo, porque você **recomenda** um caminho ao board, quase
nunca **manda**. Forma: *should + verbo base*, igual para todos (*I / he / we
should*), negativo *shouldn't*, pergunta *Should we…?*. Softeners nativos —
*"I think we should…", "we should probably…"* — deixam o conselho ainda mais
natural.

**As armadilhas.** (1) O *deveria + infinitivo* faz você colar um *to* fantasma:
o certo é *"should **prioritize**"*, **nunca** *"should **to** prioritize"*; e não
há *-s* (*"he should **present**"*, não *"presents"*). (2) Não confunda *should*
(conselho, dá para discordar) com *have to / must* (obrigação, regra externa, sem
escolha): a lei exige *"we **have to** file"*, não *"we should file"*. (3) No
negativo, *shouldn't* é *"má ideia / não é aconselhável"*; *mustn't* é
*"proibido"* — rebaixar uma proibição para *shouldn't* soa perigosamente casual
para um board. Se obrigação vs recomendação embolar, releia
`lessons/2026-08-13-unit-032.md` (must, mustn't, don't need to).
