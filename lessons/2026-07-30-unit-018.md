### 1. 📘 Unit 018 — How long have you (been)…? (present perfect 4: duration up to now)

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** **How long have** you **been** running the AI transformation now?
> **You:** I've **been leading** it **for** about eight months — **since** the board
> approved the pilot. We've **had** a dedicated data team **since** March.
> **CEO:** And the vendor?
> **You:** They've **been** with us **for** two years. We've **known** their
> leadership team **since** the first proof of concept — so the trust is already there.

Notice the pattern *before* any rule. Everything here started in the **past** and
is **still true now**: you *are still* running the transformation, the team *is
still* dedicated, the vendor *is still* with you. Portuguese would say *"faz oito
meses que lidero…"* with a **present** verb. English refuses that — for something
that began in the past and **continues to now**, it reaches for **have/has +
(been)**, not the present tense.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

For a situation that **started in the past and continues up to now**, English uses
the **present perfect** — not the present simple.

| Meaning | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Ongoing **action** (verbs of doing) | **have/has been + -ing** | *I've **been leading** it for 8 months.* |
| Ongoing **state** (be, have, know, live, work-for) | **have/has + past participle** | *They've **been** with us for 2 years.* |
| The question | **How long have/has + subject + (been)…?** | ***How long have** you **been** here?* |

**Key facts:**
- **The question is always present perfect:** *"**How long have** you worked
  here?"* / *"**How long have** you **been** learning English?"* — never *"How long
  do you work here?"*
- **Action → been + -ing:** *"I've **been waiting** for the numbers."* The action
  is still going on.
- **State verbs stay simple** (no -ing): *be, have (=possess), know, like* →
  *"I've **known** her for years,"* not *"I've been knowing."*
- **Partner words (full unit tomorrow):** **for** + a length (*for 8 months, for
  two years*); **since** + a start point (*since March, since the pilot*).
- **Short answer keeps the auxiliary:** *"How long have you been here?" → "**I've
  been** here for a week."*

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why does this structure exist?** Because some situations don't *belong* to the
past **or** the present alone — they **span both**. You started leading the
transformation eight months ago **and you're still doing it now**. Neither the
past simple (*"I led it"* → over) nor the present simple (*"I lead it"* → no
duration) can say that. The present perfect is the only tense that draws one line
**from a past start to the present moment** and holds the whole thing open. That's
the entire meaning of *"How long have you…?"* — measure the line that reaches now.

**Decision rule — present perfect vs the rival, present simple.** This is *the*
error, and it's almost purely a translation reflex. The test: **is there a
duration reaching up to now (a *for/since* / *how long*)?**

| | Present perfect (*I've been here for a year*) | Present simple (*I am here / I live here*) |
|---|---|---|
| Question | *How long **have** you **been** here?* | *Where **do** you live?* |
| Meaning | a stretch **from past start → now** | a fact with **no duration** in view |
| Trigger | *how long, for, since* | a general truth, right now, habits |

Minimal pairs — same words, the duration flips the tense:
- *"**How long have** you **been** in this role?"* → measuring the line to now. ✅
- *"**Where do** you **work**?"* → a plain present fact, no span. ✅

- *"We've **been running** the pilot **for** three months."* → started, still going.
- *"We **run** three pilots a year."* → a habit/routine, not a single ongoing span.

The hinge: **the moment a *how long / for / since* appears about something still
true, you must use the present perfect, not the present simple.** *"I live here
for three years"* is the single most common Brazilian slip — and it's wrong.

**PT→EN mapping.** Portuguese marks this duration with the **present tense** plus
*"há / faz … que"*, and that's exactly the trap:
- *"**Faz** oito meses **que** lidero isso"* / *"**Lidero** isso **há** oito
  meses"* → **not** *"I lead it for eight months"* → ✅ *"I've **been leading** it
  **for** eight months."*
- *"**Conheço** a equipe deles **desde** o primeiro PoC"* → ✅ *"I've **known**
  their team **since** the first PoC."* (state verb → no -ing).
- *"**Trabalho** aqui **há** dois anos"* → ✅ *"I've **worked** / I've **been
  working** here **for** two years."* The Portuguese present *"trabalho"* becomes
  English present perfect.

**Top Brazilian-speaker errors:**
- ❌ *I **work** here **for** two years.* → ✅ *I've **worked** here **for** two
  years.* — *a duration reaching now (for two years) forces the present perfect;
  the present simple "work" cannot carry a span up to the present.*
- ❌ *How long **do** you **know** the CFO?* → ✅ *How long **have** you **known**
  the CFO?* — *"how long … up to now" is always present perfect; "do you know"
  asks a plain present fact, not a duration.*
- ❌ *I've **been knowing** their team since March.* → ✅ *I've **known** their team
  since March.* — *"know" is a state verb; states use the simple present perfect,
  not the -ing form (only actions take been + -ing).*

---

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. **How long have** you **been** running the AI transformation? — *the "how long …
   up to now" question is always present perfect; "How long are you running it?"
   would be wrong — no present-tense duration in English.*
2. I've **been leading** the innovation portfolio **for** eight months. — *an
   ongoing action that started in the past and continues → been + -ing, not "I lead
   it for eight months".*
3. We've **had** a dedicated data team **since** March. — *state verb "have"
   (possess) → simple present perfect, no -ing; the team still exists now.*
4. The vendor has **been** with us **for** two years, and the relationship is solid.
   — *"be" is a state → have been (no -ing); the two years run right up to the
   present, so not "is with us for two years".*
5. I've **known** their leadership team **since** the first proof of concept. —
   *"know" is a state verb → present perfect simple; "have been knowing" is
   impossible in English.*
6. The board has **been debating** the AI budget **for** three weeks now. — *an
   ongoing action still unresolved → been + -ing; "for three weeks now" signals the
   span reaches the present.*
7. **How long have** you **worked** in strategy? — *career-duration question up to
   now → present perfect; "How long do you work in strategy?" is the classic slip.*
8. We've **lived** in São Paulo **since** 2019. — *everyday life; a state
   continuing to now → present perfect + since, never "we live here since 2019".*

### 8. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

- Ask your tutor to run a **"tenure interview"**: they fire *"How long have
  you…?"* questions about your real world — *"How long have you been leading
  innovation?", "How long have you known this vendor?", "How long has the board
  been discussing the AI budget?"* — and you answer with **have/has been + for/
  since**, never slipping into the present. Have them deliberately bait you with a
  Portuguese-style prompt (*"So you lead it for eight months, right?"*) so you
  practice **correcting** it to *"I've been leading it for eight months."*
- Then an **action-vs-state sorting drill**: your tutor calls out verbs from your
  routine (*lead, run, debate, wait* = actions; *know, have, be, own* = states) and
  you must instantly produce the ongoing form — *been + -ing* for actions, plain
  present perfect for states — so *"been knowing"* and *"been having"* stop feeling
  possible.

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 017)**
1. ✅ *"**Have** you **ever** **led** a company-wide AI transformation?"* — *"have"
   + "ever" between subject and verb + the past participle "led"; "ever" +
   present perfect is the standard experience question.*
2. ✅ *"I've **never** seen adoption work without a sponsor."* — *drop "haven't";
   "never" is already the whole negative, and English forbids the double negative
   "haven't never".*
3. **been** — ✅ *"The CFO has **been** to the data centre."* — *she's back, so it's
   a finished, came-back experience; **gone** would mean she is **still there** and
   couldn't be "back with a report".*

**Spaced review**
- **(Unit 011)** ✅ *"Last quarter we **went** to the board with three options and
  **spent** two months on the migration."* — *both are irregular: go → went (never
  "goed"), spend → spent (never "spendt"); don't add -ed to an irregular verb.*

**Recognition drill**
1. ❌ *"I work here for three years."* → ✅ *"I've **worked** (been working) here
   **for** three years."* — *a duration reaching now (for three years) forces the
   present perfect; the present simple can't carry a span up to the present.*
2. ✅ *"I've **been waiting** for the numbers all morning"* (action → **been +
   -ing**) and *"I've **known** the CFO since 2018"* (state → simple participle). —
   *actions in progress take been + -ing; state verbs like "know" never take -ing,
   so "been knowing" is wrong.*
3. ✅ *"**How long have** you **run** (been running) the innovation portfolio?"* —
   *"how long … up to now" is always present perfect: "have" + subject + participle
   (or been + -ing for the ongoing action), never the present simple.*
4. ✅ Habit: *"**Do** you **run** three pilots a year?"* (present simple — a
   repeated routine). ✅ Span to now: *"How long **have** you **been running** this
   pilot?"* (present perfect — one ongoing stretch to the present). — *a general
   habit uses the present simple; a single situation measured up to now uses the
   present perfect.*

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**A regra e quando usar.** Para algo que **começou no passado e continua até
agora**, o inglês usa o **present perfect**, não o presente. Ação em andamento →
**have/has been + -ing** (*"I've **been leading** it for 8 months"*); estado
(*be, have, know, live*) → **have/has + particípio** simples, **sem -ing** (*"I've
**known** them since March"*). A pergunta de duração é **sempre** present perfect:
*"**How long have** you…?"*. Use *for* + duração (*for two years*) e *since* +
ponto de partida (*since March*) — a unidade completa desses dois é amanhã.

**As armadilhas.** (1) A maior interferência do português: *"trabalho aqui **há**
dois anos" / "**faz** dois anos **que**…"* usa presente em PT, mas em inglês vira
**present perfect** — *"I've **worked** here **for** two years"*, **jamais** *"I
work here for two years"*. (2) A pergunta é present perfect: *"**How long have**
you known the CFO?"*, não *"How long **do** you know…?"*. (3) Verbos de estado não
levam -ing: é *"I've **known**"*, nunca *"I've been knowing"* — só **ações** usam
*been + -ing*. Se o contraste com o passado terminado ainda embolar, releia
`lessons/2026-07-29-unit-017.md` (have you ever) e `lessons/2026-07-26-unit-014.md`
(past simple, o rival do passado fechado).
