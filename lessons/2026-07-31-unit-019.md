### 1. 📘 Unit 019 — for / since / ago (measuring time up to now — and back from now)

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** How long has the innovation portfolio been under your control?
> **You:** **For** about a year now — **since** last July, when the board split the
> function out. We hired the first AI lead three months **ago**, and honestly the
> pace hasn't slowed **since**.
> **CEO:** And the vendor?
> **You:** We signed them two years **ago**. So we've worked with them **for** two
> years — **since** 2024. Long enough to trust them.

Notice the three little words doing three different jobs *before* any rule. **For**
measures a **length** (*for a year, for two years*). **Since** names the **start
point** and lets the clock run to now (*since last July, since 2024*). **Ago**
counts **backwards from now** to a finished past moment (*three months ago, two
years ago*) — and look where it sits: **after** the time, not before it.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

Three ways to talk about *when* and *how long*, each with its own shape and its own
tense.

| Word | Answers | Followed by | Tense it lives with | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **for** | *how long?* (a length) | a **period** (*two years, ten minutes*) | present perfect (up to now) | *I've been here **for** two years.* |
| **since** | *starting when?* | a **start point** (*Monday, 2024, I arrived*) | present perfect (up to now) | *I've been here **since** 2024.* |
| **ago** | *when? (how far back)* | comes **after** a period (*two years **ago***) | **past simple** (a finished point) | *I arrived two years **ago**.* |

**Key facts:**
- **for + a stretch of time:** *for* + *three days / an hour / ten years / a long
  time*. Never *"for Monday"*, never *"for 2024"* — those are start points, not
  lengths.
- **since + a starting moment:** *since* + *Monday / March / 2024 / the board
  approved it*. It marks *where the clock started* and keeps running to **now**.
- **ago goes after the period and points back from now:** *"two years **ago**"* =
  two years back from today. It always partners with the **past simple** (*I
  **arrived** two years ago*), because it lands on a finished moment.
- **for/since ↔ present perfect; ago ↔ past simple.** The tense is not a choice —
  the time-word chooses it for you.
- **for can be dropped, since and ago cannot:** *"I've been here (for) two years"*
  is fine; *"since"* and *"ago"* are never optional.

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why do these three words exist?** Because a duration has three different things
you might want to name, and English uses a different word for each. **How long** it
lasted → **for**. **When it started** (and it's still going) → **since**. **How far
back** a finished event sits → **ago**. Portuguese blurs the first two into one word
(*"há"* / *"desde"*... but *"há"* covers both *for* **and** *ago*), and that overlap
is exactly where Brazilian speakers slip.

**Decision rule #1 — for vs since.** Ask what follows the word: **a length or a
starting point?**
- A **length** (how much time) → **for**: *for two years, for six months, for a
  while*.
- A **start point** (a moment on the calendar) → **since**: *since 2024, since
  March, since the board approved it*.

| | **for** (a length) | **since** (a start point) |
|---|---|---|
| Follows with | *two years, ten minutes, ages* | *2024, Monday, I joined* |
| Meaning | the **size** of the gap | **where** the gap began |
| Test | *"…**for** how long?"* → a number of units | *"…**since** when?"* → a date/event |

Minimal pair — same total meaning, different word because the object differs:
- *"We've worked with them **for** two years."* → length. ✅
- *"We've worked with them **since** 2024."* → start point. ✅
- ❌ *"We've worked with them **since** two years."* → *two years is a length, not a
  start; must be **for**.*

**Decision rule #2 — for vs ago (the tense trap).** These look similar but pull
**opposite** tenses, because they measure from opposite ends.
- **for** = a stretch that **reaches now** → **present perfect**: *"I've lived here
  **for** three years"* (and I still do).
- **ago** = a point **back from now**, finished → **past simple**: *"I moved here
  three years **ago**"* (a single completed event).

| | *"…**for** three years"* | *"…three years **ago**"* |
|---|---|---|
| Points to | a span up to **now** | a finished moment in the **past** |
| Tense | present perfect (*I've worked*) | past simple (*I started*) |
| True now? | yes, still going | no, it's over |

Minimal pair:
- *"We signed the vendor two years **ago**."* → the signing is done and dated. ✅
  (past simple)
- *"We've worked with the vendor **for** two years."* → the relationship still runs.
  ✅ (present perfect)
- ❌ *"We've signed them two years ago."* → *ago forces the past simple: **we
  signed** them two years ago.*

**PT→EN mapping.** The Portuguese *"há"* is the great trap, because it does the work
of **both** *for* and *ago*:
- *"Trabalho com eles **há** dois anos"* (still true, duration up to now) → ✅ *"I've
  worked with them **for** two years."* — here *há* = **for** + present perfect.
- *"Assinei com eles **há** dois anos"* (finished event) → ✅ *"I signed with them
  two years **ago**."* — here the *same* *há* = **ago** + past simple.
- *"desde"* is the clean one: it maps almost perfectly to **since** — *"desde 2024"*
  → *"since 2024"*, *"desde que o board aprovou"* → *"since the board approved it"*.

So one Portuguese word (*há*) splits into two English words depending on whether the
situation **continues** (*for*) or is **over** (*ago*).

**Top Brazilian-speaker errors:**
- ❌ *We've worked with them **since** two years.* → ✅ *We've worked with them
  **for** two years.* — *"two years" is a length, so it takes **for**; **since**
  needs a start point (since 2024).*
- ❌ *I've joined the board three years **ago**.* → ✅ *I **joined** the board three
  years **ago**.* — *ago always pairs with the **past simple**, never the present
  perfect; the event is finished and dated.*
- ❌ *I work here **for** two years.* → ✅ *I've **worked** here **for** two years.* —
  *a "for + duration" reaching now forces the present perfect (carried over from
  Unit 018); the present simple can't hold a span up to the present.*

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. We've led the innovation portfolio **for** a year, and it's still growing. — *for
   + a length (a year) reaching now → present perfect; "since a year" would be wrong
   because a year is a duration, not a start point.*
2. I've owned this mandate **since** last July, when the board split the function
   out. — *since + a start point (last July) → present perfect; it names where the
   clock began, not how long.*
3. We hired the first AI lead three months **ago**. — *ago + past simple: a
   finished, dated event; never "have hired … three months ago".*
4. The board approved the pilot budget six weeks **ago**, so we're already halfway
   through. — *ago points back to the finished approval → "approved" (past simple);
   the consequence lives in the present, but the approval itself is done.*
5. We've worked with this vendor **since** 2024 — **for** about two years now. —
   *the same relationship, two ways: **since** + start point (2024) and **for** +
   length (two years); both take the present perfect.*
6. I haven't presented to this CFO **since** March. — *since + a start point marking
   the last time it happened; the gap runs to now → present perfect (negative).*
7. We launched the first AI product two years **ago**, and adoption has climbed
   **since**. — *"two years ago" dates the finished launch (past simple);
   stand-alone "since" then means "since then / from that point up to now".*
8. We moved to São Paulo six years **ago**, and we've lived here **since**. —
   *everyday life; the move is a finished past point (ago + moved), the living is an
   ongoing span (since + present perfect).*

### 8. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

- Ask your tutor to run a **"timeline interview"** about your real career:
  *"Since when have you led innovation?", "For how long have you worked with that
  vendor?", "When did you hire your first AI lead?"* — and force yourself to answer
  with the matching word: **since** + start point, **for** + length, and **ago** +
  past simple for the dated events. Have them deliberately bait you with a
  Portuguese-style *há* prompt (*"So you signed them for two years, back in 2024?"*)
  so you practise splitting it correctly into *"We signed them two years **ago**"*
  (finished) vs *"We've worked with them **for** two years"* (ongoing).
- Then a quick **"same fact, three ways" drill**: take one true event (e.g. *"I
  joined the company in 2019"*) and say it with all three words — *"I joined seven
  years **ago**," "I've been here **since** 2019," "I've been here **for** seven
  years"* — watching the tense flip from past simple (*ago*) to present perfect
  (*for/since*). This locks in that *ago* and *for* are never interchangeable.

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**A regra e quando usar.** São três palavras, três funções. **for** responde
*"por quanto tempo?"* e vem antes de uma **duração** (*for two years, for six
months*) — vai com o **present perfect** (*"I've worked here **for** two years"*).
**since** responde *"desde quando?"* e vem antes de um **ponto de partida**
(*since 2024, since March, since the board approved it*) — também **present
perfect**, marcando onde o relógio começou e correndo até agora. **ago** conta
**para trás a partir de hoje** até um momento **terminado**, vem **depois** do
período (*three months **ago**, two years **ago***) e anda **sempre** com o **past
simple** (*"I hired her three months **ago**"*). O tempo verbal não é escolha: a
palavra de tempo já decide por você.

**As armadilhas.** (1) *for* × *since*: se depois vem uma **duração**, use **for**;
se vem um **ponto no calendário**, use **since** — *"**since** two years"* está
errado (*"for two years"*). (2) A grande interferência do português: *"há"* faz o
trabalho de **for** *e* de **ago**. Se a situação **continua**, é **for** +
present perfect (*"trabalho com eles há dois anos"* → *"I've worked with them
**for** two years"*); se está **terminada**, é **ago** + past simple (*"assinei com
eles há dois anos"* → *"I signed with them two years **ago**"*). (3) *ago* nunca vai
com present perfect: é *"I **joined** three years ago"*, jamais *"I've joined … three
years ago"*. Se o contraste de tempos ainda embolar, releia
`lessons/2026-07-30-unit-018.md` (how long have you…?) e
`lessons/2026-07-24-unit-012.md` (past simple: o rival do evento terminado).
