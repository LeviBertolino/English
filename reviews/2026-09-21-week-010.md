# 🔁 Review Week 010 — Units 65 to 70

*The "how much, how many, which slice of the group" week.* Six units, one big theme:
**quantifiers** — the words that tell your listener how much of something there is, whether
you're counting it or measuring it, and which part of a group you mean. This is where English
splits hairs that Portuguese lumps together: *muito* becomes *much* or *many*; *nenhum* forces a
positive verb; *todo* fans out into *all*, *every*, and *whole*. Every unit this week was a
**choose-between-rivals** decision, and that's exactly how we'll consolidate it — by contrast,
not by re-learning forms.

*(Full seven-day span: Review Week 009 fell on 2026-09-14, so this week runs 65→70, six units.)*

---

## 2. 📋 Recap — one line per unit

- **Unit 065 — Countable & uncountable nouns (some / any):** the first fork of the whole week —
  can you count it (*an idea*, *two options*) or is it a mass (*information*, *feedback*, *research*)?
  Uncountables take **no plural** and a **singular verb**. Use **some** in positives (*some data*),
  **any** in negatives/questions (*any feedback*) — but flip to **some** for an **offer**
  (*"Would you like **some** feedback?"*).
- **Unit 066 — not + any / no / none:** three ways to say "zero," and **only one negative per
  clause**. *not any* (verb already negative: *"there **isn't any** budget"*), **no** + noun
  (*"we have **no** budget"* — the verb stays positive), and **none** standing alone
  (*"— Budget? — **None**."*). *none* = zero things; *no one* = zero people.
- **Unit 067 — much / many / little / few / a lot / plenty:** the amount words, split by
  countability. **many/few** for countables (*many options*, *few allies*), **much/little** for
  masses (*much time*, *little data*); **a lot of / plenty of** work for both. The killer pair:
  **a few** = *some, enough* (positive) vs **few** = *almost none* (negative).
- **Unit 068 — all / most / some / none — *of* or not:** the "which slice" words. **Bare noun**
  for the general group (*most **leaders***), but **+ of the / of + pronoun** to point at a
  **specific** set (*most **of our** leaders*, *none **of them***). *of* is obligatory before
  *the/this/my* or a pronoun.
- **Unit 069 — both / either / neither:** the same "of" rule, but reserved for **exactly two**.
  **both** = the two together (plural verb), **either** = one or the other (both fine), **neither**
  = not one, not the other (already negative → positive verb). Three or more → switch to
  *all/any/none*. Partners: *both…and*, *either…or*, *neither…**nor***.
- **Unit 070 — all / every / whole:** Portuguese *todo* split three ways. **all** + plural/mass
  (the group as a mass), **every** + singular (member by member, "no exception"), **whole** + one
  singular thing (entire). The trap pair: **all day** (one entire day = duration) vs **every day**
  (each day = frequency).

## 3. 🗺️ Confusion map — when each rival wins

This week is really **one decision tree**. Almost every wrong quantifier comes from missing one
of three questions. Ask them in order.

### Question 1 — Can you count it? (the root of the whole week)

| The noun is… | Signal | Words that fit | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **countable** (has a plural) | *one option, two options* | **many, few, a few, both, either, neither, every** | *"**many** options", "**few** allies", "**every** unit"* |
| **uncountable** (a mass, no plural) | *information*, not *~~informations~~* | **much, little, a little** | *"**much** time", "**little** data"* |
| **either** | works both ways | **some, any, no, none, all, most, a lot of, plenty of** | *"**some** data / **some** options"* |

*Sharpest interference trap of the week:* PT pluralises masses — *informações, feedbacks,
conselhos*. English does **not**: *information*, *feedback*, *advice*, *research*, *progress* are
all **singular, no -s, singular verb**.

### Question 2 — Positive, negative, or an offer? (some / any / no / none)

| Situation | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| positive statement | **some** | *"We have **some** runway."* |
| negative / question | **any** | *"We don't have **any** runway." / "Is there **any**?"* |
| **offer or request** (looks like a question) | **some** (flips back!) | *"Would you like **some** feedback?"* |
| zero, kill the noun's article | **no** + noun (verb stays **positive**) | *"We have **no** budget."* |
| zero, standing alone (no noun after) | **none** | *"— Budget left? — **None**."* |
| zero **people** | **no one / nobody** | *"**No one** approved it."* |

**One-negative rule:** English allows **one** negative per clause. *not…any* **or** *no* **or**
*none* — never two. *"We don't have **no** budget"* ❌ → *"We have **no** budget"* / *"We don't
have **any** budget"* ✅.

### Question 3 — How big is the group, and which slice? (much/many → all/most/none → both/either/neither → all/every/whole)

| You mean… | Use | Test / signal |
|---|---|---|
| a large amount (mass) | **much** (neg/quest) / **a lot of** (pos) | *"**a lot of** feedback"*, not *"much feedback"* in positives |
| a large number (count) | **many / a lot of** | *"**many** options"* |
| a small amount — *and it's fine* | **a little / a few** | you have **enough** (positive spin) |
| a small amount — *and it's a problem* | **little / few** | almost **none** (negative spin) |
| a slice of a **general** group | bare noun: **all / most / some / no** + noun | *"**most** leaders"* (leaders in general) |
| a slice of a **specific** set | **+ of the / of them**: *all of the…, most of our…, none of them* | *"**most of our** leaders"* (ours specifically) |
| **exactly two** things | **both / either / neither** | two finalists, plan A vs plan B |
| **three or more** | **all / any / none** (never *neither*) | *"**none** of the five vendors"* |
| the group as **one mass** | **all** + plural/uncountable | *"**all** the pilots are on hold"* |
| the group **member by member** ("no exception") | **every** + singular | *"**every** pilot **is** on hold"* |
| **one** thing, entire | **whole** + singular | *"the **whole** quarter"* |
| **duration** vs **frequency** | **all day** (one full day) vs **every day** (each day) | *"in reviews **all day**"* ≠ *"in reviews **every day**"* |

**The three "already-negative" words that forbid a second *not*:** *no*, *none*, *neither*. Each
carries the negative by itself, so the verb stays **positive** — the single biggest PT-interference
trap of the week (*"neither vendor **is** cheap"*, never *"neither vendor **isn't** cheap"*).

## 4. 🧪 10 recognition exercises (mostly contrast — ~6 minutes)

Answers and one-line explanations at the end (section 7). Retrieve first.

1. **(countable vs uncountable — the plural trap)** Fix if wrong: *"The board asked for more
   **informations** and two **feedbacks** on the plan."* — what's ungrammatical, and why?
2. **(some vs any — the offer flip)** Fill both: *"We don't have ___ hard numbers yet — but would
   you like ___ early signals before the board call?"* — which word in each slot, and what makes
   the second one break the negative rule?
3. **(the one-negative rule)** Fix: *"We don't have **no** runway for a tenth pilot."* — give the
   **two** clean versions and name the rule.
4. **(no vs none)** Fill both: *"We have ___ budget for new hires this quarter. — And contractors?
   — ___ ."* — which word sits before *budget*, which stands alone, and what decides it?
5. **(much vs many vs a lot of)** Fix: *"We collected **much** feedback and evaluated **much**
   vendors this quarter."* — correct **both**, and state the two different reasons.
6. **(a few vs few — the spin flip)** You tell the CEO: *"___ board members back the AI budget."*
   One version reassures (*you have allies*), the other alarms (*almost nobody*). Give both and say
   which is which.
7. **(of or nothing — general vs specific)** Fill both: *"( Most / Most of ) companies overspend on
   AI, but ( most / most of ) our pilots came in on budget."* — which slot takes *of*, and why?
8. **(neither vs none — the two-vs-many boundary)** You shortlisted **four** platforms and rejected
   all four. Is it *"Neither platform passed"* or *"None of the platforms passed"*? — and why is the
   other wrong?
9. **(neither … nor + positive verb)** Fix the **two** errors: *"Neither the price **or** the tech
   **doesn't** justify the switch."* — correct both and name each rule.
10. **(all day vs every day — duration vs frequency)** Fill both, then say what changes: *"I was
    stuck in strategy reviews ___ yesterday, and honestly I'm in one ___ this month."*

## 5. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula (3 themes worth practising)

1. **The "already-negative" words (no / none / neither) and the positive verb.** This is the single
   deepest PT trap of the week, because Portuguese *demands* the double negative
   (*"**nenhum** vendor **não** é barato"*). Ask your tutor to fire rapid prompts —
   *"How many of the five vendors passed?"*, *"What's left in the budget?"* — so you answer with
   *"**None** of them **passed**"* / *"**Neither** option **works**"* and the positive verb becomes
   automatic under pressure.
2. **much / many / a lot in real answers, plus the *a few / few* spin.** Have the tutor role-play a
   CFO grilling you (*"How much runway? How many allies on the board?"*) so you switch cleanly
   between *much* (mass) and *many* (count), default to *a lot of* in positives, and consciously
   pick **a few** (reassuring) vs **few** (alarming) depending on the message you want to send. The
   spin is a leadership tool, not just grammar.
3. **all vs every vs whole — the *todo* fan-out.** Practise describing your quarter and your routine:
   *"I reviewed **every** business unit"* (member by member, thorough), *"the **whole** roadmap
   slipped"* (one thing, entire), *"I was in reviews **all day**"* vs *"I'm in them **every day**"*
   (duration vs frequency). This is the contrast most likely to make you sound either precise or
   vague to a board.

## 6. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

Esta semana foi sobre **quantificadores** — as palavras que dizem *quanto*, *quantos* e *qual parte
do grupo*. Tudo começa numa pergunta: **dá pra contar?** Se dá (*options, vendors*), você usa
*many / few / a few / both / either / neither / every*; se é massa (*information, data, feedback*),
usa *much / little / a little* — e a massa **não tem plural** em inglês (*informations* e
*feedbacks* não existem). Depois vem o eixo **some / any**: *some* no positivo, *any* no
negativo/pergunta, mas *some* de novo quando é **oferta** (*"Would you like **some** feedback?"*). E
para "qual fatia": substantivo puro para o grupo **geral** (*most leaders*) vs *of the / of them*
para o grupo **específico** (*most of our leaders*); *both/either/neither* só para **dois**, e três
ou mais exigem *all/any/none*. Por fim, o *todo* português se abre em três: *all* (a massa), *every*
(um por um, sem exceção) e *whole* (uma coisa inteira).

O que provavelmente ainda precisa de trabalho: **(1)** a **negação dupla** — *no*, *none* e
*neither* já são negativos, então o verbo fica **positivo** (*"neither vendor **is** cheap"*, nunca
*"isn't"*); é o inverso do português. **(2)** *much* vs *many* vs *a lot of* — em frases
**afirmativas** prefira *a lot of*, não *much* (*"a lot of feedback"*, não *"much feedback"*). **(3)**
*all day* (duração — um dia inteiro) vs *every day* (frequência — todo dia), o mesmo *todo* virando
estruturas opostas. Se for reler dois arquivos, comece por **Unit 066** (no / none / not any — a
negação) e **Unit 070** (all / every / whole — o *todo*): são os dois contrastes que mais aparecem
numa conversa real com o board.

## 7. ✅ Gabarito (answers + one-line explanations)

1. Both are wrong: **information** and **feedback** are **uncountable** → no plural *-s* and a
   singular verb. ✅ *"…more **information** and some **feedback**."* PT pluralises them; English
   never does.
2. *"We don't have **any** hard numbers yet — but would you like **some** early signals?"* — *any*
   in the negative; *some* in the second slot because it's an **offer**, which flips the normal
   question rule back to *some*.
3. *"We **don't** have **any** runway"* **or** *"We have **no** runway."* — the **one-negative
   rule**: *don't* + *no* is two negatives; keep exactly one (*not…any* **or** *no*).
4. *"We have **no** budget… — **None**."* — *no* sits **before a noun** (*no budget*, verb stays
   positive); *none* **stands alone** with no noun after it. The presence/absence of a following
   noun decides.
5. *"We collected **a lot of** feedback and evaluated **many** vendors."* — *feedback* is
   uncountable **and** the clause is positive, so *much* → **a lot of**; *vendors* is countable, so
   *much* → **many**.
6. *"**A few** board members back the budget"* = you have **some allies** (reassuring). *"**Few**
   board members back it"* = **almost nobody** does (alarming). The *a* is the whole difference.
7. *"**Most** companies overspend… but **most of** our pilots came in on budget."* — bare *most* for
   the **general** group (companies at large); *most of* before *our* (a specific, named set). *of*
   is obligatory before *the/our/my* or a pronoun.
8. *"**None of the platforms** passed."* — you had **four**, and *neither* is **only for two**. Three
   or more always takes *none / any*.
9. *"Neither the price **nor** the tech **justifies** the switch."* — (a) *neither*'s partner is
   **nor**, not *or*; (b) *neither* is already negative, so drop *doesn't* — the verb goes
   **positive** (and singular: *justifies*).
10. *"…stuck in reviews **all day** yesterday, and I'm in one **every day** this month."* — *all
    day* = one **entire** day (duration); *every day* = **each** day, repeatedly (frequency). Same
    PT *todo*, opposite structures.
