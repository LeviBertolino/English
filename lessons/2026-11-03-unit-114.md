### 1. 📘 Unit 114 — *still · yet · already · (not) any more / no longer*

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** Where are we on the AI budget — has the board signed off?
> **Levi:** Not **yet**. We're **still** waiting on the CFO's numbers.
> **CEO:** I thought those were done.
> **Levi:** The forecast is — we've **already** shared it. But finance **still** hasn't come back with the final figure.
> **CFO:** And the old vendor? Are we keeping them?
> **Levi:** No. We **no longer** use them for infra — we moved everything to the new platform last quarter.
> **CEO:** Good. So nothing's blocked except the sign-off?
> **Levi:** Right. Everything else is **already** in motion; we just don't have the board's "yes" **yet**.

Four little words, four different points on a timeline. **still** = *it's continuing, nothing has changed yet* (we're **still** waiting). **yet** = *it hasn't happened — but we expect it to* (not **yet**; we don't have the "yes" **yet**). **already** = *it has happened — sooner than you might think* (we've **already** shared it). **no longer** = *it used to be true, and it stopped* (we **no longer** use them). And notice the positions: **still** sits in the **middle** (we're **still** waiting · finance **still** hasn't come back), while **yet** goes at the **end** (not **yet** · the board's "yes" **yet**).

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

Each word pins a situation to a moment on the line **before now → now → after now**:

| Word | Meaning | Typical sentence type | Position |
|---|---|---|---|
| **still** | the situation **continues**, no change | affirmative, negative, question | **middle** (after *be*/auxiliary, before the main verb) |
| **yet** | *up to now* — expected, hasn't happened | **negatives & questions** | **end** of the clause |
| **already** | it happened — often **sooner than expected** | **affirmative** (and surprised questions) | **middle** or end |
| **not … any more / any longer**, **no longer** | a past situation **has stopped** | negative (*no longer* can be affirmative in form) | *any more / any longer* → **end**; *no longer* → **middle** |

**Position, concretely:**
- **still** and **already** go in the **mid** slot — *after* **am/is/are/was/were** and *after* an auxiliary (*have*, *will*, *can*), but *before* a one-word main verb: *we're **still** negotiating · we've **already** decided · they **still** want it · she **already** knows.*
- **yet** goes at the **very end**: *we haven't decided **yet** · have they replied **yet**?* — never *"we yet haven't…"*.
- **any more / any longer** go at the **end**; **no longer** goes in the **mid** slot and is one notch more formal: *we don't invest there **any more*** = *we **no longer** invest there.*

**The tense they travel with:** *already* and *yet* ride most often with the **present perfect** (*have you signed **yet**? · we've **already** signed*); *still* rides with whatever tense shows the situation running (*we're **still** reviewing · we **still** use it*).

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why do these words exist?** Comparatives rank things; *enough / too* measure against a standard. This set does something only it can do — it places an event **on a timeline relative to now and to what you expected**. Is the thing *still going*? *Not happened but due*? *Done ahead of time*? *Over*? For a leader this is the entire grammar of a **status update**: *we've **already** shortlisted · we're **still** waiting on legal · the board hasn't approved it **yet** · we **no longer** work with that vendor.* Pick the wrong one and you misreport where a decision actually stands.

**Decision rule #1 — `still` vs `yet` (both are *ainda* — this is THE split):**

| | **still** | **yet** |
|---|---|---|
| says | the situation is **continuing** | it **hasn't happened**, but is expected |
| sentence | mostly affirmative; mid-position | negatives & questions; end-position |
| example | *"We're **still** reviewing the deal."* | *"We haven't reviewed the deal **yet**."* |

Minimal pair — same news, different angle:
- *"We're **still** deciding."* → focus on the ongoing process (it's live, in motion).
- *"We haven't decided **yet**."* → focus on the missing result (it's due, not done).

And the overlap that trips everyone — **`still` + negative** vs **`not … yet`**:
- *"They **still** haven't sent the terms."* → carries **impatience / surprise**: *this is dragging on longer than it should.*
- *"They haven't sent the terms **yet**."* → **neutral**: just reporting it's not done, as expected.
Same facts; *still* adds a frown, *yet* stays flat. Choose on tone.

**Decision rule #2 — `already` vs `yet` (both are *já*):**

| | **already** | **yet** |
|---|---|---|
| says | it **has happened** (maybe early) | *has it happened?* / it **hasn't** |
| sentence | affirmative | question or negative |
| example | *"We've **already** approved it."* | *"Have you approved it **yet**?"* |

Portuguese uses *já* for **both** — *"já aprovamos"* (affirmative) and *"vocês já aprovaram?"* (question). English splits them: affirmative → **already**, neutral question/negative → **yet**. Use *already* in a **question** only when you mean **surprise it happened so fast**: *"Have you finished **already**?"* (*wow, that was quick*) ≠ *"Have you finished **yet**?"* (*I'm waiting — is it done?*).

**Decision rule #3 — `still` vs `not … any more` (continuing vs stopped):**
- *"We **still** use that vendor."* → it continues.
- *"We don't use that vendor **any more**."* / *"We **no longer** use that vendor."* → it stopped.
These are opposites on the same scale — *still* = ongoing, *not any more / no longer* = ended.

**PT→EN mapping (where translation betrays you):**
- *ainda* (continuando) → **still**: *"ainda estamos esperando"* → *"we're **still** waiting."*
- *ainda não* → **not … yet** (neutral) / **still haven't** (impatient): *"ainda não aprovaram"* → *"they haven't approved it **yet**"* or (frustrated) *"they **still** haven't approved it."*
- *já* (afirmativo) → **already**: *"já decidimos"* → *"we've **already** decided."*
- *já …?* (pergunta neutra) → **yet**, not *already*: *"vocês já decidiram?"* → *"have you decided **yet**?"*
- *não … mais* → **not … any more / any longer** or **no longer**: *"não trabalhamos mais com eles"* → *"we don't work with them **any more** / we **no longer** work with them."* **Never** a bare *"not … more."*

**Top 3 typical Brazilian-speaker errors:**
1. ❌ *"Have you sent the report **already**?"* / *"Did you approve it **already**?"* (asking neutrally) → ✅ *"Have you sent the report **yet**?"* The Portuguese *já* pushes you to *already*, but a **neutral** question wants **yet**; *already* in a question means *"so soon?!"*.
2. ❌ *"We don't partner with them **more**."* / *"We work with them **no more**."* → ✅ *"We don't partner with them **any more**"* or *"We **no longer** partner with them."* *não … mais* is never a bare *more* — it's *(not) any more* / *no longer*.
3. ❌ *"We **yet** haven't finalised the roadmap."* / *"**Yet** we didn't finish."* → ✅ *"We haven't finalised the roadmap **yet**."* *yet* lives at the **end** of the clause, not in front of the verb. (And *still* is the opposite — *mid*: *"we **still** haven't finalised it."*)

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. **The board hasn't approved the AI budget yet.**
   *— `yet` = expected but not done, and it sits at the **end**. Neutral status report. Swap to "still hasn't approved" only if you want to signal the delay is dragging.*
2. **We've already shortlisted three vendors.**
   *— `already` = done, sooner than one might expect; mid-position after the auxiliary `have`. Affirmative, so `already`, never `yet`.*
3. **We're still waiting on the CFO's sign-off.**
   *— `still` = the waiting **continues**; mid-position after `are`. Focus on the ongoing situation, not the missing result (that would be "we don't have it yet").*
4. **That vendor no longer supports the on-prem version.**
   *— `no longer` = it used to, and stopped; mid-position, slightly formal. Equivalent to "doesn't support it any more" (which would go at the end).*
5. **Have you reviewed the innovation portfolio yet?**
   *— neutral question → `yet` at the end. "…reviewed it already?" would instead mean "so fast?!" — a different question.*
6. **They still haven't come back with the final figure.**
   *— `still` + negative = **impatience**: this should have arrived by now. Contrast #1's flat "haven't … yet." Same fact, sharper tone.*
7. **I've already had lunch, thanks — but are you still hungry?** *(everyday)*
   *— `already` = done ahead of the offer (affirmative, mid); `still` = the hunger continues (mid, after `are`). The two words side by side, each doing its own job.*
8. **We don't go to that gym any more — we moved, so it's too far.** *(everyday)*
   *— `not … any more` = a habit that **stopped**, at the end of the clause. The opposite of "we still go there." Never "we don't go there more."*

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 112 — *enough* and *too*):**
1. **very** — *"Our Q3 results were **very** strong."* *very* states a high degree neutrally, which fits a compliment; *too strong* would mean *excessively* strong (a problem), contradicting "the board was thrilled."
2. **scalable enough** — *"The platform isn't **scalable enough** to handle enterprise load."* *enough* goes **after** an adjective (but **before** a noun: *enough capacity*).
3. **too many risks** (countable plural → *many*) and **too much technical debt** (uncountable → *much*). The countability of the noun decides.

**Spaced review:**
- **Unit 108 (quite/rather/fairly):** *"It's **quite a** risky bet."* — *quite* jumps **before** the article *a*, never *"a quite risky bet."* *rather* follows the same rule (*rather a risky bet*); *fairly* and *pretty* do the opposite (*a fairly risky bet*).
- **Unit 084 (comparatives):** *"Building is **cheaper** and **faster** than licensing."* Both are **one-syllable** words → they take **-er**, never *more*. The Portuguese *mais* ( *mais barato / mais rápido*) is what pushes you to the wrong *"more cheap / more fast."*

**Recognition drill:**
1. **still**, in the **middle**: *"Legal **still** hasn't returned the signed contract."* *still* + negative signals **impatience** — this is overdue. Moving to the end with *yet* — *"Legal hasn't returned it **yet**"* — makes it **neutral**, just not-done-as-expected.
2. **yet** — *"Have you signed off on the forecast **yet**?"* A neutral check wants *yet*. *"…already?"* would instead mean *"you've signed off so fast?!"* — surprise, not a plain status question.
3. **not … any more / no longer:** *"We don't use that framework **any more**"* **or** *"We **no longer** use that framework."* *não … mais* is never a bare *more*.
4. **yet → end:** *"We haven't scheduled the board review **yet**."* *yet* belongs at the end. If a word were to stay in the **middle**, it would be *still*: *"We **still** haven't scheduled the board review"* (with an impatient tone).

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**A regra:** estas quatro palavrinhas marcam **onde** uma situação está na linha do tempo em relação a *agora* e ao que você esperava. **still** = *ainda*, a situação **continua** (vem no **meio**: *we're **still** waiting*). **yet** = *ainda (não)* — algo **esperado que não aconteceu**, usado em negativas e perguntas, no **fim** da frase (*not approved **yet** · have you signed **yet**?*). **already** = *já*, algo que **aconteceu** (muitas vezes antes do previsto), em afirmativas, no **meio** (*we've **already** decided*). E **not … any more / any longer** ou **no longer** = *não … mais*, algo que **deixou de ser verdade** (*we **no longer** use them*).

**As armadilhas (interferência do português):** (1) *ainda* é **still** quando continua, mas *ainda não* vira **not … yet** (neutro) — cuidado com a posição: *yet* vai no **fim**, nunca *"we yet haven't…"*. (2) *já* traduz **already** E **yet**: em afirmativa use *already* (*já decidimos → we've **already** decided*), mas em pergunta neutra use *yet* (*já decidiram? → have you decided **yet**?*); *already* numa pergunta quer dizer *"tão rápido assim?!"*. (3) *não … mais* nunca é um *more* solto — é *(not) any more* ou *no longer* (❌ *"we don't use it more"* → ✅ *"we don't use it **any more**"*). Em caso de dúvida entre *still* e *yet*, pergunte: a coisa **continua** (→ still, no meio) ou **ainda não aconteceu** (→ not … yet, no fim)?
