### 1. 📘 Unit 016 — I've just… / I've already… / I haven't… yet (present perfect 2)

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** Are we ready for the board on the AI investment?
> **You:** Almost. I've **just** finished the final version of the deck, and the
> CFO has **already** signed off on the numbers. We **haven't** locked the risk
> section **yet** — legal is still reviewing it.
> **CEO:** Has the vendor confirmed the timeline?
> **You:** Not **yet**. I've **already** chased them twice this morning, so I
> expect an answer before lunch.

Notice what these forms are doing *before* any rule. Every action here happened
in the **past**, but nobody says *when* — and that's the point. *I've just
finished*, *has already signed off*, *haven't locked… yet* all report a past
action **for its effect right now**: the deck is ready now, the numbers are
approved now, the risk section is still open now. The present perfect is the
past **seen from the present**.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

**First, the base form (present perfect):** **have / has** + **past participle**.

| Subject | Auxiliary | Past participle |
|---|---|---|
| I / you / we / they | **have** (**'ve**) | *finished, sent, done, seen* |
| he / she / it | **has** (**'s**) | *finished, sent, done, seen* |

The past participle is the "third form": regulars add **-ed** (*finish →
finished*); the frequent business verbs are irregular (*send → sent, do → done,
give → given, see → seen, make → made*).

**Now the three words this unit is about** — they sharpen *when* the action sits
relative to now:

| Word | Meaning | Position | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **just** | a **short time ago**, moments back (PT *acabei de*) | between *have/has* and the participle | *I've **just** sent it.* |
| **already** | **sooner than expected**, before now (PT *já*, afirmativo) | between *have/has* and the participle | *She's **already** approved it.* |
| **yet** | **up to now** — for something **still expected** (PT *já?* / *ainda não*) | at the **end** of the sentence | *We haven't started **yet**. / Have you started **yet**?* |

**Key facts:**
- **just** and **already** live **inside** the verb (after *have/has*); **yet**
  lives at the **end**.
- **already** appears in **positive** sentences; **yet** appears in **negatives**
  and **questions**. They split the Portuguese *já* between them.
- *yet* in a **negative** = *ainda não* (*not yet*); *yet* in a **question** =
  *já?* (*have you… yet?*).

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why does this structure exist?** Because sometimes what matters about a past
action is **its result now**, not the moment it happened. *I've just sent the
deck* isn't really about the sending — it's about the deck being **ready in your
inbox now**. The present perfect is the bridge that carries a past action into
the present. *just / already / yet* fine-tune where on that bridge the action
sits: **just** = it just landed; **already** = it's done, earlier than you'd
think; **yet** = it hasn't landed, but we're still expecting it.

**Decision rule — present perfect vs the rival, past simple.** This is the whole
game. Ask one question: **is there a finished past time in view?**

| | Present perfect (*have/has + participle*) | Past simple (*-ed / irregular*) |
|---|---|---|
| Time | **no** specific time — connected to **now** | a **finished** time (*yesterday, at 9, last week*) |
| Focus | the **result / news** right now | the **event** in its past moment |
| Signals | *just, already, yet, so far, up to now* | *yesterday, ago, in 2023, then, last quarter* |

Minimal pairs — same action, different frame:
- *I've **just** approved the budget.* → it's approved **now**; that's the news.
- *I **approved** the budget **yesterday**.* → a finished event at a stated time.

- *Have you sent the report **yet**?* → is it done **as of now**?
- *Did you send the report **this morning**?* → asking about a finished slot of
  time.

The rule that ends most errors: **the moment you name a finished time
(*yesterday, last week, at 3 p.m.*), you must switch to the past simple.** *just
/ already / yet* and a specific past time **cannot share a sentence**.

**PT→EN mapping.** The trap is the Portuguese **"já"**, which fuses two English
words:
- *"Eu já enviei"* (afirmativo) → *"I've **already** sent it."* ✅
- *"Você já enviou?"* (pergunta) → *"Have you sent it **yet**?"* ✅ — **not**
  *"already"* by default.
- *"Ainda não enviei"* → *"I haven't sent it **yet**."* ✅ (*yet* = *ainda não*).
- And *"acabei de enviar"* → *"I've **just** sent it,"* not *"I just sent"* in
  careful/British usage (Murphy). *Acabei de* is never *"I finished to send."*

**A nuance worth owning:** *already* **can** appear in a question — but it
changes the meaning to **surprise**: *"Have you **already** finished?"* = "So
soon?!" The **neutral** "is it done by now?" question uses **yet**. Brazilians
overuse *already* in questions because *já?* feels neutral in Portuguese; in
English, reach for *yet* unless you really mean "already?!".

**Top Brazilian-speaker errors:**
- ❌ *I've just finished the deck **yesterday**.* → ✅ *I **finished** the deck
  yesterday.* / ✅ *I've **just** finished the deck.* — *a named past time
  (yesterday) forces the past simple; drop "just/yesterday" pairing entirely.*
- ❌ *I haven't finished it **already**.* → ✅ *I haven't finished it **yet**.* —
  *negatives take "yet" (= ainda não), never "already"; "already" is for
  positives.*
- ❌ *Have you **already** sent it?* (meaning neutral "is it done?") → ✅ *Have you
  sent it **yet**?* — *"yet" is the neutral question word; keep "already?" for
  genuine surprise.*

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. I've **just** finished the final version of the board deck. — *the news is
   that it's ready **now**; "just" = moments ago, no stated time → present
   perfect, not "I finished… now".*
2. The CFO has **already** signed off on the AI budget numbers. — *"already" =
   sooner than expected, sitting between "has" and "signed"; the approval stands
   **now**.*
3. We **haven't** locked the risk section **yet**. — *negative + "yet" (= ainda
   não) at the end; it's still expected, still open right now.*
4. **Has** the vendor confirmed the timeline **yet**? — *neutral question "is it
   done by now?" → "yet" at the end, not "already".*
5. I've **already** chased them twice this morning. — *"this morning" is still
   *now* (unfinished period), so present perfect holds; "already" stresses it's
   more than expected.*
6. The board **hasn't** approved the roadmap **yet**, so we can't announce it. —
   *"yet" flags a pending, expected decision; the present result (can't announce)
   is what matters.*
7. Have you had lunch **yet**? — *everyday life; neutral "as of now?" question →
   "yet"; "Have you already had lunch?" would mean "so soon?".*
8. I've **just** got home, give me five minutes. — *off-work; "just" = seconds
   ago, reported for its present effect (I'm not ready), no clock time named.*

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Review Week 002)**
1. **were discussing** — *the discussion was **already under way** when he
   arrived → past continuous (background). "we discussed" would mean he arrived
   **first** and **then** you began — two events in sequence.*
2. ❌ *"Did the board approved…"* → ✅ **"Did the board approve the AI budget?"** —
   *the past sits on the helper "did", so the main verb snaps back to base
   "approve"; never mark the past twice.*
3. **There are** — ✅ **"There are three open risks on the roadmap right now."** —
   *this is **existence** (PT "tem" = there is/are), not possession; "have" would
   need an owner as its subject.*

**Spaced review**
- **(Unit 009)** ❌ *"Do you have got…?"* → ✅ **"Do you have the final numbers?"**
  *or* **"Have you got the final numbers?"** — *two separate systems: plain "have"
  takes do-support; "have got" inverts like "to be". Never fuse them.*

**Recognition drill**
1. ❌ *"I've just approved… yesterday."* → ✅ **"I approved the AI budget
   yesterday."** *or* **"I've just approved the AI budget."** — *a named finished
   time (yesterday) forces the past simple; "just" + a specific past time can't
   coexist.*
2. **already** — ✅ **"We've already presented the vision to the board."** — *a
   positive, done-sooner-than-expected statement takes "already" between "have"
   and the participle; "yet" is only for negatives/questions.*
3. ✅ **"Have you sent the risk memo yet?"** — *neutral "is it done as of now?" →
   present perfect + "yet" at the end. "Did you send the risk memo this morning?"
   asks about a **finished time slot** instead, focusing on that past moment, not
   the present result.*
4. ❌ *"…signed the numbers already off?"* → ✅ **"Has the CFO already signed off
   on the numbers?"** — *"already" goes right after "has", between auxiliary and
   participle — not floating after the object.*

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**A regra e quando usar.** O *present perfect* (**have/has + particípio**) conta
uma ação **passada olhada do presente** — o que importa é o **resultado agora**,
não o momento em que aconteceu. Três palavras afinam esse "agora": **just** =
*acabei de*, há segundos (*"I've **just** finished"*); **already** = *já*, antes
do esperado, em frases **afirmativas** (*"She's **already** approved it"*); e
**yet** = *já?* (pergunta) ou *ainda não* (negativa), sempre **no fim** da frase
(*"Have you started **yet**?" / "We haven't started **yet**"*). Posição é meio da
prova: *just* e *already* ficam **dentro** do verbo (depois de *have/has*); *yet*
vai **no fim**.

**As armadilhas.** (1) A regra de ouro: assim que você **nomeia um tempo
terminado** (*yesterday, last week, às 15h*), troque para o **past simple** —
*just/already/yet* **não convivem** com uma hora passada específica (*"I finished
it yesterday"*, nunca *"I've just finished it yesterday"*). (2) O *já* português
se **divide** em inglês: afirmativo → **already**, pergunta → **yet**, e *ainda
não* → **not… yet**; não use *already* na negativa. (3) *Already* numa pergunta
existe, mas vira **surpresa** ("já?! tão rápido?") — a pergunta neutra "está
pronto?" é com **yet**. Se o contraste passado-vs-presente ainda embolar, releia
`lessons/2026-07-26-unit-014.md` (past simple, o rival direto desta unidade).
