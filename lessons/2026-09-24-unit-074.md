### 1. 📘 Unit 074 — relative clauses 3: whose / where / whom

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** Which partner are we going with?
> **You:** The vendor **whose** platform we piloted last quarter — the one **whose** security scored
> highest.
> **CEO:** And **where** did the board land on budget?
> **You:** In the offsite **where** we mapped the roadmap, they approved phase one. The exec **whose**
> team owns delivery is Priya, **whom** the board already trusts.
> **CFO:** Remind me — the session **where** we killed the two weak pilots. Was that the same one?
> **You:** The very one. That's the room **where** the hard calls actually get made.

Three new little words do three different jobs. **whose** shows **possession** — it stands in for
*his / her / its / their* (*the vendor **whose** platform* = the vendor, **its** platform). **where**
points to a **place** (*the offsite **where** we mapped it* = the offsite, **in which** we mapped it).
And **whom** is the **formal object** form for a person (*the exec **whom** the board trusts*). Notice
you could never swap them: *whose* carries an idea of belonging, *where* carries an idea of location.
That's the whole unit — matching the right relative word to the job it does.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

Each of these three answers a different question about the noun in front of it.

| Word | Job it does | Replaces | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **whose** | possession (something belongs to the noun) | *his / her / its / their* | *the CEO **whose** vision set the year* |
| **where** | place | *in / at / on which* | *the room **where** we decided* |
| **whom** | object, person, formal | *him / her / them* | *the client **whom** we won back* |

**Key facts:**
- **whose** works for **people and things** alike — *the exec **whose** team* / *the vendor **whose**
  platform*. It is always followed by a **noun** (the thing possessed).
- **where** replaces a place **plus** its preposition, so you never add one: *"the city **where** I
  live,"* never *"the city where I live **in**."*
- **whom** is only for the **object** (someone else does the verb). If a **verb** follows, use **who**
  instead: *the exec **who** leads delivery* (subject), not *whom*. In everyday speech, *whom* is
  usually replaced by *who / that* — or simply **dropped** (Unit 073): *"the client **we** won back."*
  Keep *whom* for formal writing.
- Don't confuse **whose** with **who's**: *who's* = *who is / who has*. Same sound, opposite meaning.

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (a seção central)

**Why do these three exist?** Unit 073 gave you *who / that / which*. But those can't express two
things: **belonging** and **place**. If a strategist has a thesis and you want to link them in one
noun phrase, only *whose* does it cleanly — *"the strategist **whose** thesis reframed the year."*
And if you want to point at a location as the anchor, *where* packs a preposition inside one word —
*"the offsite **where** we decided"* is tighter than *"the offsite **in which** we decided."* *whom*,
finally, is just the polished, written-register version of the object *who/that* from yesterday.

**Decision rule vs the rivals — this is the whole game:**

| Situation | Right word | Wrong rival | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| something **belongs** to the noun | **whose** + noun | *who / that* | only *whose* shows possession |
| the anchor is a **place** | **where** | *which* | *where* carries the preposition; *which* would need one |
| **object**, person, formal | **whom** | *who* | *who* is for the **subject** (a verb follows) |
| **subject**, person (verb follows) | **who** | *whom* | *whom* can't be the subject |

Two quick tests settle it live:
- **whose or who's?** Try expanding to *who is*. If it fits, it's *who's*; if it doesn't, it's
  *whose*. *"The leader **whose** team delivered"* → "who is team delivered"? No → **whose**.
- **where or which?** Ask: is the noun a **real place** you could stand in? *the room, the city, the
  offsite* → **where**. An **abstract** noun (*a decision, a situation, a meeting-as-event, a
  strategy*) is **not** a place → use **which / that / in which**, not *where*.

**PT→EN mapping — where this quietly betrays Levi.**
- **whose = *cujo / cuja*.** Brazilians rarely say *cujo* in speech (it feels bookish), so they avoid
  *whose* and produce clumsy workarounds: *"the vendor **that its** platform we chose"* or *"the
  vendor **that we chose his** platform."* Both are wrong. Train yourself to reach for the one clean
  word: *"the vendor **whose** platform we chose."*
- **where = *onde*.** Here Portuguese is **too generous**: *onde* gets used for abstract things
  (*"uma decisão **onde** erramos," "uma situação **onde**…"*). Copying that gives *"a decision
  **where** we erred,"* which sounds wrong to a native ear — a decision isn't a place. Use *in which
  / that*: *"a decision **in which** we erred"* or, more naturally, *"a decision we got wrong."*

**Top 3 typical Brazilian-speaker errors:**
- ❌ *"The vendor **that its platform** scored highest won the deal."* → ✅ *"The vendor **whose
  platform** scored highest won the deal."* *(possession → one word: whose.)*
- ❌ *"That was a meeting **where** we made no progress."* (abstract event, not a place) → ✅ *"That was
  a meeting **in which** we made no progress"* / more natural *"a meeting **that** got us nowhere."*
- ❌ *"The city **where** I live **in** is expensive."* → ✅ *"The city **where** I live is expensive."*
  *(where already contains "in" — never repeat the preposition.)*

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. *"The vendor **whose** platform we piloted cleared every security gate."*
   *— possession: the platform belongs to the vendor, so "whose" + noun; "that its platform" would be the workaround error.*
2. *"She's the strategist **whose** thesis reframed our whole year."*
   *— whose links a person to what they own (the thesis); no other relative word can do this in one word.*
3. *"That's the offsite **where** we mapped the AI roadmap."*
   *— a real place → where; it swallows "in which," so you never add a preposition at the end.*
4. *"The exec **whom** the board trusts most on AI is Priya."*
   *— object + person + formal register; "the board" does the trusting, so whom (not who). In speech you'd say "the exec the board trusts most."*
5. *"The leader **who** owns delivery presented first."*
   *— contrast with #4: a VERB ("owns") follows, so this is the SUBJECT → who, never whom.*
6. *"It was a review **in which** we cut two pilots."*
   *— a review is an abstract event, not a place, so "in which" (or just "a review where" is tolerated, but "in which" is the safe, correct choice) — resist the "onde" reflex.*
7. *"The colleague **whose** laptop I borrowed left early."*
   *— everyday life: whose works for ordinary possession just as well as for strategy talk.*
8. *"That's the room **where** the hard calls actually get made."*
   *— place as the anchor; "where" is tighter and more natural than "in which the hard calls get made."*

### 8. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

1. Ask your tutor to run a **"whose" drill**: they name a person or vendor and a thing that belongs to
   them (*"the CEO — her vision," "the vendor — its platform," "the team — their KPIs"*), and you must
   fuse them into one relative clause with *whose* — *"the CEO **whose** vision…," "the vendor
   **whose** platform…"* — until the workaround (*"that its…"*) stops surfacing.
2. Role-play a **portfolio walkthrough for the board**: describe your bets using all three words —
   *"the vendor **whose** security won us over, the offsite **where** we set the roadmap, the partner
   **whom** the CFO still questions"* — and have the tutor stop you the moment you say *where* about
   an abstract noun (*"a decision where…"*) so you retrain to *in which / that*.

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 073):**
1. You can drop it in **"the vendor ___ we chose"** — a **new subject** (*we*) follows, so the
   pronoun is the **object** → droppable. In *"the vendor ___ scored highest,"* a **verb** (*scored*)
   follows → the pronoun is the **subject** → it must stay. The clue is the word right after the gap:
   verb → keep, new subject → drop.
2. Delete **it**: *"The roadmap **(that) the board approved** is now live."* Portuguese leaves the
   echo (*"que o board aprovou"* is fine, but the *"que … ele/isso"* habit adds a second object). In
   English the relative pronoun **is** the object, so a second one is a doubling error.
3. **who** (or *that*) — a *candidate* is a **person**, so *which* is impossible; and since *we*
   follows, you could drop it entirely (*"the candidate we hired"*).

**Spaced review:**
- **Unit 067:** *"**a few** pilots"* — two pilots producing signal is a **positive, small** amount
   ("some, and that's good"). *"**Few** pilots"* (no *a*) means **almost none** — a shortage, a
   warning. The single letter *a* flips optimism into alarm.
- **Unit 046:** **investing** → *"we're **committed to investing** for three years."* *to* here is a
   **preposition** (part of *committed to*), and after a preposition English always uses **-ing**.
   The *comprometido a investir* reflex wrongly produces the infinitive *"committed to invest."*

**Recognition drill:**
1. **whose** — *"The CEO **whose** vision set the year."* The test: expand to *who is* → "who is
   vision set the year" is nonsense, so it's the possessive **whose**, not *who's*.
2. **No — not *where*.** A *decision* is an **abstract** noun, not a place you can stand in, so use
   *"a decision **in which** we underestimated the risk"* or, more naturally, *"a decision **where**"*
   is a common slip — the safe, correct form is *in which / that*. This is the *onde* over-reach.
3. **whom / who** → *"the candidate **whom** we hired"* (a new subject *we* follows → **object** →
   whom, or drop it in speech) vs *"the candidate **who** built the model"* (a verb *built* follows →
   **subject** → who). What flips the choice: verb after the gap → *who*; new subject after the gap →
   *whom*.
4. *"The vendor **whose platform** scored highest won the deal."* Possession collapses into one word,
   *whose* + noun; *"that its platform"* is the Portuguese-style workaround.

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

Três palavras relativas para três trabalhos diferentes. **whose** marca **posse** (equivale a
*cujo/cuja*, ou a *his/her/its/their*): *"the vendor **whose** platform we chose"* — serve para
pessoas **e** coisas, e vem sempre seguida de um **substantivo**. **where** aponta um **lugar** e já
carrega a preposição dentro (*"the room **where** we decided"* = *in which*), então **nunca** se
repete a preposição no fim (*"the city where I live"*, jamais *"…where I live in"*). **whom** é a
forma **formal de objeto** para pessoa (*"the exec **whom** the board trusts"*); se vier um **verbo**
depois, use **who** (sujeito), e na fala normalmente se troca *whom* por *who/that* ou se **omite**.

As duas ciladas do português: (1) como *cujo* soa rebuscado, o brasileiro **evita** *whose* e inventa
*"that its platform…"* — treine a palavra única *whose*; (2) *onde* é usado demais para coisas
abstratas, e isso vira *"a decision **where**…," "a situation **where**…,"* que soa errado — decisão e
situação **não** são lugares, então use *in which / that* (ou reformule: *"a decision we got wrong"*).
Teste rápido: *whose* × *who's* → tente abrir para *who is*; *where* × *which* → só use *where* se for
um lugar real onde dá para estar. Se sujeito × objeto ainda embolar, releia
`lessons/2026-09-23-unit-073.md`.
