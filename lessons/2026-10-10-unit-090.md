### 1. 📘 Unit 090 — *still · yet · already · any more / any longer / no longer*

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** Has the board approved the AI budget **yet**?
> **You:** Not **yet** — they're **still** reviewing the risk section. We've **already** sent them the revised
> numbers, so I expect a decision this week.
> **CEO:** And the legacy vendor — are we **still** paying for that platform?
> **You:** No, we **no longer** use it. We dropped it last quarter; we don't renew contracts like that **any
> more**.
> **CFO:** So the old cost line is gone?
> **You:** Gone. But the new one isn't in the forecast **yet** — I'm **still** waiting on finance to confirm the
> figure.

Four little words, two opposite stories. **still** and **yet** say a situation is **not finished** — it
*continues* (*still reviewing*, *still waiting*) or it *hasn't happened but we expect it* (*not yet*, *isn't in
the forecast yet*). **already** says something happened **sooner than you'd expect** (*we've already sent them*).
And **any more / no longer** say a situation has **stopped / changed** (*we no longer use it*, *not any more*).
Notice the positions too: *still*, *already*, *no longer* sit in the **middle** (yesterday's adverb slot), while
*yet* and *any more* go to the **end**.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

Each word answers one question: **has the situation continued, or changed?**

| Word | Meaning | Position | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **still** | it **continues** — not changed | **middle** (before main verb / after *be*/aux) | *We're **still** waiting.* · *They **still** haven't decided.* |
| **yet** | until now, **expected** to happen (question/negative) | **END** | *Has it arrived **yet**?* · *It hasn't arrived **yet**.* |
| **already** | happened **sooner** than expected | **middle** | *We've **already** approved it.* |
| **any more / any longer** | it has **stopped** — changed | **END** (with a negative) | *We don't use it **any more / any longer**.* |
| **no longer** | it has **stopped** (same meaning, more formal) | **middle** | *We **no longer** use it.* |

- ✅ *She **still** works here.* (continues) — ✅ *She **doesn't** work here **any more**.* / *She **no longer**
  works here.* (stopped) — opposite meanings.
- ✅ *Have you finished **yet**?* — ❌ *Have you **already** finished?* is possible but means "so soon?!"; the
  neutral question uses ***yet*** at the end.
- **no longer** takes the middle slot, so with *be* it goes **after**: ✅ *It's **no longer** in the forecast.* —
  ❌ *It **no longer** is in the forecast.*

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why do these words exist?** English needs a quick way to flag *where a situation stands on a timeline* — is it
**still running**, **not started but due**, **done early**, or **finished and gone**? One small word does that
job, so the listener instantly knows the status without a long explanation. For an executive narrating progress
to a board — *"still reviewing," "not approved yet," "already signed," "no longer a cost"* — this is the
vocabulary of status itself.

**Decision rule — the big four, side by side.** Picture one project and watch the word change with the status:

| Status | Word | Sentence |
|---|---|---|
| it **continues** | **still** | *We're **still** negotiating the contract.* |
| **not done**, but expected | **yet** | *We haven't signed it **yet**.* |
| **done early** | **already** | *We've **already** signed it.* |
| **stopped / changed** | **any more / no longer** | *We don't negotiate with them **any more**. / We **no longer** negotiate with them.* |

**The *still* vs *yet* trap — the one that matters most.** Both can translate Portuguese *ainda*, but they are
**not interchangeable**:
- **still** = the action is **happening right now**, continuing: *"We're **still** reviewing it."* (positive,
  middle slot)
- **yet** = the action **hasn't happened**, and you're waiting for it: *"We haven't reviewed it **yet**."*
  (negative/question, end slot)
- Minimal pair: *"She's **still** in the meeting."* (she's in it now) vs *"She isn't out of the meeting
  **yet**."* (she hasn't come out, we expect her to).
- **still + not** is legal and useful — it stresses **impatience / longer than expected**: *"They **still**
  haven't decided"* (emphasis: this is dragging) vs the neutral *"They haven't decided **yet**"* (just stating
  the status). Same facts, different feeling.

**The *already* vs *yet* split.** *already* lives in **positive** statements (done sooner than expected); *yet*
lives in **questions and negatives** (waiting):
- ✅ *"We've **already** approved it."* / ✅ *"Have we approved it **yet**?"* / ✅ *"We haven't approved it
  **yet**."*
- ❌ *"We've approved it **yet**."* (yet doesn't go in a plain positive) ❌ *"Have we **already** approved it?"*
  as your default question — grammatical, but it means *"so soon?!"*, not a neutral status check.

**PT→EN mapping — where Portuguese blurs what English separates:**
- *ainda* is **two English words**: continuing → ***still*** (*"ainda estamos revisando"* → *"we're **still**
  reviewing"*); not-yet-but-expected → ***yet*** (*"ainda não decidimos"* → *"we haven't decided **yet**"*, or
  the impatient *"we **still** haven't decided"*). This one word splitting in two is the core difficulty.
- *já* is also **two words**: positive "done early" → ***already*** (*"já aprovamos"* → *"we've **already**
  approved"*); in a **question** it usually becomes ***yet*** (*"vocês já assinaram?"* → *"have you signed
  **yet**?"*, not *"already"*).
- *não ... mais / já não* → ***any more / no longer*** (*"não usamos mais esse fornecedor"* → *"we don't use
  that vendor **any more**"* / *"we **no longer** use that vendor"*).

**Top Brazilian-speaker errors:**
1. ❌ *"Has the board **already** approved it?"* (as your everyday question) → ✅ *"Has the board approved it
   **yet**?"* Portuguese *já* in a question maps to **yet**, not *already*. *Already* in a question sounds
   surprised — *"approved it already?!"*
2. ❌ *"We don't use that vendor **yet**."* (meaning "not any more") → ✅ *"We don't use that vendor **any more**."*
   / *"We **no longer** use that vendor."* *Yet* = still waiting for it to **start**; *any more / no longer* =
   it has **stopped**. Opposite directions on the timeline — *ainda não* vs *não mais*.
3. ❌ *"It **no longer** is in the forecast."* → ✅ *"It's **no longer** in the forecast."* *No longer* takes the
   middle slot, so with *be* it goes **after** the verb (just like yesterday's *always / never*). Also: *any
   more* goes at the **end**, never mid-sentence — ❌ *"We don't any more renew those."* → ✅ *"We don't renew
   those **any more**."*

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. **The board is still reviewing the AI budget.**
   *— `still` = the review **continues** right now. With `be` it sits **after** it: `is still`. Swap to `yet` and
   you'd need a negative — the two aren't interchangeable.*
2. **They haven't approved the roadmap yet.**
   *— `yet` = not done, but **expected**. Negative sentence, and `yet` lands at the **END**. This is the neutral
   status; `still haven't approved` would add impatience.*
3. **We've already sent them the revised numbers.**
   *— `already` = done **sooner than expected**. Positive statement, **middle** slot (after the auxiliary
   `have`). You can't use `yet` here — `yet` needs a question or negative.*
4. **Has the CFO signed off on the spend yet?**
   *— a neutral status **question** → `yet` at the end. Portuguese "já assinou?" tempts you into `already`, but
   `already` here would mean "signed off *so soon*?!"*
5. **We no longer invest in that legacy platform.**
   *— `no longer` = the investment has **stopped**. Middle slot, before the plain verb `invest`. Formal register —
   perfect for a board update.*
6. **We don't renew contracts like that any more.** *(same meaning as #5, less formal)*
   *— `any more` = stopped, and it goes at the **END** with a negative (`don't`). Compare `no longer` in the
   middle — same message, different position.*
7. **I'm still waiting on finance to confirm the figure.** *(everyday / work blend)*
   *— `still` = the waiting **continues**; `be` + `still` = `I'm still`. The situation is unchanged and ongoing.*
8. **It's almost midnight and the kids still aren't asleep.** *(everyday)*
   *— `still + aren't` = the expected thing **hasn't happened** and it's dragging (impatience). Neutral version:
   "the kids aren't asleep **yet**." Both true; `still` adds the frustration.*

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 089):**
1. **before the plain verb / after *be* / after the auxiliary** — ✅ *"I **always** go to the board sync"* (one
   plain verb → adverb **before**), ✅ *"She **is always** prepared"* (verb *be* → adverb **after**), ✅ *"We
   **have always** approved the budget"* (aux + main → adverb **between**, after the auxiliary). The test:
   **how many verbs, and is one of them *be*?**
2. ✅ *"I **also** want the risk section."* *also* goes in the **middle** (before the main verb), never after it
   (*"want also"* is wrong). Alternative: move it to the end with ***too*** — *"I want the risk section **too**."*
3. ✅ *"We **always** review the budget **carefully**."* *always* (frequency) → **middle**, before the main verb;
   *carefully* (manner) → **end**, after the object. They can't swap because each adverb type has its own fixed
   home — middle for frequency, end for manner.

**Spaced review:**
- **Unit 083 (*enough / too*):** ✅ *"We have **enough runway** to fund one flagship bet."* *enough* goes
   **before a noun** (*enough runway*) but **after an adjective** (*senior enough*, *good enough*). *"runway
   enough"* wrongly uses the after-adjective order with a noun.
- **Unit 062 (*a / an*):** ✅ *"Give me **an** hour with the numbers and **a** one-page summary."* The choice is
   decided by **sound, not spelling**: *hour* starts with a silent *h* (a vowel sound) → *an*; *one-page* starts
   with a *w* sound (*"wun"*) → *a*.

**Recognition drill:**
1. ✅ *"The vendor contract isn't cancelled **yet**; legal is **still** working on it."* **yet** marks the
   *not-done-but-expected* status (negative, end slot); **still** marks the *continuing* action (middle slot,
   after *be*).
2. **(b)** *"Has the board approved the budget **yet**?"* is the neutral status question. (a) with *already*
   implies surprise — *"approved it **so soon**?!"* — which isn't a plain status check. Portuguese *já* in a
   question maps to **yet**, not *already*.
3. ✅ *"We don't use that forecasting tool **any more**."* (or *"We **no longer** use that forecasting tool."*)
   *yet* would mean we're still **waiting to start** using it; but the situation has **stopped / changed**
   (*switched last quarter*), which is *any more / no longer*.
4. ✅ *"It's **no longer** on the roadmap, and we haven't told the team **yet**."* Two fixes: *no longer* takes
   the middle slot, so with *be* it goes **after** — *"It's no longer"*, not *"It no longer is"*; and a negative
   "waiting" statement needs ***yet*** at the end, not *already* (*already* is for positive "done early").

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

Esta unidade dá o vocabulário do **status de uma situação na linha do tempo**. **still** = *ainda* no sentido de
**continua** (*"we're **still** reviewing"* — está acontecendo agora), e fica **no meio** (depois de *be*/
auxiliar, antes do verbo principal). **yet** = *ainda* no sentido de **ainda não aconteceu, mas é esperado** —
só em **perguntas e negativas**, e vai no **fim** (*"have you decided **yet**?"*, *"we haven't decided
**yet**"*). **already** = *já*, algo feito **mais cedo do que o esperado**, em frases **afirmativas**, no meio
(*"we've **already** approved it"*). E **any more / no longer** = *não ... mais / já não*, quando a situação
**parou / mudou** (*any more* no fim, *no longer* no meio).

As duas armadilhas vêm do português colapsar palavras diferentes. *Ainda* vira **dois** termos: *continua* →
**still**; *ainda não* → **yet** (ou *still + not* para dar tom de impaciência: *"they **still** haven't
decided"*). *Já* também vira **dois**: afirmativo "mais cedo" → **already**, mas em pergunta normalmente vira
**yet** (*"vocês já assinaram?"* → *"have you signed **yet**?"*, não *already*). E cuidado com a direção na linha
do tempo: **yet** = ainda esperando **começar**; **any more / no longer** = já **parou**. Decore o gatilho:
continua → *still*; ainda não (esperado) → *yet*; já feito (cedo) → *already*; acabou → *no longer / any more*.
