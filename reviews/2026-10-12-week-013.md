# 🔁 Review Week 013 — Units 86 to 91

*Consolidation through contrast — no new grammar this week. The job is to feel
**which structure wins** when two or three of them compete for the same slot.
(Unit 085 fell on last week's Review day, so this cycle runs 086 → 091.)*

---

### 2. 🧩 Recap — one line per unit

- **Unit 086 — *as … as* / *not as … as* / *the same as*:** equality and difference. *as good as* = level pegging; *not as expensive as* = the soft way to say "cheaper"; *the same as* takes a **noun**, not an adjective.
- **Unit 087 — superlatives (*the + -est* / *the most …*):** ranking one thing against **the whole group**. Always *the*; short word → *-est*, long word → *the most*; never both at once.
- **Unit 088 — word order 1:** keep **verb + object together** (never wedge an adverb between them), and put **place before time** (*in Europe last quarter*, not *last quarter in Europe*).
- **Unit 089 — word order 2 (middle adverbs):** *always / never / already / also / probably* sit in the **middle** — after *be*, after the first auxiliary, before a single main verb.
- **Unit 090 — *still / yet / already / any more / no longer*:** has the situation **continued** (*still*) or **changed** (*no longer / any more*)? *yet* = not-done-but-expected (questions & negatives); *already* = sooner than expected.
- **Unit 091 — two objects:** *give **me** the deck* (person + thing) → once both become pronouns, you must flip to *give **it to me*** (thing + *to* + person).

### 3. 🗺️ Confusion map — when each structure wins

**A. The comparison family — three gears for the same engine**

| You want to say… | Use | Example | Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Two things are **level** | *(not) as … as* | *as accurate **as** theirs* | join word is **as**, never *than* |
| One thing **beats** the other | comparative *-er / more* + **than** | *more scalable **than** ours* | join word is **than**, never *that* / *of* |
| One thing tops the **whole group** | superlative *the -est / the most* | ***the** biggest risk*, ***the most** important call* | never drop *the*; never *most biggest* |

Decision rule: **two things → *than* / *as*; the whole field → *the …-est / the most*.**

**B. Where the adverb goes (Units 088–089)**

| Adverb type | Home | Example | Never |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency / certainty (*always, never, already, probably, also*) | **middle** (after *be* / after 1st auxiliary / before main verb) | *She **is always** ready* · *We **have already** approved it* | *~~We approved already it~~* |
| Manner / place / time (*carefully, in Europe, yesterday*) | **end**, and **place before time** | *reviewed the budget **carefully*** · *launched it **in Europe last quarter*** | never split verb + object: *~~reviewed carefully the budget~~* |

Decision rule: **short "when/how-often/how-sure" words go middle; "how/where/when" details go to the end, verb and object glued together.**

**C. Continued or changed? (Unit 090)**

| Meaning | Word | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Situation **continues** now | *still* (before the verb) | *We're **still** waiting* |
| Expected, **not done** yet | *yet* (end; questions & negatives) | *They haven't decided **yet*** |
| Happened **sooner** than expected | *already* | *We've **already** approved it* |
| Situation has **stopped/changed** | *no longer* / *not … any more* | *They **no longer** use that vendor* |

Decision rule: **ongoing → *still*; stopped → *no longer / any more*; both mean *ainda* / *não mais* — the choice is continuation vs. change, not the Portuguese word.**

**D. Two objects — the pronoun flip (Unit 091)**

- Two **nouns** → *verb + person + thing*: *give **the board the plan***.
- Thing becomes a **pronoun** → flip and add *to*: *give **it to the board*** → *give **it to them***.
- Rule: **once the *thing* is *it / them*, it must come first, and the person moves behind *to*.**

### 4. 🧪 10 recognition exercises (mostly contrast)

*Answers in section 7. Decide first, then check.*

1. **as or than?** *"Our in-house model is just as reliable ___ the vendor's — but it scales better ___ theirs."* Fill **both** blanks.
2. **comparative or superlative?** *"Of the four bets on the table, talent is the ___ (risky) one."* *-er* or *the …-est*? Build it.
3. **spot the error:** *"It's the most biggest decision this board has ever made."*
4. **the join word (three-way):** *"Scenario B is ___ expensive ___ Scenario A"* — meaning they **cost the same**. Which two words?
5. **split trap:** *"We presented carefully the roadmap to the board."* Fix the word order.
6. **middle or end?** Put *always* and *carefully* in their homes: *"The CFO ___ reviews the numbers ___ before a board call."*
7. **place before time:** order the tail — *"We opened a hub ___ ___"* (fragments: *last month* / *in Berlin*).
8. **still or yet?** *"The board hasn't approved the budget ___, so we're ___ waiting on the risk sign-off."* Fill both.
9. **continued or changed?** *"We ___ use that legacy vendor — we dropped the contract last quarter."* (*still* / *no longer*)
10. **the pronoun flip:** rewrite with **both** objects as pronouns — *"Send the CFO the figures."* → ?

### 5. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

- **The comparison gears (A).** Ask your tutor to fire rapid pairs at you — *"Compare the two vendors… now rank all four… now say they're equal"* — so you switch between *as … as*, *more … than*, and *the most …* without stalling on the join word (*as* vs *than* vs *the*).
- **Middle vs. end adverbs (B) under pressure.** Role-play a board update where you must drop in *always, already, probably* (middle) and *carefully, in Europe, last quarter* (end) live: *"We've **already** submitted it and we'll **probably** present it **in Berlin next month**."* This is the week's most mechanical habit — drill it until the slots feel automatic.
- **Still vs. no longer (C).** Have the tutor ask *"Are you still using X? Has the board decided yet?"* and answer with the continuation/change contrast, since both map to Portuguese *ainda / não mais* and that overlap is where interference bites.

### 6. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**O que já deve estar sólido:** a família de comparação (Unidades 86–87). A regra de ouro é a palavra de ligação: **duas coisas** usam *than* (quando uma supera) ou *as* (quando empatam); **o grupo inteiro** usa *the …-est* / *the most*. Nunca *most biggest*, nunca *than* depois de *as*. E *the same* pede um **substantivo** (*the same budget*), não um adjetivo.

**O que provavelmente ainda precisa de trabalho:** (1) **Ordem das palavras** — não enfie o advérbio entre verbo e objeto (*reviewed **carefully** the budget* ❌ → *reviewed the budget **carefully*** ✅), e lugar vem antes de tempo (*in Europe last quarter*). Advérbios de frequência/certeza (*always, already, probably, also*) ficam no **meio**: depois de *be*, depois do primeiro auxiliar, antes do verbo principal. (2) ***still* vs *no longer*** — os dois cabem onde o português usa *ainda / não mais*; a escolha é **continuou** (*still*) ou **mudou/parou** (*no longer / any more*), não a palavra em português. (3) **Dois objetos** — quando a coisa vira pronome (*it / them*), ela vai **na frente** e a pessoa vai para trás do *to*: *give **it to me***.

**Reler se puder:** `lessons/2026-10-09-unit-089.md` (advérbios no meio — o hábito mais mecânico da semana) e `lessons/2026-10-10-unit-090.md` (*still / yet / no longer* — a armadilha de interferência mais traiçoeira).

### 7. ✅ Gabarito

1. **as … than** — *as reliable **as*** (equality) + *scales better **than*** (one beats the other). Two gears, two join words.
2. **the riskiest** — ranking against the **whole group** of four → superlative, short word takes *-est* and keeps *the*.
3. **"the most biggest" → "the biggest"** — double-marking. Pick *one*: *-est* **or** *most*, never both.
4. **as … as** — *as expensive **as*** means equal cost; *than* would mean one is pricier.
5. *"We presented **the roadmap** carefully to the board."* — verb + object glued; the manner adverb moves to the end.
6. *"The CFO **always** reviews the numbers **carefully**."* — *always* = frequency → middle (before the main verb); *carefully* = manner → end.
7. *"…**in Berlin last month**."* — **place before time**.
8. **yet … still** — *hasn't approved … **yet*** (not-done-but-expected, negative → end); *we're **still** waiting* (continuation).
9. **no longer** — the situation **changed** (contract dropped); *still* would mean it continues.
10. *"**Send them to the CFO.**"* / *"**Send them to her.**"* — thing-pronoun (*them*) comes first, person moves behind *to*.
