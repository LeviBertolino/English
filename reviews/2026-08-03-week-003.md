# 🔁 Review Week 003 — Units 16 to 21

One theme ran through the whole week: the **present perfect** — the tense that
carries a past action into the **now**. You sharpened it with *just / already /
yet* (Unit 016), stretched it across your **whole life** with *ever / never /
been* (Unit 017), used it to measure a **duration up to now** with *how long*
(Unit 018), and paired it with the right time-words — *for / since / ago* (Unit
019). Then came the two payoff units: the **referee**, present perfect **vs**
past simple (Unit 020), and a change of viewpoint entirely — the **passive**,
*is done / was done* (Unit 021). This is not new grammar; it's about making the
**choices between** these forms automatic, because in a real board update or
strategy recap they collide in every sentence. Read time: ~15 minutes.

(Note: there is no Unit 015 lesson to recap — that day of the cycle was Review
Week 002, so the count jumps from 014 straight to 016.)

---

## 1. 📋 Recap — one line per unit

- **Unit 016 — I've just… / already… / …yet.** The present perfect fine-tuned:
  **just** = it landed moments ago (*acabei de*), **already** = done sooner than
  expected (*já*, positive), **yet** = still expected, at the sentence **end**
  (negatives/questions). *just/already* sit **inside** the verb; *yet* sits at
  the **end**.
- **Unit 017 — Have you ever…? / I've never… / been.** The present perfect over
  your **whole life**: **ever** in questions (*já… alguma vez?*), **never** as a
  *single* negative (never *"haven't never"*), and **been** = went **and came
  back** (experience), against **gone** = went **and is still there**.
- **Unit 018 — How long have you (been)…?** A situation that **started in the
  past and still continues** → present perfect, **not** present simple. Actions
  take *have been + -ing* (*I've been leading it*); state verbs stay simple
  (*I've known her*). The *how long* question is **always** present perfect.
- **Unit 019 — for / since / ago.** Three time-words, and each one **chooses its
  tense for you**: **for** + a length and **since** + a start point both live
  with the **present perfect** (up to now); **ago** points **back from now** and
  lives with the **past simple** (finished).
- **Unit 020 — I have done vs I did.** The referee of the week: present perfect
  when you're saying **THAT** it happened / the result **now** (no time named);
  past simple when you're saying **WHEN** it happened (a finished, dated moment).
  A finished-time word and the present perfect **cannot share a sentence**.
- **Unit 021 — is done / was done (the passive).** A change of **viewpoint**:
  `be` + past participle puts the **thing acted on** in the spotlight and pushes
  the **doer** offstage (or drops it, unless *by…* matters). The tense lives in
  **`be`** (*is approved / was approved*); the participle never moves.

## 2. 🗺️ Confusion map — when each form wins

The week turns on **five decisions**. Master these five reflexes and the whole
present-perfect zone — plus the passive — falls into place.

### Decision A — present perfect or past simple? (the master rule)

This one governs Units 016, 017, 019, 020 all at once. Ask a single question:
**is a finished past time in view — stated or understood?**

| | Present perfect (*have/has + participle*) | Past simple (*-ed / irregular*) |
|---|---|---|
| Time | **no** named time — connected to **now** | a **finished** time (*yesterday, in 2023, ago*) |
| Focus | the **result / news** right now | **when** it happened / the event |
| Cues | *just, already, yet, ever, never, so far, for, since* | *yesterday, last…, ago, in 2024, then, **when…?*** |

Minimal pair — same action, the time-frame flips the tense:
- *"We've **approved** the AI budget."* → it's approved **now**; you can spend. ✅
- *"We **approved** the AI budget **on Monday**."* → a finished, dated event. ✅

The rule that ends most errors: **the instant a finished time appears
(*yesterday, ago, in 2024, on Monday*), you must switch to the past simple** —
and *"When…?"* is **always** past simple (*"When **did** you present it?"*, never
*"When have you presented…?"*).

### Decision B — inside the present perfect: just / already / yet

Same tense, but position **and** polarity differ:

| Word | Meaning (PT) | Position | Appears in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **just** | *acabei de* — moments ago | **inside** (after have/has) | positive |
| **already** | *já* — sooner than expected | **inside** (after have/has) | positive |
| **yet** | *ainda (não)* / *já?* | **end** of the sentence | negatives & questions |

*already* and *yet* split the Portuguese *já*: **already** in positives (*"She's
**already** approved it"*), **yet** in negatives/questions (*"We haven't started
**yet**" / "Have you started **yet**?"*).

### Decision C — ever vs never, and been vs gone

- **ever** lives in **questions** (*"Have you **ever** presented to this
  board?"*); **never** is its negative and is a **single** negative — the verb
  stays positive: *"I've **never** seen that work"* (✅), never *"haven't never"*
  (❌).
- **been** = went **and is back** (an experience): *"I've **been** to two AI
  summits."* **gone** = went **and is still there** (location now): *"She's
  **gone** to the summit"* (she's there right now).

### Decision D — for vs since vs ago

Two sub-decisions here. First, **what follows the word?**

| | **for** (a length) | **since** (a start point) |
|---|---|---|
| Follows with | *two years, six months, ages* | *2024, March, the board approved it* |
| Meaning | the **size** of the gap | **where** the gap began |
| Test | *"…for how long?"* → a number of units | *"…since when?"* → a date/event |

Then, **which tense?** — the word decides, it's not a free choice:

| Word | Measures | Tense |
|---|---|---|
| **for / since** | a stretch that **reaches now** | **present perfect** |
| **ago** | a point **back from now**, finished | **past simple** |

Minimal contrasts:
- ✅ *"We've worked with them **for** two years."* / ✅ *"…**since** 2024."*
- ❌ *"…**since** two years"* → *two years is a length, not a start → **for***.
- *"I joined **for** three years"* (still here, present perfect) vs *"I joined
  three years **ago**"* (finished point, past simple).

### Decision E — present perfect or present *simple*? (the duration trap)

This is the pure translation reflex, and the week's single most common Brazilian
slip. Test: **is there a duration reaching up to now (a *how long / for /
since*) about something still true?**

| | Present perfect (*I've been here for a year*) | Present simple (*I live here*) |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | *how long, for, since* — a span to **now** | a fact/habit, **no** duration in view |
| Example | *"**How long have** you **been** in this role?"* | *"Where **do** you **work**?"* |

❌ *"I **live** here for three years"* → ✅ *"I've **lived** here for three
years."* Portuguese marks this with the **present** + *"há / faz… que"* (*"faz
três anos que moro aqui"*), and that present tense is exactly the trap.

### Decision F — active or passive?

Same event, flipped viewpoint. Ask: **who cares about the doer, and what do I
want at the front?**

| Go **active** when… | Go **passive** when… |
|---|---|
| the **doer** is the point (*The **CFO** made that call*) | the doer is obvious, unknown, or better unnamed |
| you want to lead with **who** acted | you want to lead with the **thing acted on** |

Minimal pairs:
- *"The board **approved** the budget."* (about **the board**) vs *"The budget
  **was approved**."* (about **the budget**; the doer may go unstated).
- *"Someone **stole** my laptop."* (clumsy, vague doer up front) vs *"My laptop
  **was stolen**."* (natural — drop the unknown doer).

Remember: the tense lives in **`be`** (*is reviewed* / *was reviewed*); the
participle is fixed.

## 3. 🧪 10 recognition exercises (contrast-focused)

Retrieve first — all answers with explanations are in section 5.

1. **Perfect or past?** *"We ___ the AI roadmap ___ (already / last quarter)."* —
   which time-word forces which tense, and why can't they swap?
2. **just / already / yet — pick and place:** *"— Have you sent the board deck
   ___? — Yes, I've ___ sent it; I've ___ finished it two minutes ago."* Fill the
   three gaps and say why each word sits where it does.
3. **ever or never (and mind the polarity):** *"___ you ___ run a full turnaround?
   — No, I've ___ done one."* What's the trap in the second gap for a Portuguese
   speaker?
4. **been or gone?** *"The CFO isn't here — she's ___ to the summit"* vs *"The CFO
   is back; she's ___ to two summits this year."* Which is which, and why?
5. **for or since?** *"We've been running the pilot ___ March, so ___ about five
   months now."* Fill both and name the test you used.
6. **for or ago (the tense trap):** *"I ___ this role two years ___,"* and *"I ___
   in this role ___ two years."* Fill each pair with the right verb form + word.
7. **Fix the duration slip:** *"I **work** here **for** three years."* Rewrite it
   and explain the rule.
8. **Perfect or past — the *When* test:** *"___ have you launched / When did you
   launch the assistant?"* Which opening is impossible, and why?
9. **Active or passive?** *"The results ___ every fortnight"* (nobody in
   particular does it; it's a process) — active *"we review"* or passive *"are
   reviewed"*? What tips the choice?
10. **Fix the passive tense:** *"The budget **is approved** last week."* What's
    wrong, and what's the fix?

## 4. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

Bring the **most confusable contrasts**, not the individual forms:

- **The referee drill (Decision A) — the highest-value round.** Ask your tutor to
  feed you the same executive event twice — once as **news** (no time), once
  **dated** — and you produce both tenses back to back: *"We've launched the AI
  assistant"* → *"We launched it three weeks ago, and adoption jumped."* Then have
  them slip a finished-time word (*yesterday, in Q2, when I joined*) into a
  sentence and you must **catch** it and force the past simple. The reflex to
  build: a clock or calendar in the sentence **kills** the present perfect.
- **The duration trap (Decision E) — role-play an intro.** Have your tutor play a
  new board member and interview you: *"How long have you led innovation? How long
  have you been with the company?"* You answer with **present perfect + for/since**
  every time, fighting the Portuguese *"faz… que"* pull that wants *"I lead it for
  eight years."* Add a *for-vs-since* volley: they give you a length or a date, you
  supply the matching word instantly.
- **Active ↔ passive re-framing (Decision F).** Ask your tutor to give you blunt
  active sentences with an awkward or unimportant doer (*"Someone made a mistake in
  the forecast," "The team approved the budget," "People will review the roadmap"*)
  and you re-cast each in the **passive** to move the focus onto the **what** and
  drop the finger-pointing: *"A mistake was made in the forecast," "The budget was
  approved," "The roadmap will be reviewed."* This is exactly how outcomes get
  reported to a C-Level without blame.

## 5. ✅ Gabarito — exercises

1. **"We've already approved the AI roadmap"** (present perfect) vs **"We approved
   the AI roadmap last quarter"** (past simple). — *"already" belongs to the
   time-less "up to now" zone → present perfect; "last quarter" is a finished,
   dated period → past simple. They can't swap because a named finished time and
   the present perfect never share a sentence.*
2. **"Have you sent the board deck yet? — Yes, I've already sent it; I just
   finished it two minutes ago"** → careful: *"two minutes ago"* forces **past
   simple** → **"I finished it two minutes ago."** — *"yet" goes at the **end** of
   the question; "already" sits **inside** the positive answer; and the moment
   "ago" appears you drop the perfect and use the past simple (finished).*
3. **"Have you ever run a full turnaround? — No, I've never done one."** — *"ever"
   opens the life-experience question; "never" is a **single** negative and keeps
   the verb positive. The trap: Portuguese doubles it (*"nunca fiz"*), tempting
   *"I haven't never"* — wrong in English.*
4. **"She's gone to the summit"** (still there) vs **"she's been to two summits"**
   (went and is back). — *gone = went and is still away; been = experienced and
   returned. "Back" in the second clue is the giveaway.*
5. **"since March, so for about five months."** — *March is a **start point** →
   since; five months is a **length** → for. Test: "since when?" → a date; "for how
   long?" → a number of units.*
6. **"I took this role two years ago"** (past simple + ago, finished) and **"I've
   been in this role for two years"** (present perfect + for, still true). — *ago
   points back to a finished moment → past simple; for measures a stretch reaching
   now → present perfect. Same span, opposite ends, opposite tenses.*
7. ❌ *"I work here for three years"* → ✅ **"I've worked here for three years"** (or
   *"I've been working here…"*). — *A duration reaching up to now needs the present
   perfect, not the present simple. Portuguese "faz três anos que trabalho" hides
   the present tense that misleads you.*
8. **"When did you launch the assistant?"** is the only possible one; **"When have
   you launched…?"** is impossible. — *"When…?" asks for a specific finished point,
   so it's **always** past simple. The present perfect refuses a named moment.*
9. **"The results are reviewed every fortnight"** (passive). — *No specific doer
   matters — it's a recurring **process**, so lead with the thing acted on and drop
   the doer. Active "we review" would wrongly put a "we" in the spotlight.*
10. ❌ *"The budget is approved last week"* → ✅ **"The budget was approved last
    week."** — *the tense lives in `be`; "last week" is finished, so `be` must be
    **past** → was approved. The participle "approved" never changes.*

## 6. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**O que já deve estar sólido.** A semana inteira gira em torno do **present
perfect** — a ponte que traz uma ação passada para o **agora**. Você já sabe
afiná-lo: **just** (*acabei de*), **already** (*já*, afirmativo) e **yet** (*ainda
não* / *já?*, no fim da frase); esticá-lo pela **vida toda** com **ever** (em
perguntas) e **never** (uma **única** negação); medir uma **duração até agora**
com *how long have you (been)…?*; e casar cada palavra de tempo com o tempo verbal
certo — **for/since** puxam o present perfect, **ago** puxa o past simple. A
grande decisão da semana é o **juiz** (Unidade 020): *have done* quando você diz
**QUE** aconteceu / o resultado **agora** (sem citar quando), *did* quando você diz
**QUANDO** aconteceu (momento terminado e datado). E, virando a câmera, a **voz
passiva** (Unidade 021): *be + particípio* coloca a **coisa** no centro e tira o
**agente** de cena — o tempo mora no **`be`** (*is/was approved*), o particípio não
muda.

**O que provavelmente ainda precisa de trabalho.** Quatro armadilhas costumam
sobreviver. **(1)** A regra-mãe: **assim que aparece um tempo terminado**
(*yesterday, ago, in 2024, on Monday*), troque para o **past simple** — e
*"When…?"* é **sempre** past simple. Present perfect e uma data terminada **não
convivem** na mesma frase. **(2)** A cilada da **duração**: *"faz três anos que
trabalho aqui"* vira *"I've **worked** here for three years"*, **nunca** *"I work
here for three years"* — o presente do português engana. **(3)** *for* × *since* ×
*ago*: **for** + duração, **since** + ponto de início (jamais *"since two years"*),
**ago** + passado terminado. **(4)** A negação **única**: *"I've **never**…"*,
nunca *"haven't never"*; e **been** (foi e voltou) × **gone** (foi e está lá). Se
algum contraste ainda embola, **releia dois arquivos**:
`lessons/2026-08-01-unit-020.md` (o juiz *have done* vs *did* — o coração da
semana) e `lessons/2026-07-31-unit-019.md` (*for/since/ago* e o tempo que cada um
exige).
