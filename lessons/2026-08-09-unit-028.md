### 1. 📘 Unit 028 — will / shall (1)

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CFO:** The board wants a one-pager on the AI spend by Friday. Nobody's on it.
> **You:** No problem — **I'll** do it. **I'll** send you a draft tomorrow morning.
> **CFO:** And someone needs to brief the audit committee.
> **You:** Leave it with me. **I'll** ask Marina to prep the numbers, and if she's
> stretched, **I'll** cover it myself.
> **CFO:** Appreciated. This helps.
> **You:** **Shall I** also loop in the CEO before it goes out?

Notice *when* each *I'll* appears: the moment the need lands on the table. You
didn't plan to write the one-pager an hour ago — you **decide it right there, as
you speak**. That instant "okay, I'll handle it" is exactly what *will* is for.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

*will* is a modal: same form for every subject, always followed by the **base
verb**. No *-s*, no *do/does*.

| | Positive | Negative | Question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Form | **'ll / will** + base | **won't** (will not) + base | **Will** + subject + base? |
| Example | *I **'ll call** you.* | *I **won't forget**.* | ***Will** you **join**?* |

- In speech and email, the short form **'ll** is normal: *I'll, we'll, they'll,
  it'll*. The full *will* sounds emphatic or formal.
- Negative is **won't** (not *willn't*): *I **won't** be late.*
- **shall** survives mainly in two offers/suggestions with *I* and *we*:
  ***Shall I** …?* (shall I do this for you?) and ***Shall we** …?* (let's — a
  proposal). Everywhere else, modern English uses *will*.

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why does this structure exist?** *will* is the future you **decide at the
moment of speaking** — plus the things that ride on that instant decision:
**offers, promises, and refusals**. It is the verb of *"okay, I'll handle it."*
Nothing was arranged beforehand; the choice is being made *now*, out loud.

**Decision rule — *will* vs *going to* (yesterday's structure, the one rival that
matters).** Both point at the future. The split is **when the decision was made**:

| | *will* (I'll) | *going to* |
|---|---|---|
| Decision made | **now**, as you speak | **already**, before you speak |
| Feels like | spontaneous, reactive | a plan / intention |
| Trigger | someone asks, a need appears | you're reporting a settled plan |

Minimal pair — same words, opposite backstory:
- *"The report's late." → "Don't worry, **I'll** finish it tonight."* → you
  decide it *in response*, on the spot. (will)
- *"Why are you staying late?" → "**I'm going to** finish the report."* → the plan
  was already in place; you're just describing it. (going to)

And the offer/promise/refusal family — pure *will* territory:
- **Offer:** *"**I'll** carry that for you." / "**Shall I** open the meeting?"*
- **Promise:** *"**I'll** have the numbers to you by nine."*
- **Refusal:** *"The vendor **won't** move on price."* (won't = refuses to)

**PT→EN mapping.** Brazilian Portuguese leans on the present or *ir + infinitivo*
for almost everything future, so the *will* / *going to* line has no clean
equivalent — and that's where the interference lives:
- *"Eu faço isso"* (deciding now) → **"I'll do it,"** not *"I do it."* PT uses the
  present for on-the-spot offers; English **must** use *will* there.
- *"Vou apresentar amanhã"* is a **plan** → *"I'm going to present"* / *"I'm
  presenting."* Don't reach for *will* just because PT said *vou*.
- *Shall I…?* has no direct PT word — it's the polite *"Quer que eu…?"* /
  *"Abro a reunião?"*

**Top Brazilian-speaker errors:**
- ❌ *"I think I will to send it."* → ✅ *"I think **I'll send** it."* — *will* +
  **base verb**, never *will to*.
- ❌ *"Tomorrow I will meet the board"* (when it's a fixed arrangement) → ✅
  *"Tomorrow **I'm meeting** the board."* — a diary item isn't a *will* decision.
- ❌ *"I will do it"* said flatly for a plan you made yesterday → ✅ *"**I'm going
  to** do it."* — reserve *will* for the decision made *as you speak*.

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. *"The board needs a summary? **I'll** draft it this afternoon."*
   *— Offer decided on the spot; the need appears and you take it → will, not going to.*
2. *"**I'll** send the AI roadmap to the CEO before end of day."*
   *— A promise made now; commitment in the moment is pure will.*
3. *"The vendor **won't** lower the licence fee, so we walk."*
   *— won't = refuses to; will's negative carries "declines," not just "future not."*
4. *"**Shall I** present the investment case, or would you rather lead?"*
   *— Offer/suggestion with I → Shall I…?, the polite "quer que eu…?"*
5. *"If the pilot fails, **we'll** kill it and reallocate the budget."*
   *— Decision contingent on a future event, formed now — classic will in an if-plan.*
6. *"I don't think the CFO **will** sign off before the re-forecast."*
   *— will for a judgement about the future; going to would imply present evidence.*
7. *"Hold on — **I'll** get you the exact figures."* (mid-meeting)
   *— Spontaneous reaction to what was just asked; the hallmark of I'll.*
8. *"You look lost — **I'll** walk you to the room."*
   *— Everyday on-the-spot offer; you decide the instant you notice.*

### 8. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

- Ask your tutor to role-play a CFO dropping urgent, unplanned asks on you ("the
  audit committee needs numbers by Friday", "nobody's briefed the CEO"). Respond
  to each **only with *I'll…* and *Shall I…?***, so on-the-spot offers become reflex.
- Practise the *will* vs *going to* split out loud: for each future item, first
  say the **plan** (*"I'm going to restructure the AI portfolio"*), then react to a
  surprise with an **instant decision** (*"The vendor pulled out? Fine — I'll find
  another"*). Feel the difference in *when* the choice happens.

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 027 — going to):**
1. *"we **are going to** increase"* — an intention decided before you speak; *going
   to* reports a plan already formed, not a snap decision.
2. *"we **are going to** overshoot"* — the run-rate is **evidence you can see now**;
   *going to* predicts from present signs, while *will* would be a bare guess.
3. *"I'm going to present"* = the intention (still in your head); *"I'm presenting
   on Thursday"* = a fixed **arrangement**, a slot already in the diary.

**Spaced review:**
- **Unit 021:** *"The AI budget **was approved** at the last board meeting."* The
  passive needs **be + past participle** — drop *was* and there's no verb at all;
  *approved* alone can't stand as the main verb here.
- **Unit 006:** *"The team **doesn't have** the data maturity."* Two faults: the
  subject *the team* takes **doesn't** (not *don't*), and after the auxiliary the
  main verb goes back to **base form** — *have*, never *has*.

**Recognition drill:**
1. ***going to*** — *"I'm going to finalise"*: the blocked calendar shows it's
   already planned, not decided in the moment.
2. *"I think **I'll join** the strategy call."* — *will* + **base verb**; *will to*
   is never correct.
3. ***I'll check*** — you decide it *right now*, reacting to what was just said; the
   spontaneous offer is *will*, not *going to*.
4. ***Shall I*** open the board meeting — an **offer/suggestion** with *I*; *Will I*
   would be asking about your own future, which isn't the meaning.

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**A regra e quando usar.** *will* (forma curta *'ll*) é o futuro que você **decide
na hora de falar**: alguém pede algo, surge uma necessidade, e você responde *"tá,
eu faço"* → *"I'll do it."* Daí vêm também as **ofertas, promessas e recusas**:
*I'll send it by nine* (promessa), *The vendor won't move on price* (won't =
*recusa-se a*), e *Shall I…? / Shall we…?* para se oferecer ou propor algo com *I*
e *we*. É sempre *will + verbo na base* — sem *-s*, sem *do/does*, e o negativo é
*won't*.

**As armadilhas.** A confusão-mãe é *will* vs *going to* (a unidade de ontem): use
*going to* para um **plano já decidido antes** de falar; use *will* para a decisão
**tomada no momento**. O português atrapalha de dois jeitos: (1) usamos o presente
para ofertas na hora (*"eu faço isso"*), mas o inglês **exige** *I'll do it*, nunca
*I do it*; (2) o *vou* de *"vou apresentar"* puxa para *will*, mas plano é *going
to* / present continuous, não *will*. E nunca escreva *will to* — depois de *will*
vem o verbo puro.
