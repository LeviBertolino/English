### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **You (to the team):** Right — **let's focus**. **Don't spread** the budget
> across ten pilots. **Pick** the two that touch revenue, and **park** the rest.
> **Director:** And the board deck?
> **You:** **Let's keep** it to three slides. **Open** with the vision, **show**
> the numbers, **close** with the ask. **Don't overload** them with detail.
> **Director:** And if the CFO pushes on cost?
> **You:** **Let's not** guess. **Bring** the sensitivity table — but only if she
> asks.

Notice the pattern before any rule. Every instruction here drops the subject and
uses the **bare verb**: *Pick*, *Open*, *show*, *close*, *Bring*. The warnings use
**Don't + verb**: *Don't spread*, *Don't overload*. And when you rally the room to
act **together**, it's **Let's + verb**: *let's focus*, *Let's keep*, *Let's not
guess*. No *you*, no *to*, no *-s* — the most direct voice English has.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

The imperative has **no subject** and uses the **base verb**. Same shape whether
you're talking to one person or the whole board.

| Use | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Instruction / order | **base verb** | ***Pick*** the two pilots. |
| Prohibition / warning | **Don't** + base | ***Don't overload*** the board. |
| Joint action (we) | **Let's** + base | ***Let's focus*** on revenue. |
| Joint action (negative) | **Let's not** + base | ***Let's not*** rush the call. |
| Softened request | **Please** + base *(or)* base + **please** | ***Please review*** the deck. |
| Warm emphasis | **Do** + base | ***Do sit down*** — this'll take a minute. |

**Key facts:**
- **No subject, no *-s*, no *to*.** *"Send the deck,"* never *"You send"*, *"Sends"*
  or *"To send."*
- The negative is **Don't + base** (*Don't forget*), never *"No forget"* or
  *"Don't forgets."*
- **Let's = let us.** It always **includes you** — it's a proposal for *us,
  together*, not an order aimed at someone else. Negative: **Let's not + base**.
- **Please** softens it (*Please send…*), but it stays an imperative — no *to*
  after *please*: *"Please review,"* not *"Please to review."*
- Emphatic **Do + base** is warm, not bossy: *"Do let me know,"* *"Do have a
  look."* It adds insistence or warmth, the opposite of coldness.

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why does the imperative exist?** It's English's most **direct** verb form — the
one for **instructions, steps, directions, warnings, offers, and calls to joint
action**. When you script a deck (*"Open with the vision, close with the ask"*),
run a process (*"Save the file, then push"*), warn someone (*"Don't share these
figures"*), or rally a room (*"Let's focus"*), the imperative is the natural,
economical choice. Its power is also its risk: aimed at the wrong person, a bare
order can sound blunt.

**Decision rule #1 — bald imperative vs polite request (the register trap).** The
imperative is perfect for **your own team, sequences of steps, directions, and
offers**; it can sound like a **demand** when you're asking a **favour of a
senior** or a peer. There, English softens to the *Could you…? / I'd like…* you
drilled in Units 031 and 037.

| Bald imperative — fits | Softened request — safer |
|---|---|
| *"**Pick** the two revenue pilots."* (to your team) | *"**Could you** send me the updated numbers?"* (to the CFO) |
| *"**Open** with the vision, **close** with the ask."* (deck steps) | *"**I'd like** your read on the acquisition."* (a favour) |
| *"**Have a look** at slide three."* (a friendly offer) | *"**Would you** review the deck before Thursday?"* (an ask) |

Decision rule: **giving instructions / directions / offers → imperative. Asking a
senior for something → soften it.**

**Decision rule #2 — *Let's* vs *We should* vs *Shall we?*** All three point the
group at an action, with different force:
- *"**Let's** start with the roadmap."* → an **inclusive call to act now**,
  energetic; it assumes the room is with you.
- *"**We should** start with the roadmap."* → **advice / recommendation** (Unit
  034) — weaker, more "here's my opinion" than "let's go."
- *"**Shall we** start with the roadmap?"* → a **polite check** that the group
  agrees before you move — a proposal phrased as a question.

**Decision rule #3 — *Don't* vs *mustn't*.** Both forbid, but at different levels:
- *"**Don't** share these figures."* → a **direct instruction, here and now**, to
  the person in front of you.
- *"You **mustn't** share these figures."* → a **standing rule / prohibition**
  (Unit 032), true beyond this moment.
So *Don't* is the immediate "not now / not this"; *mustn't* states the policy.

**PT→EN mapping — where the Portuguese imperative helps and where it betrays.**
The core map is clean: *"Faça isso" → **Do this***; *"Não faça isso" → **Don't do
this***; and above all *"Vamos + infinitivo" → **Let's + base*** (*"Vamos começar"
→ **Let's start***). Three places it betrays Levi:
- (1) **The dropped subject.** Portuguese hides the subject in the verb ending, so
  Levi sometimes **adds *you*** in English: *"You send me the numbers"* reads as a
  cold order, not a clean instruction. Drop the *you*.
- (2) **The *"Let's to"* / *"to"* leak.** *Vamos* feels like it needs a *to* before
  the verb — it doesn't: *"**Let's** start,"* never *"Let's **to** start"*; *"Please
  **review**,"* never *"Please **to** review."*
- (3) **The two faces of *Vamos*.** *"Vamos apresentar amanhã"* can mean a **future
  plan** (*"We're going to present tomorrow"*), not a suggestion. As a proposal to
  act **now**, it's *Let's*; as a plan for later, it's *we're going to* (Unit 026).

**Top Brazilian-speaker errors:**
- ❌ *"**Let's to** align** on the vision before the call."* → ✅ *"**Let's align**
  on the vision before the call."* — *Let's* is followed by the **base verb**, no
  *to*.
- ❌ *"**Don't forgets** the ask,"* / *"**No share** these figures."* → ✅ *"**Don't
  forget** the ask,"* / *"**Don't share** these figures."* — the negative is
  **Don't + base**: no *-s*, never *no + verb*.
- ❌ *"**Please to review** the deck,"* / *"**You review** the deck."* → ✅
  *"**Please review** the deck."* — no *to* after *please*, and no subject.

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. *"**Let's** position the company as an AI-first player."*
   *— A call to act together now; "Let's" rallies the room, warmer and more decisive than "We should position."*
2. *"**Don't spread** the budget across ten pilots — **pick** the two that touch revenue."*
   *— Prohibition + instruction to your own team; the bald imperative is the natural register here, not a demand.*
3. *"**Open** with the vision, **show** the numbers, and **close** with the ask."*
   *— A sequence of steps for the deck; imperatives are how you script a process, one bare verb per move.*
4. *"**Let's not** rush the acquisition before the Q4 numbers are in."*
   *— "Let's not" proposes we hold back together; softer and more collegial than "We mustn't rush."*
5. *"**Please send** the board the revised forecast by Thursday."*
   *— "Please + base" softens a work request while keeping it an imperative; note: no "to" after please.*
6. *"**Have a look** at slide three before the sync — I'd value your read."*
   *— The imperative as a friendly offer/invitation, not an order; English uses it freely for "here, do this for your benefit."*
7. *"**Don't worry** — the pilot is already live and stable."*
   *— Everyday reassurance; "Don't + base" is the standard way to reassure, nothing bossy about it.*
8. *"**Turn** left at reception and **take** the second lift to the boardroom."*
   *— Everyday directions; the imperative is the default voice for instructions and directions off-work too.*

### 8. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

- Ask your tutor to run a **"register switch" drill**: they name a situation —
  *"ask the CFO for the updated numbers," "tell your team to cut a pilot," "guide a
  board visitor to the room," "warn a director not to share the figures"* — and you
  fire back with either a **bald imperative** or a **softened request** (*"Could
  you…?" / "I'd like…"*), then justify the choice. The goal is to feel instantly
  when a direct imperative is natural (your own team, steps, directions, offers) and
  when it needs softening (a favour from a senior).
- Then a **"Let's pitch" role-play**: open a mock strategy meeting speaking almost
  entirely in *Let's / Let's not* — *"Let's focus on two bets," "Let's not spread
  thin," "Let's keep the deck to three slides," "Let's close with the ask"* — to
  build the inclusive, energising proposal voice you'd use to rally the C-Level,
  distinct from the weaker *"We should…"*.

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 037 — would like):**
1. ***Would you like me to*** — *"**Would you like me to** walk the board
   through…?"* English offers to do something *for* someone with **would like + me
   to**; *"Would you like that I…"* is a word-for-word copy of *"gostaria que
   eu…"* and isn't English.
2. ***Would you like to*** — *"**Would you like to** join us?"* actually **invites**
   her to this event; *"Do you like to join…?"* only asks whether she **enjoys**
   offsites in general — not an invitation at all.
3. **Add *to*** — *"I'd like **to** propose a partnership."* Before a verb, *would
   like* always needs **to**; *"I'd like propose"* drops the required infinitive
   marker.

**Spaced review:**
- **(Unit 031 — can and could)** ***were able to*** — *"we finally **were able
  to** sign the vendor contract."* This is **one specific past success**, so
  English uses *was able to / managed to*; *could* fits only a **general** past
  ability, never a single achievement.
- **(Unit 014 — past continuous vs past simple)** ***were discussing*** — *"When
  the CEO arrived, we **were discussing** the AI budget"* means the discussion was
  **already under way** when he walked in. *"we discussed"* would mean he arrived
  **first** and **then** we began — two events in sequence, not an interruption.

**Recognition drill:**
1. ***"Could you send me the updated numbers?"*** — with a **senior**, the polite
   request is the safe register; the bald imperative *"Send me…"* can read as a
   **cold order / demand**. (A bald imperative is fine for your own team, steps,
   directions or offers — just not for a favour up the chain.)
2. **Remove *to*** → *"**Let's align** on the vision before the board call."*
   *Let's* is followed by the **base verb**; *"Let's to align"* wrongly inserts an
   infinitive *to*.
3. ***Let's*** — *"**Let's** start with the roadmap"* is an **inclusive call to act
   now, together**. *"We should start"* turns it into **advice / a recommendation**
   (weaker, more "my opinion"), not a call to move.
4. **Two fixes** → *"**Please review** the deck and **don't forget** the ask."*
   No *to* after *please* (*please + base*), and the negative is **Don't + base**
   with **no *-s*** (*don't forget*, never *don't forgets*).

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**A regra e quando usar.** O imperativo é a voz mais **direta** do inglês: sem
sujeito e com o **verbo na base**. Serve para **instruções, passos, direções,
avisos, ofertas** e para chamar o grupo à ação. Três formas: afirmativa (*"**Pick**
the two pilots"*), negativa **Don't + verbo** (*"**Don't overload** the board"*) e
**Let's + verbo** para "nós, juntos" (*"**Let's focus**"*, negativo *"**Let's
not** rush"*). *Please* suaviza (*"Please send…"*), mas continua imperativo — sem
*to*. Compare com os vizinhos: *Let's* = chamado a agir agora, juntos; *We should*
= conselho, mais fraco (Unit 034); *Shall we?* = proposta em forma de pergunta. E
*Don't* = instrução imediata; *mustn't* = regra/proibição permanente (Unit 032).

**As armadilhas.** (1) **Registro:** o imperativo puro é natural com a sua equipe,
em passos e direções, mas soa como **ordem** ao pedir um favor a um sênior — aí use
*"Could you…?" / "I'd like…"* (Units 031 e 037). (2) **Sem sujeito e sem *to*:** o
português esconde o sujeito no verbo, então evite *"You send…"* e nunca escreva
*"Let's **to** start"* nem *"Please **to** review"* — depois de *Let's* e de
*please* vem o verbo puro. (3) **Negativo:** é sempre *Don't + base* — *"Don't
forget"*, jamais *"don't forgets"* ou *"no forget"*. (4) *Vamos* tem dois sentidos:
proposta agora = *Let's*; plano para depois = *we're going to* (Unit 026). Se o
contraste conselho × ação ainda embolar, releia `lessons/2026-08-15-unit-034.md`
(should) e `lessons/2026-08-13-unit-032.md` (must / mustn't).
