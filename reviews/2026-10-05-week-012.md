# 🔁 Review Week 012 — Units 78 to 84

*One week of **describing and comparing** — the grammar of saying how good, how much,
and how one option stacks up against another. This is the language you use every time
you rate a plan, qualify a bet, or recommend A over B to the board.*

> consolidates **Units 79–84** — the six lessons actually covered. Nothing is lost:
> these six form one tight family (adjectives → adverbs → intensifiers → comparatives).

## 2. 📋 Recap — one line per unit

- **Unit 79 — Adjective order + after *look / feel / sound*.** Opinion before fact
  (*a **bold, ambitious long-term** strategy*); after linking verbs (*be, seem, look,
  feel, sound*) use an **adjective**, because it describes the subject, not the action
  (*the plan **looks realistic***, not *realistically*).
- **Unit 80 — Adjective vs adverb (*quick* / *quickly*).** Adjective describes a **noun**
  (*a **quick** decision*); adverb describes a **verb/adjective/adverb** (*decide
  **quickly***). *good → well* is irregular (*the plan went **well***, never *good*).
- **Unit 81 — The exceptions (*well · fast · late · hard / hardly · lately*).** *fast,
  hard, late, early* are the same word as adjective and adverb (no *-ly*). But **hardly**
  = *almost not* and **lately** = *recently* — separate words, not the adverbs of
  *hard / late*.
- **Unit 82 — *so* and *such*.** Both = *very* + a forward arrow to a **result**. Choose
  by what follows: adjective/adverb alone → **so** (*so expensive*); a noun → **such**
  (*such a risk*). *a/an* only ever goes with *such*.
- **Unit 83 — *enough* and *too*.** Opposite verdicts against a threshold: **enough** =
  *sufficient, we can proceed*; **too** = *excess, it's now a problem*. *enough* goes
  **after** an adjective but **before** a noun; *too* always goes **before**.
- **Unit 84 — Comparatives (*-er* vs *more* + *than*).** Short word → *-er* (*cheaper,
  faster, riskier*); long word → *more* (*more scalable*). Never both. Connector is
  always **than**. Boosters: *much / far / a bit* (never *very*).

## 3. 🗺️ Confusion map — when each one wins

The whole week is about **pointing a describing word at the right target** and **rating
against the right reference**. Here is the week contrasted against itself:

| You want to… | Use | Giveaway | Trap it beats |
|---|---|---|---|
| describe a **noun** | **adjective** (*a rapid rollout*) | there's a noun right there | using the *-ly* adverb |
| describe a **verb / how** | **adverb** (*rolled out rapidly*) | it answers *how?* | using the plain adjective |
| describe the subject after *look/feel/seem* | **adjective** (*looks solid*) | linking verb, no action | *looks solidly* ❌ |
| say "with effort" | **hard** | *worked hard* | *hardly* (= almost not) |
| say "almost not" | **hardly** | *hardly worked* | *hard* (= with effort) |
| say "recently" | **lately** | *grown lately* | *late* (= after time) |
| emphasise an **adjective** → result | **so** (*so risky that…*) | adjective alone follows | *such risky* ❌ |
| emphasise a **noun** → result | **such** (*such a risk that…*) | a noun follows; *a/an* present | *so a risk* ❌ |
| say "sufficient, we can go" | **enough** (*senior enough*) | sits **after** adj / before noun | *too* (wrong direction) |
| say "excess, it's a problem" | **too** (*too risky*) | sits **before** adj | *very* (no verdict) |
| rank **two** options | **comparative + than** (*cheaper than*) | there's a *than* / two things | mixing *more* + *-er* |

**The three sharpest minimal pairs of the week:**

1. **so vs such** — *It's **so** complex.* / *It's **such a** complex deal.* → *a* decides it.
2. **too vs enough** — *too risky to approve* (we **can't**) / *safe enough to approve*
   (we **can**) → opposite verdicts.
3. **hard vs hardly** — *We worked **hard**.* (a lot) / *We **hardly** worked.* (almost nothing)
   → the *-ly* reverses the meaning.

## 5. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

Three contrasts from this week are the most confusable in real speech — bring these to
your tutor:

1. **so vs such, live.** Ask your tutor to throw you a rapid mix of adjectives and nouns
   (*"expensive" / "a risk" / "bold moves" / "much pressure"*) and you snap back *so* or
   *such* + a consequence clause (*"…so expensive **that** we'd freeze two bets"*). This
   trains the "is there a noun?" reflex under time pressure.
2. **too vs enough as a go / no-go verdict.** Role-play a budget review: the tutor pitches
   investments, you gate each one using *too … to* (reject) or *enough to* (approve) —
   *"too early to scale"*, *"mature enough to fund"*. This makes the threshold meaning
   automatic.
3. **Comparatives in a build-vs-buy debate.** Argue two vendors or build-vs-buy out loud
   using *-er / more … than* with boosters — *"far more scalable than ours, but a bit
   slower to ship"*. Have the tutor catch any *more cheaper* / *very bigger* slips.

## 6. ✅ Gabarito

1. **quickly** — it describes **how** they approved (the verb), so it must be an adverb.
2. **looks realistic** — *look* is a linking verb; the word describes the *plan* (subject),
   so adjective, not adverb.
3. **hardly** — meaning *almost not / very little progress*. *hard* would mean "with effort".
4. **such** — a noun (*opportunity*) follows, and *a* is present; *a/an* always rides with *such*.
5. **senior enough** — *enough* goes **after** an adjective (*enough* before a noun, but after an adj/adv).
6. **cheaper** — one syllable → *-er*; never *more cheap* or the double *more cheaper*.
7. **lately** — meaning *recently*. *late* would mean "after the expected time".
8. **too** — *too + adjective + to* = excess that **blocks** the action (we can't approve). *so risky* would need a *that*-clause, not a *to*-verb.
9. **so much** — *data* is uncountable → *so much* (countable plural would take *so many*).
10. **far more ambitious** — *ambitious* is long → *more*; boost with *far* (never *very* before a comparative, never the double *more ambitiouser*).

## 7. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**O que já deve estar sólido.** A semana toda gira em torno de *descrever e comparar*. A
regra mãe: **adjetivo descreve substantivo** (*a **quick** decision*), **advérbio descreve
verbo** (*decide **quickly***) — exceto depois de *be, seem, look, feel, sound*, onde volta
o adjetivo porque ele fala do sujeito, não da ação (*the plan **looks solid***). Para
*so / such*: olhe o que vem **depois** — adjetivo sozinho → *so*; substantivo (com *a/an*)
→ *such*. Para *too / enough*: são vereditos opostos contra um limite — *enough* = "dá pra
seguir", *too* = "virou problema". Comparativo: palavra curta → *-er*, longa → *more*,
nunca os dois, e sempre com *than*.

**As armadilhas que mais pegam brasileiro.** (1) *hardly* **não** é "duramente" — é *quase
não*; e *lately* **não** é "atrasado" — é *recentemente*. Trocar inverte a frase. (2) Dizer
*"so a risk"* — o *a* sempre exige *such* (*such a risk*). (3) Posição de *enough*: depois
do adjetivo (*senior enough*), antes do substantivo (*enough runway*) — a ordem **inverte**.
(4) O comparativo dobrado (*more cheaper*, *very bigger*) é o deslize mais audível: escolha
*-er* **ou** *more*, nunca ambos, e use *much / far* (não *very*) para reforçar.
