### 1. 📘 Unit 061 — myself / yourself / themselves (reflexive pronouns) & each other

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** Who's presenting the AI roadmap to the board — you or the team?
> **You:** I'll present it **myself**. But the team built the model **themselves**, so I want them
> in the room.
> **CEO:** Good. And the target — is it realistic?
> **You:** We set **ourselves** a stretch target on purpose. The board committed **itself** to the
> strategy last quarter, so we owe them ambition.
> **CEO:** One thing — the CFO and I don't always agree with **each other** on the pace.
> **You:** Understood. I'll talk to both of you before we align.

Notice two different jobs here. *"I'll present it **myself**"* just **emphasises** *I* — nobody
else, me personally. But *"we set **ourselves** a target"* means the action **loops back**: *we*
did it **to us**. And *"agree with **each other**"* is different again — it's *A ↔ B*, back and
forth between two people. Portuguese covers all of this with *me / se / nos* and *um ao outro*, and
sprinkles those little pronouns onto far more verbs than English does. That mismatch is today's
lesson.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

A **reflexive pronoun** is used when the **subject and the object are the same person/thing** — the
action bounces back onto the doer.

| Subject | Reflexive |
|---|---|
| I | **myself** |
| you (one) | **yourself** |
| you (plural) | **yourselves** |
| he | **himself** |
| she | **herself** |
| it | **itself** |
| we | **ourselves** |
| they | **themselves** |

**Key facts:**
- **Reflexive (bounce-back):** subject = object. *"I hurt **myself**."* / *"The board committed
  **itself**."* The doer and the receiver are one.
- **Emphatic (for emphasis):** *"I'll do it **myself**"* = *I* personally, no one else. You could
  **remove** it and the sentence still works — it just adds stress. Position: right after the
  subject, or at the end.
- **by + myself** = **alone / without help.** *"She built the model **by herself**."*
- **each other** = **reciprocal** — two (or more) people doing something **to one another**, A ↔ B.
  *"They trust **each other**."* This is **not** a reflexive: *"They trust **themselves**"* means
  something completely different (each trusts *only* their own self).
- Note the spelling split: singular ends in **-self** (myself, yourself, himself), plural ends in
  **-selves** (ourselves, yourselves, themselves). *"yourself"* = one person; *"yourselves"* = a
  group.

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why do reflexives exist?** English needs a way to say the action **turned back on the doer** —
*I* did something **to me**. Plain *me* can't do that job next to its own subject (*"I hurt me"* is
wrong), so English uses **myself**. It also borrows the same word for **emphasis** (*I'll do it
myself*). The trap for Levi isn't the form — it's **overuse**: Portuguese marks dozens of ordinary
verbs with a reflexive pronoun (*me sinto, nos reunimos, se concentra, se prepara*) where English
uses **no reflexive at all**.

**Decision rule — three rival ideas. Ask what the action actually does:**

| The action… | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| loops back onto the same subject (subject = object) | **-self / -selves** | *"The board committed **itself**."* |
| just needs emphasis ("me personally / no one else") | **-self / -selves** (removable) | *"I'll present it **myself**."* |
| goes **back and forth between two+ people** (A ↔ B) | **each other** | *"They challenge **each other**."* |
| is a normal action with a normal object (not the subject) | **plain verb / object pronoun** | *"I feel confident."* / *"We meet on Fridays."* |

Minimal pairs — the choice changes the meaning completely:
- *"The partners help **each other**."* = partner A helps B, B helps A (reciprocal). ✅
- *"The partners help **themselves**."* = each one helps **their own** self (and/or *serve
  themselves* — as at a buffet). Different sentence entirely.
- *"I'll present it **myself**."* (emphatic — *me*, personally) vs *"I hurt **myself**."* (reflexive
  — the action lands on me). Same word, two jobs.

**PT→EN mapping — where the reflexive betrays Levi.** Portuguese uses *me / se / nos* on many verbs
that are **not** reflexive in English. Translating them straight produces a wrong extra word:
- *"eu **me** sinto confiante"* → ✅ *"I **feel** confident"* — **not** *"I feel myself confident."*
- *"nós **nos** reunimos toda sexta"* → ✅ *"we **meet** every Friday"* — **not** *"we meet
  ourselves."*
- *"ele **se** concentra / **se** prepara"* → ✅ *"he **concentrates** / **gets ready**"* — no
  *himself.*
- *"vamos **nos** alinhar"* → ✅ *"let's **align**"* / *"let's get aligned"* — no *ourselves.*

And the reverse trap: Portuguese *um ao outro* (reciprocal) sometimes gets lost, and Levi says
*themselves* where English needs **each other**.

**Top typical Brazilian-speaker errors:**

1. ❌ *"I **feel myself** confident about the roadmap."* / *"We **met ourselves** yesterday."* → ✅
   *"I **feel** confident…"* / *"We **met** yesterday."* — *sentir-se, reunir-se* carry a reflexive
   in Portuguese; the matching English verbs don't.
2. ❌ *"The two teams don't trust **themselves**."* (meaning they don't trust one another) → ✅ *"The
   two teams don't trust **each other**."* — reciprocal A ↔ B is **each other**, never *themselves*.
3. ❌ *"I built the deck **by myself**"* when you mean *"I built it **myself**"* — both exist but
   differ: *by myself* = **alone/without help**; bare *myself* = **emphasis** (I personally, though
   others may have helped). Pick the one you actually mean.

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. *"I'll present the AI roadmap to the board **myself**."*
   *— emphatic: me personally, not a delegate. Removable ("I'll present it") — it just adds stress.*
2. *"The board committed **itself** to the AI strategy last quarter."*
   *— true reflexive: the board (subject) commits the board (object). "itself" because a board is one it.*
3. *"We set **ourselves** an aggressive adoption target on purpose."*
   *— reflexive: we set the target for us. "ourselves", not "us", because subject = object.*
4. *"The CFO and I don't always agree with **each other** on the pace."*
   *— reciprocal A ↔ B (me ↔ CFO). "agree with themselves" would be nonsense here.*
5. *"The engineering team built the pilot **themselves**, with no outside vendor."*
   *— emphatic (they, personally / on their own). "by themselves" would sharpen it to "alone/unaided".*
6. *"Great leaders push **each other** to think bigger, not just their own teams."*
   *— reciprocal between leaders; "push themselves" would mean each pushes only their own self — a different point.*
7. *"Be careful — don't overcommit **yourself** before the budget is approved."*
   *— reflexive: you commit you. "yourself" (one person). To a group it'd be "yourselves".*
8. *"I taught **myself** to read financial models years ago, before any of this."*
   *— everyday-ish reflexive: I taught me. Note "I feel confident" would take NO reflexive — the trap.*

1. Ask your tutor to run a **"drop the reflexive" drill**: they fire Portuguese pronominal verbs

   (*the CEO and CFO, two business units, you and the board chair*) and you say whether the action
   is **reciprocal** (*they challenge each other*) or **reflexive** (*the unit reorganised itself*).
   Land the emphatic *myself* too: *"I'll defend the AI budget myself in front of the board."*

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 060 — the possessive 's):**
1. *"**The board's** decision."* Owner-first with **'s**, because the board is a **group of people**;
   *"the decision of the board"* is grammatical but heavier/more formal — the *'s* version is what a
   native uses in a live meeting.
2. **investors'** — *"the **investors'** feedback"* (apostrophe **after** the -s, plural owner).
   *"investor's"* would mean **one** investor's feedback. The apostrophe position is the whole
   difference.
3. **this quarter's results** — a **time** expression takes *'s*; *"the results of this quarter"* is
   the direct-from-*deste-trimestre* interference error English avoids.

**Spaced review:**
- *(Unit 054 — have)* *"Let's **have** a call"* is the **action-have** — it can go continuous
  (*"we're having a call"*) and **cannot** take *have got*. *"We **have** a strong AI team"* is
  **possession** — it **can** take *have got* (*"we've got a strong team"*) and **cannot** go *-ing*.
- *(Unit 031 — can and could)* both blanks are **could**. *"Last year we **couldn't** even run these
  models"* is the **past** of *can* (ability then). *"**Could** you give me two more engineers?"* is
  **not past at all** — it's a **polite request** happening now. Same word, two jobs.

**Recognition drill:**
1. **each other** — *"…have always backed **each other**"* (co-founder A backs B, B backs A —
   reciprocal). *"backed **themselves**"* would mean each backed **only their own** self, which isn't
   the mutual support you meant.
2. Both wrong → ✅ *"In every board meeting I **feel** calm, and the team **concentrates** on the
   numbers."* — *sentir-se* and *concentrar-se* carry a reflexive in Portuguese; the English verbs
   *feel* and *concentrate* take **none**.
3. **myself** — *"I reviewed the whole model **myself**"* = emphasis (I, personally; others may have
   helped). *"**by myself**"* would add **alone / with no help** — a stronger, different claim.
4. **align** — *"We should **align** before the offsite."* *Alinhar-se* is reflexive in Portuguese,
   but English *align* here works with **no reflexive** (*"align ourselves"* sounds forced/physical,
   like arranging objects in a row). Same trap as *feel* and *concentrate*.

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

Os pronomes reflexivos (**myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves,
themselves**) fazem dois trabalhos. (1) **Reflexivo de verdade**: a ação volta para o próprio
sujeito — sujeito = objeto — *"the board committed **itself**"*, *"we set **ourselves** a target"*,
*"I taught **myself**"*. (2) **Ênfase**: *"I'll present it **myself**"* = eu, pessoalmente, ninguém
mais (dá pra tirar a palavra e a frase continua de pé). Além disso, **by myself** = *sozinho / sem
ajuda*, e **each other** é **recíproco** (A ↔ B): *"they trust **each other**"* (um confia no outro)
— nunca confunda com *"they trust themselves"* (cada um confia em si mesmo). Singular termina em
**-self**, plural em **-selves**.

A grande armadilha é o **excesso**, porque o português marca muito mais verbos com *me / se / nos*
do que o inglês. *"Eu **me** sinto confiante"* → *"I **feel** confident"* (sem *myself*); *"nós
**nos** reunimos"* → *"we **meet**"*; *"ele **se** concentra / **se** prepara"* → *"he
**concentrates** / **gets ready**"*; *"vamos **nos** alinhar"* → *"let's **align**"*. Traduzir o
reflexivo direto gera o erro *"I feel myself confident"*. E cuidado com o inverso: quando a ação é
**um ao outro** entre duas pessoas, é **each other**, não *themselves*. Se a diferença entre sujeito
e objeto (*I/me, he/him*) ainda embolar, releia `lessons/2026-09-09-unit-059.md`.
