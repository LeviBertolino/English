### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CFO:** I tried to reach you at four yesterday.
> **You:** Sorry — at four I **was presenting** the AI roadmap to the board. While
> I **was walking** them through the budget, the CEO **was taking** notes the whole
> time.
> **CFO:** And the pushback?
> **You:** It came later. When the questions **started**, I **was already wrapping
> up** — so we handled them in the hallway.

Notice the shape before any rule. Yesterday's past (Unit 011–012) reported
**finished** events: *it came*, *the questions started*. Here a new past appears —
**was/were + -ing** — and it does something different: it freezes a past moment and
shows an action **in progress**, still unrolling, not yet finished: *I **was
presenting***, *the CEO **was taking** notes*. The short *started* lands **inside**
that longer, ongoing *was wrapping up*.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

The past continuous is **was / were + the -ing form** of the verb.

| Subject | was/were | verb-ing | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| I / he / she / it | **was** | presenting | *I **was presenting** at four.* |
| you / we / they | **were** | presenting | *They **were presenting** at four.* |

| | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive** | subject + **was/were** + **-ing** | *We **were reviewing** the numbers.* |
| **Negative** | subject + **wasn't/weren't** + **-ing** | *We **weren't reviewing** them yet.* |
| **Question** | **Was/Were** + subject + **-ing**? | ***Were** you **reviewing** them?* |
| **Short answer** | Yes, … **was/were** / No, … **wasn't/weren't** | *Yes, I **was**. / No, we **weren't**.* |

**Key facts:**
- **was** goes with **I / he / she / it**; **were** goes with **you / we / they**.
  This is the one agreement to get right.
- **The main verb is always -ing** — never a second past form: *was **going*** (not
  *was went*), *was **taking*** (not *was took*).
- **Negatives and questions stay on was/were** — you do **not** bring in *did* here.
  *Were you presenting?* ✅ / *Did you presenting?* ❌.
- **Spelling of -ing:** *make → making* (drop mute -e), *run → running* (double the
  final consonant), *plan → planning*.

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why does this structure exist?** The past simple gives you a **finished** event
seen as a whole dot on the timeline (*I presented*). The past continuous gives you
that same event **from the inside, mid-action** — a line, not a dot. It says: at
this past moment, the action was **already underway and not yet over**. Nothing the
simple past can do carries that "in progress, unfinished" meaning.

It shows up in three typical situations:
- **An action in progress at a specific past time:** *At 4 p.m. I **was
  presenting**.* (I started before 4 and finished after 4 — 4 catches me in the
  middle.)
- **A longer action interrupted by a shorter one:** *While I **was presenting**,
  the CFO **called**.* The long background is continuous; the sudden event is
  simple.
- **Two long actions happening in parallel:** *While the CEO **was taking** notes,
  I **was walking** them through the budget.*

**Decision rule vs the rival — past continuous vs past simple.** This is the whole
game. Ask: do I mean the action was **in progress** (unfinished, a background), or
that it **happened and completed**?

| | Past continuous (*was/were + -ing*) | Past simple (*-ed / irregular*) |
|---|---|---|
| Shows | action **in progress**, unfinished | action **completed**, whole |
| Timeline | a line — the middle of it | a dot — the end of it |
| Typical cue | *at 4 p.m., while, when X happened* | *yesterday, then, at 4 p.m. it happened* |
| Role in a story | the **background** | the **event** |

Minimal pairs — same words, different meaning:
- *When the CEO **arrived**, I **presented** the roadmap.* → he arrived **first**,
  **then** I started. Two finished events in sequence.
- *When the CEO **arrived**, I **was presenting** the roadmap.* → I had **already
  started**; his arrival caught me **mid-presentation**. Background + interruption.

- *We **discussed** the AI budget for an hour.* → the whole discussion, seen as one
  finished block.
- *At noon we **were discussing** the AI budget.* → noon caught us **in the
  middle** of it; we're inside the action.

**PT→EN mapping.** Good news: Portuguese has an almost exact match — **estava +
gerúndio** (*"eu estava apresentando"* = *"I was presenting"*). Map *estava/estavam*
→ *was/were* and the *-ndo* ending → *-ing*, and you're usually right. Two traps,
though:
- **The Portuguese imperfect (*"eu apresentava"*) is ambiguous** — it can mean a
  past **habit** ("I used to present") **or** an action **in progress**. English
  splits these: *used to / would* for the habit, *was presenting* only for the
  action in progress. Don't reach for *was presenting* to say "I regularly
  presented".
- **State verbs don't go continuous** — same as in the present. *Know, want, need,
  have (=possess), agree, believe* describe states, not actions in progress:
  *"I **knew** the answer"* ✅, never *"I was knowing"* ❌ — even though Portuguese
  won't stop you (*"eu estava sabendo"* exists colloquially).

**Top Brazilian-speaker errors:**
- ❌ *When the CFO called, I **presented** to the board.* → ✅ *When the CFO called,
  I **was presenting** to the board.* — *the presentation was already **in
  progress** when the call interrupted it; the simple past would wrongly mean I
  started **after** the call.*
- ❌ *The directors **was** reviewing the portfolio.* → ✅ *The directors **were**
  reviewing the portfolio.* — *plural subject → **were**; matching was/were to the
  subject is the one agreement this tense demands.*
- ❌ *At that time I **was knowing** the risks.* → ✅ *At that time I **knew** the
  risks.* — *"know" is a state verb; states use the simple past, not the
  continuous, even where Portuguese tolerates the -ndo form.*

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. At four o'clock yesterday I **was presenting** the AI roadmap to the board. —
   *a specific past time (4:00) catches the action **in progress** → continuous, not
   the finished "I presented".*
2. While I **was walking** the board through the budget, the CEO **was taking**
   notes. — *two long parallel actions → both continuous; neither is a single
   finished event.*
3. When the tough questions **started**, we **were already wrapping up**. — *short
   finished event ("started", simple past) lands inside the longer background
   ("were wrapping up", continuous).*
4. The vendor **called** while we **were finalising** the investment case. — *the
   ongoing work is the background (continuous); the call is the interrupting event
   (simple).*
5. This time last year we **weren't investing** in generative AI at all. — *negative
   continuous describing the state-of-play in progress at a past period; "weren't",
   plural "we".*
6. **Were** the directors **pushing back** on the numbers, or did they agree? —
   *question form stays on "were" + -ing — never "did … pushing".*
7. I **knew** the board wasn't ready, so I **held** the vote. — *"know" is a state →
   simple past "knew", not "was knowing"; the decision "held" is also a finished
   event.*
8. When you rang me last night, I **was putting** the kids to bed. — *everyday life;
   the ongoing bedtime routine (continuous) interrupted by the finished "rang"
   (simple).*

### 8. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

- Ask your tutor to **narrate an interruption drill**: they name a sudden event
  (*"The CEO walked in", "The vendor called", "The fire alarm went off"*) and you
  answer with what you **were doing** at that moment — *"When the CEO walked in, I
  **was reviewing** the AI budget."* This drills the background-continuous +
  interrupting-simple pattern until it's reflexive.
- Then a **"where were you at 4 p.m.?" scenario**: your tutor picks past times
  across your day and you describe what **was happening** in the office at each one
  (*"At nine we **were finalising** the board deck; by eleven the team **was
  running** the numbers"*), so the "in-progress at a past moment" meaning stops
  competing with the plain past.

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 012)**
1. ❌ *"Did the board approved…"* → ✅ **"Did the board approve the AI budget?"** —
   *"did" already carries the past, so the main verb returns to base "approve";
   the past lives on the auxiliary, never twice.*
2. ✅ **"We didn't approve the vendor contract."** — *English negates the past with
   "didn't + base verb", not by placing a bare "not" before the verb ("we not
   approved" is Portuguese interference).*
3. **"usually the board does approve"** / **"but did they approve this one
   yesterday?"** — *"usually" = a present habit → "does"; "yesterday" = a finished
   past event → "did". The base verb "approve" stays the same; only the auxiliary
   moves to mark the time.*

**Spaced review**
- **(Unit 006)** ❌ *"He doesn't approves…"* → ✅ **"He doesn't approve
  early-stage bets."** — *the -s already lives on "doesn't"; the main verb returns
  to its base form. Never mark the negative twice.*

**Recognition drill**
1. ❌ *"The two directors was…"* → ✅ **"The two directors were reviewing the
   portfolio at noon."** — *plural subject → "were"; "was" only goes with
   I/he/she/it.*
2. ❌ *"Were you presented…"* → ✅ **"Were you presenting when the CFO called?"** —
   *the past continuous needs the -ing form after was/were; "presented" is a past
   simple and can't follow "were" here.*
3. ❌ *"we were wanting…"* → ✅ **"At that time we wanted a clearer business
   case."** — *"want" is a state verb → simple past "wanted", not the continuous.*
4. **"When the CEO arrived, I was presenting the roadmap."** — *"was presenting"
   shows the action was **already in progress** when he arrived. "I presented"
   would mean I started **after** he arrived — two events in sequence, not an
   interruption.*

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**A regra e quando usar.** O *past continuous* é **was/were + verbo-ing** e mostra
uma ação **em andamento** num momento passado — uma linha, não um ponto. Use
*was* com I/he/she/it e *were* com you/we/they. Ele serve para três situações: uma
ação em progresso numa hora específica (*"At four I **was presenting**"*), uma ação
longa **interrompida** por uma curta (*"While I **was presenting**, the CFO
**called**"* — o fundo é contínuo, o evento é simples), e duas ações longas em
paralelo. Contraste sempre com o *past simple* (Unidades 11–12): o simples mostra o
evento **terminado** (um ponto: *"I presented"*), o contínuo mostra o **meio** dele
(*"I was presenting"*). A boa notícia: o português tem um equivalente quase exato —
**estava + gerúndio** (*"eu estava apresentando"* = *"I was presenting"*).

**As armadilhas.** (1) Não use o contínuo para **hábitos** passados: o imperfeito
português (*"eu apresentava"*) pode significar hábito **ou** ação em progresso, mas
o inglês separa — hábito é *used to / would*, progresso é *was presenting*. (2)
**Verbos de estado** (know, want, need, have=possuir, agree) **não** vão para o
contínuo: é *"I **knew**"*, nunca *"I was knowing"*, mesmo que o português tolere o
*-ndo*. (3) Acerte o *was/were* com o sujeito (plural → *were*) e lembre que
negativas e perguntas ficam em *was/were + -ing* — **nunca** entra *did* aqui:
*"Were you presenting?"*, não *"Did you presenting?"*.
