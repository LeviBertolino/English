### 1. 📘 Unit 033 — there is / there are

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** How does the AI portfolio look going into the board meeting?
> **You:** Solid. **There's** one flagship bet and **there are** four smaller
> pilots behind it. **There isn't** a single project without a clear owner now.
> **CEO:** Any blockers I should know about?
> **You:** **There's** a budget gap on the data platform, and **there are** two
> vendor contracts still unsigned. **Is there** anything the board flagged last
> time we haven't closed?
> **You:** No — **there aren't** any open items from last quarter.

Notice the pattern before any rule. When you **introduce** that something
**exists** — a bet, four pilots, a gap, two contracts — English opens with
*there is / there are*, choosing *is* or *are* by what comes **right after**:
*there's a gap* (one), *there are two contracts* (more than one). Portuguese does
this with *há* or, in speech, *tem* (*"tem uma reunião amanhã"*) — and that *tem*
is the trap, because its English shadow is **not** *have*.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

Use *there* + *be* to say that something **exists** or is **present** somewhere.
The verb agrees with the noun that **follows** it, not with *there*.

| Meaning | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| One thing / uncountable | **there is (there's)** + singular | *There's **a budget gap**.* |
| More than one thing | **there are** + plural | *There are **two contracts** open.* |
| Nothing (singular) | **there isn't** / there's no | *There **isn't** a clear owner.* |
| Nothing (plural) | **there aren't** / there are no | *There **aren't** any blockers.* |
| Yes/no question | **Is there…? / Are there…?** | *Are there **any risks** left?* |
| Counting question | **How many … are there?** | *How many pilots **are there**?* |

- *there's* is the everyday contraction of *there is*. *there are* has **no**
  common written contraction — write *there are*, not *there're*.
- Agreement follows the **first** noun after the verb: *There's a gap and two
  contracts* (spoken) but carefully written it's *There **are** two contracts and
  a gap* / *There **is** a gap and there **are** two contracts*.
- *any* is the natural partner in negatives and questions: *there aren't **any**
  blockers*, *are there **any** risks?*
- Past forms (*there was / there were*) come in Unit 37 — today is the present.

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why does *there is / there are* exist?** English needs a way to **announce that
something exists** without first naming *who has it* or *where exactly* it sits.
*"There's a risk"* puts the risk on the table as **new information**. It's the
sentence you use to **introduce** something for the first time; once it's on the
table, you switch to *it / they* to talk about it: *"There's a budget gap. **It**
sits on the data platform."*

**Decision rule #1 — *there is* vs *it is* (existence vs identification).** This
is the confusion that matters most. *there is* says **something exists**; *it is*
**points at** or **describes** something already known.

| *there is* = announces existence | *it is* = identifies / describes |
|---|---|
| *"**There's** a problem with the forecast."* → a problem exists (new) | *"**It's** a problem with the forecast."* → naming *what* the known issue is |
| *"**There are** three risks in the plan."* → three risks exist | *"**They're** the three risks the board flagged."* → pointing at known ones |
| First mention, brings it into view | Second mention, refers back |

Rule of thumb: if you could start with *"Existe / Existem…"* or *"Há…"*, you want
*there is / there are*. If you're pointing at something already in view (*"É
o…", "Ele é…"*), you want *it is / they are*.

**Decision rule #2 — *is* or *are*? Look right, not left.** The verb agrees with
the noun **after** it, never with *there*:
- *"There **is** one flagship bet."* (one → *is*)
- *"There **are** four pilots."* (four → *are*)
- Uncountables are singular: *"There **is** strong interest from the board"* (not
  *are*), *"There **is** a lot of work left."*

**PT→EN mapping — the two big interference errors.**
- Colloquial Portuguese **"tem"** for existence → *there is / there are*, **never
  *have***. *"Tem uma reunião amanhã"* = *"**There's** a meeting tomorrow,"* not
  *"Has a meeting tomorrow"* / *"Have a meeting tomorrow."* This is the single
  most common Brazilian slip.
- Formal **"há / existe(m)"** → *there is / there are*. *"Há dois contratos em
  aberto"* = *"**There are** two contracts open."*
- Careful with agreement: Portuguese *"tem/há"* never changes for plural, so the
  reflex is to freeze on *there is*. English **must** switch to *there are* the
  moment the noun is plural.

**Top Brazilian-speaker errors:**
- ❌ *"Have a budget gap on the platform."* → ✅ *"**There's** a budget gap on the
  platform."* — existence is *there is*, not *have*.
- ❌ *"There is two contracts still open."* → ✅ *"There **are** two contracts
  still open."* — plural noun after → *there are*.
- ❌ *"It has a risk in this plan"* / *"It's a risk in this plan"* (to introduce
  it) → ✅ *"**There's** a risk in this plan."* — first mention of something that
  exists takes *there is*, not *it has / it is*.

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. *"**There's** a strong case for making AI a board-level priority."*
   *— Announces that the case exists (new information); "it's a strong case" would wrongly assume the case is already known.*
2. *"**There are** four pilots behind the flagship bet."*
   *— Plural noun (four pilots) → there are; agreement looks right, not at "there".*
3. *"**There isn't** a single project without a clear owner now."*
   *— Negative existence, singular; "there isn't" = há/existe in the negative, not "doesn't have".*
4. *"**Are there** any risks the board flagged that we haven't closed?"*
   *— Yes/no question about existence; "any" is the natural partner in questions.*
5. *"**There's** strong interest from the C-suite in an AI-first strategy."*
   *— Uncountable (interest) counts as singular → there is, never "there are interest".*
6. *"How many vendors **are there** on the shortlist?"*
   *— Counting question; the verb sits at the end, agrees with the plural "vendors".*
7. *"**There's** a meeting with the CFO tomorrow to defend the budget."*
   *— Classic "tem uma reunião" → there's a meeting, NOT "has/have a meeting".*
8. *"**There are** no open items from last quarter."*
   *— "there are no" = an emphatic plural negative; equivalent to "there aren't any".*

### 8. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

- Ask your tutor to run a **"tem → there is/are" reflex drill**: they throw
  Portuguese existence lines at you fast (*"tem uma reunião amanhã," "há dois
  contratos abertos," "tem muito trabalho pela frente"*) and you snap the English
  back, forcing *there is / there are* and killing the *have* interference — with
  a plural in the mix each round so you also train the *is → are* switch.
- Then a **board-opener drill**: role-play the first 30 seconds of a portfolio
  update where you must **introduce** three new items (*"there's a budget gap,"
  "there are two unsigned contracts," "there's strong C-suite interest"*) and only
  then describe each with *it / they* — practising the *there is → it is* handoff
  that keeps an executive update sounding native.

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 032 — must, mustn't, don't need to):**
1. ***mustn't*** — *"You **mustn't** share these numbers outside the board."*
   Sharing is **forbidden**. Pick *don't need to* and you'd say sharing is merely
   *optional* — the opposite message.
2. **Drop *to*** — *"I **must align** the roadmap with finance this week."* *must*
   is a modal followed by the **base verb**; *must to* is never correct.
3. ***doesn't need to join*** — "not necessary" for a third person takes *doesn't
   need to* + base. *mustn't join* would **forbid** it; *doesn't must* is
   impossible (*must* never takes *do/does*).

**Spaced review:**
- **(Unit 026)** The offsite takes **present simple** — *"The offsite **starts**
  at nine"* — because it's fixed by a **timetable/clock**. Your pitch takes
  **present continuous** — *"I**'m presenting** on Thursday"* — because it's a
  **personal arrangement** you decided.
- **(Unit 005)** *"Each squad **owns** one bet and the lead **reports** the
  metrics."* A single *he/she/it*-type subject adds **-s**; *we/you/they* keep the
  base verb, so no *-s* there.

**Recognition drill:**
1. ❌ *Have a budget gap* → ✅ *"**There's** a budget gap on the data platform we
   need to close."* — existence is *there is*, never *have* (the *"tem"* trap).
2. ***are*** — *"There **are** two vendor contracts and one compliance sign-off
   still pending."* The verb agrees with the **first noun after it**, the plural
   *two contracts*, so *are*.
3. ***There's*** — *"**There's** a problem with the Q3 forecast."* You're
   **introducing** it (new information). *It's a problem…* would wrongly assume
   the problem is **already known** and you're just describing it.
4. **No owner exists at all.** *"There **isn't** a clear owner"* denies the
   **existence** of an owner. To say one exists but is unclear you'd say *"The
   owner **isn't** clear"* — a different sentence entirely.

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**A regra e quando usar.** *there is / there are* serve para **anunciar que algo
existe** — equivalem ao *há / existe(m)* e ao *tem* coloquial do português:
*"Há uma reunião amanhã"* → *"**There's** a meeting tomorrow."* Use quando você
**apresenta** algo pela primeira vez (*"there's a risk," "there are two
contracts"*); depois que já está na mesa, troque para *it / they* para
comentá-lo. A concordância olha para o **substantivo que vem depois**, nunca para
*there*: singular/incontável → *there is*; plural → *there are*.

**As armadilhas.** (1) A cilada nº 1 é traduzir *"tem"* como *have*: *"Tem um
problema no plano"* é *"**There's** a problem in the plan,"* **jamais** *"Have/Has
a problem."* (2) O português *tem/há* não muda no plural, então o reflexo é
travar em *there is* — mas em inglês você **precisa** mudar para *there are* assim
que o substantivo é plural (*"there **are** two contracts"*). (3) Não confunda
*there is* (existência, primeira menção) com *it is* (identificar/descrever algo
já conhecido). Se a diferença *must* vs *there is* nas obrigações embolar, releia
`lessons/2026-08-13-unit-032.md`.
