### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **You:** We're aligned on going AI-first, **aren't we?**
> **CEO:** We are. But the board hasn't signed off on the budget yet, **has it?**
> **You:** Not yet. You've seen the revised numbers, though, **haven't you?**
> **CEO:** I have. They're stronger than Q2.
> **You:** So we should bring it back to the board this month, **shouldn't we?**
> **CEO:** Let's do that. Nothing's blocking us now, **is there?**

Notice the pattern before any rule. Every sentence ends with a tiny two-word
question hooked onto a statement: *aren't we?*, *has it?*, *haven't you?*,
*shouldn't we?*, *is there?*. Each little tag **flips** the statement in front of
it — a positive statement gets a negative tag, a negative statement gets a positive
one — and each one reuses the statement's own helping verb. You're not really
asking; you're **inviting agreement** and keeping the C-level in the conversation.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

A question tag is a mini-question added to the end of a statement. Build it from
**two pieces**: the right **auxiliary** + the matching **pronoun**. Two iron rules:

1. **Reverse the polarity.** Positive statement → **negative** tag. Negative
   statement → **positive** tag.
2. **Echo the auxiliary.** The tag repeats the statement's auxiliary (*is, are,
   was, have, will, should, can*…). If the statement has **no** auxiliary (present
   or past simple), the tag borrows **do / does / did**.

| Statement | Tag | Full sentence |
|---|---|---|
| Positive, has auxiliary | negative auxiliary + pronoun | *We **should** move now, **shouldn't we**?* |
| Negative, has auxiliary | positive auxiliary + pronoun | *The board **hasn't** signed off, **has it**?* |
| Positive, no auxiliary (simple) | *don't / doesn't / didn't* + pronoun | *You **saw** the numbers, **didn't you**?* |
| Negative, no auxiliary | *do / does / did* + pronoun | *He **doesn't** report to you, **does he**?* |

**Special cases worth memorising:**
- **I am** → tag is **aren't I?** *"I'm on the agenda, **aren't I?**"* (never *amn't I*).
- **Let's…** → tag is **shall we?** *"Let's take it to the board, **shall we?**"*
- **Imperative** → tag is **will you? / won't you? / could you?** *"Send the deck,
  **will you?**"*
- **there is / are** → the tag keeps *there*: *"There's still time, **isn't there?**"*
- **this / that / nothing / everything** → the pronoun in the tag is **it**:
  *"That's the real risk, **isn't it?**"*; *"Nothing's blocking us, **is there?**"*
  (*nothing* = negative, so the tag is positive).

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why does this structure exist?** A bare statement asserts; a statement **+ tag**
does something a plain statement can't: it hands the floor to the other person
while signalling what you expect. *"We're aligned on this"* closes the door.
*"We're aligned on this, **aren't we?**"* opens it — it checks agreement, softens a
strong claim, and pulls the CEO or board into the conversation without you losing
your position. For a leader, tags are a **register tool**: they make you sound
collaborative and confident at once, which is why executives lean on them in the
room.

**Decision rule #1 — falling tag (I expect a "yes") vs rising tag (I really don't
know).** Same words, different **intonation**, opposite meaning:
- **Falling** tag (voice goes down) = *I'm confident; agree with me.* Not a real
  question — you're seeking confirmation. *"This is the right call, **shouldn't we**
  do it?"* → you expect a yes.
- **Rising** tag (voice goes up) = *I'm genuinely unsure; is that true?* A real
  question. *"The board approved it, **didn't they?**"* (rising) → you're checking
  because you're not certain.
Decision rule: seeking agreement / rapport → **falling**; genuinely asking →
**rising**. In executive talk, the **falling** tag is the workhorse.

**Decision rule #2 — the polarity flip (the mechanical trap).** The tag must be the
**opposite sign** of the statement. Getting this backwards is the #1 form error:
- ✅ *"We're on track, **aren't we?**"* (positive → negative)  ❌ *"We're on track,
  **are we?**"* (both positive — sounds sarcastic/aggressive, a different meaning)
- ✅ *"It **isn't** final, **is it?**"* (negative → positive)  ❌ *"It isn't final,
  **isn't it?**"* (both negative — not English)
And the tag **echoes the auxiliary**, never invents a new one: *"You **can** join,
**can't you?**"* (not *don't you?*); *"She **has** decided, **hasn't she?**"* (not
*doesn't she?*).

**PT→EN mapping — this is where Portuguese betrays Levi the most.** Brazilian
Portuguese has **one invariant tag** — *né? / não é? / certo? / não?* — that you
staple onto everything. English has **no single tag**: it forces you to **rebuild
the auxiliary and flip the polarity every time**. So the direct translation of
*né?* is a trap:
- *"Estamos alinhados, **né?**"* → ✅ *"We're aligned, **aren't we?**"*, ❌ *"We're
  aligned, **no?**"* / *"…, **isn't it?**"* (wrong pronoun *and* wrong verb).
- *"Você viu os números, **né?**"* → ✅ *"You saw the numbers, **didn't you?**"*, ❌
  *"You saw the numbers, **right?**"* is understandable but flat/informal, and *"…,
  **no?**"* is not English.
- *"O board não aprovou, **né?**"* → ✅ *"The board didn't approve it, **did they?**"*
The reflex to build: the moment you'd say *né?* in Portuguese, **stop** and ask
*"which auxiliary was in my statement, and what's its opposite?"*

**Top Brazilian-speaker errors:**
- ❌ *"We should move now, **no?**"* / *"…, **isn't it?**"* → ✅ *"We should move now,
  **shouldn't we?**"* — English tags are **not** invariant; rebuild the auxiliary
  (*should → shouldn't*) and match the pronoun (*we*), never a frozen *no? / isn't
  it?* copied from *né?*.
- ❌ *"You've seen the deck, **have you?**"* → ✅ *"You've seen the deck, **haven't
  you?**"* — a positive statement needs a **negative** tag; *"have you?"* here
  reverses the meaning (sounds like surprised suspicion).
- ❌ *"The team finished the pilot, **didn't the team?**"* → ✅ *"…, **didn't
  they?**"* — the tag always uses a **pronoun**, never a repeated noun.

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. *"We're aligned on going AI-first, **aren't we?**"*
   *— Positive "we're" → negative tag "aren't we"; a falling tag here checks agreement without reopening the decision. "…, no?" (né?) would be pure Portuguese.*
2. *"The board hasn't approved the AI budget yet, **has it?**"*
   *— Negative "hasn't" → positive tag; echo the auxiliary "has", and "the budget/board" becomes the pronoun "it". Not "hasn't it" (double negative) and not "doesn't it".*
3. *"You've seen the revised numbers, **haven't you?**"*
   *— Perfect "you've" → tag reuses "have": "haven't you". A falling tag = "I'm sure you have"; you're confirming, not really asking.*
4. *"We should bring this back to the board this month, **shouldn't we?**"*
   *— Modal "should" → "shouldn't we"; the tag turns a recommendation into a shared decision — exactly the collaborative register you want with the CEO.*
5. *"The team delivered the pilot on time, **didn't they?**"*
   *— No auxiliary in "delivered" (past simple) → borrow "did" → "didn't they". Pronoun "they" for the team; never "didn't the team".*
6. *"Let's take the vision deck to the offsite, **shall we?**"*
   *— "Let's…" is the one case where the tag is "shall we?", proposing jointly rather than instructing.*
7. *"That's the real risk in rushing the vendor choice, **isn't it?**"*
   *— "That" → pronoun "it" in the tag; positive "that's" → "isn't it". Here "isn't it?" is correct precisely because the statement's verb is "is".*
8. *"You couldn't send me the CFO's figures by five, **could you?**"*
   *— Everyday-ish ask: negative statement "couldn't" → positive tag "could you"; a rising tag makes it a soft, polite request rather than a demand.*

### 8. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

- Ask your tutor to run a **"kill the *né?*" drill**: they feed you flat statements
  from your world — *"We're going AI-first," "The board approved the budget," "You
  haven't seen the forecast," "The pilot slipped a week"* — and you must add the
  correct English tag out loud, rebuilding the auxiliary and flipping the polarity
  each time (*"…, aren't we?" / "…, didn't they?" / "…, have you?" / "…, didn't
  it?"*). Goal: replace the single Portuguese *né?* with the right auxiliary reflex.
- Then a **falling-vs-rising intonation round**: your tutor gives you a scenario —
  *"You're confident the CEO agrees"* vs *"You genuinely don't know if the board
  approved"* — and you say the **same** tagged sentence twice, once with a falling
  tag (seeking agreement) and once rising (real question). This locks in that the
  meaning lives in the melody, not just the words.

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 039 — It… the empty subject):**
1. **Add *It's* at the start** → *"**It's** important to move now on the AI budget."*
   English requires a subject before the verb; Portuguese *"É importante…"* hides it,
   English can't drop it.
2. ***There's*** a lot of uncertainty (uncertainty **exists** — you're introducing
   it) vs ***It's*** hard to forecast (**describing** the act of forecasting, empty
   *It* + adjective + *to*). *There* = something exists; *It* = what it's like /
   empty subject.
3. **Two changes** → *"**It takes** about six months to migrate the platform."* Add
   the empty subject *It*, and change *take* → *takes* (third person after *it*).

**Spaced review:**
- **(Unit 033)** ***there are*** — *"**There are** four pilots behind the flagship
  bet."* The noun is **plural** (*four pilots*), so the verb agrees: *there are*, not
  *there is*.
- **(Unit 012)** ***didn't approve*** — *"The board **didn't approve** the budget
  last quarter."* The past sits on the helper **didn't**; after *didn't* the main
  verb goes back to its **base form** *approve* — *"didn't approved"* would double
  the past.

**Recognition drill:**
1. **Should be *aren't we?*** → *"We're on track for Q3, **aren't we?**"* A positive
   statement takes a **negative** tag. *"…, are we?"* (both positive) reads as
   sceptical or challenging — *"oh, are we really?"* — the opposite of seeking
   agreement.
2. ***"We're aligned on the roadmap, aren't we?"*** — positive *"we're"* → negative
   *aren't* + pronoun *we*. *"…, no?"* is a raw copy of *né?* and isn't English;
   *"…, isn't it?"* uses the wrong pronoun (*it* for *we*) and the wrong verb.
3. ***didn't he?*** — *"The CFO signed off…, **didn't he?**"* *Signed* is past simple
   with **no auxiliary**, so the tag borrows **did** → *didn't*. *Doesn't he?* is
   present (wrong tense); *hasn't he?* would need a perfect statement (*has signed*).
4. ***"Let's move the review to Thursday, shall we?"*** and ***"I'm still on the
   agenda, aren't I?"*** — two fixed exceptions: **Let's** always tags with **shall
   we?**, and **I am** tags with **aren't I?** (English has no *amn't I*).

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**A regra e quando usar.** Uma *question tag* é uma mini-pergunta colada no fim de
uma afirmação — o equivalente funcional do nosso *né? / não é?*. Mas, diferente do
português, o inglês **não tem uma tag única**: você monta a tag toda vez com duas
peças — o **auxiliar certo** + o **pronome**. Duas regras de ferro: (1) **inverta o
sinal** — afirmação positiva pede tag **negativa** (*"We're aligned, **aren't
we?**"*), afirmação negativa pede tag **positiva** (*"It isn't final, **is it?**"*);
(2) **repita o auxiliar** da frase (*is, have, should, can*…), e se a frase não tiver
auxiliar (present/past simple), use **do / does / did** (*"You saw it, **didn't
you?**"*). Serve para **buscar concordância, suavizar** uma afirmação forte e
**trazer o CEO/board para a conversa** sem você perder a posição — soa colaborativo
e seguro ao mesmo tempo. A entonação decide o sentido: voz **caindo** = "concorde
comigo" (o padrão executivo); voz **subindo** = pergunta de verdade.

**As armadilhas.** (1) A cilada nº 1 é traduzir *né?* por uma tag fixa: *"…, no?"* e
*"…, isn't it?"* colados em tudo estão **errados** — o inglês exige reconstruir o
auxiliar e o pronome a cada frase. (2) **Polaridade invertida**: positivo → tag
negativa, negativo → tag positiva; repetir o mesmo sinal (*"We're on track, are
we?"*) muda o sentido para desconfiança/ironia. (3) O tag usa **pronome**, nunca o
substantivo repetido (*"…, didn't they?"*, não *"…, didn't the team?"*). (4) Decore
as exceções: **Let's → shall we?** e **I am → aren't I?**. Se a mecânica do auxiliar
ainda embolar, releia `lessons/2026-07-24-unit-012.md` (do/does/did em perguntas e
negativas) — é o mesmo auxiliar que reaparece nas tags.
