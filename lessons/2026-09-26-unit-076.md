### 1. 📘 Unit 076 — -ing and -ed clauses (reduced relative clauses)

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** Walk me through who owns what on the AI programme.
> **You:** The exec **leading delivery** is Priya. The budget **approved by the board** covers phase
> one only. The vendor **shortlisted last week** ships in Q1.
> **CEO:** And the risks?
> **You:** The main risk **flagged in the review** is talent — the engineers **building the model**
> are three people **stretched across two products**.
> **CFO:** So the plan **presented on Monday** was optimistic.
> **You:** The version **shown to the board**, yes. The revised one is tighter.

Look at the bold parts. Not one of them uses *who* or *that* — yet each is doing exactly the job of a
relative clause. *The exec **leading** delivery* means *the exec **who is leading** delivery*. *The
budget **approved** by the board* means *the budget **that was approved** by the board*. English lets
you **drop the *who/that* + verb** and keep just the *-ing* or *-ed* form. That's the whole unit — and
notice the split: **-ing** when the noun is **doing** the action, **-ed** when the action is **done to**
it.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

These are **shortened relative clauses**. Take a full relative clause, delete the pronoun and the
*be/is/was*, and you're left with a participle:

| Full relative clause | Reduced clause | Which form |
|---|---|---|
| the exec **who is leading** delivery | the exec **leading** delivery | **-ing** (active) |
| the team **that is building** the model | the team **building** the model | **-ing** (active) |
| the budget **that was approved** by the board | the budget **approved** by the board | **-ed** (passive) |
| the risk **that was flagged** in the review | the risk **flagged** in the review | **-ed** (passive) |

**Key facts:**
- **-ing = active** (the noun **does** the action): *the woman **talking** to the CEO* = the woman
  **who is talking**. Use it when the noun is the **doer**.
- **-ed = passive** (the action is **done to** the noun): *the plan **presented** on Monday* = the plan
  **that was presented**. Use it when the noun **receives** the action. (Irregular verbs use their past
  participle: *the model **built** in Q1*, *the decision **taken** last week*.)
- **The word right after the noun tells the listener everything.** *…**building*** → this noun is
  working; *…**built*** → something was done to this noun. One letter of difference, opposite meaning.
- **It replaces both defining and non-defining clauses:** *The engineers **building the model** are
  stretched* (defining) · *Priya, **leading delivery**, will present* (with commas, non-defining).
- **-ing can also mean "at the same time / because":** *"**Feeling** the room turn, I paused the pitch"*
  = *because I felt / as I felt*. Same *-ing*, now opening the sentence.

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (a seção central)

**Why does this structure exist?** Speed and weight. A relative clause is precise but heavy: *"the
budget **that was approved** by the board"* has five words before you reach the point. The reduced
version — *"the budget **approved** by the board"* — carries the identical meaning in two. In a board
deck, a strategy memo, or a fast verbal update, this is how fluent English **compresses** description
onto a noun without a string of *who is / that was*. It's not slang or shortcut English; it's what
polished writing does by default.

**Decision rule — the whole game is one question: is the noun DOING it, or is it DONE TO?**

| The noun… | Form | Example | Full version |
|---|---|---|---|
| **does** the action (active) | **-ing** | the team **building** the model | …that **is building** |
| **receives** the action (passive) | **-ed** | the model **built** by the team | …that **was built** |
| does something **at that moment / as a cause** | **-ing** (sentence-front) | **Facing** a flat budget, we cut two bets | Because we faced… |

Minimal pair — same noun, one letter flips the whole meaning:
- *"The consultant **advising** the board is expensive."* → the consultant **gives** advice (active).
- *"The board **advised** by the consultant is cautious."* → the board **receives** advice (passive).

Get the form wrong and you reverse who's doing what to whom.

**PT→EN mapping — where this helps Levi and where it trips him.**
- The **-ing = active** side maps cleanly onto the Portuguese **gerúndio**: *"o executivo **liderando**
  a entrega"* → *"the exec **leading** delivery."* Trust that instinct — the active case transfers.
- The trap is the **passive -ed** side. Portuguese uses the **particípio** (*aprovado, decidido,
  contratado*), and Levi sometimes reaches for *-ing* by reflex: ❌ *"the budget **approving** by the
  board."* If the noun **receives** the action, English needs the **-ed / past participle**, never
  *-ing*: ✅ *"the budget **approved** by the board."*
- Second trap: keeping the full *"that is / that was"* everywhere because Portuguese keeps *"que está /
  que foi."* It's not wrong, but it sounds heavy and non-native. Dropping it is the upgrade.

**Top 3 typical Brazilian-speaker errors:**
- ❌ *"The strategy **deciding** by the board is bold."* → ✅ *"The strategy **decided** by the board is
  bold."* *(the strategy is done to → passive → -ed, not -ing.)*
- ❌ *"The engineers **built** the model are overloaded."* (looks like a full past-tense verb) → ✅ *"The
  engineers **building** the model are overloaded."* *(the engineers are the doers → active → -ing.)*
- ❌ *"The plan **that was presented** by me, **that was reviewed** by the CFO, is ready."* (chain of
  full clauses) → ✅ *"The plan **presented** by me and **reviewed** by the CFO is ready."* *(reduce
  both; it reads as executive English, not a transcript.)*

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. *"The exec **leading** delivery is Priya."*
   *— Priya's team is doing the leading → active → -ing; = "the exec who is leading."*
2. *"The budget **approved by the board** covers phase one only."*
   *— the budget receives the approval → passive → -ed; = "the budget that was approved."*
3. *"The engineers **building the model** are stretched across two products."*
   *— the engineers do the building → active → -ing; note the plural verb "are" still agrees with "engineers."*
4. *"The main risk **flagged in the review** is talent."*
   *— the risk was flagged (done to it) → passive → -ed; contrast with #3's active -ing.*
5. *"We should retire the pilots **draining** the innovation budget."*
   *— the pilots are doing the draining → active → -ing; replaces "that are draining."*
6. *"The version **shown to the board** was optimistic; the revised one is tighter."*
   *— the version was shown (passive) → -ed; "shown" is the irregular participle of "show," not "showed."*
7. *"**Facing** a flat budget, we cut two bets and doubled down on one."*
   *— sentence-front -ing = "because we faced / as we faced"; the -ing clause gives the reason.*
8. *"The neighbour **renovating** the flat next door starts drilling at 7 a.m."*
   *— everyday life: the neighbour does the renovating → active → -ing; = "who is renovating."*

### 8. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

1. Ask your tutor to run a **doer-vs-done-to drill**: they name a noun plus an action (*"the board /
   approve the budget," "the team / build the model," "the CFO / challenge the plan"*) and you must
   produce **both** reduced clauses aloud — the active one (*"the board **approving** the budget"*) and
   the passive one (*"the budget **approved** by the board"*) — saying who does what in each. This kills
   the *-ing*-for-passive reflex, which is the one error that reverses your meaning.
2. Role-play a **60-second portfolio walkthrough for the board**: describe four or five bets using a
   reduced clause on each named item instead of full *who/that* clauses (*"the pilot **draining** the
   budget," "the platform **shortlisted** last week," "the risk **flagged** in the review," "the exec
   **leading** delivery"*). Have the tutor stop you every time you say *that is / that was* where a bare
   participle would sound tighter — the goal is to hear the compressed version as your default.

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 075):**
1. *"My colleagues **who work in AI** back the plan"* (**no** commas) implies **some don't** — the
   clause selects a subset (only the AI ones). *"My colleagues**,** who work in AI**,** back the plan"*
   (commas) = **all** back it, and by the way they all work in AI. The commas turn *"which ones"* into
   *"extra fact."*
2. Banned word: **that**. It's an extra-information clause (you have one CEO, already identified), and
   after a comma English forbids *that* → *"Our CEO, **who** set the vision, is stepping down."* Only
   *who / which / whose / where* after a comma, and you can't drop the pronoun.
3. **which** — *"…beat every target, **which** nobody expected."* It points at the **whole event** (the
   pilot beating every target), not at one noun. A clause commenting on the whole sentence takes
   *which*, never *what*.

**Spaced review:**
- **Unit 069:** **nor** → *"**Neither** the price **nor** the tech separates them."* *neither* pairs
   with *nor*. If you start with *either*, the partner is *or*: *"**Either** the price **or** the tech
   could be the dealbreaker."*
- **Unit 044:** **to give** → *"I want you **to give** me the mandate."* The pattern is *verb + object
   + to + base verb* (*want you to, told him to, expect us to*). The Portuguese *que* pushes you to
   ❌ *"I want that you give"* — English has no *that* here; it uses *to* + verb.

**Recognition drill:**
1. **taken** — *"the decision **taken** by the board last week."* The deciding question: *does the
   decision do the action, or receive it?* A decision is **made/taken** (it receives the action) →
   passive → past participle. *"taking"* would mean the decision is doing something, which is nonsense
   here.
2. Wrong: *advised* reads as a **full past-tense verb** ("the consultant advised the board" = a complete
   sentence), so the reader loses the thread. You mean the consultant who **gives** advice → active →
   **-ing**: *"The consultant **advising** the board is very expensive."*
3. *"the roadmap **presented** on Monday."* The two words that disappear are **that + was** (*that was
   presented* → *presented*). Passive meaning, so the participle stays *presented* (-ed), not *-ing*.
4. *"the board **advising** the CEO"* = the board **gives** advice (active, -ing). *"the board **advised**
   by the CEO"* = the board **receives** advice from the CEO (passive, -ed). Same noun, opposite
   direction — the participle form alone decides who advises whom.

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

Uma oração relativa pode ser **encurtada** para um particípio, sem *who/that* e sem *is/was*. A regra é
só uma pergunta: **o substantivo FAZ a ação ou RECEBE a ação?** Se ele **faz** (ativo), use **-ing**:
*"the exec **leading** delivery"* (= o executivo que está liderando). Se ele **recebe** (passivo), use
**-ed / particípio**: *"the budget **approved** by the board"* (= o orçamento que foi aprovado). Serve
tanto para orações restritivas (*"the engineers **building** the model"*) quanto explicativas (*"Priya,
**leading** delivery, …"*). O *-ing* no começo da frase também vale por *"porque / ao"*: *"**Facing** a
flat budget, we cut two bets."*

As ciladas do português: (1) o lado **ativo** bate com o gerúndio (*liderando → leading*), então confie
nele; (2) o lado **passivo** é a armadilha — o português usa o particípio (*aprovado, decidido*), mas
você às vezes coloca *-ing* por reflexo: ❌ *"the budget **approving** by the board"* → ✅ *"the budget
**approved** by the board."* Se recebe a ação, é **-ed**, nunca *-ing*. (3) evite encadear *"that is /
that was"* em tudo (o *"que está / que foi"* do português) — reduzir para o particípio é o que faz seu
inglês soar executivo e não uma transcrição. Se a base das orações relativas com vírgula ainda embolar,
releia `lessons/2026-09-25-unit-075.md`.
