### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** Can you send me the AI investment deck before the board call?
> **You:** I'll **send it to you** tonight. I also owe the CFO the updated figures — I'll **get them to her**
> first thing.
> **CEO:** Good. And **show the board the three scenarios**, not just the recommended one.
> **You:** Will do. I'll **bring them a one-page summary** too, so nobody drowns in detail.
> **CFO:** Can you **lend me an analyst** for a day? I want to pressure-test the numbers.
> **You:** Of course — I'll **give you two**. Just **give me the questions** in advance and I'll **pass them to
> the team**.

Count the little hand-offs. Almost every verb here moves a **thing** to a **person**: *send me the deck*, *show
the board the scenarios*, *lend me an analyst*, *give you two*. Notice there are **two shapes** doing the same
job — *"send **me the deck**"* (person first) and *"send **it to you**"* (thing first, then *to*). They mean the
same, but watch the switch: the moment the thing becomes a pronoun — *it, them* — English stops saying *"send me
it"* and insists on *"send **it to** me."* That one rule is the whole unit.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

Some verbs carry **two objects**: a **person** (who gets it) and a **thing** (what they get). You can order them
two ways — *and they mean the same*:

| Shape | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **A — person first** | verb + **person** + **thing** | *Send **me the deck**.* · *I gave **the board my recommendation**.* |
| **B — thing first** | verb + **thing** + **to / for** + **person** | *Send **the deck to me**.* · *I gave **my recommendation to the board**.* |

**The verbs that do this** (and which preposition they take in shape B):

| → **to** (hand it over) | → **for** (do it on someone's behalf) |
|---|---|
| give, send, lend, show, offer, pay, tell, bring, hand, pass, owe, teach | buy, get, make, find, cook, build, save |

**The one hard rule — pronoun things take shape B.** When the **thing** is a pronoun (*it / them*), you **must**
put it first with *to / for*:

- ✅ *Send **it to me**.* — ❌ *Send **me it**.*
- ✅ *I gave **them to the board**.* — ❌ *I gave **the board them**.*
- ✅ *I'll **get them for you**.* — ❌ *I'll **get you them**.*

(When the thing is a full noun, both shapes are fine: *"give me the deck"* = *"give the deck to me."*)

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why do two shapes exist?** English puts the **most important / newest** information last. If the surprise is
*who* gets it, lead with the thing and end on the person — *"I'll send the deck **to the CEO**, not the board."*
If the surprise is *what* they get, lead with the person — *"I'll send the CEO **the full model**, not just the
summary."* Having two orders lets you steer the listener's attention without changing the words.

**Decision rule — the pronoun test.** This is the one that actually trips people:

| Is the **thing** a pronoun (*it / them*)? | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **No** (a real noun: *the deck, the figures*) | either shape | *Give me the deck* **=** *Give the deck to me* |
| **Yes** (*it, them*) | **only shape B** (thing + *to/for*) | *Give **it to me*** (never *give me it*) |

So the default you can always fall back on is **shape B** — *"send it to me," "show them to the board," "get it
for her."* It is correct with pronouns **and** with nouns. If in doubt, say the thing first with *to / for*.

**PT→EN mapping — where Portuguese sets the trap:**
- Portuguese loves the clitic: *"Me manda o relatório"*, *"Me dá isso."* That *me* glued to the front pushes you
  toward *"**send me it**"* / *"**give me it**"* — exactly the shape English forbids with a pronoun. ✅ *"Send
  **it to me**," "give **it to me**."*
- *"Manda ele pra mim"* maps cleanly to shape B (*send it to me*) — so when the thing is a pronoun, **trust the
  *pra mim / pra ele* version**, not the clitic version.
- Beware the **false two-object verbs**. Portuguese *explicar*, *sugerir*, *descrever* take a person like a
  recipient (*"me explica o plano"*), but English *explain / suggest / describe* **never** take the person first —
  they always need *to + person*: ✅ *"explain the plan **to me**," "suggest a date **to the board**."*

**Top Brazilian-speaker errors:**
1. ❌ *"Can you send me it?"* → ✅ *"Can you send **it to me**?"* A pronoun thing (*it*) must go first with *to*.
   This is the single most common slip — the Portuguese clitic *me* fights the English rule.
2. ❌ *"Explain me the strategy."* / *"He suggested me a vendor."* → ✅ *"**Explain** the strategy **to me**." /
   "He **suggested** a vendor **to me**."* *Explain, suggest, describe* are **not** give-type verbs; the person
   always comes after *to*.
3. ❌ *"I'll buy you it"* / *"get you them"* → ✅ *"I'll **buy it for you**," "get **them for you**."* Same
   pronoun rule, but these verbs take **for** (doing it on someone's behalf), not *to*.

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. **Send me the AI investment deck before the call.**
   *— shape A (person + thing). The thing is a full noun (*the deck*), so person-first is fine; this foregrounds
   the urgency of the request. Rival shape: "send the deck to me" — same meaning.*
2. **I'll send it to you tonight.**
   *— the thing is now the pronoun *it*, so **only** shape B works: *it* + *to you*. ❌ "I'll send you it" is the
   classic error. This is the sentence to burn in.*
3. **Show the board the three scenarios, not just the recommended one.**
   *— shape A. The contrast ("not just…") lands on the **thing**, so ending on the thing keeps the emphasis there.
   *Show* takes *to* in shape B: "show the scenarios to the board."*
4. **I'll get them to her first thing.**
   *— pronoun thing (*them*) → shape B, forced. Note *get* here means "deliver / hand over," so it takes **to**,
   not *for*.*
5. **Can you lend me an analyst for a day?**
   *— shape A with a noun thing. "for a day" is a time phrase, not the *for + person* of shape B — don't confuse
   them. Rival: "lend an analyst to me."*
6. **Just give me the questions and I'll pass them to the team.**
   *— two moves in one line: *give me the questions* (noun → shape A fine) then *pass them to the team* (pronoun
   *them* → shape B forced). Watch the switch happen mid-sentence.*
7. **I'll get you a coffee — or should I get it for you decaf?** *(everyday)*
   *— *get* = "buy/fetch on your behalf" → **for**, not *to*. Noun thing (*a coffee*) allows shape A ("get you a
   coffee"); pronoun thing (*it*) forces shape B ("get it **for** you").*
8. **Can you explain the plan to me before Monday?** *(work)*
   *— *explain* is a **false** two-object verb: ❌ "explain me the plan." The person always rides *to*. Same for
   *suggest* and *describe*.*

### 8. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

- Ask your tutor to run a **"swap the shape" drill**: they give you a sentence in shape A (*"Send the CEO the
  figures"*) and you must instantly produce shape B (*"Send the figures to the CEO"*), then they replace the
  thing with a pronoun (*"…and now with 'them'"*) so you're forced into *"Send them to the CEO"* — never *"send
  the CEO them."* Loop until the pronoun version is automatic.
- Then a **hand-off role-play**: play out a board-prep exchange where you're delegating — *send me / I'll send it
  to you / get them to the CFO / lend me an analyst / I'll get you two*. Ask the tutor to throw in *explain* and
  *suggest* traps so you practise forcing those onto *to + person* under light pressure.

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 090):**
1. ✅ *"We're **still** reviewing the risk section"* (continues now — middle slot) and *"The board hasn't decided
   **yet**"* (not-done-but-expected — **end**). *still* = ongoing; *yet* = waiting, and it's the one at the end.
2. ✅ *"Have you signed **yet**?"* is the neutral question. Portuguese *já* in a question maps to **yet**, not
   *already*. *"Have you signed already?"* accidentally implies surprise — *"signed **so soon**?!"*
3. ✅ *"We don't use that vendor **any more**."* (or *"We **no longer** use that vendor."*) *yet* = still waiting
   to **start**; but the situation has **stopped / changed** (*dropped it last quarter*) → *any more / no longer*.

**Spaced review:**
- **Unit 084 (comparatives):** ✅ *"the vendor's model is **more scalable than** anything we'd ship"* (long
   adjective → **more** + *than*) and *"the second scenario is **riskier**"* (ends in *-y* → drop *y*, add
   *-ier*). The word gluing the two sides is always **than** — never *that*, never *of*.
- **Unit 061 (reflexives & *each other*):** ✅ *"I'll present it **myself**"* (emphasis = *-self* word) and
   *"the CFO and I don't always agree with **each other**"* (A ↔ B between two people = *each other*, not a
   *-self* word).

**Recognition drill:**
1. **(b)** *"I'll send **it to you** now."* The thing is the pronoun *it*, so it must come first with *to* —
   **shape B is forced**. ❌ "send you it" is the error.
2. ✅ *"I gave **my recommendation to the board**."* Both shapes mean the same, but the *to*-version ends on **the
   board**, so it subtly emphasises **who** received it (e.g., *the board*, not the CEO alone); shape A (*"gave
   the board my recommendation"*) ends on — and foregrounds — **what** you gave.
3. ✅ *"The consultant **explained** the roadmap **to us** and **suggested** a vendor **to me**."* *explain* and
   *suggest* are **false** two-object verbs — the person **never** comes first; it always follows **to**.
4. ✅ *"Can you lend **it to me** for the meeting? I'll get **them for you** by Friday."* Two fixes: pronoun things
   (*it, them*) must take shape B — *it **to** me* (*lend* → *to*) and *them **for** you* (*get* = fetch on your
   behalf → *for*). ❌ "lend me it" and "get you them" are both the forbidden person-first-with-pronoun order.

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

Vários verbos em inglês carregam **dois objetos** — uma **pessoa** (quem recebe) e uma **coisa** (o que recebe) —
e há **duas ordens** com o mesmo sentido: *"send **me the deck**"* (pessoa primeiro) ou *"send **the deck to
me**"* (coisa primeiro + *to/for*). Verbos de "entregar" pedem **to** (*give, send, show, lend, tell, pay, bring,
pass, owe*); verbos de "fazer em nome de alguém" pedem **for** (*buy, get, make, find*). A escolha entre as duas
ordens serve para colocar no fim a informação mais importante — *quem* recebe, ou *o quê*.

A regra que realmente pega: quando a **coisa é um pronome** (*it / them*), **só** vale a segunda ordem — *"send
**it to me**," "give **it to me**," "get **them for you**"* — nunca *"send me it."* É aí que o português atrapalha:
o clítico *"me manda isso"* / *"me dá isso"* empurra para o errado *"send me it"*; confie na versão *"manda pra
mim"* → *"send it to me."* E cuidado com os **falsos** verbos de dois objetos: *explicar, sugerir, descrever* em
inglês (*explain, suggest, describe*) **nunca** levam a pessoa na frente — sempre *to + pessoa*: ✅ *"explain the
plan **to me**," "suggest a vendor **to me**."* Regra de bolso: na dúvida, diga a **coisa primeiro com *to / for***
— funciona sempre.
