### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** How do you want to frame us to the board next week?
> **You:** As **an** AI-first company — not **a** vendor that bolts AI on at the end. That's **a**
> completely different story.
> **CEO:** And the new hire everyone's talking about?
> **You:** She's **a** strategist, not **an** engineer. She brings **an** unusual mix of finance and
> tech, which is exactly what we need.
> **CEO:** Good. Give me **an** hour with the numbers and **a** one-page summary before Friday.

Notice two quiet things. First, English forces a little word — *a* or *an* — in front of a single
countable thing, even where Portuguese happily drops it: *"She's **a** strategist"* (not *"She's
strategist"*). Second, the choice between *a* and *an* is decided by **sound**, not spelling: *"**an**
hour"* (the *h* is silent, so it starts with a vowel sound) but *"**a** one-page summary"* (*one*
starts with a *w* sound). That's today's whole lesson.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

*a* / *an* = the **indefinite article** — it goes before a **singular countable noun** that is **not
specific / not already known** (one of many, mentioned for the first time). It literally comes from
*one*.

| Rule | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| before a **consonant sound** | **a** | *a* company · *a* CEO · *a* university · *a* one-off |
| before a **vowel sound** | **an** | *an* investor · *an* hour · *an* MBA · *an* AI model |
| naming a **job / role** | **a / an** (required!) | *"She's **a** consultant."* / *"He's **an** analyst."* |
| = **one** (counting) | **a / an** | *"Give me **an** hour."* / *"in **a** week"* |
| in an exclamation | **What a…!** | *"**What a** bold vision!"* |

**Key facts:**
- The pick is by **sound**, not the written letter. Silent *h* → vowel sound → *an* (*an hour, an
  honest answer*). Letters that are written as vowels but **sound** like a consonant → *a* (*a
  university* = "a **you**-niversity", *a European partner*, *a one-day offsite*).
- **Acronyms**: say the letter out loud. *M* is said "**em**" (vowel sound) → *an* MBA, *an* MVP,
  *an* HR issue. *C* is "**see**", *U* is "**you**" → *a* CEO, *a* URL, *a* UX team.
- *a / an* is for **singular countable** nouns only. Never before a **plural** (*"~~an
  investors~~"*) or an **uncountable** (*"~~an advice~~"*, *"~~an information~~"*).

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why does *a/an* exist?** It marks a noun as **one, and not yet identified** — *some* member of a
class, brought up for the first time. *"We need **a** roadmap"* = some roadmap, any one, first
mention. Compare *the*: *"We need **the** roadmap"* = the specific one we already know about. English
refuses to leave a singular countable noun **bare** — it must carry *a/an*, *the*, or a word like
*my/this/one*. Portuguese is far more relaxed about dropping the article, and that gap is where Levi
loses points.

**Decision rule — three seams to keep straight:**

| The situation… | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| a **job / role** after *be* (or *as*) | **a / an** — obligatory | *"She's **a** strategist."* / *"as **a** company"* |
| **one specific** thing, already known to both of you | **the** (not a/an) | *"Send me **the** deck"* (the one we discussed) |
| a **plural** or an **uncountable** noun, general | **no article** | *"We hire **strategists**."* / *"We need **funding**."* |
| a single, **first-mentioned / non-specific** countable thing | **a / an** | *"We're building **a** platform."* |

Minimal pairs — the choice changes the meaning:
- *"We need **a** plan."* = any plan, we don't have one yet (first mention). vs *"We need **the**
  plan."* = the specific plan we already agreed on. First-mention → *a*; shared/known → *the*.
- *"She's **an** engineer."* (one of the class — her profession) vs *"She's **the** engineer."* (the
  particular one we're waiting for). Role/first mention → *a/an*.
- *"**a** university"* (consonant *y*-sound) vs *"**an** hour"* (silent *h*, vowel sound) — same
  logic, opposite result, purely by ear.

**PT→EN mapping — where the article betrays Levi.** Portuguese **drops** the article in exactly the
places English **requires** it:
- *"Ela é **estrategista**."* → ✅ *"She's **a** strategist."* — **not** *"She's strategist."*
  Professions/roles **always** take *a/an* in English.
- *"Como **empresa**, precisamos…"* → ✅ *"**As a** company, we need…"* — **not** *"As company…"*.
- *"Ele tem **visão** de longo prazo."* (abstract, no article in PT) → here it's countable and
  specific enough to say *"He has **a** long-term view."*
- And the reverse: Portuguese *um/uma* before a **plural-feeling** or uncountable idea tempts a wrong
  *a*: *"~~an information~~"*, *"~~a feedback~~"* → ✅ *"**some** information"*, *"**some** feedback"*
  (uncountable — no *a*).

**Top typical Brazilian-speaker errors:**

1. ❌ *"He is **CFO** of the group."* / *"I work **as consultant**."* → ✅ *"He is **the** CFO…"* /
   *"I work **as a** consultant."* — English never leaves a singular job noun bare (*the* if it's a
   unique role like CFO, *a/an* otherwise).
2. ❌ *"That's **a** great news."* / *"Can you give me **an** advice?"* → ✅ *"That's **great
   news**."* / *"Can you give me **some advice** / **a piece of advice**?"* — *news, advice,
   information, feedback, research* are **uncountable**; no *a/an*.
3. ❌ *"We need **a** MVP by Q3."* / *"It'll take **a** hour."* → ✅ *"We need **an** MVP…"* / *"It'll
   take **an** hour."* — decide by **sound**: *M* = "em" and silent *h* both start with a vowel
   sound, so *an*.

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. *"We want to position ourselves as **an** AI-first company, not **a** follower."*
   *— role/category after "as"; obligatory a/an. "an" before the vowel sound of AI ("ay"), "a" before "follower".*
2. *"She's **a** strategist with **an** MBA and **a** strong finance background."*
   *— profession takes a; "an MBA" because M sounds "em" (vowel), "a strong" (consonant). All by ear.*
3. *"Give me **an** hour and I'll have **a** one-page summary ready."*
   *— "an hour" (silent h → vowel sound); "a one-page" (one = "wun", consonant sound). Spelling lies; sound decides.*
4. *"The board hasn't approved **a** budget for the pilot yet."*
   *— first mention, non-specific: some budget, not one we've already named. Later it becomes "the budget".*
5. *"As **a** leader, you set the tone for how the company treats risk."*
   *— "as a leader" — English demands the article; PT "como líder" drops it, which is the trap.*
6. *"That's **a** completely different story from the vendor pitch."*
   *— a + adjective + singular noun; the article still sits at the front, before the adjective.*
7. *"He gave us **some** useful feedback, not just **a** vague opinion."*
   *— "feedback" is uncountable → some, no "a"; "opinion" is countable → "a". The contrast is the whole point.*
8. *"What **a** turnaround — six months ago no one believed in the AI bet."*
   *— exclamation pattern "What a…!" with a singular countable noun. Everyday register, fully idiomatic.*

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 061 — reflexives & each other):**
1. **itself** — *"The board committed **itself**."* A board is treated as a single *it*, so the
   reflexive is *itself*, not *themselves*. (Subject = object → reflexive.)
2. **No reflexive** → ✅ *"I **feel** confident."* *Sentir-se* carries *se* in Portuguese, but the
   English verb *feel* takes **none** — *"I feel myself confident"* is the classic overuse error.
3. **each other** — *"…don't always agree with **each other**."* Reciprocal, A ↔ B (CEO ↔ CFO).
   *"agree with themselves"* would mean each agrees with their own self — not the mutual sense you
   wanted.

**Spaced review:**
- *(Unit 055 — phrasal verbs)* particle: *"roll **out** the new AI policy."* With a **pronoun**, it
  **must split**: ✅ *"roll **it** out"*, never *"~~roll out it~~"*. Rule: a pronoun object goes
  **between** the verb and the particle (a full-noun object can go either side).
- *(Unit 039 — empty It)* both blanks are **It**: *"**It** takes six months…"* and *"**It**'s
  important to align the board."* English needs a **subject** in every sentence, so it fills the slot
  with an empty *it* (there's no real "thing" doing the action). Portuguese can drop it (*"É
  importante…", "Leva seis meses…"*); English cannot.

**Recognition drill:**
1. **an hour** / **a one-page brief.** *hour* has a **silent h** → starts with a vowel sound → *an*.
   *one-page* starts with a *w* sound ("wun") → consonant sound → *a*. Decide by **sound**, not the
   letter.
2. Both wrong → ✅ *"She works **as a** strategist, and before that she was **an** engineer at a
   startup."* Professions/roles after *be* or *as* **require** *a/an* in English; PT drops it (*"como
   estrategista", "era engenheira"*).
3. **No article** → ✅ *"…gave us **valuable information**"* (or *"**some** valuable information"*).
   *information* is **uncountable** — never *"an information"* or *"a valuable information."*
4. **a new platform** (first mention, non-specific) → then **The platform** (now it's the specific
   one we just introduced). First time it's new → *a*; once it's shared/known → *the*.

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

*a / an* é o **artigo indefinido** — vai antes de um **substantivo contável no singular** que é
**novo / não específico** (uma coisa qualquer, mencionada pela primeira vez): *"we need **a**
roadmap"*. Contraste com *the*, que é a coisa **específica que os dois já conhecem**. A escolha entre
*a* e *an* é pelo **som**, não pela letra: *an hour* (o *h* é mudo → som de vogal), mas *a university*
e *a one-page summary* (som de "iu" e "u" = consoante). Em siglas, fale a letra: *an MBA* ("em"), mas
*a CEO* ("cí"). Só use *a/an* com contável **singular** — nunca com plural nem com incontável.

A grande armadilha para brasileiro é **omitir** o artigo onde o inglês **exige**, porque o português
solta: *"Ela é estrategista"* → *"She's **a** strategist"*; *"como empresa"* → *"**as a** company"*;
*"ele é CFO"* → *"he's **the** CFO"*. Profissão/papel depois de *be* ou *as* **sempre** pede artigo. E
o erro inverso: colocar *a/an* em incontável — *"~~an information~~", "~~a feedback~~", "~~a news~~"*
→ o certo é *some information / some feedback / good news* (sem *a*). Se a diferença entre *a* e *the*
(primeira menção × coisa já conhecida) ainda embolar, ela volta com força nas próximas unidades sobre
*the*.
