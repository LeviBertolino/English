### 1. 📘 Unit 098 — relative clauses 2: *whose · where · when* + defining vs non-defining

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** Who's actually behind the turnaround in the data team?
> **You:** That's Rafael — the leader **whose team shipped the first model that made money**. He joined us in the quarter **when we pivoted to AI-first**.
> **CEO:** And the market you keep flagging?
> **You:** Southeast Asia — **the region where our only serious competitor is still weak**. That's the opening.
> **CFO:** Remind me how the budget cleared so fast last spring.
> **You:** The board**, which rarely moves quickly,** approved it in one meeting. And our Head of Strategy**, who built the entire case,** is the reason it landed.

Two new tools are doing quiet work. **whose** replaces *his/her/its/their* (*the leader **whose team** shipped* = the leader, **his team** shipped); **where** and **when** point at a place and a time (*the region **where**…*, *the quarter **when**…*). Then look at the last two clauses — *the board**, which rarely moves,*** and *our Head of Strategy**, who built the case.*** They sit **between commas**, and they don't tell you *which* board or *which* person (you already know). They just add extra information. That comma, and what it changes, is the heart of today's unit.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

**Part A — three new relative words:**

| Word | Replaces / means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **whose** | possessive *his / her / its / their* | *the exec **whose** team shipped first* (= her team) |
| **where** | *in / at* a **place** | *the market **where** we win* |
| **when** | *at / in* a **time** | *the quarter **when** we pivoted* |

**Part B — the big distinction: defining vs non-defining.** Same-looking clauses, opposite jobs:

| | **Defining** (identifying) | **Non-defining** (extra info) |
|---|---|---|
| **Commas?** | ❌ no commas | ✅ a **pair** of commas (or comma + full stop) |
| **Job** | tells you *which one* | adds a detail to an **already-identified** noun |
| **Can you use *that*?** | ✅ yes | ❌ **never** — only *who / which / whose / where* |
| **Can you drop the pronoun?** | ✅ yes, if it's the object | ❌ **never** |
| **Remove the clause and…** | the sentence loses *which one* — breaks | the sentence still makes full sense |

*The directors **who opposed the plan** resigned.* → defining: **only** the ones who opposed resigned.
*The directors**, who opposed the plan,** resigned.* → non-defining: **all** the directors resigned — and, by the way, they all opposed. **The commas change the facts, not just the style.**

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why do these structures exist?**
*whose* saves you from a clumsy restart: instead of *"the startup — its founder just left —"* you say *"the startup **whose founder** just left."* *where / when* let you pin a noun to a place or moment in one move. And the **defining / non-defining** split exists to answer a precise question the listener always has: *are you telling me **which** one, or just adding colour?* For an executive this is not decoration — it's the difference between *"The VPs who missed targets were replaced"* (some VPs — the ones who missed) and *"The VPs, who missed targets, were replaced"* (**all** the VPs were replaced). Same words, different headcount.

**Decision rule — defining vs non-defining (the rival pair):**
Ask: **if I delete the clause, does the sentence still identify the noun?**
- **Still clear which one → non-defining → use commas, and only *who / which* (never *that*, never dropped).**
  *Our CFO**, who joined in 2020,** backs the spend.* (we have one CFO; the clause is extra)
- **Needs the clause to know which one → defining → no commas, *that* is fine, droppable if object.**
  *The analyst **(who) we hired last month** owns this model.* (which analyst? the one we hired — essential)

Quick tell: if the noun is **already unique** — a named person, *our CEO*, *the board*, *this company* — the clause is almost always **non-defining** (commas). You're adding, not selecting.

**PT→EN mapping:**
- **whose = *cujo / cuja***. Brazilians avoid *cujo* in speech and smuggle in a possessive: *"a empresa **que o** CEO **dela** saiu"* → the trap becomes ❌ *the company **that its** CEO left*. English wants *the company **whose** CEO left*.
- **where = *onde*, when = *quando*** — these map cleanly **for real places and times**. The betrayal: Portuguese *onde* is used loosely for abstract nouns (*"uma decisão **onde** o board decide"*), but English *where* needs a **literal place**. For a thing/idea, go back to *that / which*: *a decision **that** only the board can make*.
- **Commas:** Portuguese tolerates *que* with or without commas and rarely bans it. English is strict: once there are commas, ***that* and dropping are both forbidden** — only *who / which / whose*.

**Top typical Brazilian-speaker errors:**

1. **Dodging *whose* with *"that its / that his"*** (from *"que o … dele"*):
   - ❌ *The vendor **that its** platform crashed lost the contract.*
   - ✅ *The vendor **whose** platform crashed lost the contract.*
2. ***that* (or a dropped pronoun) inside a non-defining, comma'd clause:**
   - ❌ *Our CEO**, that** joined in 2020, championed the pivot.*
   - ✅ *Our CEO**, who** joined in 2020, championed the pivot.* (commas ⇒ *who/which* only, never *that*, never dropped)
3. ***where* for a non-place** (abstract *onde*):
   - ❌ *This is a strategy **where** only the board can decide.*
   - ✅ *This is a strategy **that / which** only the board can decide on.* (*where* needs a literal place: *the market **where** we compete* ✅)

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. **Rafael is the leader whose team shipped our first profitable model.**
   *Executive. *whose* = possessive (**his** team) — replaces a clumsy "the leader — his team shipped…". Not "that his team".*
2. **Southeast Asia is the region where our only serious competitor is still weak.**
   *Executive. *where* for a **literal place** (a region). A clause that identifies which region → defining, no commas.*
3. **We pivoted to AI-first in the quarter when margins first turned positive.**
   *Executive. *when* pins the noun to a **time** (a quarter). Defining — it tells you which quarter, so no commas.*
4. **Our CEO, who rarely overrides a committee, approved this in one call.**
   *Executive. Non-defining: we have one CEO, so the clause is **extra**. Hence commas + *who* (never "that"), and it can't be dropped.*
5. **The board rejected the vendor whose contract had no exit clause.**
   *Executive. *whose* again — the vendor's contract. Defining (which vendor? the one whose contract…), so no commas here.*
6. **The directors who opposed the raise resigned — the others stayed.**
   *Executive. Defining on purpose: no commas means **only** the opposing directors resigned. Add commas and you'd be saying they all did.*
7. **The hotel where we held the offsite double-booked our room.** *(everyday)*
   *Everyday. *where* = a real place (a hotel). Not "the hotel that we held the offsite in it" — no resumptive pronoun.*
8. **My neighbour, whose car alarm goes off at 6 a.m., finally apologised.** *(everyday)*
   *Everyday. Non-defining (I have one specific neighbour): commas + *whose*. The clause adds colour, it doesn't pick a neighbour out of many.*

### 6. 🧪 Recognition drill (4 items, ~2 minutes)

Answers in the Gabarito (section 9), not here.

1. **Fix the *whose*-dodge:** *"The startup **that its** founder left is now up for sale."*
2. **Commas on or off — and what changes?** Two versions: *"The VPs who missed their targets were replaced."* vs *"The VPs, who missed their targets, were replaced."* Which one means **all** the VPs were replaced, and which means **only some**? Say why the commas decide it.
3. **Spot the error:** *"Our Head of AI, **that** we hired from a hyperscaler, starts Monday."*
4. **Choose — *where* or *that/which*?** *"That's the market ___ we have no real rival."* vs *"That's a decision ___ only the board can take."* Fill both and say which one *where* is wrong for, and why.

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 097):**
1. Drop it in the **second** blank: *"the deal **(that) we** closed"* — the next word is a new subject (*we*), so the pronoun is the **object** and droppable. In the first, *"the leader **who drives** change,"* the next word is a **verb** (*drives*), so the pronoun is the **subject** — **keep it**. Test: *verb after it → keep; new subject after it → may drop.*
2. ***who*** (or *that*) — *"the investor **who** backed our seed round."* A person is **never** *which*; use *who*, or the universal *that*.
3. Delete ***it*** → *"The report **(that) I sent** to the board had the final numbers."* *I* is already the subject, so the relative pronoun is the object — the extra *it* is the Portuguese resumptive-pronoun habit (*"o relatório que eu enviei **ele**"*), forbidden in English.

**Spaced review:**
- **Unit 091:** *"I'll **send it to you** tonight."* Once the thing is a **pronoun** (*it*), English blocks the person-first order — ❌ *"send you it"* is unnatural. Rule: **pronoun thing ⇒ thing first + *to* + person** (*send it to you*, *give them to her*).
- **Unit 068:** *"**Most** boards… but **most of** our directors…"*. Bare *most* + general plural (*most boards*) for the industry-wide claim; *most **of*** the moment the group is **specified** (*our* directors). The possessive *our* (like *the / this / these*) is what forces the *of*.

**Recognition drill:**
1. ❌ *that its* → ✅ *"The startup **whose** founder left is now up for sale."* *whose* carries the possessive (the founder **of** the startup); "that its" is the *que … dele* trap.
2. **Commas = all; no commas = only some.** *"The VPs, who missed their targets, were replaced"* (commas, non-defining) ⇒ **all** the VPs were replaced, and as a side note they all missed. *"The VPs who missed their targets were replaced"* (no commas, defining) ⇒ **only** those VPs who missed were replaced; others stayed. The commas mark the clause as *extra* vs *identifying*.
3. ❌ *…, **that** we hired…* → ✅ *"Our Head of AI**, who** we hired from a hyperscaler, starts Monday."* The commas make it **non-defining** (we have one Head of AI), and non-defining clauses **ban *that*** — use *who* (and you can't drop it here either).
4. *"the market **where** we have no real rival"* — *where* ✅, a **literal place** (a market/region). *"a decision **that / which** only the board can take"* — ❌ *where*: a decision is **not a place**; use *that/which*. *where* needs real location.

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

Três ferramentas novas: **whose** = *cujo/cuja* (*the leader **whose team** shipped* = o líder cuja equipe entregou); **where** = *onde*, para **lugar real**; **when** = *quando*, para **tempo**. E o coração da unidade é a diferença **defining × non-defining**. A oração **defining** diz *qual* — vai **sem vírgula**, aceita *that* e pode omitir o pronome (*the analyst (who) we hired*). A oração **non-defining** só **acrescenta** informação sobre um substantivo já identificado — vai **entre vírgulas**, e aí *that* é **proibido** e omitir o pronome também: use só *who / which / whose*. Teste: tire a oração; se a frase ainda sabe *qual* é, era extra (vírgulas). As vírgulas mudam o **fato** — *"The VPs, who missed targets, were replaced"* (todos) × *"The VPs who missed targets were replaced"* (só alguns).

Armadilhas que vêm do português: (1) fugir de *whose* com ❌ *"that its / that his"* (do *"que o … dele"*) — diga *the company **whose** CEO left*; (2) usar *that* ou omitir o pronome dentro de uma oração entre vírgulas — ❌ *"Our CEO, that joined…"* → ✅ *"Our CEO, who joined…"*; (3) usar *where* para coisa abstrata (o *onde* solto) — ❌ *"a decision **where** the board decides"* → ✅ *"a decision **that/which** the board decides on"*. *where* só para lugar de verdade.
