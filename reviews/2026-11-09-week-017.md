# 🔁 Review Week 017 — Units 114 to 119

*Consolidation through contrast — no new grammar, no writing tasks. This week
was two big families: the **timeline words** (114), then three kinds of
**linkers and clauses** that glue ideas together — **contrast** (115),
**condition** (116), and **relative clauses** (117–119). The traps all come
from the same place: one Portuguese word (*ainda*, *já*, *apesar*, *que*,
*onde*, *cujo*) covering two or three different English jobs. This review
drills the exact borders.*

---

### 1. Recap — one line per unit

- **Unit 114 — *still · yet · already · (not) any more / no longer*:** four points on a timeline. **still** = it's continuing (middle position); **yet** = hasn't happened but is expected (end, negatives/questions); **already** = happened, sooner than expected (middle); **no longer / not … any more** = used to be true, stopped.
- **Unit 115 — *although · even though · though · in spite of · despite*:** contrast / concession ("this is true, *but* here's what matters anyway"). The split is **shape**: *although / even though* + a **clause** (subject + verb); *despite / in spite of* + a **noun**. No *"of"* after *despite*; always *"of"* after *in spite*.
- **Unit 116 — *unless · as long as · provided / providing (that)*:** they set a **condition**, not a fact. *unless* = *if … not*. All of them, like *if*, use the **present tense for future meaning** (*unless we **respond***, not *will respond*).
- **Unit 117 — defining relative clauses *who · which · that*:** identify *which one* (no commas, essential). Drop the pronoun **only when it's the object** (a new subject follows it): *the vendor (that) **we** chose* ✅; *the vendor **that scored** highest* ❌ can't drop.
- **Unit 118 — *whose · whom · where / when / why*:** possession (*whose* + noun, never dropped), person-as-object after a preposition (*to whom* → spoken *we report to*), and place/time/reason (*where / when / why*, not *onde* stretched over a time).
- **Unit 119 — non-defining relative clauses:** the **comma** version — extra, removable information. **Never *that*, never drop the pronoun.** Plus **sentence *which*** that points back at a whole idea (*They delayed it, **which** nobody expected*).

### 2. Confusion map — when each one wins

This is the heart of the review. Each row is a border where Portuguese blurs
two English choices.

**A. Timeline words (Unit 114)**

| You want to say… | Use | Not | Position |
|---|---|---|---|
| it's **continuing**, no change | **still** | *yet* | middle (*we're **still** waiting*) |
| it **hasn't happened**, but we expect it | **yet** | *still* | end (*not approved **yet***) |
| it **happened** — sooner than expected | **already** | *yet* | middle (*we've **already** signed*) |
| it **stopped** (was true, now isn't) | **no longer / not … any more** | *still not* | *no longer* middle; *any more* end |

> *ainda* = **still** (positive) **or** *yet* (negative). *já* = **already** (statement) **or** *yet* (question). A neutral question takes **yet** (*Has it shipped **yet**?*); *already* in a question signals surprise (*Has it shipped **already**?!*).

**B. Contrast vs condition (Units 115 & 116)** — the big one

| Meaning | Linkers | What follows | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Contrast** — a fact points one way, I go the other | *although, even though, though* | a **clause** | ***Although** the costs were high, we scaled.* |
| **Contrast** — same, with a thing | *despite, in spite of* | a **noun** | ***Despite** the high costs, we scaled.* |
| **Condition** — only if this is true | *unless, as long as, provided, providing* | a **clause** (present tense) | *We'll scale **as long as** adoption holds.* |

> Test for 115: **does a subject+verb follow?** Yes → *although / even though*. A noun or *-ing*? → *despite / in spite of*. And burn in: ❌ *despite **of*** · ✅ *in spite **of***.
> Test for 116: *unless* = *if not*, so it already carries one negative — **don't add a second** (❌ *unless we don't respond*).

**C. Contrast *though* vs other uses** — *though* at the **end** of a sentence = a casual *however* (*We're the smaller player, **though***). Front *though* = informal *although*.

**D. Relative clauses — the three-unit cluster (117–119)**

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Does the clause **identify** the noun (essential)? | **Defining** → no commas; *that* allowed; object pronoun droppable |
| Is the noun **already identified** (extra info)? | **Non-defining** → commas; **never *that***; **never drop** |
| Person or thing? | **who** (people) · **which** (things) · **that** (either, defining only) |
| Possession (*of which/of whom*)? | **whose** + noun — never dropped, either flavour |
| Can I drop the pronoun? | Only **defining + object** (a new subject follows). Never if it's the subject; never in a non-defining clause |
| *which* pointing at a **whole idea**? | sentence ***which*** — comma + *which*, never *that*, never *what* |

> One sentence captures the comma rule: *My colleague **who** speaks Mandarin will join* (I have several colleagues — defining) vs *My colleague, **who** speaks Mandarin, will join* (I have one — extra fact). The commas change the meaning, not just the look.

### 3. 10 recognition exercises (mostly contrast)

*Answers with explanations in section 4. Choose between the rivals — don't just fix form.*

**1. (114 — still vs yet)** Place each word correctly and pick the right one:
*"We're ___ negotiating with the vendor, and we haven't signed ___."*
(*still / yet* — one goes in the middle, one at the end.)

**2. (114 — already vs yet)** You ask a plain, neutral question in a board prep:
*"Has finance released the figures ___?"* — *already* or *yet*? What would the other one imply?

**3. (115 vs 116 — contrast vs condition)** One linker fits:
*"___ the board approves the forecast, the budget stays frozen."*
(a) *Although* (b) *Unless* (c) *Despite* — which, and what does each wrong one do to the meaning?

**4. (115 — clause vs noun)** Fix if needed:
*"**Despite** the pilot exceeded every target, the board wants another quarter of data."*

**5. (115 — the *apesar* traps)** Two errors — correct both:
*"We launched **despite of** the delay, **in spite** a tight budget."*

**6. (116 — unless double-negative)** Choose the verb:
*"**Unless** the CFO (**signs** / **doesn't sign**) off, we can't commit to the vendor."*

**7. (116 — present for future)** Choose and justify:
*"The second tranche unlocks **as long as** adoption (**stays** / **will stay**) above sixty percent."*

**8. (117 — keep or drop)** In which one can you **delete** the relative pronoun — and what's the one-word test?
(a) *"the advisor **who** led the audit"* (b) *"the advisor **who** we poached."*

**9. (117 vs 119 — comma changes meaning)** Which sentence means Levi has **only one** CFO?
(a) *"Our CFO **who** joined last year is cautious."* (b) *"Our CFO, **who** joined last year, is cautious."*

**10. (118 / 119 — whose, that, sentence *which*)** Three items — pick the correct word:
(a) *"Vextra, ___ renewal is up in March, is the account I'd flag."* (*whose / who's / that*)
(b) *"Marta, ___ now runs strategy, will present."* (*that / who*)
(c) *"The CEO approved it on Friday, ___ honestly surprised me."* (*what / which / that*)

### 4. ✅ Gabarito (answers + one-line why)

1. **"We're *still* negotiating … and we haven't signed *yet*."** *still* = the situation continues (middle, after *are*); *yet* = expected but not done (end, in a negative). Swapping them breaks both position and meaning.
2. **yet.** A neutral question asks whether it has happened → *yet*. *Already* in a question signals surprise ("so soon?!"), which you don't want in a straight board prep.
3. **(b) Unless.** The main clause is the *negative* outcome ("budget stays frozen"), unlocked only if the board **doesn't** approve — that's *unless* (= *if … not*). *Although* would wrongly concede ("even though they approve, it stays frozen"); *Despite* can't take a clause at all.
4. **"*Although* the pilot exceeded every target…"** A full clause follows (*the pilot exceeded*), so you need a clause linker. To keep *despite*: *"**Despite** the pilot's success / **despite** exceeding every target…"* (turn the clause into a noun or *-ing*).
5. **"We launched *despite* the delay, *in spite of* a tight budget."** *despite* takes **no *of***; *in spite* **always** takes *of*.
6. **signs.** *Unless* already means *if not*, so it carries the negative. *"Unless he signs"* = *"if he doesn't sign."* Adding *doesn't* makes a double negative that flips it back to the wrong meaning.
7. **stays.** After conditional linkers (like after *if*), the future is written in the **present tense** — *unless / as long as / provided / providing* all follow this. ❌ *will stay*.
8. **(b) — you can drop it:** *"the advisor we poached."* Test: if a **new subject** follows the pronoun (*we*), it's the object → droppable. In (a), a **verb** follows (*led*) → the pronoun is the subject → keep it.
9. **(b).** The commas (non-defining) mark extra info about the one CFO already known. (a) with no commas (defining) implies you're singling one CFO out from several — "the one who joined last year," as opposed to others.
10. (a) **whose** (possession, + noun directly; *who's* = *who is*). (b) **who** (non-defining clause — **never *that***). (c) **which** (sentence *which*, pointing at the whole idea "the CEO approved it"; never *that*, never *what*).

### 5. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

Three contrasts from this week are the most worth drilling out loud with your
tutor — they're the ones that slip in real executive speech:

1. **Contrast vs condition under pressure (115 + 116).** Ask your tutor to
   role-play a skeptical CFO. Every time you push back, force yourself to open
   with the right linker: *"**Although** the infra bill was high…"* (conceding a
   fact) vs *"We'll commit **as long as** adoption holds…"* (setting a term).
   The goal is to stop defaulting to *but* and to never say *despite that the…*.
2. **The *unless* reflex (116).** Practice stating conditions where the
   outcome is negative: *"**Unless** the board objects, I sign Friday."* Have
   the tutor catch any double-negative (*unless … doesn't*) — it's the error
   that survives longest because Portuguese *a não ser que* lets you add the
   second *not*.
3. **Defining vs non-defining in introductions (117 + 119).** Introduce the
   people on your team twice — once *defining* ("the advisor **who** owns the
   AI account"), once *non-defining* ("Marta, **who** owns the AI account, …").
   Train the tiny pause that is the comma, and the habit of never using *that*
   after a name.

### 6. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

Esta semana teve dois blocos. Primeiro, as **palavras de linha do tempo**
(Unidade 114): *still* = ainda continua (meio da frase), *yet* = ainda não,
mas é esperado (fim, em negativa/pergunta), *already* = já aconteceu — antes do
esperado (meio), e *no longer / not … any more* = deixou de ser verdade. O
perigo é que *ainda* e *já* cada um cobre dois papéis diferentes em inglês.
Depois, três tipos de conectores: **contraste** (115: *although/even though* +
oração; *despite/in spite of* + substantivo — e nunca *despite of*),
**condição** (116: *unless* = *se não*; todos usam **presente com sentido de
futuro**, como depois de *if*), e as **orações relativas** (117–119).

O que deve estar sólido: a diferença de *forma* entre *although* (oração) e
*despite* (substantivo), e a regra do presente depois de *unless/as long as*.
O que provavelmente ainda precisa de treino: (1) não colocar um segundo *not*
depois de *unless*; (2) **derrubar** o pronome relativo só quando ele é
**objeto** (quando vem um novo sujeito logo depois); (3) a **vírgula** que
separa o relativo defining do non-defining — ela muda o sentido, e no
non-defining **nunca** se usa *that* nem se omite o pronome; (4) o *which* que
aponta para uma ideia inteira (onde o português usa *o que*). Se um bloco
ainda pesa, releia **`lessons/2026-11-05-unit-116.md`** (condições) e
**`lessons/2026-11-08-unit-119.md`** (relativas com vírgula) — são os dois
pontos de interferência mais fortes da semana.
