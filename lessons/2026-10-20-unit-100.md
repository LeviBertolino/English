### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** Give me the one-paragraph version of where we stand.
> **You:** The team **leading the AI pivot** is six weeks ahead of plan. The budget **approved by the board** in March is already 40% committed. The two risks **raised in last week's review** are both under control.
> **CFO:** And the vendors?
> **You:** The three firms **shortlisted for the platform** are strong. The one **recommended by our architects** is also the cheapest — rare.
> **CEO:** Good. Send me the deck.
> **You:** The version **showing the revised numbers** is in your inbox now.

Read the noun phrases carefully. Not one of them uses *who* or *that* or *which* — yet each one is really a relative clause in disguise. *The team **leading** the pivot* = the team **that is leading** the pivot. *The budget **approved** by the board* = the budget **that was approved**. English quietly deletes the *who is / that was* and keeps only the verb-form — **-ing** when the noun *does* the action, **-ed** when the noun *gets* the action done to it. That one choice is today's whole lesson.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

A relative clause built on the verb *be* (**who is / that is / which was / that were**…) can be shortened: drop the pronoun **and** the form of *be*, and keep the verb as **-ing** or **-ed**.

| Meaning | Form | Full version | Short version |
|---|---|---|---|
| the noun **does** the action (active) | noun **+ -ing** | the team **that is leading** the pivot | the team **leading** the pivot |
| the noun **gets** the action done to it (passive) | noun **+ -ed / past participle** | the budget **that was approved** | the budget **approved** |

- **-ing = active, ongoing.** *the people **working** on the model* (they work), *the market **growing** fastest* (it grows).
- **-ed = passive.** *the decision **taken** by the board* (someone took it), *the risks **raised** in the review* (someone raised them). Use the **past participle** — *taken, chosen, shown, raised, given* — not the simple past.
- **Position:** the clause comes **right after the noun** it describes. *The report **showing the numbers*** — not *The showing report*.
- **It can carry its own objects and phrases:** *the firm **recommended by our architects***, *the team **leading the AI pivot since January***.

**Fast test:** turn it back into a full clause. If the natural expansion is *"that **is/are** + -ing"* → use **-ing**. If it's *"that **was/were** + past participle"* → use **-ed**.

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why does this structure exist?**
It is the executive's compression tool. Full relative clauses are correct but heavy: *"The budget **that was approved** by the board, **which is** now 40% committed…"* reads like minutes. Reduced clauses strip the scaffolding — *"The budget **approved** by the board, now 40% committed…"* — and the sentence moves at the speed of a briefing. In writing (board decks, strategy memos) and in confident speech, this is how fluent English sounds dense without sounding long. It carries **exactly** the meaning of the relative clause it replaces — no more, no less — just shorter.

**Decision rule — the real choice is *-ing* vs *-ed* (active vs passive), not short vs long:**
Ask one question about the noun: **does it *do* the action, or *receive* it?**
- **The noun acts → -ing.** *the analyst **running** the model* (the analyst runs it).
- **The noun is acted on → -ed.** *the model **run** last quarter* (someone ran the model).

Minimal pair — same verb, opposite direction:
- *The consultants **advising** the board* → the consultants give advice (active).
- *The board **advised** by the consultants* → the board receives advice (passive).

**And when you *can't* reduce — the limit:** you may drop only *who/that/which* **+ be**. If the relative clause has a different verb (a one-time completed action with *have/do/make*, or a simple-present habit), you usually **keep the full clause**.
- ✅ *the deal **(that) we closed** last week* — stays full (no *be* to delete; *we closed* is a plain past action). You can drop *that*, but not reduce to *-ing/-ed*.
- ❌ *the deal **closing** last week* (sounds like it's still closing — wrong meaning).
So: *be* + -ing/-ed → reduce freely. A plain action verb → keep the relative clause you learned in Units 097–098.

**PT→EN mapping:**
- **-ing clause ≈ gerúndio (*-ndo*)**: *"a equipe **liderando** o projeto"* → *the team **leading** the project.* This maps cleanly — trust it for **active** meaning.
- **-ed clause ≈ particípio (*-ado / -ido*)**: *"o orçamento **aprovado** pelo board"* → *the budget **approved** by the board.* Also clean — **when** you remember English uses the **past participle**, which for irregular verbs is *not* the simple past (*chosen* not *chose*, *taken* not *took*, *shown* not *showed*).
- **The betrayal:** Portuguese lets you say *"a decisão **tomando** forma"* loosely, and Brazilians then reach for *-ing* even when the meaning is passive. If the noun **receives** the action, English forces **-ed**, never *-ing*: *"a decisão **tomada** pelo board"* is *the decision **taken** by the board*, never *the decision **taking*** by the board.

**Top typical Brazilian-speaker errors:**

1. ***-ing* where the meaning is passive** (the #1 trap — wrong direction):
   - ❌ *The budget **approving** by the board is already committed.*
   - ✅ *The budget **approved** by the board is already committed.* *(the budget doesn't approve — it gets approved → -ed)*
2. **Simple past instead of the past participle:**
   - ❌ *the strategy **chose** last year* · *the risks **shown** → shew*… ❌ *the plan **took** to the CEO*
   - ✅ *the strategy **chosen** last year* · *the plan **taken** to the CEO* *(-ed clause needs the participle: chosen, taken, shown — not chose, took, showed)*
3. **Trying to reduce a clause that has no *be* (a plain action):**
   - ❌ *the deal **closing** last week with the client* (means still ongoing)
   - ✅ *the deal **(that) we closed** last week* *(a one-time past action → keep the relative clause, don't force -ing/-ed)*

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. **The team leading the AI pivot** is six weeks ahead of plan.
   *— -ing: the team does the leading (active). Shorter than "the team that is leading."*
2. **The budget approved by the board** in March is 40% committed.
   *— -ed: the budget gets approved (passive). Participle "approved," never "approving," because the board is the actor.*
3. **The two risks raised in last week's review** are both under control.
   *— -ed: someone raised the risks → passive. "Raising" would wrongly mean the risks do the raising.*
4. **The firm recommended by our architects** is also the cheapest.
   *— -ed: the firm receives the recommendation. Contrast "the firm recommending us" = the firm that advises us (active).*
5. Only the directors **attending the board offsite** saw the full forecast.
   *— -ing, and note: no commas → it's defining (which directors? the ones attending). This is Unit 098's rule surviving the reduction.*
6. **The money spent on last year's pilot** taught us more than the pilot itself.
   *— -ed: the money is spent by someone (passive). "Spent," not "spending," and not "spended."*
7. The man **talking to the CFO by the window** is our new Head of Data.
   *— -ing: he is doing the talking. Everyday register; still just "the man who is talking" made short.*
8. Most of the issues **flagged in the audit** were fixed before the holidays.
   *— -ed clause riding on a Unit 068 quantifier ("most of the issues"): someone flagged them → passive → "flagged."*

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 098):**
1. **Without commas** (*The VPs who missed targets were replaced*) = **only the VPs who missed** were replaced (defining — tells you *which* ones). **With commas** (*The VPs, who missed targets, were replaced*) = **all** the VPs were replaced, and as an aside, they all missed (non-defining). The comma changes the headcount, not just the style.
2. ***whose*** → *The vendor **whose** platform crashed lost the deal.* The error comes from Portuguese *"o fornecedor **que a** plataforma **dele** caiu"* — Brazilians dodge *cujo/cuja* and smuggle in a possessive (*that its*). English demands *whose*.
3. ***which*** (or *that*) → *This is a strategy **which/that** only the board can decide.* *Where* needs a **literal place**; a strategy is a thing/idea, so Portuguese's loose *onde* betrays you here.

**Spaced review:**
- **Unit 093:** ***approves*** — *"We'll move as soon as the board **approves** the budget."* The time clause stays in the **present**; *will* lives only in the main half (*we'll move*). The Portuguese **future subjunctive** (*"assim que o board **aprovar**"*) tempts you toward *will approve*, but that tense doesn't exist in English.
- **Unit 068:** *"**Most** boards move slowly, but **most of** our directors have read the forecast."* First blank = **general** (boards everywhere → *most* + noun, no *of*). Second = **specific** (*our* directors → *most **of** our*). The word *our* forces the *of* shape.

**Recognition drill:**
1. ***taken*** — the decision *gets* made by the board (passive) → past participle *-ed*. *Taking* would mean the decision performs the action.
2. **❌ *leaded* → ✅ *leading*** — the team *does* the leading (active) → *-ing*. (*Lead* is also irregular: its participle is *led*, never *leaded* — but here it's active, so *leading*.) Fixed: *The team **leading** the integration delivered three weeks early.*
3. **(b) is correct.** *(that) we signed* is a plain one-time past action with no form of *be* to delete, so it keeps the relative clause from Unit 097. (a) *The contract signing* wrongly implies the contract is still in the act of signing itself — wrong meaning and wrong direction (a contract is signed, it doesn't sign).
4. *The consultants **advising** the board …; the board **advised** by those consultants …* — first the consultants *do* the advising (**-ing**, active); then the board *receives* advice (**-ed**, passive). Same verb, opposite direction.

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

Uma oração relativa construída sobre o verbo *be* (*that is / who was / which were…*) pode ser **encurtada**: apaga-se o pronome e o *be*, e sobra só o verbo em **-ing** ou **-ed**. Use **-ing** quando o substantivo **faz** a ação (*the team **leading** the pivot* = a equipe que lidera) e **-ed / particípio** quando o substantivo **recebe** a ação (*the budget **approved** by the board* = o orçamento que foi aprovado). É a ferramenta de compressão do executivo: transforma minutas pesadas em frases de briefing, carregando exatamente o mesmo sentido da relativa, só que mais curto.

Cuidado com duas armadilhas de interferência. **(1) Direção:** se o substantivo *recebe* a ação, o inglês exige **-ed**, nunca *-ing* — *the decision **taken** by the board*, jamais *the decision taking*. **(2) Particípio irregular:** a oração *-ed* pede o **particípio passado**, que para verbos irregulares não é o passado simples — *chosen* (não *chose*), *taken* (não *took*), *shown* (não *showed*), *spent* (não *spended*). E lembre o limite: só dá para reduzir quando havia um *be* para apagar; uma ação pontual no passado (*the deal **we closed** last week*) mantém a relativa inteira das Units 097–098, sem virar *-ing/-ed*.
