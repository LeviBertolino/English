### 1. 📘 Unit 027 — I'm going to (do something)

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** So what's the plan for the AI portfolio next year?
> **You:** We**'re going to** double down on three bets and kill the rest. I**'m
> going to** present the shortlist to the board on Thursday.
> **CEO:** And the budget?
> **You:** That's the risk. Look at the run-rate — if nothing changes, we**'re
> going to** overshoot by Q3. So I**'m going to** ask the CFO for a re-forecast.
> **CEO:** Good. This **is going to** be a hard conversation.

Notice the pattern *before* any rule. *We're going to double down, I'm going to
present, I'm going to ask* — each is an **intention already decided**, not a plan
fixed to a calendar slot yet. And *"we're going to overshoot", "this is going to
be hard"* aren't intentions at all — they're **predictions you can see coming**
from the evidence in front of you (the run-rate). Portuguese does both with *"vou
/ vamos"* — and this is the one place the direct translation actually helps you.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

**going to** has one fixed shape and two jobs.

| | am / is / are | going to | base verb |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | **am** (I'm) | going to | present the roadmap. |
| He / she / it | **is** ('s) | going to | be a hard call. |
| We / you / they | **are** ('re) | going to | double down on AI. |

**Structure:** *am/is/are + going to + base verb.*

**Key facts:**
- The verb after *going to* is always the **base form** — *going to **present***,
  *going to **be***, never *going to presenting / going to presents*.
- **Two meanings, same form:**
  1. **Intention** — a decision you've **already made**: *"I'm going to ask the
     CFO."*
  2. **Prediction from present evidence** — something you can **see coming now**:
     *"Look at the numbers — we're going to miss the target."*
- Questions and negatives use *am/is/are*: *"**Are** you **going to** present?"* /
  *"We **aren't going to** approve it."*
- *going to go* is fine but often trimmed: *"I'm going to go to São Paulo"* → most
  people say *"I'm going to São Paulo"* (present continuous of *go*).

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why does this structure exist?** English needs a way to say *"I've decided —
this is my intention"* that doesn't yet require a booked slot, **and** a way to
predict what the evidence in front of you makes obvious. *going to* carries both:
the decision is made (intention), or the outcome is already visible on its way
(prediction). *"We're going to invest in AI"* = the call is made, even if no
meeting is scheduled. *"This is going to be expensive"* = I can already see it
coming.

**Decision rule vs the rival structures.** Two rivals matter here.

**Rival 1 — going to vs present continuous (yesterday's arrangement).** Both talk
about future plans, and they often overlap — but they answer different questions.

| Meaning | ✅ Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| An **intention / decision** (maybe not fixed yet) | **going to**: *We**'re going to** restructure the portfolio.* | the call is made; no slot needed |
| A **fixed arrangement** (time + place, on the calendar) | **present continuous**: *I**'m presenting** the shortlist on Thursday.* | it's booked, in motion |

Minimal pair — same topic, different focus:
- *"We**'re going to** move to an AI-first model."* → the **decision**; when/how is
  open. ✅
- *"We**'re moving** to the new model **on the first of March**."* → a **fixed
  arrangement**, dated and settled. ✅

Decision rule: **just decided, not yet scheduled → going to; booked with a
day/place → present continuous.** In practice both are often fine — but if a
concrete time and place exist, English leans to the continuous.

**Rival 2 — going to vs *will* (the prediction/decision split).** This is the one
Brazilians most need. Both point forward, but:

| | going to | will |
|---|---|---|
| Decision | made **before** now (already decided) | made **at the moment of speaking** |
| Prediction | from **evidence you see now** | from **opinion / belief** |
| Example | *We**'re going to** hire a Head of AI — it's decided.* | *The phone's ringing — I**'ll get** it.* |
| Example | *Look at the burn rate — we**'re going to** run out of cash.* | *I think AI **will** reshape the whole sector.* |

Minimal pair — same event, different trigger:
- *"Watch the adoption curve — this pilot **is going to** succeed."* → prediction
  from **evidence on the screen**. ✅ *going to*
- *"Don't worry, the pilot **will** succeed."* → prediction from **belief /
  reassurance**, no data pointed at. ✅ *will*

**PT→EN mapping — the good news, and the trap.** Portuguese *"ir + infinitivo"*
(*"vou apresentar", "vamos investir", "isso vai dar errado"*) maps **almost
perfectly** onto *going to*: *"vou apresentar"* → *"I'm going to present"*,
*"vamos investir em IA"* → *"we're going to invest in AI"*. This is rare — use it.
Where it betrays Levi: (1) *"vou"* is a **single word**, but English needs the
full frame *am/is/are + going to* — dropping the *to be* gives *"I go to present"*,
which is wrong. (2) When the plan is **fixed with a date and place**, English
often prefers the present continuous over *going to* (*"I'm presenting on
Thursday"*, not *"I'm going to present on Thursday"* — both understood, but the
continuous sounds more settled).

**Top Brazilian-speaker errors:**
- ❌ *Tomorrow I **go to present** the AI roadmap.* → ✅ *Tomorrow I**'m going to
  present** the AI roadmap.* — *"vou apresentar" needs the full frame **am/is/are
  + going to + base verb**; "I go to present" drops the *to be* and reads as a
  reason ("I go in order to present").*
- ❌ *We**'re going to launching** the pilot in March.* → ✅ *We**'re going to
  launch** the pilot in March.* — *after* going to *comes the **base verb**, never
  the -ing form.*
- ❌ *Look at these numbers — the budget **will overshoot**.* → ✅ *Look at these
  numbers — the budget**'s going to overshoot**.* — *a prediction from **evidence
  you can see now** takes* going to*;* will *is for a belief with nothing pointed
  at.*

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. **We're going to position the company as an AI-first player.**
   *An intention already decided → going to; no meeting is booked, so the present
   continuous ("we're positioning") would over-claim a fixed arrangement.*
2. **I'm going to raise the innovation portfolio with the CEO on Monday.**
   *A decision to act, loosely timed → going to; if it were a locked calendar slot
   you might say "I'm meeting the CEO on Monday" instead.*
3. **Look at the run-rate — we're going to overshoot the AI budget by Q3.**
   *Prediction from evidence in front of you → going to, not* will*; the numbers,
   not a hunch, make it visible.*
4. **The board isn't going to approve the full budget this cycle.**
   *A confident prediction from what you already know of the board → going to; the
   negative keeps the am/is/are frame ("isn't going to").*
5. **Are you going to present the 2027 vision at the offsite?**
   *Asking about someone's **intention** → going-to question; "Are you presenting
   at the offsite?" would ask about a fixed arrangement instead.*
6. **This is going to be a hard conversation with the CFO.**
   *Prediction you can already see coming → going to; a natural, high-frequency
   executive phrase.*
7. **We're going to consolidate three tools into one and retire the rest.**
   *A strategic decision already taken → going to; the "when" is deliberately
   open.*
8. **It's cloudy and the wind's picking up — it's going to rain.**
   *Everyday life: prediction from **visible evidence** (clouds, wind) → going to,
   the textbook case for choosing it over* will*.*

### 8. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

1. Ask your tutor to run an **"intentions" drill**: they ask *"What are you going
   to do about the budget? The vendor lock-in? The AI talent gap?"* and you answer
   every one with a decided intention — *"I'm going to re-forecast with the CFO",
   "We're going to consolidate the stack", "I'm going to open two senior roles"*.
   Have them bait you with a Portuguese-style prompt (*"So tomorrow you go to
   present?"*) so you correct *"I go to present"* → *"I'm going to present"* out
   loud.
2. Then a **going-to vs will sorting drill**: your tutor throws mixed prompts — one
   where evidence is on the table (*"the burn rate is climbing…"*), one where it's
   a snap decision (*"the phone's ringing…"*), one that's pure belief (*"do you
   think AI reshapes the sector?"*) — and you decide fast between *"…is going to…"*
   (decided / from evidence) and *"…will…"* (decided now / from opinion), saying
   each aloud.

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 026)**
1. **starts / 'm chairing** — ✅ *"The offsite **starts** at nine, but I**'m
   chairing** the strategy review at ten."* — *the offsite time is set by the
   **schedule** → present simple; the review is **your arrangement** → present
   continuous.*
2. **'m meeting** — ✅ *"It's already in my calendar — I**'m meeting** the CFO at
   four."* — *already **arranged** → present continuous;* will *is for a decision
   made **at the moment of speaking**, not a booked plan.*
3. *"What **do** you **do** this weekend?"* asks about a **habit / routine**;
   *"What **are** you **doing** this weekend?"* asks about your **plans**. — *the
   present continuous is the one that asks about arrangements.*

**Spaced review**
- **(Unit 020)** ❌ *"I've presented … last Tuesday."* → ✅ *"I **presented** the
  roadmap **last Tuesday**."* — *a named finished time (last Tuesday) bans the
  present perfect; the date word wins and pulls the verb into the past simple.*
  With **no date**, to mean only *it's done and ready now*: *"I've **presented**
  the roadmap"* (present perfect, result now).
- **(Unit 004)** ❌ *"You are presenting the roadmap today?"* → ✅ *"**Are you**
  presenting the roadmap today?"* — *English forms a question by **inverting**
  (*to be* before the subject); Portuguese asks with intonation alone, keeping
  statement order, and that reflex is the trap.*

**Recognition drill**
1. ❌ *"…going to **restructuring**…"* → ✅ *"Next quarter we**'re going to
   restructure** the AI portfolio."* — *after* going to *comes the **base verb**,
   never the -ing form.*
2. **is going to be** — ✅ *"Look at the adoption curve — this feature **is going
   to be** a hit."* — *"look at…" points at **evidence you can see now**, and
   evidence-based predictions take* going to*, not* will*.*
3. **'m presenting** — ✅ *"It's booked for the 14th — I**'m presenting** the
   shortlist to the board."* — *a **fixed calendar slot** (booked, dated) prefers
   the **present continuous**;* going to *would fit a decided-but-unscheduled
   intention.*
4. ❌ *"Tomorrow I **go to defend**…"* → ✅ *"Tomorrow I**'m going to defend** the
   budget with the CFO."* — *"vou defender" needs the full frame **am/is/are +
   going to + base verb**; "I go to defend" drops the* to be *and misreads as a
   reason.*

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**A regra e quando usar.** *going to* tem uma só forma — **am/is/are + going to +
verbo base** — e dois usos. (1) **Intenção**: uma decisão que você **já tomou**,
mesmo sem hora e lugar marcados — *"We**'re going to** invest in AI", "I**'m going
to** ask the CFO"*. (2) **Previsão a partir de evidência que você vê agora** —
*"Look at the run-rate — we**'re going to** overshoot", "This **is going to** be a
hard conversation"*. A boa notícia: o português *"ir + infinitivo"* (*"vou
apresentar", "vamos investir", "isso vai dar errado"*) mapeia quase perfeitamente
em *going to* — aproveite. Contra o *present continuous* de ontem: se o plano já
tem **dia e lugar na agenda**, o inglês prefere o continuous (*"I'm presenting on
Thursday"*); se é só a **decisão/intenção**, use *going to*.

**As armadilhas.** (1) *"Vou"* é uma palavra só, mas o inglês exige o quadro
completo *am/is/are + going to* — *"I **go to** present"* está errado; o certo é
*"I**'m going to** present"*. (2) Depois de *going to* vem o **verbo base**: *"going
to **launch**"*, nunca *"going to launching"*. (3) *going to* vs *will*: previsão
com **evidência na mesa** é *going to* (*"the numbers are going to drop"*); *will*
é decisão tomada **na hora** (*"I'll get it"*) ou crença/opinião sem dado apontado
(*"AI will reshape the sector"*). Se plano-decidido vs plano-marcado ainda embolar,
releia `lessons/2026-08-07-unit-026.md` (present tenses for the future).
