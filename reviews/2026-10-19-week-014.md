# 🔁 Review Week 014 — Units 93 to 98

*Six structures, one hidden spine: English keeps its future/conditional marker (**will / would**) out of the subordinate half — the `if`-part, the time-part, the `wish`-part — and the relative clauses teach the matching precision for pointing at a noun. This week was about **which half carries the tense** and **which little word carries the meaning**. No new rules below — just the contrasts that decide between them.*

*(Note: Unit 092's slot fell on last week's Review day, so this cycle's Unit 92 wasn't taught — this review covers the six lessons actually studied, 93–98.)*

---

## 2. 📋 Recap — one line per unit

- **Unit 093 — Time conjunctions (*when · after · before · while · until · as soon as · once*):** after a time word, the verb goes **present**, never *will* — one *will*, in the result half only. (*plus* *while* + clause vs *during* + noun; *until* = up to a point vs *by* = a deadline.)
- **Unit 094 — *if* + present (real / 1st conditional):** *If the board approves, we'll move.* No *will* after *if*; **if** = it *might* happen, **when** = it *will* happen.
- **Unit 095 — *if* + past → *would* (unreal / 2nd conditional):** *If we went all-in, we'd lead.* No *would* after *if*; one step back in tense = "this is a thought experiment, not a plan"; *If I **were** you*.
- **Unit 096 — *I wish*:** *wish* + **past** for a state you'd change (*I wish I knew*), *wish* + **would** for behaviour you want changed (*I wish the board would decide*); **wish ≠ hope** (wish = contrary to fact; hope = still possible).
- **Unit 097 — Relative clauses 1 (*who · that · which*):** people → *who/that* (never *which*); drop the pronoun when it's the **object** (*the deck we sent*); never a second subject (*the vendor that ~~it~~ missed…*).
- **Unit 098 — Relative clauses 2 (*whose · where · when* + commas):** *whose* = *cujo*; *where/when* only for a **literal** place/time; **commas = non-defining** → only *who/which/whose*, never *that*, never dropped.

## 3. 🗺️ Confusion map — when each one wins

### A. The "no *will*/*would* in the subordinate half" family

| Structure | The subordinate half takes… | The result half takes… | Meaning it carries |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Time clause** (U93) | **present** (*when the board **approves***) | *will* | *when* — the event is certain, only the timing is open |
| **Real / 1st conditional** (U94) | **present** (*if the board **approves***) | *will* | *if* — a genuine, live possibility (might or might not) |
| **Unreal / 2nd conditional** (U95) | **past** (*if the board **approved***) | *would* | hypothetical — "I'm imagining this, not proposing it" |
| ***wish* about a state** (U96) | **past** (*I wish I **had** the data*) | — | contrary to fact *now* — regret/longing |
| ***wish* about behaviour** (U96) | **would** (*I wish they **would** decide*) | — | someone keeps doing / won't do something — frustration |

**The single tell that separates them all:** *will* and *would* live in **one half only** — the main/result half. The subordinate half (after *if*, *when*, *wish*) stays **present** (real) or steps **one tense back** (unreal). Portuguese pushes you to double the marker because its future-subjunctive and *-ria* conditional *feel* like they belong in the *if*-part. They don't.

### B. The "which word points correctly" family (relative clauses)

| Choice | Picks the left option when… | Picks the right option when… |
|---|---|---|
| ***who* vs *which*** | the noun is a **person** → *who* | the noun is a **thing/idea** → *which* (*that* = safe for either in a defining clause) |
| **keep vs drop the pronoun** | it's the **subject** — nothing else does the verb → **keep** (*the roadmap **that** unlocks Q1*) | it's the **object** — another subject follows → **may drop** (*the roadmap (that) the board approved*) |
| **defining vs non-defining** | needs the clause to know *which one* → **no commas**, *that* OK, droppable | already unique (named person, *our CEO*) → **commas**, only *who/which/whose*, never *that* |
| ***where* vs *that/which*** | a **literal** place/time → *where / when* | an abstract noun (decision, strategy) → *that / which* (**not** *where*) |

### C. The two "same Portuguese word, two English words" splits

- **while vs during** (both *durante*): a **verb clause** follows → *while* (*while we were presenting*); a **noun** follows → *during* (*during the presentation*).
- **until vs by** (both can be *até*): **continuously up to a point** → *until* (*hold it until Q3*); **a deadline, one moment** → *by* (*send it by Friday*).
- **wish vs hope** (Portuguese blurs via *querer/gostaria* vs *espero*): already lost / contrary to fact → *wish* + past; still realistically possible → *hope* + present/*will*.

## 4. 🧪 10 recognition exercises

*Mostly contrast items — each forces a choice between rivals. Answers with explanations at the end. Retrieve first; don't peek.*

1. **Choose:** *We'll take it to the board as soon as the figures ___ (are / will be) final.*
2. **Choose:** *___ (If / When) the CEO lands tomorrow, I'll brief her — her flight's already confirmed.*
3. **Spot & fix (one word is wrong):** *If we would double the AI budget, we'd outpace every competitor in the segment.*
4. **Choose:** *The deal fell through last month. I ___ (wish / hope) we had closed it when we had the chance.*
5. **Choose:** *The CFO ___ (who / which) challenged the forecast turned out to be right.*
6. **Choose:** *This is a strategy ___ (where / that) only the board can approve.*
7. **Spot & fix (one word is extra):** *The report that I sent it yesterday had the final headcount numbers.*
8. **Choose:** *Our CEO___ (, who / that) joined in 2020, championed the AI pivot.* (pick the whole option, commas included)
9. **Choose:** *Let's park the budget question ___ (while / during) we discuss the roadmap.*
10. **Choose — real or unreal?** *___ (If we hire / If we hired) two ML leads next quarter, we'll ship the platform by Q2 — the reqs are already approved.*

## 5. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

Three contrasts from this week are worth drilling out loud with your tutor — they're the ones most likely to slip under pressure in a real board or C-Level exchange:

1. **The "no *will* after *if*/*when*" reflex (U93–95).** Ask your tutor to fire rapid future scenarios at you — *"When the board approves…", "If the pilot works…", "If we went all-in…"* — and have you finish each with the correct half. The goal: *present after when/if-real, one-step-back after if-unreal*, with *will/would* landing only in the result. This is the single highest-value habit of the week.
2. **if vs when, real vs unreal — as a commitment signal (U94–95).** Have your tutor play a CEO and you describe the same move three ways: *"When we scale…"* (certain), *"If we scale…"* (live option), *"If we scaled…"* (hypothesis). Notice how each one changes what you're *promising*. This is register, not just grammar.
3. **wish vs hope (U96).** Quick-fire: the tutor names a situation ("you lost the enterprise deal" / "the vote is next week"), and you react instantly with the right one — *I wish we had won* vs *I hope we win*. Brazilians reliably swap these, and it changes whether you sound defeatist or out of touch.

## 6. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**O que já deve estar sólido.** O coração da semana foi uma regra só, vista de seis ângulos: em inglês, o marcador de futuro/condicional (*will / would*) fica **numa metade só** — a metade do resultado. A outra metade (depois de *if*, *when*, *as soon as*, *wish*) fica no **presente** (quando é real) ou dá **um passo atrás no tempo** (quando é hipotético). Então: *when the board **approves***, *if the pilot **works***, *if we **went** all-in … we'd lead*, *I wish I **knew***. Também devem estar firmes os três pares "mesma palavra em português, duas em inglês": *while* (+ oração) vs *during* (+ substantivo), *until* (até um ponto, contínuo) vs *by* (prazo, um momento), e *wish* (já perdido / contrário ao fato) vs *hope* (ainda possível).

**O que provavelmente ainda precisa de trabalho.** A interferência do português é teimosa em dois pontos. Primeiro, o **subjuntivo futuro** (*quando eu tiver*, *assim que aprovar*) e o **condicional em *-ria*** (*lideraríamos*) empurram sua mão para o *will/would* na metade errada — é o erro nº 1 da semana e o que mais denuncia o falante não-nativo. Segundo, nas **relative clauses**: o português usa *que* para tudo e não deixa cair o pronome, então sobram três traps — *which* para pessoas (❌ *the CFO which*), o pronome repetido (❌ *the report that I sent **it***), e *where* para coisa abstrata (❌ *a strategy **where** the board decides* → use *that/which*). E lembre: **vírgula manda** — clause com vírgula (non-defining) só aceita *who/which/whose*, nunca *that*, nunca omitido.

**Reler se der tempo:** o arquivo da **Unit 095** (real vs unreal — o degrau de tempo que muda tudo) e o da **Unit 096** (*wish* vs *hope* — a distinção que o português apaga). São os dois que mais pagam revisão.

### ✅ Gabarito

1. **are** — time clause (*as soon as*) → present, never *will*. (U93)
2. **When** — the flight is confirmed, so the arrival is **certain**; only the timing is open → *when*, not *if*. (U94)
3. **If we ~~would~~ double → *If we doubled*** — no *would* in the *if*-part of an unreal conditional; *would* stays in the result (*we'd outpace*). (U95)
4. **wish** — the deal is already lost (contrary to fact), so *wish* + past perfect; *hope* would be for something still open. (U96)
5. **who** — the CFO is a **person** → *who* (or *that*); never *which* for people. (U097)
6. **that** — "a strategy" is an abstract thing, not a literal place, so *where* is wrong → *that* (or *which*). (U098)
7. **Delete *it*** → *The report that I sent yesterday…* — *that* is already the object; the resumptive *it* is copied from Portuguese and forbidden. (U097)
8. **, who** → *Our CEO, who joined in 2020, championed…* — a unique noun (our CEO) ⇒ non-defining ⇒ commas ⇒ *who*, never *that*. (U098)
9. **while** — a verb clause (*we discuss*) follows, so *while*; *during* needs a noun (*during the discussion*). (U093)
10. **If we hire** (real) — the reqs are approved, so it's a **live, costed option** → *if* + present + *will*; *if we hired* would signal you're only imagining it. (U094/095)
