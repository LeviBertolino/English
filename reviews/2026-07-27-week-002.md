# 🔁 Review Week 002 — Units 9 to 14

Seven days, one big move: from the present into the **past**. You took the past
of *to be* (*was/were*), the **past simple** in all three shapes (statement,
negative, question), the **past continuous** (*was doing*), and — the payoff —
how the simple and the continuous **work together** in one story. Unit 009
(*have / have got*) rounds out possession in the present. This week is not new
grammar; it's about making the **choices between** these forms automatic,
because in a real recap or post-mortem they collide in every sentence. Read
time: ~15 minutes.

(Note: there is no Unit 008 lesson to recap — that day of the cycle was
Review Week 001, so the count jumps from 007 straight to 009.)

---

## 1. 📋 Recap — one line per unit

- **Unit 009 — I have… / I've got….** Two forms, **same meaning** for
  possession. Plain *have* uses **do/does** (*Do you have…?*); *have got*
  **inverts** like *to be* (*Have you got…?*). Never mix into *"do you have
  got"*.
- **Unit 010 — was / were.** The **past of am/is/are** — how something **was**
  at a finished past point. Choose by the subject: *was* (singular) / *were*
  (plural). Questions **invert**, never take *did*.
- **Unit 011 — worked / got / went (past simple, positive).** The **finished
  action** in the past — what someone **did**. One form for all subjects;
  regulars add **-ed**, the common business verbs are **irregular** (*go→went,
  spend→spent, speak→spoke*).
- **Unit 012 — I didn't… / Did you…? (past simple, neg. & questions).** The
  past jumps onto the helper **did/didn't** and the main verb snaps back to
  **base** — *did you **go**?*, *we **didn't approve***. Never *did + went*.
- **Unit 013 — I was doing (past continuous).** *was/were + -ing* for an action
  **in progress** at a past moment — a **line**, not a dot. Neg./questions stay
  on *was/were* (never *did*); state verbs (*know, want*) don't go continuous.
- **Unit 014 — I was doing **and** I did.** Choosing between the two in one
  story: the **continuous** paints the background (what was going on), the
  **simple** delivers the events (what happened). *While I **was presenting**,
  she **interrupted**.*

## 2. 🗺️ Confusion map — when each form wins

The whole week turns on **four decisions**. Get these four reflexes and every
past-tense sentence falls into place.

### Decision A — a **state** (*was/were*) or an **action** (past simple)?

| The sentence is about… | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **how** something **was** (a state, a feeling, a fact) | ***was / were*** | *The meeting **was** tense.* |
| **what** someone **did** (a finished action) | **past simple** | *The board **rejected** it.* |

The trap: fusing them. *be* describes a condition; it does **not** perform an
action. ❌ *The board **was rejected** the draft.* → it's either *was tense*
(state) **or** *rejected* (action), never *was* + a past action verb.

### Decision B — a completed event (past simple) or one in progress (past continuous)?

| | Past **simple** (*-ed / irregular*) | Past **continuous** (*was/were + -ing*) |
|---|---|---|
| Shows | a **complete** action | an action **in progress**, unfinished |
| Timeline | a **dot** (the end of it) | a **line** (the middle of it) |
| Role in a story | the **event** | the **background** |
| Signal words | *yesterday, then, at nine it happened* | *at 4 p.m., while, when X happened* |

Minimal pair — same words, different story:

- *When the CEO **arrived**, I **presented**.* → he arrived **first**, **then**
  I began. (two finished events, in sequence)
- *When the CEO **arrived**, I **was presenting**.* → I had **already started**;
  his arrival caught me **mid-flow**. (background + interruption)

Decide **each verb separately**: was it already unrolling (continuous), or did
it happen and finish (simple)? And remember: a **chain** of finished steps is
**all simple** — *"I arrived, opened the deck and started"*, never *"I was
arriving, was opening…"*.

### Decision C — where does the **past** live in a negative/question?

In the past simple, the tense sits on the helper **did**, so the main verb is
**base**. In *was/were* and the continuous, there is **no** *did* — the verb
itself (or *was/were*) carries it.

| | Carrier of the past | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Past simple neg./question | **did / didn't** + **base** verb | *Did you **speak**? / We **didn't go**.* |
| *was/were* neg./question | **was / were** (invert; no *did*) | ***Were** you happy? / It **wasn't** enough.* |
| Past continuous neg./question | **was / were** + **-ing** (no *did*) | ***Were** you **presenting**? / We **weren't reviewing**.* |

Two classic slips: ❌ *Did you **spoke**?* (never *did* + past — base only) and
❌ *Did you **presenting**?* (the continuous never borrows *did* — use *were*).

### Decision D — plain *have* vs *have got*, and *have got* vs *got* alone

| Contrast | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| plain **have** vs **have got** | same meaning; choose by **register** — plain *have* is neutral (board email), *have got* is spoken/informal | *Do we have approval?* / *Have we got approval?* |
| **have got** (present) vs **got** alone (past) | *have got* = possess **now**; *got* (no *have*) = **received/acquired**, a past action | *I've **got** a strong team* (now) vs *I **got** a new analyst last month* (past) |

Never mix the two possession systems: ❌ *Do you **have got** the numbers?* →
pick one — *Do you have…?* **or** *Have you got…?*.

## 3. 🧪 10 recognition exercises (contrast-focused)

Retrieve first — all answers with explanations are in section 6.

1. **State or action?** *"The kickoff ___ tense, so the CEO ___ the agenda on
   the spot."* — fill with **was/were** and an action verb like **changed**.
   Which slot gets each, and why can't one word do both?
2. **Simple or continuous?** *"When the vendor called, I ___ the investment
   model."* — **finished** or **was finishing**, if the call caught you
   **mid-task**? What would the other imply?
3. **Simple or continuous?** *"When the CEO arrived, we ___ the AI budget."* —
   **discussed** or **were discussing**, if the discussion was **already under
   way**?
4. **Fix the helper:** *"Did the board **approved** the AI budget?"*
5. **Fix the negative:** *"We **didn't went** to the board with a second
   option."*
6. **Fix the over-continuous chain:** *"I **was arriving**, **was opening** the
   deck and **was starting** the pitch."*
7. **was or were?** *"The quarterly results ___ below forecast, and there ___
   three open risks."*
8. **have or there is/are?** *"___ four vendors on the shortlist right now."*
   (Portuguese *"Tem quatro fornecedores…"*) — why can't it be *have*?
9. **have got or got?** *"Last month I ___ a new strategy analyst, and now I've
   ___ real bandwidth."* — which slot is a **past action**, which is **present
   possession**?
10. **do/does or did?** *"___ the board usually ___ big investments, but ___
    they ___ this one **yesterday**?"* — fill each with **Do/Does** or **Did** +
    the base verb, and say what forces the choice.

## 4. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

Bring the **most confusable contrasts**, not the individual forms:

- **The two-layer story (Decisions A + B) — the highest-value drill.** Ask your
  tutor to run an **"interruption vs sequence"** round: they give you two verbs
  (*present / interrupt*, *review / call*, *finalise / break*) plus a meaning —
  "she cut in **while** you were mid-flow" vs "she cut in **and then** you
  started" — and you build the sentence with the right mix, keeping the
  background continuous and the event simple. Then have them push you to narrate
  a **real board meeting** in 5–6 sentences, marking what *was going on* against
  what *happened*.
- **The *did + base* reflex (Decision C).** Have your tutor **interrogate you
  about last week** — rapid-fire *"Did you…? Why did…? Did the board…?"* — and
  you answer in short form first (*"No, they didn't"*) then a full sentence,
  never letting an *-ed* or a *went/spoke* leak back in after the helper. Add a
  **deny-and-correct** twist: they state something false (*"So you approved the
  contract"*) and you negate the English way — *"No, we **didn't approve** it"* —
  against the pull of the Portuguese *"não aprovamos"*.
- **Possession, neutral vs spoken (Decision D).** Ask your tutor to fire the
  same possession question in **both systems** — *"Do we have the numbers?" /
  "Have we got the numbers?"* — and you mirror each, then say which you'd put in
  a **board email** vs a **hallway chat**, so the register choice becomes a
  reflex and *"do you have got"* never appears.

## 5. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**O que já deve estar sólido.** A semana toda é o **passado**. Você tem agora o
passado do *to be* (**was/were** — *was* singular, *were* plural), o **past
simple** nas três formas (afirmativa com **-ed**/irregular, negativa e pergunta
com **did/didn't + verbo base**) e o **past continuous** (*was/were + -ing*)
para a ação **em andamento**. E o principal: sabe **combinar** os dois numa
história — o **contínuo** pinta o fundo (*o que estava acontecendo*), o
**simples** entrega os eventos (*o que aconteceu*): *"While I **was presenting**,
she **interrupted**"*. Some a isso a Unidade 009: **have** e **have got** dizem a
mesma posse, mudando só o registro (o *have* puro é o neutro dos e-mails).

**O que provavelmente ainda precisa de trabalho.** Quatro armadilhas costumam
sobreviver. **(1) did + verbo base**, jamais *did + passado*: *"Did you
**speak**?"*, nunca *"Did you spoke?"* — os irregulares (go, spend, speak) são os
que mais enganam porque "parecem passado". **(2)** O contínuo **nunca** usa
*did*: negativa e pergunta ficam em *was/were + -ing* (*"Were you presenting?"*,
não *"Did you presenting?"*). **(3)** Não inverta as camadas nem jogue tudo no
contínuo — o fundo é contínuo, o evento é simples, e uma **sequência** de ações
terminadas é toda no simples (*"cheguei, abri, comecei"* = *"I arrived, opened,
started"*). **(4)** O *ter* do português engana: **posse** → *have/have got*,
mas **existência** ("tem três riscos") → *there is/are*, e **idade** → *be* (*"I
**was** 30"*, não *"I had 30 years"*). Se um contraste ainda embola, **releia
dois arquivos**: `lessons/2026-07-26-unit-014.md` (simples vs contínuo na mesma
frase — o coração da semana) e `lessons/2026-07-24-unit-012.md` (o *did + base*).

## 6. ✅ Gabarito — exercises

1. **"The kickoff was tense, so the CEO changed the agenda on the spot."** —
   *"was" preenche o slot de **estado** (como o kickoff estava); "changed"
   preenche o de **ação** (o que o CEO fez). "be" descreve uma condição, não
   executa a ação — por isso um verbo só não cobre os dois.*
2. **was finishing** — *"When the vendor called, I **was finishing** the model"
   significa que você estava **no meio da tarefa** quando a ligação chegou. "I
   finished" diria que você terminou **depois** da ligação — uma sequência, não
   uma interrupção.*
3. **were discussing** — *a discussão já estava **em andamento** quando o CEO
   entrou. "we discussed" diria que ele chegou **primeiro** e **depois** vocês
   começaram — dois eventos em sequência.*
4. ❌ *"Did the board approved…"* → ✅ **"Did the board approve the AI budget?"**
   — *o "did" já carrega o passado, então o verbo principal volta à base
   "approve"; nunca marque o passado duas vezes.*
5. ❌ *"didn't went"* → ✅ **"We didn't go to the board with a second option."** —
   *depois de "didn't" o verbo é base "go", nunca o passado "went".*
6. ❌ *"was arriving… was opening… was starting"* → ✅ **"I arrived, opened the
   deck and started the pitch."** — *uma sequência de passos concluídos é toda no
   past simple; nada aí era um fundo em andamento.*
7. **were** / **were** — *"results" é plural → **were**; "three open risks" é
   plural → **there were**. "was" só entra com sujeito singular.*
8. ❌ *"Have four vendors…"* → ✅ **"There are four vendors on the shortlist right
   now."** — *isto é **existência** (o "tem" = there is/are), não posse; "have"
   precisaria de um dono como sujeito.*
9. **got** / **got** — ✅ **"Last month I got a new strategy analyst, and now
   I've got real bandwidth."** — *o primeiro "got" (sem have) é **ação passada**
   (adquiri); o segundo "'ve got" é **posse no presente** (tenho agora) — mesma
   palavra, dois sentidos.*
10. **"Does the board usually approve big investments, but did they approve this
    one yesterday?"** — *"usually" = hábito → **does** (presente); "yesterday" =
    evento terminado → **did** (passado). O verbo base "approve" fica igual; só o
    auxiliar muda para marcar o tempo.*
