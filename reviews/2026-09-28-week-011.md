# 🔁 Review Week 011 — Units 72 to 77

This week was one connected story: **how English attaches a describing idea onto a noun**. You
started with the basic relative clause (*who / that / which*), learned when the pronoun can vanish,
added the special words (*whose / where / whom*), split defining from extra-information clauses (the
comma that changes meaning), then **compressed** the whole thing into a participle (*-ing / -ed*
clauses) — and finished with the twin adjectives (*exciting* vs *excited*) that run on the exact
same *-ing = doer / -ed = receiver* logic.

Almost every trap this week is a **choose-between-rivals** decision, and that's how we'll
consolidate: by contrast. One core idea ties five of the six units together — **is the noun the
DOER or the RECEIVER of the action?** Hold on to that and the week collapses into a single instinct.

---

## 2. 📋 Recap — one line per unit

- **Unit 072 — relative clauses 1 (subject: who / that / which).** A person → *who*, a thing → *which*,
  either (defining) → *that*; when the pronoun **does the verb**, you **keep** it.
- **Unit 073 — relative clauses 2 (object relative & dropping).** When a **new subject** follows the
  pronoun, the pronoun is the **object** and you can **drop** it: *the vendor **(that) we chose***.
- **Unit 074 — whose / where / whom.** *whose* = possession (+ a noun), *where* = a real place (it
  swallows the preposition), *whom* = the formal object form for people.
- **Unit 075 — extra-information (non-defining) clauses.** With **commas**: never *that*, never drop the
  pronoun; use **which** to comment on a whole idea.
- **Unit 076 — reduced relative clauses (-ing / -ed).** Delete *who is / that was* → a participle:
  **-ing = active** (noun does it), **-ed = passive** (noun receives it).
- **Unit 077 — -ing vs -ed adjectives.** Same logic on feelings: **-ing = the cause** (*exciting
  news*), **-ed = the person's feeling** (*I'm excited*).

## 3. 🗺️ Confusion map — when each rival wins

This week has **three** decisions that recur. Master these three and the whole week is yours.

### Decision 1 — Keep the pronoun, or drop it? (Units 72 vs 73)

Look at the **word right after** the relative pronoun.

| What follows the pronoun | The pronoun is… | Do this | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| a **verb** | the **subject** | **keep it** | *the leader **who owns** the portfolio* |
| a **new subject** (we, she, the CFO…) | the **object** | **drop it** (optional) | *the leader **(who) we promoted*** |

One test, every time: **verb next → keep; new subject next → drop.**

### Decision 2 — Which relative word? (Units 72 & 74)

| You need to express… | Word | Not this | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| a **person**, subject | **who** / *that* | *which* | animacy: people are never *which* |
| a **thing / idea**, subject | **which** / *that* | *who* | things are never *who* |
| **possession** (something belongs to the noun) | **whose** + noun | *that its…* | only *whose* shows belonging |
| a **real place** as the anchor | **where** | *which* (+ prep) | *where* already carries *in/at* |
| an **object**, person, formal | **whom** | *who* | *who* is for the subject |

Quick checks: *whose or who's?* → expand to "who is"; if it fits, it's *who's*. *where or which?* → is
the noun a place you could **stand in**? A *decision / meeting / situation* is **not** a place → use
*which / in which*, never *where*.

### Decision 3 — Commas or no commas? (Unit 75) — the meaning-changer

Delete the clause: do you still know **which** noun we mean?

| If deleting the clause… | Commas? | Word | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| leaves *which one?* unanswered (it **identifies**) | **no** | who / that / which / — | *the exec **who owns delivery** presented* |
| loses nothing (noun already unique; clause just **adds**) | **yes** | who / which / whose / where — **never** *that*, **never** drop | *Priya**,** who owns delivery**,** presented* |
| comments on the **whole sentence** | **yes** | **which** (never *what*) | *the deal closed early**,** which impressed the board* |

The comma does strategic work: *"My colleagues **who work in AI** back it"* (only the AI ones) vs
*"My colleagues**,** who work in AI**,** back it"* (all of them — and they all work in AI).

### The through-line — Decision 4 — DOER or RECEIVER? (Units 76 & 77)

The one idea under both -ing/-ed units:

| The noun / person… | Ending | Relative clause (76) | Adjective (77) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **does** the action / **causes** the feeling | **-ing** | the team **building** the model | the plan is **exciting** |
| **receives** the action / **has** the feeling | **-ed** | the budget **approved** by the board | the board is **excited** |

Same one letter, opposite meaning: *the consultant **advising** the board* (gives advice) vs *the board
**advised** by the consultant* (gets advice); *"the CFO is **frustrating**"* (he's the problem) vs
*"the CFO is **frustrated**"* (he feels it).

## 4. 🧪 10 recognition exercises (mostly contrast — ~6 minutes)

Retrieve first. Answers with explanations in section 7.

**1. (keep / drop — Unit 72 vs 73)** Which is more natural, and can the pronoun be dropped?
   *"The vendor ___ scored highest won the deal."* — is this a keep case or a drop case?

**2. (who / which — Unit 72)** *"The strategist ___ framed our AI thesis is presenting."* → **who** or
   **which**?

**3. (whose / who's — Unit 74)** *"The exec ___ team shipped the pilot got promoted."* → **whose** or
   **who's**?

**4. (where / which — Unit 74)** *"That was a decision ___ we got the sequencing wrong."* → **where** or
   **in which**? Why can't it be *where*?

**5. (commas — Unit 75)** Which meaning?
   (a) *"Our engineers who work on the model are stretched."*
   (b) *"Our engineers, who work on the model, are stretched."*
   In which sentence are **all** the engineers stretched?

**6. (that after a comma — Unit 75)** Fix it: *"Our CFO, that joined in May, backs the plan."*

**7. (which for a whole idea — Unit 75)** Fix it: *"They approved the full budget, what surprised
   everyone."*

**8. (-ing / -ed reduced clause — Unit 76)** Choose: *"The strategy ___ by the board is bold."* →
   **deciding** or **decided**?

**9. (-ing adjective vs full verb — Unit 76)** Fix it: *"The engineers built the model are overloaded."*

**10. (-ing / -ed adjective — Unit 77)** Choose both: *"I'm very ___ in the partnership, and the
   roadmap is genuinely ___."* → interested/interesting for each blank.

## 5. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula (3 themes worth practising)

- **The keep-or-drop reflex (Units 72–73).** Ask your tutor to feed you noun phrases about your team
  and portfolio and have you build a relative clause on each *out loud* — half where the pronoun stays
  (*"the exec who owns delivery"*) and half where it drops (*"the vendor we chose"*). Speed is the goal:
  the *verb-next → keep / subject-next → drop* test should become automatic.
- **whose and where under pressure (Unit 74).** These are the two Brazilians dodge most. Have your tutor
  role-play a board Q&A and force answers using *whose* (*"the vendor **whose** platform we chose"*) and
  correct place-vs-abstract *where* (*"the offsite **where** we decided"* vs *"a decision **in which** we
  erred"*). Practise the *cujo → whose* jump so it stops feeling bookish.
- **The DOER/RECEIVER instinct (Units 76–77).** This is the highest-value one and the most error-prone.
  Ask your tutor to describe situations and have you react with the correct pair: *"The results are
  **exciting**; the board is **excited**"*; *"the budget **approved** by the board"* vs *"the team
  **approving** the budget."* Drill the two famous slips until they're gone: **"I'm interested in AI"**
  (not *interesting*) and **"I'm confused"** (not *confusing*).

## 6. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

Esta semana foi **um assunto só**: como o inglês gruda uma ideia descritiva num substantivo. **(1)** O
pronome relativo se divide onde o português usa só *que*: pessoa → **who**, coisa → **which**, ambos
(restritiva) → **that**. Se um **verbo** vem logo depois, o pronome é sujeito e **fica**; se vem um
**novo sujeito** (we, she, o CFO), ele é objeto e você pode **derrubá-lo** (*the vendor we chose*) — algo
que o português nunca faz. **(2)** Para posse use **whose** (= *cujo*, uma palavra só, sem *"that its…"*)
e para lugar **real** use **where** (que já contém a preposição — nunca *"where … in"*). **(3)** A
vírgula muda o sentido: sem vírgula a oração **identifica** qual; com vírgula só **acrescenta** um fato —
e aí **nunca** use *that* nem derrube o pronome; para comentar a frase inteira use **which**, nunca
*what*.

O que provavelmente ainda precisa de treino: **(a)** o reflexo de **derrubar** o pronome — traduzindo do
português você tende a repetir *that* sempre, deixando o inglês pesado, e às vezes deixa um objeto
duplicado (*"the report that the CFO liked **it**"*). **(b)** **whose/where**: reaproprie-se do *cujo* e
não use *where* para coisas abstratas (*"a decision **in which** we erred"*, não *"where"*). **(c)** O
maior perigo é o eixo **-ing / -ed** das Units 76–77: **-ing = quem faz / a causa** (*the budget
approving* ❌ → *the team approving*), **-ed = quem recebe / sente** (*the budget **approved***; *I'm
**interested***, não *interesting*; *I'm **confused***, não *confusing*). Se travar, **releia as Units
074 (whose/where) e 077 (-ing vs -ed)** — são as duas que mais escapam.

## 7. ✅ Gabarito (respostas + explicação em uma linha)

1. **Keep it** — *"the vendor **that/who** scored highest"*. A verb (*scored*) follows the pronoun → it's
   the **subject** → cannot be dropped. (Unit 72/73 test: verb next → keep.)
2. **who** — a strategist is a **person**; *which* is only for things. (Unit 72: animacy decides.)
3. **whose** — it shows possession (*the exec's team*) and is followed by a noun. Test: "who is team
   shipped"? No → it's **whose**, not *who's*. (Unit 74.)
4. **in which** — a *decision* is **abstract**, not a place you can stand in, so *where* is wrong (that's
   the *onde* interference). More natural still: *"a decision we got wrong."* (Unit 74.)
5. **(b)** — with commas, the clause is **extra info**, so **all** the engineers are stretched (and they
   all work on the model). In (a), no commas → **only** the model engineers are stretched. (Unit 75.)
6. *"Our CFO, **who** joined in May, backs the plan."* — after a comma, **that is banned**; use *who*.
   (Unit 75.)
7. *"They approved the full budget, **which** surprised everyone."* — to comment on the **whole sentence**
   use *which*, never *what*. (Unit 75.)
8. **decided** — the strategy **receives** the action (it was decided *by the board*) → passive → **-ed**.
   *deciding* would mean the strategy is the doer. (Unit 76.)
9. *"The engineers **building** the model are overloaded."* — the engineers **do** the building → active →
   **-ing**. *"engineers built the model"* reads as a full past-tense verb and breaks the sentence.
   (Unit 76.)
10. **interested** / **interesting** — *you* have the feeling → **-ed** (*"I'm interested"*; *interesting*
    would call yourself fascinating); the *roadmap* **causes** the feeling → **-ing** (*"genuinely
    interesting"*). (Unit 77.)
