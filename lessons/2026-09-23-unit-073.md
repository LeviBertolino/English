### 1. 📘 Unit 073 — relative clauses 2: the object relative (and dropping it)

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** Remind me — which platform did we land on?
> **You:** The one **we chose** after the security review. It's the vendor **the CFO pushed back on**
> at first, but the numbers won her over.
> **CEO:** And the deck?
> **You:** The version **the board approved** last quarter. I dropped the two slides **nobody read**.
> **CFO:** Good. The decision **I'd never delegate** is the timing — let's lock it today.
> **You:** Agreed. That's the one call **we can't get wrong**.

Notice: after *the one, the vendor, the version, the two slides, the decision, the one call*, a
relative clause follows — but this time there's **no *who / that / which*** in front of it. *"The one
**we chose**," "the vendor **the CFO pushed back on**," "the slides **nobody read**."* Each of those
missing words could be put back (*the one **that** we chose*), yet the sentence sounds more natural
**without** it. That's the whole unit: when the relative pronoun is the **object** of its clause, you
can leave it out.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

In Unit 072 the relative pronoun was the **subject** — it did the verb (*the report **that**
convinced them*), so it **had** to stay. Here the relative pronoun is the **object** — someone
**else** does the verb, and the pronoun just receives the action. When that happens, you can
**drop** it.

| The pronoun is… | Can you drop it? | Example |
|---|---|---|
| the **subject** (a verb comes right after it) | **No — keep it** | *the vendor **that** scored highest* |
| the **object** (a new subject comes right after it) | **Yes — optional** | *the vendor **(that) we** chose* |

**How to see which one you have — the one-second test:** look at the word right **after** the
relative pronoun.
- If a **verb** comes next → the pronoun is the **subject** → **keep it** (*the deal **that closed***).
- If a **new subject** (a noun / pronoun like *we, she, the board*) comes next → the pronoun is the
  **object** → you may **drop it** (*the deal **(that) she** closed*).

**Key facts:**
- Dropping is only for **defining** clauses (no commas) — the everyday case.
- With a **preposition**, English normally leaves it at the **end**: *the platform **(that) we
  migrated to**, the person **(who) I spoke to**.* Never repeat the object after that preposition.
- **whom** is the formal object form for people (*the candidate **whom** we hired*) — correct but stiff;
  in speech, drop it or use *that/who*.

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (a seção central)

**Why does this "dropping" exist?** English lets you strip out a word that carries **no new
information**. When the pronoun is the object, the listener already knows *which* noun you mean from
the noun itself — so *that/which/who* is doing nothing but taking up space, and native speakers
delete it. *"The vendor we chose"* is cleaner and more natural than *"the vendor that we chose."* Both
are correct; the short one is what a fluent speaker reaches for.

**Decision rule vs the rival — subject relative (Unit 072) vs object relative (this unit).** This is
the whole game, and the one-second test above settles it every time:

| Subject relative — **keep** the pronoun | Object relative — **drop** it |
|---|---|
| *the leader **who owns** the portfolio* (verb *owns* follows) | *the leader **(who) we promoted*** (subject *we* follows) |
| *the report **that convinced** the board* | *the report **(that) the CFO liked*** |
| *the pilot **that missed** its KPI* | *the pilot **(that) we shut down*** |

Same nouns, opposite rule. Ask only: **verb next → keep; new subject next → drop.**

**PT→EN mapping — where this quietly betrays Levi.** Portuguese **never** drops *que*: *"o fornecedor
**que** escolhemos," "o relatório **que** o CFO gostou," "a decisão **que** eu tomei."* The *que* is
obligatory. So the interference goes **two ways**:
- You **over-insert** *that*: *"the vendor **that** we chose"* isn't wrong, but doing it every time
  makes your English sound heavy and translated. Learning to **drop** it is what lifts you toward
  natural.
- Worse, you copy the Portuguese habit of **leaving a second object pronoun** in the clause — the
  *"que … ele/isso"* echo: *"the report that the CFO liked **it**."* In English the relative pronoun
  **is** the object, so a second one is a doubling error.

**Top 3 typical Brazilian-speaker errors:**
- ❌ *"The report **that the CFO liked it** ran six pages."* → ✅ *"The report **(that) the CFO
  liked** ran six pages."* *(the relative pronoun already IS the object — never add "it".)*
- ❌ *"The candidate **which we hired** started Monday."* → ✅ *"The candidate **(who / that) we
  hired** started Monday."* *(a person → who/that, never which — even when it's droppable.)*
- ❌ *"The leader **we promoted her** owns delivery"* / dropping in the wrong place — ❌ *"The leader
  **owns** the portfolio is Priya."* → ✅ *"The leader **who owns** the portfolio is Priya."*
  *(here the pronoun is the SUBJECT — verb "owns" follows — so it can't be dropped.)*

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. *"The platform **we chose** cleared every security gate."*
   *— object relative: subject "we" follows, so "that/which" is dropped. Natural, board-ready English.*
2. *"The report **the CFO liked** ran only six pages."*
   *— "the CFO" is a new subject → object relative → drop the pronoun; adding "it" at the end would be the "que…ele" error.*
3. *"The vendor **that scored highest** on security won the deal."*
   *— contrast: a VERB ("scored") follows, so the pronoun is the SUBJECT and must stay (this is Unit 072).*
4. *"The two slides **nobody read** are gone from the deck."*
   *— "nobody" is the subject of "read" → object relative → droppable; the noun already tells us which slides.*
5. *"She's the strategist **whom the board trusts** most on AI."*
   *— formal object form for a person; correct in a written memo, but in speech you'd just say "the strategist the board trusts most."*
6. *"The platform **we migrated to** last year still can't scale."*
   *— the preposition "to" stays at the end and the pronoun is dropped; never "the platform to which we migrated it."*
7. *"The house **we rented** for the summer had a terrible kitchen."*
   *— everyday life: object relative, pronoun dropped; "we" is the subject doing the renting.*
8. *"That's the one decision **I'd never delegate**."*
   *— "I" is the subject → object relative → drop; the clause pins down exactly which decision you mean.*

### 8. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

1. Ask your tutor to run a **"drop the *that*" drill**: they give you a heavy sentence with the
   pronoun in (*"the vendor **that** we chose," "the report **which** the CFO liked," "the deck
   **that** the board approved"*) and you say the natural version back with it **removed**. Then flip
   it: they give a **subject** relative (*"the leader **who** owns delivery"*) and you must **keep**

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 072):**
1. **who / which** → *"the strategist **who** framed the thesis"* (a **person**) and *"the strategy
   **which** framed our year"* (a **thing**). **Animacy** decides: person → *who*, thing/idea →
   *which*.
2. **that** → *"Everything **that** we promised…"* After *everything / all / the thing*, English uses
   **that**, never **what** — the *"tudo que"* reflex is what pushes Brazilians to *what*.
3. **No** — *she* does **not** belong: *"The CFO **who** owns the numbers pushed back."* *who* is
   already the subject, so adding *she* double-marks it (the *"que ela"* habit).

**Spaced review:**
- **Unit 066:** *"We **don't** have **any** benchmark"* **or** *"We have **no** benchmark."* English
   allows **one** negative per clause — on the verb (*don't … any*) **or** on the noun (*no*) — never
   both, so *"don't have no benchmark"* (the Portuguese *não…nenhum* stack) is wrong.
- **Unit 042:** **prioritizing** → *"I recommend **prioritizing** the two bets."* *recommend* (like
   *suggest*) takes **-ing**, never *to*; the *recomendar + infinitivo* reflex produces the wrong
   *"recommend to prioritize."*

**Recognition drill:**
1. You can drop it in **"the vendor (that) we chose"** — a **new subject** (*we*) follows, so the
   pronoun is the **object** → droppable. In *"the vendor that scored highest,"* a **verb** (*scored*)
   follows → the pronoun is the **subject** → it must stay.
2. Delete **it**: *"The roadmap **(that) the board approved** is now live."* The relative pronoun is
   already the object of *approved*; a second *it* is the *"que … ele/isso"* doubling error.
3. **that** — a *candidate* is a **person**, so *which* is impossible; *that* (or *who*) is correct,
   and since *we* follows, you could also drop it entirely (*"the candidate we hired"*).
4. **No — keep it.** A **verb** (*built*) comes right after, so the pronoun is the **subject**:
   *"The consultant **who / that** built the model is presenting first."* The one-second test (verb
   next → keep) decides it.

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

Quando o pronome relativo é o **objeto** da oração (alguém **outro** faz o verbo), o inglês permite
**omiti-lo**: *"the vendor **(that) we chose**," "the report **(that) the CFO liked**."* Isso é o
oposto da Unit 072, onde o pronome era o **sujeito** e **não** podia sair (*"the report **that
convinced** them"*). O teste de um segundo: olhe a palavra **depois** do pronome — se vier um
**verbo**, o pronome é sujeito → **mantenha**; se vier um **novo sujeito** (*we, she, the board*), o
pronome é objeto → **pode cair**. Preposições ficam no **fim** (*"the platform **we migrated to**"*),
e *whom* é a forma formal de objeto para pessoas (*"the candidate whom we hired"*), rara na fala.

A armadilha vem do português, que **nunca** derruba o *que* (*"o fornecedor **que** escolhemos"*).
Dois efeitos: (1) você **põe *that* toda vez**, deixando o inglês pesado e "traduzido" — aprender a
**cortar** o pronome é o que soa nativo; e (2) o pior, você copia o eco *"que … ele/isso"* e deixa um
**segundo objeto** na oração: *"the report that the CFO liked **it**"* → correto é *"the report the
CFO liked."* E lembre: mesmo quando o pronome é omissível, pessoa continua sendo **who/that**, nunca
*which*. Se a diferença sujeito × objeto ainda embolar, releia `lessons/2026-09-22-unit-072.md`.
