# 🔁 Review Week 016 — Units 107 to 112

**Theme of the week: grading, comparing, and setting limits.** Six units that all answer one executive question — *how much, compared to what, and is it enough or too much?* This is the language of thresholds, go/no-go calls, and head-to-head reads. The traps nearly all trace back to one Portuguese word doing too many jobs: **muito**.

Read-only consolidation (~12 min). No writing tasks — the goal is to feel *which structure wins* when two of them compete for the same sentence.

---

## 1. Recap — one line per unit

- **Unit 107 — *too* / *enough* (degree & insufficiency):** *too* = more than it should be (a problem); *enough* = as much as needed — and *enough* sits **before** a noun (*enough time*) but **after** an adjective (*good enough*).
- **Unit 108 — *quite · rather · fairly · pretty*:** the dials **below** *very* for the middle ground — *fairly* (modest), *quite* (neutral middle), *rather* (with a frown/surprise), *pretty* (informal) — plus the *quite*-split: *quite good* = moderately, *quite sure* = completely.
- **Unit 109 — strengthening comparatives:** put a **size** on a comparison — *much / far / a lot* (big gap), *a bit / slightly* (small), *even* (bigger still) — plus *bigger and bigger* and *the sooner, the better*. Booster for a comparative is **much/far**, never *very*.
- **Unit 110 — comparisons of equality:** *as … as* (equal), *not as … as* (falls short, keeps the other as benchmark), *the same as* (match). Base adjective inside *as … as*; *same* always takes *as*.
- **Unit 111 — superlatives:** rank **one** thing against a **whole group** — *the biggest*, *the most important*, *the best*. *the* is obligatory; places/groups take **in** (not *of*); top-of-all-time takes present perfect + *ever*.
- **Unit 112 — *enough* / *too* (thresholds):** the same two words as Unit 107, framed as **go/no-go** — *strong enough to explore*, *too risky to close*. The core line to burn in: **too ≠ very**.

## 2. 🗺️ Confusion map — when each one wins

The whole week is a fight between structures that English keeps separate but Portuguese blurs. Six decisions settle almost everything:

### A. Grading ONE adjective: *fairly → quite → rather → very → too*

| You mean… | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| decent, deliberately modest (below *very*) | **fairly** | *fairly strong* |
| the calm neutral middle | **quite** (+ gradable) | *quite risky* |
| more than you'd like / a surprise | **rather** | *rather expensive* |
| genuine strong praise, neutral high degree | **very / really** | *very strong* |
| **more than it should be — a problem** | **too** | *too expensive* |

> **The hinge: *very* vs *too*.** *very strong* = a compliment or neutral fact. *too strong* = a problem, a blocker, a reason to stop. If the sentence is praise → *very*; if it's a complaint → *too*. (Units 108 + 112.)

### B. Boosting a COMPARATIVE: *much / far* — never *very*

- base adjective → **very**: *very good, very fast*
- comparative → **much / far / a lot** (big), **a bit / slightly** (small), **even** (bigger still): *much better, far more scalable, a bit slower*
- ❌ *very better · very more scalable* — these simply aren't English. (Unit 109.)

> **The hinge: is the word base or comparative?** *very* only touches a **base** adjective; the moment it becomes *better / more …*, the booster switches to *much / far / a lot*.

### C. Ranking: equal vs two vs the whole group

| How many things? | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| two things, **level** | **as … as** (base adj) | *as scalable as ours* |
| two things, one **falls short** | **not as … as** | *not as mature as we are* |
| two things, **ranked** | comparative *-er / more … than* | *stronger than Q2* |
| one vs a **whole group (3+)** | superlative *the …est / the most* | *the strongest quarter of the year* |
| two things **match** | **the same as** | *the same as last year* |

> **The hinge: count the things.** Two → comparative (or *as … as*). One against everything → superlative (and don't forget **the**). (Units 110 + 111.)

### D. *enough* — position flips around its word
*enough* **before** a noun (*enough data*), **after** an adjective (*mature enough*). Never *enough mature*, never *time enough*. (Units 107 + 112.)

### E. The preposition tells on you
- superlative + place/group → **in**: *the biggest player **in** the market* (not *of*)
- superlative + set/number/time → **of**: *the best **of** the three*
- *the same* → always **as** (never *than / of*)
- *not as … as* → both *as*'s, nothing swapped. (Units 110 + 111.)

### F. The one Portuguese word behind most errors: *muito*
*muito* splits three ways in English — pick by meaning:
- neutral/compliment, base adj → **very** (*muito forte* = *very strong*)
- excess/problem → **too** (*caro demais* = *too expensive*)
- in front of a comparative → **much / far** (*muito melhor* = *much better*)

## 3. 🧪 10 recognition exercises (mostly contrast)

Answers with one-line explanations at the end. Decide first — most of these force a choice between two rivals from the map above.

1. **(very vs too)** Levi praises a result: *"Our Q3 numbers were ___ strong — the board was thrilled."* Fill with *very* or *too*, and say what the wrong one would accidentally imply.

2. **(very vs much — base vs comparative)** Choose and justify: *"The new platform is (**very** / **much**) more scalable than our current stack."* Why does the other option break?

3. **(too much / too many / too)** Complete all three slots: *"There's ___ risk in phase one, ___ dependencies on one vendor, and the whole thing is ___ fragile."*

4. **(comparative vs superlative — count the things)** Choose: *"Of all our 2027 bets, AI infrastructure is (**a more defensible** / **the most defensible**) one."* What phrase in the sentence decides it?

5. **(as … as — base form + the frame)** Fix the error: *"Their model is **as more accurate as** ours."* Give the corrected sentence and name the rule.

6. **(not as … as vs comparative — meaning check)** *"The challenger is **not as mature as** we are."* Who is more mature — and rewrite the same fact as a plain comparative.

7. **(the *quite*-split)** In which phrase does *quite* mean **completely** — *"the forecast is **quite good**"* or *"I'm **quite certain** we'll close"*? What feature of the adjective decides it?

8. **(enough placement)** Fix: *"The team isn't **enough big** to integrate another product, and we don't have ___ (enough) engineers either."* Correct the misplaced one and place *enough* right in the second slot.

9. **(the *do mundo* / *do mercado* trap — in vs of)** Fix: *"We want to be the biggest cloud provider **of** Latin America and the strongest player **of** the market."*

10. **(the same + as; and *too … to* vs *… enough to*)** Two fixes in one: *"Our run costs are **the same than** last year, and the deal is mature **enough close** this quarter."*

## 4. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

Three contrasts from this week are the most worth drilling live with your tutor, because each is a reflex a Portuguese speaker has to overwrite:

1. **The *very* / *too* / *much* triangle (the *muito* reflex).** Ask your tutor to feed you ten statements from your world (*"the market is ___ competitive," "this plan is ___ better than the old one," "the results were ___ strong"*) and you must pick *very*, *too*, or *much/far* on the fly — then justify in one line. Rule: *too* only for a real problem, *much/far* only in front of a comparative. Have them buzz you the instant *muito* leaks through as the wrong one.

2. **Equal vs ranked vs top-of-group (a head-to-head round).** They name two things (our platform vs the challenger, this quarter vs last) and you describe them three ways: *as … as* (level), *not as … as / more … than* (one wins), and — adding a third rival — a full superlative (*the strongest of the three*, *the biggest in the market*). The enforced rule: base adjective inside *as … as*, **the** + *in* on every superlative.

3. **Go / no-go with *enough* and *too*.** They pitch a decision; you answer in two halves — one *strong enough to …* (why it's ready), one *too risky to …* (why it's blocked) — keeping *enough* **after** each adjective and *too* reserved for the blocker.

## 5. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**O que deve estar sólido:** o eixo da semana é *quanto, comparado a quê, e é suficiente ou demais?* A forma já deve sair natural — *as … as* para igualdade, *-er/more … than* para comparar dois, *the …est/the most* para o topo de um grupo, *enough/too* para limite. E o mapa de uma palavra só: **muito** vira **very** (neutro/elogio), **too** (demais/problema) ou **much/far** (na frente de comparativo).

**O que provavelmente ainda precisa de trabalho** (os erros de interferência que mais aparecem nesta semana): (1) *too* usado como elogio — *too* é sempre problema, elogio é *very*; (2) *very better / very more scalable* — com comparativo o reforço é *much/far*, nunca *very*; (3) *do mundo/do mercado → "of"* — lugares e grupos levam **in** (*the biggest in the world*); (4) *the same than/of* — *same* só aceita **as**; (5) *enough* no lugar errado — depois do adjetivo (*mature enough*), antes do substantivo (*enough data*); (6) o *quite*-split — *quite good* = moderadamente, mas *quite sure* = completamente.

## ✅ Gabarito — respostas dos 10 exercícios

1. **very** — *"very strong — the board was thrilled."* A compliment needs the neutral high degree. *too strong* would mean *excessively* strong (a problem), contradicting "thrilled."
2. **much** — *more scalable* is a **comparative**, so it takes *much / far / a lot*. *very* only sits in front of a **base** adjective (*very scalable*), never a comparative — *"very more scalable"* isn't English.
3. **too much** risk (uncountable), **too many** dependencies (countable plural), **too** fragile (adjective, no noun). Match *much/many* to the noun; plain *too* before an adjective.
4. **the most defensible** — the phrase *"of all our 2027 bets"* sets a whole group (3+), so you rank one at the top → superlative. A comparative would only beat a single rival.
5. **"as accurate as ours"** — inside *as … as* the adjective must be **base** form; *more accurate* is a comparative and can't sit there. (Want the comparative? Drop the frame: *more accurate than ours.*)
6. **We are more mature.** *not as mature as we are* means the challenger falls short, with us as the benchmark. As a comparative: *"The challenger is **less mature than** we are."*
7. **"quite certain"** — there *quite* means **completely**, because *certain* is a **non-gradable/absolute** adjective. In *"quite good,"* *good* is **gradable**, so *quite* means only *moderately*. The adjective type decides it.
8. **"big enough"** (enough **after** the adjective) and **"enough engineers"** (enough **before** the noun). Same word, opposite position depending on what it modifies.
9. **in** both times — *"the biggest cloud provider **in** Latin America and the strongest player **in** the market."* Places and groups take **in**; the Portuguese *da América Latina / do mercado* is the trap that pulls you to *of*.
10. **"the same as last year"** (*same* always takes *as*, never *than*) and **"mature enough to close"** (*adjective + enough + to* — the *to* tail names what the maturity allows).
