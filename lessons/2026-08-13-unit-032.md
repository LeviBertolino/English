### 1. 📘 Unit 032 — must, mustn't, don't need to

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** Before the board vote, what's non-negotiable?
> **You:** Two things. We **must** show a clear ROI horizon, and we **mustn't**
> promise savings we can't defend — that's the one line we can't cross.
> **CEO:** And the vendor benchmark deck?
> **You:** You **don't need to** read all forty slides. The summary covers it —
> you **needn't** open the appendix at all.
> **CEO:** Good. So I **must** focus on the ROI slide and can skip the rest.
> **You:** Exactly.

Notice the pattern before any rule. *must* marks what is **necessary / an
obligation** (*we must show ROI*). *mustn't* is **not** the simple opposite —
it means it's necessary **not** to do it, a hard line you can't cross (*we
mustn't promise what we can't defend*). And *don't need to / needn't* says
something completely different again: it's simply **not necessary** — no
obligation either way (*you don't need to read all forty slides*). Three ideas,
three shapes.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

All three take the **base verb** — no *to*, no *-s*, same for every subject.

| Meaning | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Necessary / obligation** | must + base | *We **must** show a clear ROI.* |
| **Necessary NOT to (forbidden)** | mustn't + base | *We **mustn't** overpromise.* |
| **Not necessary** | don't need to + base | *You **don't need to** read it all.* |
| **Not necessary** (formal twin) | needn't + base | *You **needn't** open the appendix.* |

- *must* is a modal, like *can* and *might*: **must + base verb**, never *must
  to*, never *musts*. Question form is rare but exists: *"Must we decide today?"*
- *mustn't* = *must not* contracted. It is a **prohibition** — the strong "don't
  you dare." It is **not** "it's not necessary."
- *don't need to* behaves like an ordinary verb (it takes *don't / doesn't*):
  *he **doesn't need to** join*. *needn't* is the modal twin — *needn't + base*,
  more formal, same meaning.
- **Key asymmetry:** the opposite of *must* (obligation) is **not** *mustn't* —
  it's *don't need to*. *mustn't* is the opposite of a **permission**.

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why does *must* exist?** It carries **strong necessity felt by the speaker** —
your own conviction that something has to happen, or a rule you're stating with
force. It's heavier than *should* (advice) and more personal than the neutral
*have to* you'll meet in Unit 34. When you say *"we must protect the brand,"*
you're not suggesting — you're drawing a line.

**Decision rule #1 — *mustn't* vs *don't need to* (the error that changes your
meaning entirely).** These feel like opposites of *must*, but only one is:

| *mustn't* = forbidden (necessary NOT to) | *don't need to / needn't* = not necessary |
|---|---|
| *"You **mustn't** share these numbers."* → sharing is **prohibited** | *"You **don't need to** share these numbers."* → sharing is **optional / unnecessary** |
| *"We **mustn't** miss the board deadline."* → missing it is a red line | *"We **needn't** rush the board deadline."* → no pressure to hurry |
| = *é proibido / não pode* | = *não precisa* |

Say the wrong one and you either **forbid** something you meant to make optional,
or **permit** something you meant to ban. This is the single most important thing
in the unit.

**Decision rule #2 — *must* vs *should* (obligation vs advice).** *should* (your
next unit) recommends; *must* obligates.
- *"We **should** benchmark two more vendors."* — good idea, still optional.
- *"We **must** benchmark two more vendors before signing."* — non-negotiable, a
  condition. Reach for *must* only when you mean the strong line.

**Decision rule #3 — *must* vs *have to* (a preview of Unit 34).** Both express
obligation, but the **source** differs: *must* is usually the **speaker's own**
authority or conviction (*"I must call the CFO today"* — I decide it matters);
*have to* points to an **outside** rule or circumstance (*"I have to file this
by law"*). In the negative they split completely — *mustn't* = forbidden,
*don't have to* = not necessary (same idea as *don't need to*).

**PT→EN mapping.** The trap is Portuguese **"não precisa"**:
- *"Você não precisa ler tudo"* → **"You don't need to read it all,"** **not**
  *"You mustn't read it all"* (that would mean reading is *forbidden*).
- *"Você não pode compartilhar esses números"* / *"É proibido…"* → **"You
  mustn't share these numbers."**
- *"Eu tenho que / preciso apresentar isso ao board"* → **"I must present this
  to the board"** — *tenho que / preciso* maps to *must* (base verb, no *to*).
- Watch *"não deve"*: Portuguese *"você não deve fazer isso"* usually means
  advice (*shouldn't*), but can sound like prohibition — in English pick
  deliberately: *shouldn't* (bad idea) vs *mustn't* (forbidden).

**Top Brazilian-speaker errors:**
- ❌ *"I must to prepare the board deck."* → ✅ *"I **must prepare** the board
  deck."* — *must* + **base verb**, never *must to*.
- ❌ *"You mustn't send the report today"* (meaning: it isn't necessary) → ✅
  *"You **don't need to** send the report today."* — *mustn't* forbids; use
  *don't need to* for "not necessary."
- ❌ *"He doesn't must join the call."* → ✅ *"He **doesn't need to** join the
  call."* — *must* has no *do/does*; for "not necessary" switch to *don't/doesn't
  need to*.

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. *"We **must** show a clear ROI horizon before the board votes."*
   *— Speaker's strong necessity: a condition for the vote, heavier than "should".*
2. *"We **mustn't** promise savings we can't defend."*
   *— Prohibition (necessary NOT to): a red line, not merely "unnecessary".*
3. *"You **don't need to** read all forty slides — the summary covers it."*
   *— Not necessary: reading is optional; note it does NOT forbid reading.*
4. *"You **needn't** open the appendix at all."*
   *— Same "not necessary" meaning as don't need to, one notch more formal.*
5. *"The AI budget **must** be approved before we sign any vendor."*
   *— Non-negotiable sequencing; must marks the hard condition, not a suggestion.*
6. *"We **mustn't** let the pilot leak before the announcement."*
   *— Forbidden action; don't need to would wrongly turn a ban into "optional".*
7. *"You **don't need to** cc the whole board — just the CFO is fine."*
   *— Removing an obligation, not creating a prohibition: ccing is simply unneeded.*
8. *"I **must** call the CFO today; the re-forecast can't wait."*
   *— Personal conviction/obligation from the speaker — classic must, base verb, no "to".*

### 6. 🧪 Recognition drill (4 items, ~2 minutes)

Answers in the Gabarito — decide first.

1. **Fix if wrong:** *"I must to align the AI roadmap with finance this week."*
2. **Rival choice (the core one):** *"These figures are confidential — you ___
   forward them to anyone outside the board."* — **mustn't** or **don't need
   to**? What changes if you pick the other?
3. **Not-necessary form:** *"He ___ join the 4 p.m. sync — the notes will cover

   *doesn't must join*.
4. **Meaning check:** Does *"You don't need to present the appendix"* **forbid**
   presenting the appendix, or just make it **optional**? Say why.

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 031 — can and could):**
1. **Not the past** — *"Could you send the board the revised numbers today?"* is
   a **polite request happening now**; *could* softens the ask, it doesn't move
   it into the past.
2. ***were able to*** — *"we finally **were able to** sign the vendor contract."*
   One **specific past success** takes *was able to / managed to*, not *could*
   (which fits only **general** past ability).
3. ***can*** — *"An unmanaged AI rollout **can** create real reputational risk."*
   This is a **general, standing** possibility ("this is a thing that happens"),
   which is *can*'s job; *might* would mean "maybe this specific time."

**Spaced review:**
- **(Unit 025)** *"There **didn't use to** be a governance layer."* — the
  negative is *didn't use to* + base, and after *did* the *-d* **drops**: *use
  to*, not *used to*.
- **(Unit 003)** *"We **are rolling out** the AI copilot… right now."* — present
  continuous (*are* + *-ing*); the phrase **right now** forces an in-progress
  action, so simple present *"we roll out"* would be wrong.

**Recognition drill:**
1. ❌ *must to* → ✅ *"I **must align** the AI roadmap with finance this week."*
   — *must* is a modal, followed by the **base verb**, never *must to*.
2. ***mustn't*** — *"you **mustn't** forward them to anyone outside the board."*
   The figures are confidential, so forwarding is **forbidden**. Pick *don't need
   to* and you'd say forwarding is merely *optional* — the opposite message.
3. ***doesn't need to join*** — "not necessary" for a third person takes
   *doesn't need to* + base. *mustn't join* would **forbid** it; *doesn't must*
   is impossible (*must* never takes *do/does*).
4. **Just optional.** *"You don't need to present the appendix"* removes the
   **obligation** — presenting is unnecessary but allowed. To **forbid** it you'd
   need *"You mustn't present the appendix."*

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**A regra e quando usar.** *must* + **verbo na base** marca **necessidade forte /
obrigação sentida por quem fala** — mais pesado que *should* (conselho) e mais
pessoal que *have to* (regra externa, Unit 34). Use *must* quando você traça uma
linha: *"We must show a clear ROI."* O ponto mais importante da unidade é que o
oposto de *must* **não** é *mustn't*: *mustn't* significa "é necessário **não**
fazer" — **proibição** (*é proibido / não pode*). Para dizer que algo **não é
necessário**, use *don't need to* / *needn't* (*não precisa*).

**As armadilhas.** (1) A cilada nº 1 é o *"não precisa"*: *"Você não precisa ler
tudo"* é *"You **don't need to** read it all"*, **jamais** *"You mustn't read it
all"* (isso proíbe a leitura). (2) Nunca *must to* nem *musts* — depois de *must*
vem o verbo puro. (3) *must* não aceita *do/does*: para a forma negativa de "não
precisa" troque para *doesn't need to* (*"He **doesn't need to** join"*), nunca
*"doesn't must"*. (4) Cuidado com *"não deve"*: em inglês escolha de propósito
entre *shouldn't* (má ideia) e *mustn't* (proibido). Se a diferença *must* vs
*could/can* embolar, releia `lessons/2026-08-12-unit-031.md`.
