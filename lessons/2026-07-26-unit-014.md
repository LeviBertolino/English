### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** So how did the AI investment case land with the board?
> **You:** Well — while I **was presenting** the roadmap, the CFO **interrupted**
> with the cost question. I **was still explaining** the three-year horizon when
> she **raised** it, so I **paused**, **answered** her directly, and then
> **finished** the slide.
> **CEO:** And they approved it?
> **You:** They **did**. By the time I **sat down**, the room **was already
> nodding**.

Notice the two pasts working *together*. The long backgrounds run on the
continuous — *I **was presenting***, *I **was still explaining***, *the room
**was already nodding***. The sharp, finished events cut in on the simple —
*she **interrupted***, *she **raised** it*, *I **paused, answered, finished***.
This unit is about choosing between them in the same sentence.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

Both tenses were built in Units 011–013. This unit is about **which one to
pick**. The forms, side by side:

| | Past simple | Past continuous |
|---|---|---|
| Form | base + **-ed** / irregular (*went, raised*) | **was/were** + verb**-ing** |
| Shows | a **complete** action (a dot) | an action **in progress** (a line) |
| Negative | *didn't* + base | *wasn't/weren't* + -ing |
| Question | *did* + subject + base | *was/were* + subject + -ing |

The three patterns this unit lives on:

| Pattern | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Interruption** | long *was -ing* + short *did* | *While I **was presenting**, she **interrupted**.* |
| **Sequence** | *did* then *did* | *I **paused**, **answered**, and **finished**.* |
| **Parallel** | *was -ing* + *was -ing* | *I **was talking** while she **was taking** notes.* |

**Key facts:**
- **while / as** usually introduce the **longer, continuous** background;
  **when** usually introduces the **short, simple** event. *While I was
  presenting…* / *…when she interrupted.*
- **Two simple pasts = a sequence** (one finished, **then** the next). **One
  continuous + one simple = an interruption** (the short one cuts into the
  long one).
- The continuous does **not** tell you the action finished; the simple does.
  *I was reading the report* (maybe unfinished) vs *I read the report* (done).

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why does this contrast exist?** A story needs two layers: a **background**
that was already unrolling, and the **events** that happen against it. The past
continuous paints the background (the line — *what was going on*); the past
simple delivers the events (the dots — *what happened*). Mixing them lets one
sentence say "this was in progress **when** that suddenly happened." Neither
tense alone can carry both layers.

**Decision rule — the two questions.** For each verb, ask:
1. Was this action **already in progress** at that moment, a *background*? →
   **past continuous** (*was/were + -ing*).
2. Did this action **happen and complete**, a single *event* or a step in a
   sequence? → **past simple**.

The trap is a sentence with two verbs. Decide **each verb separately**:

| Sentence | Longer/background → continuous | Short/event → simple |
|---|---|---|
| *While I **was presenting**, the CFO **interrupted**.* | was presenting | interrupted |
| *When the board **arrived**, we **started**.* | (neither — sequence) | arrived, started |

**Interruption vs sequence — the pair that changes meaning.** Same two verbs,
different tenses, different story:
- *When the CEO **arrived**, I **was presenting**.* → I had **already started**;
  his arrival caught me **mid-flow**. (background + interruption)
- *When the CEO **arrived**, I **presented**.* → he arrived **first**, **then**
  I began. (two finished events, in order)

- *While we **were discussing** the budget, the CFO **left**.* → the discussion
  was **ongoing**; she walked out **during** it.
- *We **discussed** the budget and the CFO **left**.* → discussion finished,
  **then** she left. Two closed events.

**PT→EN mapping.** Portuguese runs the same split, so the meanings transfer
cleanly: **estava apresentando** (*was presenting*, background) vs
**apresentei / apresentou** (*presented*, finished event). *"Enquanto eu estava
apresentando, o CFO interrompeu"* maps almost word-for-word. Two traps:
- **The Portuguese imperfect can hide the choice.** *"Quando o CEO chegou, eu
  apresentava"* — that *apresentava* is ambiguous in Portuguese, but English
  forces you to decide: *was presenting* (interruption) or *presented*
  (sequence)? You must pick the meaning, not the surface form.
- **Don't default everything to the continuous.** A plain chain of finished
  actions is **all simple past** — *"cheguei, abri o deck e comecei"* → *I
  **arrived**, **opened** the deck and **started***, not *I was arriving, was
  opening…*. Reserve the continuous for the action that was genuinely *in
  progress*.

**Top Brazilian-speaker errors:**
- ❌ *While I **presented**, the CFO **was interrupting**.* → ✅ *While I **was
  presenting**, the CFO **interrupted**.* — *the tenses are reversed: the
  presentation is the long background (continuous), the interruption is the
  short event (simple).*
- ❌ *When the vendor **called**, I **finished** the model — so I missed the
  call.* → ✅ *When the vendor **called**, I **was finishing** the model.* —
  *if you were mid-task when the call came, the task is the ongoing background;
  the simple past would wrongly mean you finished it only **after** the call.*
- ❌ *I **was arriving**, **was opening** the deck and **was starting**.* → ✅ *I
  **arrived**, **opened** the deck and **started**.* — *a sequence of completed
  steps is simple past throughout; the continuous is not a decoration for "past".*

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. While I **was presenting** the AI roadmap, the CFO **interrupted** with the
   cost question. — *long background (continuous) cut by a short event (simple)
   — the core interruption pattern.*
2. I **was still explaining** the three-year horizon when she **raised** the
   budget concern. — *"when + simple" marks the moment; "was still explaining"
   shows I hadn't finished — the concern landed mid-explanation.*
3. When the board **approved** the budget, they **added** two conditions. — *two
   finished events in sequence → both simple past; no action was "in progress",
   so no continuous.*
4. While the CEO **was reviewing** the deck, I **was drafting** the follow-up
   email. — *two long parallel actions → both continuous; neither interrupts
   the other.*
5. We **were finalising** the vendor shortlist when the market news **broke**. —
   *ongoing work (continuous) interrupted by a sudden event (simple) — the news
   didn't unfold slowly; it hit.*
6. The board **wasn't paying** attention when I **reached** the risk slide. —
   *negative continuous for the ongoing (non-)state of the room; "reached" is
   the single finished moment that met it.*
7. I **paused**, **answered** her question, and then **finished** the slide. —
   *a clean chain of completed actions → simple past all the way; sequence, not
   background.*
8. When you **called** last night, I **was putting** the kids to bed. —
   *everyday life; the short finished "called" interrupts the ongoing bedtime
   routine "was putting" — same pattern, off-work.*

### 8. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

- Ask your tutor to run an **"interruption vs sequence" contrast drill**: they
  give you two verbs (*present / interrupt*, *review / call*, *finalise /
  break*) and a meaning — "she cut in **while** you were mid-flow" vs "she cut
  in **and then** you started" — and you build the sentence with the right
  mix. They correct only reversed tenses (background made simple, event made
  continuous). This makes the two-layer choice automatic.
- Then a **board-story narration**: retell a real meeting in 5–6 sentences,
  forcing yourself to mark the *background* (what was going on when you walked
  in — *"the team **was still debating** the numbers"*) against the *events*
  (what happened — *"the CEO **arrived**, **opened** the floor and **asked**
  for the case"*). This trains you to keep the continuous for genuine
  in-progress actions and the simple for the storyline.

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 013)**
1. ❌ *"Were you presented…"* → ✅ **"Were you presenting when the CFO called?"** —
   *the past continuous needs the -ing form after was/were; "presented" is a
   past simple and can't sit there.*
2. ❌ *"we were wanting…"* → ✅ **"At that time we wanted a clearer business
   case."** — *"want" is a state verb → simple past "wanted", never the
   continuous.*
3. ❌ *"The two directors was…"* → ✅ **"The two directors were reviewing the
   portfolio at noon."** — *plural subject → "were"; "was" only goes with
   I/he/she/it.*

**Spaced review**
- **(Unit 007)** ❌ *"Does the CFO knows…"* → ✅ **"Does the CFO know the numbers
  behind it?"** — *the -s already lives on "does"; the main verb returns to its
  base form. Never mark it twice.*

**Recognition drill**
1. ❌ *"While I presented, the CFO was interrupting me."* → ✅ **"While I was
   presenting, the CFO interrupted me."** — *the presentation is the long
   background (continuous); the interruption is the short event (simple). The
   original reverses both.*
2. **"was finishing"** — *"When the vendor called, I was finishing the model"
   means you were **mid-task** when the call came. "I finished" would mean you
   completed it **after** the call — a sequence, not an interruption.*
3. ❌ *"I was arriving, was opening… was starting."* → ✅ **"I arrived, opened the
   deck and started the pitch."** — *a chain of completed steps is simple past
   throughout; nothing here was an ongoing background.*
4. **"were discussing"** — *"When the CEO arrived, we were discussing the AI
   budget" means the discussion was **already under way** when he walked in.
   "we discussed" would mean he arrived **first** and **then** we began — two
   events in sequence.*

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**A regra e quando usar.** Esta unidade é sobre **escolher** entre o *past
simple* e o *past continuous* dentro da mesma história — as formas você já viu
nas Unidades 11–13. Pense em duas camadas: o **fundo** (o que **já estava
acontecendo**) vai no contínuo — *was/were + -ing*; os **eventos** (o que
**aconteceu** e terminou) vão no simples. Três padrões: **interrupção** (fundo
longo no contínuo + evento curto no simples: *"While I **was presenting**, she
**interrupted**"*), **sequência** (vários eventos terminados, tudo no simples:
*"I **paused, answered and finished**"*) e **paralelo** (duas ações longas, as
duas no contínuo). Regra prática: decida **cada verbo separadamente** — este
estava *em progresso* (contínuo) ou *aconteceu e terminou* (simples)? E lembre:
o contínuo **não** diz que a ação terminou; o simples diz.

**As armadilhas.** (1) Não inverta as camadas — o fundo é contínuo e o evento é
simples, nunca o contrário (*"While I was presenting, she interrupted"*, não
*"While I presented, she was interrupting"*). (2) Não jogue tudo no contínuo:
uma sequência de ações terminadas é **toda** no simples (*"cheguei, abri, comecei"*
= *"I arrived, opened, started"*, não *"I was arriving…"*). (3) O imperfeito
português (*"eu apresentava"*) esconde a escolha — o inglês obriga você a decidir
entre *was presenting* (interrupção) e *presented* (sequência) pelo **sentido**,
não pela forma de superfície. A boa notícia: português e inglês fazem a mesma
distinção, então *estava apresentando* → *was presenting* e *apresentei* →
*presented* mapeiam quase direto.
