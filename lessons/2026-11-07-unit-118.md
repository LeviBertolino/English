### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** Remind me which account we lost last quarter.
> **Levi:** The enterprise client **whose** renewal slipped to a competitor — the one **whose** CFO never bought into the AI roadmap.
> **CFO:** And who owns the relationship now?
> **Levi:** Marta — the advisor **to whom** the account team reports. In plain terms, the advisor **we report to**.
> **CEO:** Where did the deal actually fall apart?
> **Levi:** In that Q2 review — the meeting **where** we promised a timeline **we couldn't hold**.
> **CFO:** So what changed?
> **Levi:** 2026 was the year **when** we stopped over-promising. That's the real reason **why** the pipeline is healthier now.

Watch three new ways to pin down a noun. First, **whose** — it names the noun by what it *owns*: *the client **whose** renewal slipped* (= the renewal **of** that client). Portuguese has *cujo* for exactly this, but you almost never use it in speech — so your instinct is to split it into two pieces and leave a stray *dele/dela* behind. Second, **whom** — the object form of *who*, which you really only need right after a **preposition** (*to whom*); everyday English just drops it and strands the preposition: *the advisor **we report to***. Third, **where / when / why** — for **place**, **time** and **reason**: *the meeting **where** we promised…*, *the year **when** we pivoted*, *the reason **why** it worked*. The trap hiding here is Portuguese *onde*, which you stretch over times and abstract ideas where English wants *when* or *in which*.

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

These pronouns extend yesterday's defining clause — each covers a relationship *who/which/that* can't:

| Pronoun | Replaces / means | Use it for | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **whose** | *of who / of which* — possession | the noun **owns** the next thing | *the client **whose** contract we lost* |
| **whom** | object form of *who* | a **person** as **object** (esp. after a preposition) | *the advisor **to whom** we report* |
| **where** | *in which / at which* | a **place** | *the room **where** we pitched* |
| **when** | *on / in which* | a **time** | *the year **when** we pivoted* |
| **why** | *for which* | a **reason** | *the reason **why** we paused* |

**Key facts:**
- **`whose` is followed directly by a noun** — the thing that is owned: *the startup **whose valuation** tripled*. No *the* in between: ❌ *whose the valuation*.
- **`whose` is never dropped.** Unlike the object *that/who* from yesterday, *whose* carries essential meaning (possession) and always stays.
- **`whom` only earns its place right after a preposition** — *to whom, with whom, for whom*. Everywhere else, everyday English uses *who / that* or drops it: *the advisor **(who) we report to***. Spoken English almost always strands the preposition and drops the pronoun.
- **`where` needs a real place.** For a location, *where* replaces the clumsy *in which / at which*: *the office **where** I work* (not *the office which I work in* in speech, though that's also grammatical).
- **`when` for time, `why` for reason** — and both are often **droppable**: *the year (when) we pivoted*, *the reason (why) we paused*. You can also say *the day **that** we signed* or just *the day we signed*.

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why do these exist?** Yesterday's *who/which/that* name a noun by what it **does** or what is **done to it**. But sometimes you need to pin a noun down by what it **owns** (*the client whose renewal slipped*), by its role **after a preposition** (*the advisor we report to*), or by **where/when/why** it sits (*the meeting where it happened*). Each of these is a different handle on the noun — and *whose/whom/where/when/why* are the handles. For a leader this is the grammar of exact reference: *the division whose margins collapsed*, *the quarter when we turned it around*, *the reason why the board approved it*.

**Decision rule #1 — `whose` vs a clumsy split (THE core):**
*whose* bundles *of which / of whom* into one word, placed before the thing owned. Portuguese has *cujo/cuja*, but because you rarely say it out loud, your reflex is to rebuild the sentence with *que … dele/dela* — and that produces an **echo pronoun** in English, exactly the error from yesterday:
- ❌ *"the client **that** we lost **his** contract"* → ✅ *"the client **whose** contract we lost."*
- ❌ *"a startup **that its** valuation tripled"* → ✅ *"a startup **whose** valuation tripled."*
The fix: whenever you'd say *dele / dela / its / his / their* **inside** a relative clause, that's the signal to use **whose** and delete the stray possessive.

**Decision rule #2 — `whom` vs `who` (and when to just drop it):**
*whom* is the **object** form of *who*. In modern English you need it in only one place: **right after a preposition** — *the advisor **to whom** we report*, *the partners **with whom** we negotiated*. Everywhere else, *whom* sounds stiff, and natural English does one of two things:
- **Strand the preposition and drop the pronoun** (the everyday default): *the advisor **we report to***, *the partners **we negotiated with***.
- Use *who / that*: *the colleague **who** I mentioned*.
So: *to whom we report* (formal, correct) = *who we report to* (neutral) = *we report to* (most natural). Never use *whom* as a **subject**: ❌ *the advisor **whom** leads the team* → it's doing the verb, so it's *who*.

**Decision rule #3 — `where` vs `which` vs the *onde* trap:**
*where* works **only for a real place or physical space**. Portuguese *onde* is stretched far wider — over times, situations, and abstractions — and that leaks into English:
- ✅ place: *the room **where** we pitched*, *the market **where** we compete*.
- ❌ time: *"the year **where** we pivoted"* → ✅ *"the year **when** we pivoted."*
- ❌ abstract: *"a situation **where** costs explode"* is tolerated in speech, but precise English prefers *"a situation **in which** costs explode"*; and *"the reason **where**…"* is simply wrong → *"the reason **why**…"*.
Rule of thumb: if you could point at it on a map or a floor plan, *where*. If it's a point in time, *when*. If it's a reason, *why*.

**PT→EN mapping (where translation betrays you):**
- *cujo / cuja / cujos / cujas* → **whose** (for both people and things): *"a empresa **cuja** avaliação…"* → *"the company **whose** valuation…"*. Portuguese *cujo* is correct but formal/rare in speech, so you under-use its English twin — train it deliberately.
- *a quem / com quem / para quem* → **to whom / with whom / for whom** (formal) or, more naturally, drop + strand: *"a pessoa **com quem** trabalho"* → *"the person **I work with**."*
- *onde* → **where** only for places; **when** for times; **in which / why** for abstractions and reasons. ⚠️ This is the big one: *onde* ≠ always *where*.
- *quando* (relative) → **when**: *"o ano **quando**…"* → *"the year **when**…"* (and often droppable: *the year we pivoted*).

**Top 3 typical Brazilian-speaker errors:**
1. ❌ *"The client **that we lost his contract** moved to a rival."* (split + echo) → ✅ *"The client **whose contract we lost** moved to a rival."* The *his* is the tell — collapse it into *whose*.
2. ❌ *"2026 was the year **where** we pivoted."* (*onde* over place) → ✅ *"…the year **when** we pivoted."* *where* is for places only; a year is a **time** → *when*.
3. ❌ *"Marta is the advisor **whom** leads the account team."* (*whom* as subject) → ✅ *"…the advisor **who** leads…"* *whom* is only ever the **object**; here it's doing the verb *leads*, so it's *who*.

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. **The enterprise client whose renewal slipped to a competitor was our biggest account.**
   *— `whose` = possession ("the renewal **of** that client"); it sits directly before the owned noun (`renewal`) and can never be dropped. Portuguese reflex would split this into "the client that lost its renewal" — resist it.*
2. **We finally landed the startup whose valuation tripled after the funding round.**
   *— `whose` for a **thing** (a company), not just people; "the valuation **of** which" compressed into one word. No "the" after whose: ❌ "whose the valuation."*
3. **Marta is the advisor to whom the account team reports.**
   *— `to whom`: `whom` is the object of the preposition `to`, so it's correct and a touch formal — fitting for a board-level statement. The everyday twin is example 4.*
4. **Marta is the advisor the account team reports to.**
   *— the natural, spoken version of #3: pronoun **dropped**, preposition `to` **stranded** at the end. Same meaning, less stiff. This is how you'd actually say it in a meeting.*
5. **The Q2 review was the meeting where we promised a timeline we couldn't hold.**
   *— `where` for a real event/place (a meeting). Note the second clause "(that) we couldn't hold" is yesterday's object-drop — the two patterns stack in one sentence.*
6. **2026 was the year when we stopped over-promising to the board.**
   *— `when` for a **time**, not `where` (the classic *onde* trap). You could also drop it: "the year we stopped over-promising."*
7. **That's the real reason why the pipeline is healthier now.**
   *— `why` for a **reason** (never `where`); also droppable: "the real reason the pipeline is healthier." Fits explaining a strategic shift to the C-level.*
8. **The office where I work just moved to the new campus.** *(everyday)*
   *— `where` for a physical place, replacing the clumsy "the office **which** I work **in**." Point-at-it-on-a-map test: it's a place → `where`.*

### 8. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

1. Drill the **`whose` collapse**, because Portuguese lets you dodge *cujo* and you won't in English. Ask your tutor to feed you two-sentence facts from your world — *"We lost a client. Its CFO blocked the AI roadmap."* / *"There's a startup. Its valuation tripled."* — and you must fuse each into one noun phrase with **whose**: *"the client **whose** CFO blocked the AI roadmap," "the startup **whose** valuation tripled."* Have them buzz you the instant you leave a stray *its / his / their* behind.
2. Run a **"which handle?" round** for *where / when / why* against the *onde* trap. The tutor names a noun — *a meeting, a year, a reason, a market, a quarter, a decision* — and you must attach the right relative word and a clause from your executive routine: *"the quarter **when** we turned it around," "the market **where** we compete," "the reason **why** the board approved it."* Enforce the rule out loud: **place → where, time → when, reason → why.** Then push into *to whom / we report to* so you can switch between the formal and the natural version on demand.

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 117 — defining relative clauses):**
1. **Wrong — *which* is never for people.** → ✅ *"Marta is the specialist **who** (or **that**) led the audit."* A specialist is a person, so only *who* or the neutral *that* works; *which* is for things. (Portuguese *que* covers all three, which is why *which* leaks onto people.)
2. **(b)** — *"the vendor **(that) we chose."*** Test: if a **new subject** (*we*) follows the pronoun, it's the **object** → droppable. In (a) a **verb** (*scored*) follows, so *that* is the **subject** → it must stay.
3. **The extra word is *it*.** → ✅ *"The platform **(that) we migrated to** is finally stable."* English names the object **once**; the relative slot already is the object, so repeating it with *it* is the colloquial-Portuguese echo. The preposition *to* stays stranded at the end, no *it*.

**Spaced review:**
- **Unit 111 (superlatives):** change *decision* → **decisions**: *"It's **one of the most important decisions** we'll make this year."* *one of the* + superlative always takes a **plural** noun — it names one item out of several top ones, so the noun must be plural.
- **Unit 088 (word order):** ✅ *"We launched **the product** (verb+object together) **in Europe** (place) **last quarter** (time)."* Two rules: (1) keep the **verb and its object glued** — *launched the product*, nothing wedged between them; (2) **place before time** — *in Europe* then *last quarter*. Portuguese lets you scatter these; English doesn't.

**Recognition drill:**
1. **whose** — *"the client **whose** contract was our biggest."* *whose* absorbs the possessive, so *its* **disappears** (it's already inside *whose*). ❌ *"the client that its contract…"* keeps the echo; ✅ *whose* is the one-word fix.
2. **Wrong — *where* over a time.** → ✅ *"…the year **when** we froze all hiring."* A year is a **point in time**, not a place, so it takes *when*; *where* is the *onde* trap. (Also valid: *"the year we froze all hiring,"* dropping *when*.)
3. **who** — *"the partner **who** negotiated the whole deal."* Test: the pronoun is the **subject** (it's doing the verb *negotiated*), and *whom* can only ever be an **object**. Subject → *who*, always.
4. **(a) whose** — *"the division **whose** margins collapsed"* (possession: the margins **of** the division). **(b) where** — *"the room **where** we pitched"* (a physical place). **(c) why** — *"the reason **why** we paused"* (a reason, never *where*; also droppable: *the reason we paused*).

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**A regra:** estes pronomes estendem a oração relativa de ontem, cada um para uma relação que *who/which/that* não cobrem. **whose** = *cujo/cuja* — identifica o substantivo por aquilo que ele **possui**, e vem **direto antes** da coisa possuída (*the client **whose** contract we lost*); nunca é apagado e nunca leva *the* depois. **whom** = forma de **objeto** de *who* — você só precisa dele **logo após preposição** (*to whom, with whom*); no inglês do dia a dia, solte a preposição pro fim e apague o pronome (*the advisor **we report to***). **where / when / why** para **lugar / tempo / motivo**: *the room **where***, *the year **when***, *the reason **why*** (os dois últimos muitas vezes podem cair: *the year we pivoted*).

**As armadilhas (interferência do português):** (1) Você foge do *cujo* na fala, então reconstrói com *que … dele/dela* e sobra um pronome-eco em inglês — ❌ *"the client **that** we lost **his** contract"* → ✅ *"the client **whose** contract we lost."* Sempre que fosse dizer *dele/dela/its/his* **dentro** da relativa, é sinal de **whose**. (2) *onde* ≠ sempre *where*: *where* é só para **lugar**; tempo pede **when** (❌ *"the year where"* → ✅ *"the year when"*), e motivo pede **why**. (3) *whom* é só **objeto** — nunca sujeito: ❌ *"the advisor **whom** leads"* → ✅ *"…**who** leads…"*. Treine de propósito o **whose** e a troca *where → when/why*, que são os dois pontos onde o português mais te atrapalha.
