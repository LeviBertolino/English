### 1. 📘 Unit 047 — Adjective + to-infinitive (hard to predict / ready to scale / the first to move)

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** How confident are you on the 2027 AI bet?
> **You:** Honestly, it's **hard to predict** the market that far out — but we're
> **ready to move** now. The platform is **easy to scale**, and the team is
> **quick to learn**.
> **CEO:** And the board?
> **You:** They were **surprised to see** the payback numbers. I think they're
> **willing to fund** the first phase.
> **CEO:** Good. I'm **glad to hear** it. Let's be **the first to move** on this.

Notice the pattern before any rule. After every adjective, the next verb arrives as
**to + base verb**: *hard **to predict***, *ready **to move***, *easy **to scale***,
*quick **to learn***, *surprised **to see***, *willing **to fund***, *glad **to
hear***, *the first **to move***. The adjective sets the **judgement or feeling**;
the *to*-verb says **which action** it's about. This is the everyday grammar of an
executive weighing a call: *hard to justify, easy to sell, ready to commit, quick
to adapt, difficult to reverse.*

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

An adjective is very often followed by **to + base verb** (the *to*-infinitive).
Three frames cover almost everything:

| Frame | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Empty-*it*** (judgement about an action) | *It's + adjective + **to** + verb* | *It's **hard to predict** the market.* |
| **Feeling / readiness** (about the subject) | *subject + be + adjective + **to** + verb* | *We're **ready to scale**. / I'm **glad to help**.* |
| **the first / last / next / only + to** | *…the first + **to** + verb* | *We want to be **the first to move**.* |

**Key facts:**
- **Keep the *to*.** The verb after the adjective is **to + base verb**, never the
  bare verb: *"hard **to** predict"*, *"ready **to** scale"* — never *"hard predict"*.
- **Empty *it*, not "is".** When you judge an action, English needs a subject:
  *"**It's** important to align early"* — never *"Is important to align"* (that
  hidden-subject slip comes straight from Portuguese *"É importante…"*).
- **No repeated object.** When a thing is the subject, don't add it back after the
  verb: *"The platform is **easy to use**"* — never *"easy to use **it**"*. The
  platform is already the thing being used.
- **Two ways to say the same idea.** *"It's easy to use the platform"* (empty *it*)
  ≈ *"The platform is easy to use"* (thing as subject). Both correct; the second
  drops the object because the subject already fills that slot.

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why does this structure exist?** An adjective gives a **verdict** (*hard, easy,
ready, glad, willing, surprised, difficult*). The *to*-infinitive after it names
the **action that verdict is about**. Together they let a leader compress a whole
judgement into four words: *"hard to justify"*, *"ready to commit"*, *"quick to
adapt"*, *"difficult to reverse"*. It is the grammar of **evaluating and deciding**
— exactly the register of a board room.

**Decision rule — adjective + *to* vs the rival, adjective + preposition + -ing
(yesterday's Unit 046).** This is *the* confusion, because the same adjective can
take either, and the meaning changes:

| | Adjective + **to** + verb | Adjective + **preposition** + **-ing** |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | most adjectives of judgement/feeling: *hard, easy, ready, glad, willing, the first* | an adjective **glued to a preposition**: *interested **in**, good **at**, committed **to**, worried **about** |
| Verb form | **to + base** (*to predict*) | **-ing** (*predicting*) |
| Example | *"It's **hard to predict** the market."* | *"I'm **good at predicting** the market."* |

Minimal pairs — same adjective feel, the trigger flips the form:
- *"We're **ready to invest**."* (ready + **to**) vs *"We're **committed to
  investing**."* (committed + preposition *to* → **-ing**). ✅ both — see how
  *ready* takes the infinitive but *committed to* takes *-ing*.
- *"I'm **glad to help**."* (glad + **to**) vs *"I'm **interested in helping**."*
  (interested + preposition *in* → **-ing**). ✅ both.

**The one that trips everyone: *afraid to do* vs *afraid of doing*.** Both exist,
and they mean different things:
- *"I was **afraid to raise** the risk."* = I was scared, so **I didn't do it**
  (*to* + verb = the action I hesitated over).
- *"I was **afraid of losing** the deal, so I moved fast."* = I feared it **might
  happen** (*of* + -ing = the outcome I dreaded).

**Test:** does the adjective come with a **fixed little preposition** (*in, at, of,
about, to* as in *committed to*)? → **-ing**. Is it a bare adjective of
judgement/feeling (*hard, easy, ready, glad, willing, quick, surprised*)? → **to +
base verb**.

**PT→EN mapping — where Portuguese betrays Levi.**
- *"É importante **alinhar** cedo"* → ✅ *"**It's** important **to align** early"*, ❌
  *"Is important to align"* (Portuguese drops the subject; English can't).
- *"É difícil **prever** o mercado"* → ✅ *"It's hard **to predict** the market"*, ❌
  *"It's hard predict"* (Portuguese shows no *to*, so it's easy to forget it).
- *"Fácil **de usar**"* → ✅ *"easy **to use**"*, ❌ *"easy to use **it**"* (the *de*
  and the hidden object of *usar* tempt a repeated *it*).
- *"Fico **feliz em** ajudar"* → ✅ *"I'm **glad to help**"*, ❌ *"glad in helping" /
  "glad of helping"* (Portuguese *em* is **not** English *in* here).
- *"Fomos os primeiros **a agir**"* → ✅ *"We were **the first to move**"*, ❌ *"the
  first **in moving** / **for move**"* (Portuguese *a* maps to *to* + base verb).

**Top Brazilian-speaker errors:**
- ❌ *"**Is important to align** on the vision before the board meeting."* → ✅
  *"**It's important to align** on the vision before the board meeting."* — English
  needs the empty subject *it*; *"É importante…"* hides it, but English can't.
- ❌ *"The new platform is **easy to use it**."* → ✅ *"The new platform is **easy to
  use**."* — no repeated object; the platform is already the thing being used.
- ❌ *"It's **difficult predict** the payback."* → ✅ *"It's **difficult to predict**
  the payback."* — keep the *to*; the bare verb after an adjective is never right.

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. *"**It's hard to predict** the market three years out, but the direction is clear."*
   *— empty-it + adjective + to; "It's hard predict" drops the obligatory "to", the classic slip from Portuguese "difícil prever".*
2. *"**We're ready to scale** the platform the moment the board approves."*
   *— subject + adjective + to; "ready to" takes the base verb, unlike "committed to" which would take -ing.*
3. *"The board was **surprised to see** the payback under eighteen months."*
   *— feeling adjective (surprised) + to; the to-verb names what caused the reaction.*
4. *"We want to be **the first to move** on agentic AI, not the last to react."*
   *— the first/last + to-infinitive; "the first in moving" is the direct-from-Portuguese error ("os primeiros a agir").*
5. *"**It's difficult to justify** the spend without a clearer ROI story."*
   *— empty-it + to; note "difficult to justify", never "difficult of justifying" (that -ing belongs to a preposition adjective).*
6. *"The new dashboard is **easy to read** and **quick to update**."*
   *— thing-as-subject + adjective + to, no repeated object: "easy to read", never "easy to read it".*
7. *"I'm **glad to help** the finance team model the AI budget scenarios."*
   *— glad + to (readiness/feeling); "glad in helping" copies Portuguese "feliz em" and breaks — the preposition is wrong and so is the -ing.*
8. *"The kids are always **the first to notice** when I'm distracted at dinner."*
   *— everyday life; "the first to notice" — same the-first-to pattern, base verb after "to".*

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 046 — preposition + -ing):**
1. ***to investing*** — *"We're **committed to investing**…"* The *to* in *committed
   to* is a **preposition** (part of the fixed phrase), not an infinitive marker, so
   the verb takes **-ing**; *"committed to invest"* is the trap.
2. ***to presenting*** — *"I **look forward to presenting** the 2027 vision."* Here
   *to* is a **preposition** (fixed phrase *look forward to*), so the verb is
   **-ing**, never *"to present"*.
3. ***Before committing*** — *before* is a preposition, so the verb is **-ing**;
   there is no *before* + *to* + verb. *"Before to commit"* copies the Portuguese
   *"antes de decidir"* and breaks.

**Spaced review:**
- **(Unit 040)** ***has it?*** — *"The board hasn't signed off yet, **has it?**"* The
  statement is **negative** (*hasn't*), so the tag flips to **positive**, and it
  echoes the auxiliary *has* + the pronoun *it*.
- **(Unit 019)** ***ago*** and ***since*** — *"We signed them two years **ago**, so
  we've worked with them **since** 2024."* *Ago* counts back from now to a finished
  point and lives with the **past simple** (*signed*); *since* names the start point
  and lives with the **present perfect** (*we've worked*).

**Recognition drill:**
1. First blank → ***to commit*** (*"We're **ready to commit**"* — bare judgement
   adjective *ready* + **to**-infinitive). Second → ***to committing*** (*"we're
   **committed to committing**"* — *committed to* ends in a **preposition** *to*, so
   **-ing**). Same word "commit", but *ready* triggers the infinitive and *committed
   to* triggers *-ing*.
2. ***It's*** and ***to align*** — *"**It's** important **to align** on the vision…"*
   English needs the empty subject *it*; the verb after the adjective is **to +
   base** (*to align*), never *"is important align"*.
3. **Nothing goes in the blank** — *"The new platform is **easy to use**."* The
   platform is already the subject (the thing being used), so no repeated object;
   *"easy to use it"* is the Portuguese-*de-usar* error.
4. ***to raise*** — *"I was **afraid to raise** the risk, so I stayed quiet"* = I was
   scared and therefore **didn't do it**. The other form, *"afraid **of raising**"*,
   would mean I feared the act *might happen* — which doesn't fit "so I stayed quiet".

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**A regra e quando usar.** Em inglês, um adjetivo é muito frequentemente seguido de
**to + verbo na base** (o infinitivo com *to*). O adjetivo dá o **veredito ou o
sentimento** (*hard, easy, ready, glad, willing, surprised, difficult*) e o *to*-verbo
diz **qual ação** ele descreve: *"hard **to predict**", "ready **to scale**", "glad
**to help**", "the first **to move**"*. Três moldes cobrem quase tudo: (1) *It's +
adjetivo + to + verbo* para julgar uma ação (*"It's important **to align** early"* —
com o sujeito vazio *it*, que o inglês **nunca** larga); (2) *sujeito + be + adjetivo
+ to + verbo* para sentimento/prontidão (*"We're **ready to move**"*); (3) *the
first/last/only + to + verbo* (*"the first **to move**"*).

**As armadilhas.** (1) **Nunca solte o sujeito nem o *to***: diga *"**It's** important
**to align**"*, jamais *"Is important align"* — o português *"É importante alinhar"*
esconde as duas coisas. (2) **Não repita o objeto**: *"easy **to use**"*, nunca *"easy
to use **it**"* (o *"fácil de usar"* engana). (3) **Adjetivo + to × adjetivo +
preposição + -ing** (a ligação com o Unit 046): adjetivos "nus" de julgamento pedem
**to + base** (*ready to, glad to, hard to*), mas adjetivos **colados a uma
preposição** pedem **-ing** (*committed **to** investing, interested **in** helping,
good **at** predicting*). E a dupla clássica: *"afraid **to** raise"* (tive medo e
**não** fiz) × *"afraid **of** losing"* (temi que **acontecesse**). Se o par to × -ing
ainda embolar, releia `lessons/2026-08-27-unit-046.md` (preposition + -ing) e
`lessons/2026-08-26-unit-045.md` (to for purpose).
