### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** You were in the room. How did finance take the number?
> **You:** I **watched the CFO hesitate** for a second, then nod. And I **heard the
> board debate** it for a full hour before they moved on.
> **CEO:** And the market?
> **You:** We **saw adoption climbing** all quarter — it never flattened. This morning
> I **watched the stock react** the moment the announcement hit.
> **CEO:** Good. I **noticed you pause** on the risk slide, though. Was that deliberate?
> **You:** It was. I wanted them to **feel the tension building** before I answered it.

Notice the pattern before any rule. After **watch, hear, see, notice, feel**, a person
comes first, then a verb — and that verb arrives in **two shapes**. Sometimes **bare**
(*watched the CFO **hesitate***, *heard the board **debate***, *noticed you **pause***),
sometimes **-ing** (*saw adoption **climbing***, *feel the tension **building***). Both
are correct — and the shape you pick **changes the meaning**. That choice is today's
whole lesson.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

Verbs of perception — **see, hear, watch, notice, listen to, feel** — take an
**object (a person/thing) + a verb**, and that verb comes in one of **two forms, no
*to***:

| Form | Meaning it carries | Example |
|---|---|---|
| object + **base verb** | you perceived the **complete** action, start to finish | *"I **saw her leave**."* (she left; I saw the whole thing) |
| object + **-ing** | you perceived the action **in progress**, a slice of it | *"I **saw her leaving**."* (I caught her mid-exit) |

**Key facts:**
- **No *to* — ever.** Like *make* and *let* yesterday, it's *"I saw her **leave**"*,
  **never** *"I saw her **to** leave"*. The object is followed by the bare verb or by
  *-ing*, but the little *to* is banned.
- **The object is obligatory.** You *see **somebody** do*, *hear **something**
  happen* — there is always a person or thing in the middle: *saw **the CFO**
  hesitate*, *heard **the board** debate*.
- **base verb = the whole action (complete / a fact).** You witnessed it from start
  to end, or as a single finished event: *"I heard him **shut** the door"* (one clean
  action, done).
- **-ing = the action ongoing (in the middle, a snapshot).** You saw a stretch of it,
  not necessarily its end: *"I heard him **shutting** the door"* (the noise was going
  on as I passed) — maybe I didn't stay for the click.
- **This family only.** The two-form rule belongs to perception verbs (*see, hear,
  watch, notice, feel, listen to*). It is **not** how *want/tell/ask* work (those keep
  *to*: *"I told him **to** leave"*).

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why does this structure exist?** English lets you report **what your senses caught**
and, in the same breath, say **how much** of the action you caught — the whole thing or
just a moment of it. That's a real distinction in a meeting: *"I saw the CFO **approve**
it"* (done — the approval happened, I witnessed it) is a different claim from *"I saw the
CFO **approving** it"* (I caught her mid-signing — she was in the act). Same room, two
truths, and the verb form is the only thing that separates them.

**Decision rule #1 — base verb vs -ing (the core seam).** Both are grammatical; pick by
meaning:

| | **object + base verb** | **object + -ing** |
|---|---|---|
| What you saw | the **complete** action | a **slice**, action in progress |
| Feel | a finished fact / single event | a snapshot, still going |
| Example | *"I **watched him present** the whole deck."* (start to end) | *"I **watched him presenting** when I walked in."* (mid-talk) |

Minimal pairs — the form flips the meaning:
- *"I **heard the CEO announce** the merger."* = I heard the announcement, the complete
  event (it happened). vs *"I **heard the CEO announcing** the merger."* = I caught him
  in the middle of doing it (the words were rolling out as I listened).
- *"We **saw adoption double**."* = it doubled — I witnessed the full result. vs *"We
  **saw adoption climbing**."* = it was on the way up, in progress (no endpoint claimed).

**Decision rule #2 — perception verbs (no *to*) vs want/tell/ask (+ *to*).** This is the
same seam you drilled with *make/let* yesterday, and it's where the *to* wrongly sneaks
in:
- *"I **saw her leave**."* (see → **no** *to*, bare verb) vs *"I **wanted her to leave**."*
  (want → **to**). Same object *her*, opposite form.
- The perception family (*see, hear, watch, notice, feel*) joins *make* and *let* in the
  **no-*to*** club. If the verb reports a **sense** or a **cause/permission**, drop the
  *to*.

**PT→EN mapping — where Portuguese betrays Levi.**
- *"Eu **vi ela sair**"* → ✅ *"I **saw her leave**"*, ❌ *"I saw her **to** leave"*
  (Portuguese uses a plain infinitive *sair* with **no** *to*-equivalent — but the
  English reflex to insert *to* is strong; resist it).
- *"Eu **vi ela saindo**"* → ✅ *"I **saw her leaving**"* — here Portuguese *saindo* maps
  cleanly to English *-ing* (action in progress). This one transfers well.
- *"Eu **ouvi** o CEO **falar** da aquisição"* → ✅ *"I **heard the CEO mention** the
  acquisition"* (complete — he mentioned it) **or** *"…**mentioning**…"* (I caught him
  mid-sentence). Portuguese *ouvir alguém falar* doesn't force the choice; English does —
  pick by whether you mean the whole thing or a slice.
- **Watch the "complete vs ongoing" nuance Portuguese hides:** *vi* + infinitive
  (*vi ela sair*) leans **complete** → base verb; *vi* + gerúndio (*vi ela saindo*)
  leans **in progress** → *-ing*. Portuguese already has both shapes, so lean on that
  instinct — just never add *to* to the base one.

**Top Brazilian-speaker errors:**
- ❌ *"I **saw the CFO to hesitate**."* → ✅ *"I **saw the CFO hesitate**."* — perception
  verbs take the **bare** verb; the *to* never appears (same rule as *make/let*).
- ❌ *"I **heard he arriving**."* → ✅ *"I **heard him arriving**."* — the middle slot is
  an **object** (*him/her/the CFO*), not a subject pronoun (*he/she*).
- ❌ *"We **saw adoption to climb** all quarter."* → ✅ *"We **saw adoption climbing** all
  quarter."* — an action clearly **in progress** (all quarter, no endpoint) wants *-ing*,
  and either way there's **no** *to*.

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. *"I **watched the CFO hesitate** for a second, then nod."*
   *— base verb: a complete, single flash of an action (she hesitated, I saw all of it); "hesitate", never "to hesitate".*
2. *"I **heard the board debate** the budget for a full hour."*
   *— base verb frames the whole event as one thing you witnessed end to end; compare "heard the board debating" = caught in the middle.*
3. *"We **saw adoption climbing** all quarter — it never flattened."*
   *— -ing: the action was in progress, ongoing, no endpoint claimed; "all quarter" screams middle-of-it, so -ing not base.*
4. *"This morning I **watched the stock react** the moment the announcement hit."*
   *— base verb: one clean, complete reaction tied to a single moment ("the moment…"); a finished event → bare "react".*
5. *"I **noticed you pause** on the risk slide."*
   *— notice + object + bare verb; a single complete action spotted (you paused, once); no to after "notice".*
6. *"I wanted them to **feel the tension building** before I answered."*
   *— feel + object + -ing: the tension was mounting, in progress; "building" (ongoing) not "build". (Note: after "wanted them to feel" the to belongs to want, not to feel.)*
7. *"I **heard the CEO mention** the acquisition, but I **saw the CFO frowning** as he said it."*
   *— both in one line: "mention" = complete fact (he said it); "frowning" = ongoing reaction happening at the same time.*
8. *"I **watched my daughter take** her first steps last night."*
   *— everyday life; base verb for the complete, once-only event; "watched her take", no to. "watched her taking" would just mean I caught her mid-stumble.*

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 048 — make & let):**
1. ***reconsider*** (bare) — *"The CEO **made me reconsider** the timeline."* After
   *make* the verb is the **base** form; *"made me **to** reconsider"* is the #1 slip
   because Portuguese *fazer alguém fazer* carries no *to*.
2. ***made*** — *"The disappointing numbers **made me question** the whole roadmap."*
   *Make* = force/cause (the numbers left no choice). *Let* = permit, and numbers don't
   grant permission, so it can't fit.
3. ***makes*** — *"Presenting to the board always **makes me nervous**."* A **feeling**
   caused by something is *make*, not *let*. Portuguese *"me deixa nervoso"* (*deixar*)
   pushes you toward *let*, but here *deixar* maps to English **make**.

**Spaced review:**
- **(Unit 042)** ***preparing*** — *"Have you **finished preparing** the board deck?"*
  *Finish* is one of the verbs that force the **-ing** form; *"finished **to** prepare"*
  is wrong (English *finish* never takes a *to*-infinitive).
- **(Unit 019)** ***ago / for / since*** — *"We signed the vendor two years **ago**, so
  we've worked with them **for** two years, **since** 2024."* *Ago* counts back from now
  and lives with the **past simple** (*signed*); *for* (a length) and *since* (a start
  point) both live with the **present perfect** (*we've worked*).

**Recognition drill:**
1. ❌ *"…saw the CFO **to** hesitate…"* → ✅ *"I **saw the CFO hesitate** when I raised
   the number."* — perception verbs take the **bare** verb (like *make/let*); the *to*
   never appears.
2. Both are grammatical, but they mean different things. ***presenting*** fits *"when I
   walked in"*: *"I **saw the CEO presenting** the vision"* = the presentation was **in
   progress** when I entered (I caught a slice). ***present*** (base) would mean I saw the
   **whole** presentation, start to finish — which clashes with just *walking in* on it.
3. ***him*** — *"I heard **him** arriving late."* The middle slot is the **object** of
   *heard*, so it takes the object pronoun *him/her/them*, never the subject *he/she*.
4. ***"I saw the board approve the AI budget."*** — **base verb** *approve*, because the
   **whole vote** happened in front of me (a complete event); no *to* after *saw*. With
   *wanted* it flips to a *to*-infinitive: *"I **wanted** the board **to approve** the AI
   budget"* — *want* reports a wish, not a sense, so it keeps the *to*.

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**A regra e quando usar.** Os verbos de percepção — *see, hear, watch, notice, feel,
listen to* — vêm com **objeto + verbo**, e esse verbo tem **duas formas, sem *to***:
a **base** (*"I saw her **leave**"*) para a ação **completa**, vista do início ao fim
(ou um evento único, um fato); e o ***-ing*** (*"I saw her **leaving**"*) para a ação
**em andamento**, um flagrante no meio dela. Numa reunião isso é uma distinção real:
*"I saw the CFO **approve** it"* (aprovou — evento completo) ≠ *"I saw the CFO
**approving** it"* (peguei no meio da assinatura). A forma do verbo é a única coisa que
separa os dois sentidos. E, como *make/let* de ontem, **não entra *to***: *"I saw her
**leave**"*, nunca *"I saw her **to** leave"*.

**As armadilhas.** (1) **Nunca ponha *to*** depois de *see/hear/watch/notice*: é o mesmo
erro do *make/let* (*"made me rethink", "saw the CFO hesitate"*). (2) **O meio é
objeto**: *"I heard **him** arriving"*, não *"heard **he**"*. (3) **Complete × em
andamento**: seu instinto de português já ajuda — *vi ela **sair*** (infinitivo →
completo → **base verb**) vs *vi ela **saindo*** (gerúndio → em andamento → ***-ing***);
confie nele, só não acrescente o *to*. Se o par "sem *to* × com *to*" ainda embolar,
releia `lessons/2026-08-29-unit-048.md` (make/let) — é o vizinho direto desta unidade no
mesmo clube do "sem *to*".
