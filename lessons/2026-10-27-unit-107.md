### 1. 📘 Unit 107 — *too* and *enough*: saying "more than it should be" and "as much as it needs to be"

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CFO:** The roadmap looks strong, but the timeline worries me. Phase one in six weeks?
> **Levi:** Honestly, six weeks is **too tight**. We don't have **enough engineers** freed up, and the ones we do have aren't **senior enough** to run it alone.
> **CFO:** So what's realistic?
> **Levi:** Ten weeks. That gives us **enough time** to hire, and the plan becomes **safe enough to take to the board**. Six weeks is ambitious — ten is **ambitious enough** without being reckless.

Notice: *too tight* (a problem — more tight than it should be), but *ambitious enough* (sufficient — as much as it needs to be). And watch where *enough* sits: **before** the noun (*enough engineers, enough time*) but **after** the adjective (*senior enough, safe enough*).

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

**`too`** = *more than is needed or wanted* — always a problem, always negative in feel.
**`enough`** = *as much as is needed* — neutral/positive; *not enough* = less than needed.

| Pattern | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **too** + adjective/adverb | `too` + adj/adv | *too expensive · too slowly · too risky* |
| **too much** + uncountable noun | `too much` + noun | *too much risk · too much detail* |
| **too many** + countable noun | `too many` + noun | *too many initiatives · too many meetings* |
| **enough** + noun | `enough` + **noun** | *enough time · enough people · enough budget* |
| adjective/adverb + **enough** | adj/adv + `enough` | *good enough · fast enough · senior enough* |
| + *to* + verb | `too`/`enough … to` | *too risky **to** approve · experienced **enough to** lead* |
| + *for* + person | `too`/`enough … for` | *too complex **for** the board · clear **enough for** everyone* |

The one position rule that trips everyone: **`enough` goes _after_ an adjective or adverb, but _before_ a noun.** *Good enough* (after adj) · *enough people* (before noun).

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why does this structure exist?** English needs a clean way to say two different things that Portuguese squeezes into one word, *muito*:
- *"É muito caro"* can mean **"it costs a lot"** (neutral fact) **or** **"it costs more than we can pay"** (a problem).
- English forces the choice: **very** = high degree, no judgement. **too** = excess, something is wrong because of it.

So *too* is never just "a strong *very*." *too* always carries a hidden "…and that's a problem." *The strategy is too ambitious* = so ambitious it will fail. *The strategy is very ambitious* = boldly ambitious (often a compliment). Choosing *too* when you mean *very* accidentally criticises your own plan.

**Decision rule — `too` vs `very`:**

| You mean… | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| a lot of it — neutral or positive | **very** | *The results are **very** strong.* (good news) |
| more than it should be — a problem | **too** | *The results are coming in **too** slowly.* (bad — we must act) |

Minimal pair: *"The deck is **very** detailed"* (thorough — praise) ≠ *"The deck is **too** detailed"* (so detailed the board will lose the point — fix it).

**Decision rule — `too much/many` vs `too`:** is the word after it a **noun** or an **adjective**?
- noun → *too much* (uncountable) / *too many* (countable): *too much risk, too many pilots.*
- adjective → just *too*: *too risky, too expensive.* Never *too much expensive.*

**PT→EN mapping:**
- *muito* → **very** when it's a plain high degree (*muito importante* = *very important*); → **too** only when there's a "problem" meaning (*caro demais* / *muito caro [pra gente pagar]* = *too expensive*).
- *demais* = **too / too much** (always the excess meaning) — a reliable signal for *too*.
- *o suficiente · bastante* = **enough** (*tempo suficiente* = *enough time*; *bom o suficiente* = *good enough*).

**Top 3 typical Brazilian-speaker errors:**
- ❌ *"This project is too ambitious"* (meaning it as praise) → ✅ *"This project is **very** ambitious"* — *too* would mean it's doomed. Use *too* only when you mean "and that's a problem."
- ❌ *"It's too much expensive / too much important"* → ✅ *"It's **too** expensive / **too** important"* — *too much* only before a noun, never before an adjective.
- ❌ *"We don't have **enough experienced** people… "* is fine, but ❌ *"people experienced enough… no — the team isn't **enough qualified**"* → ✅ *"the team isn't **qualified enough**"* — *enough* goes **after** the adjective.

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. **The AI budget is too tight to cover both bets** this year.
   *— too + adj + to-infinitive: "tight" is a problem (we can't do both). Not "too much tight."*
2. We don't have **enough senior engineers** to staff the pivot.
   *— enough + noun: "enough" sits before the noun "engineers." Contrast #3.*
3. Two of the leads aren't **experienced enough** to own the roadmap alone.
   *— adj + enough: "enough" goes after the adjective "experienced." Same word, opposite position from #2.*
4. The model is **very** accurate, but it's **too** slow for real-time decisions.
   *— the contrast in one line: "very accurate" = praise (high degree); "too slow" = a problem. Same sentence, two jobs.*
5. The board saw **too much detail** and missed the headline.
   *— too much + uncountable noun ("detail"). The excess of detail caused a problem — classic "too."*
6. Is the thesis **clear enough for** the CFO to defend without us in the room?
   *— enough + for + person + to: sufficiency framed around who needs it. "Clear enough," not "enough clear."*
7. We're running **too many** small pilots and funding none of them properly.
   *— too many + countable noun ("pilots"): the number is more than we can support — a problem.*
8. The coffee's **too hot** to drink, so give it a minute.
   *— everyday life: too + adj + to-infinitive. "Too hot" = hotter than you want; a problem you solve by waiting.*

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 105 — *so* / *such*):**
1. **such** → *"It was **such a bold bet** that…"* — there is a **noun** (*bet*) in the phrase, so *such*, not *so*. *So* only attaches to a bare adjective/adverb.
2. *"The forecast was **so** optimistic"* (adjective alone → *so*) · *"It was **such** an optimistic forecast"* (adjective + **noun** → *such*). The noun is what flips *so* to *such*.
3. *"The pivot was **so** fast **that** half the team couldn't keep up."* — *so* + adjective, then *that* + the result.

**Spaced review:**
- **Unit 100:** **approved** — *the budget **approved** by the board*. The budget **receives** the action (someone approves it → passive), so the *-ed* participle. *Approving* would mean the budget does the approving.
- **Unit 072:** **that** — *"Everything **that** matters…"* After *everything / all / the thing*, use **that**, never **what**. ("Tudo **que**" → *everything **that***, not *everything what*.)

**Recognition drill:**
1. **very** — *"very ambitious — exactly the stretch we need."* **too** would mean it's *so* ambitious it will fail, flatly contradicting the praise that follows.
2. *"aren't **prepared enough** to present"* — *enough* goes **after** the adjective, not before it.
3. *"There's **too much** risk (uncountable noun), **too many** dependencies (countable noun), and the whole thing is **too** fragile (adjective)."*
4. *"We finally have **enough budget** (enough **before** the noun), and the architecture is now stable **enough** (enough **after** the adjective)."*

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**A regra:** *too* quer dizer "mais do que deveria" — sempre carrega um problema escondido (*too expensive* = caro **demais**, não dá pra pagar). *very* é só "muito", grau alto e neutro (*very expensive* = bem caro, sem julgamento). Use *too + adjetivo* (*too risky*), *too much/many + substantivo* (*too much risk, too many pilots*) e *enough* para suficiência: **antes** do substantivo (*enough time*) mas **depois** do adjetivo (*good enough, senior enough*).

**As armadilhas (interferência do português):** o *muito* do português vira **very** OU **too** em inglês, e o inglês te obriga a escolher — se você disser *"the plan is too ambitious"* achando que é elogio, na verdade disse que o plano vai dar errado; use *very* para elogiar. Nunca diga *too much expensive* (só *too expensive* — *too much* só antes de substantivo), e nunca *enough prepared* (o certo é *prepared enough*, com *enough* depois do adjetivo). O sinal mais seguro: *demais* → *too*; *o suficiente / bastante* → *enough*.
