### 1. 📘 Unit 053 — do and make

---

### 2. 🧠 Mini-context

> **CEO:** Where are we on the AI platform? We need to **make a decision** this week.
> **You:** Almost there. The team **did the due diligence** on both vendors, and we've
> **made real progress** on the numbers.
> **CEO:** Any concerns?
> **You:** One. If we **make the wrong call** here, it sets the roadmap back a year. So I'd
> rather **do a bit more analysis** before we commit.
> **CEO:** Fair. **Make sure** the CFO signs off first.
> **You:** Will do. I'll **make an appointment** with her for tomorrow.

Notice something a Portuguese speaker never has to think about. *Make a decision*, *do the
due diligence*, *made progress*, *do more analysis*, *make sure*, *make an appointment* —
in Portuguese every one of those is the same verb: **fazer**. English splits *fazer* into
two, **do** and **make**, and there's no room to choose freely: *"make a decision"* is
right and *"do a decision"* is simply wrong. That split — and how to feel which side a word
lands on — is today's whole lesson.

---

### 3. 📐 A regra (form)

Both mean *fazer*, but they divide the work:

| Verb | Core idea | Typical partners |
|---|---|---|
| **make** | **produce / create** a result — something that didn't exist before now does | a decision, a plan, a mistake, progress, a suggestion, an offer, a deal, money, a profit, a difference, an appointment, sure |
| **do** | **perform / carry out** an activity, task or work — the *doing* itself | the research, the analysis, the work, a job, business, a course, homework, damage, your best, a review |

**Key facts:**
- **make → an outcome appears.** You *make a decision* and now a decision **exists**; you
  *make a plan* and now there's a plan. Focus is on the **thing produced**.
- **do → an activity happens.** You *do the research* — the focus is the **work/effort**,
  not a product you can point at. Also *do* for vague/general actions: *"What are you
  **doing**?"*, *"I'll **do** something about it."*
- **These are collocations — fixed pairings you memorise, not rules you calculate.** The
  "produce vs perform" idea guides you ~80% of the time; the rest (*do business, make an
  effort*) you learn as blocks.
- **A fast test:** if the result is a **noun you now "have"** (a decision, a plan, a
  mistake, a profit), it's almost always **make**. If you're describing **the activity of
  working on something** (research, analysis, a task), it's **do**.
- Both are **irregular**: *do → did → done*; *make → made → made*.

---

### 4. 🎯 Quando usar — e quando não (THE core section)

**Why does this split exist?** English wants to separate **creating a result** from
**carrying out an activity**. *Make* is the verb of production — at the end you're holding a
new thing (a decision, an offer, a profit). *Do* is the verb of activity — it names the work
or the effort, with no fresh object to point at. Portuguese collapses both into **fazer**,
so a Brazilian speaker has *one* word doing two jobs and no built-in signal for which
English verb to reach for. That single fact is the source of nearly every error below.

**Decision rule — make (a product) vs do (an activity).** Ask: *at the end, is there a new
thing, or was there just activity?*

| | **make** — a result is produced | **do** — an activity is performed |
|---|---|---|
| Test | You now **have** X (a decision, a plan) | You **spent effort** on X (research, work) |
| Executive | *"We **made a decision** on the vendor."* | *"We **did the analysis** on the vendor."* |
| Executive | *"The unit **made a profit** this quarter."* | *"The unit **did well** this quarter."* |
| Everyday | *"I **made** dinner."* (dinner now exists) | *"I **did** the dishes."* (an activity) |

Minimal pair — same territory, different verb, different meaning:
- *"We need to **make a deal** with the vendor."* = *produce/close an agreement* (a result).
- *"We need to **do business** with the vendor."* = *have an ongoing working relationship*
  (an activity). Both are natural — but they say different things, and each takes its **own**
  verb. You can't swap them.

**PT→EN mapping — where *fazer* betrays Levi.** Translate *fazer* by feel and you'll pick
wrong half the time. Learn these as blocks:
- *"**fazer** uma decisão / um plano / um erro / progresso"* → ✅ **make** a decision / a
  plan / a mistake / progress — never *"**do** a decision"*.
- *"**fazer** pesquisa / a análise / o trabalho / um curso"* → ✅ **do** research / the
  analysis / the work / a course — never *"**make** a research"*.
- *"**fazer** negócio"* → ✅ **do** business; but *"**fechar** negócio"* → ✅ **make** a deal.
- *"**fazer** uma apresentação"* → ✅ **give** / **do** a presentation (both fine); *"**make**
  a presentation"* is possible but less natural for the act of presenting.

**Top Brazilian-speaker errors:**
- ❌ *"We need to **do a decision** by Friday."* → ✅ *"We need to **make a decision** by
  Friday."* — a decision is a **result you produce**, so it's *make*. This is the number-one
  slip, straight from *fazer uma decisão*.
- ❌ *"The team **made** a lot of **research** before the board."* → ✅ *"The team **did** a
  lot of **research**."* — *research/analysis/work* are **activities**, so they take *do*.
- ❌ *"I **made** a mistake in the deck… I **did** a mistake."* → ✅ *"I **made** a mistake."*
  — a mistake is something you **produce** (unintentionally, but still a result): *make*.

### 5. ✏️ 8 annotated examples

1. *"We need to **make a decision** on the AI platform this week."*
   *— make: a decision is a result you produce; "do a decision" is the classic* fazer *slip.*
2. *"The team **did the due diligence** on both vendors before we committed."*
   *— do: due diligence is an activity/task (the work of investigating), never "made the due diligence".*
3. *"We've **made real progress** on the 2027 roadmap this quarter."*
   *— make + progress is a fixed block; progress is treated as something produced. "Did progress" is wrong.*
4. *"If we **make the wrong call** here, it sets the roadmap back a year."*
   *— make a call/decision = produce a choice; the outcome (right or wrong) is the point.*
5. *"The AI unit **made a profit** for the first time, even while we **did** the platform migration."*
   *— make a profit (a result: money now exists) vs do the migration (the activity/work).*
6. *"**Make sure** the CFO signs off before we send it to the board."*
   *— make sure is a set phrase (= guarantee/confirm); no "do sure" exists.*
7. *"Can you **make an appointment** with the vendor for Thursday? I still have to **do** the prep."*
   *— everyday-ish: make an appointment (you create a slot) vs do the prep (the activity).*
8. *"I **made** dinner while my daughter **did** her homework."*
   *— everyday: make dinner (dinner now exists) vs do homework (an activity). The clean split in one line.*

### 8. 🗣️ Para levar para a aula

- Ask your tutor to run a **"fazer swap" drill**: they fire Portuguese sentences from your
  world (*"Precisamos fazer uma decisão"*, *"A equipe fez a análise"*, *"Fizemos progresso
  no roadmap"*, *"Fechamos negócio com o fornecedor"*, *"Fiz um erro no forecast"*) and you
  answer instantly in English, picking **make** or **do** without pausing. Goal: kill the
  reflex of translating *fazer* by feel.
- Then a **collocation-block round**: have your tutor call out executive nouns (*decision,
  progress, research, analysis, a deal, business, an offer, a case, due diligence, a
  profit*) and you say the full phrase with the right verb *and* a real sentence from a
  board or strategy setting. These pairings are memorised as blocks, not calculated — this
  is the practice that locks them in.

### 9. ✅ Gabarito

**Warm-up (Unit 052 — "get"):**
1. ***get aligned*** — *"Let's **get aligned** on the budget before Thursday."* *Get +
   adjective* = *become* aligned (we're not there yet); it's a change *into* the state.
   *"Be aligned"* would claim you **already** agree — a bolder, riskier statement to the CEO.
2. ***get to the boardroom … get home*** — *get **to** + a place* = *arrive at / reach* it,
   so *"get **to** the boardroom"*. But *home, here, there* drop the preposition entirely:
   *"get **home**"*, never *"get to home"*. Same verb, two patterns.
3. ***got to present*** — *Get to + verb* = *had the opportunity / managed to* (matches *"a
   real privilege"*). *Have to present* would mean an **obligation** — the opposite feeling.
   The little word *to* is identical; *get* vs *have* flips privilege into duty.

**Spaced review:**
- **(Unit 046)** ***investing*** — *"We're **committed to investing** for three years."* In
  *committed to*, the *to* is a **preposition** (test: *"committed to **it**"* works), and
  after a preposition a verb becomes **-ing** — never the infinitive. Portuguese uses the
  plain infinitive (*comprometidos a **investir***), which is exactly why *"committed to
  invest"* feels right but is wrong.
- **(Unit 023)** ***Does … have*** — *"**Does** the board **have** everything it needs?"*
  *Does* is the **helper** that builds the question (it carries no meaning); *have* is the
  **main verb** meaning *possess*. You need both — *"**Has** the board everything?"* is
  old-fashioned/British-formal; modern English uses the *do*-helper: *does … have*.

**Recognition drill:**
1. ❌ *"**do** a decision"* → ✅ *"The board still has to **make a decision** on the budget."*
   — a decision is a **result you produce**, so it takes *make*. *"Do a decision"* comes
   straight from *fazer uma decisão*.
2. ***did*** — *"…the team **did** three weeks of **analysis**."* Analysis is an
   **activity/task** (the work of analysing), not a product you hold, so it takes *do*.
   *"Made analysis"* is the mirror-image error to *"do a decision"*.
3. ***made … done*** — *"We finally **made** a deal with the cloud provider we'd **done**
   business with for years."* *Make a deal* = **produce/close** an agreement (a result);
   *do business* = **carry on** a working relationship (an activity). Different ideas →
   different verbs, even though both are *fazer* in Portuguese.
4. ***made … did*** — *"I **made** a mistake in the forecast, but the team **did** a great
   job fixing it."* A mistake is something you **produce** (a result) → *make*; a job is an
   **activity performed** → *do*. *Fazer* labels both, so the instinct is to swap them —
   *"did a mistake / made a great job"* are the two classic slips.

### 10. 🇧🇷 Resumo em português

**A regra e quando usar.** *Do* e *make* traduzem o mesmo verbo português — **fazer** — mas
dividem o trabalho. **make = produzir / criar um resultado**: no fim existe uma coisa nova
que você "tem" (*make a decision, a plan, a mistake, progress, a profit, an offer, a deal*).
**do = realizar uma atividade / tarefa / trabalho**: o foco é o *fazer* em si, sem um produto
para apontar (*do the research, the analysis, the work, business, a course, homework*). Teste
rápido: se sobra um **substantivo que você agora possui** (uma decisão, um plano), é quase
sempre **make**; se você está descrevendo **a atividade de trabalhar em algo** (pesquisa,
análise), é **do**. Boa parte disso são *collocations* — pares fixos que se **decoram** em
bloco, não se calculam (*make an effort*, *do business*).

**As armadilhas.** O português tem só *fazer*, então falta o sinal de qual metade usar — e é
daí que vêm quase todos os erros. (1) *Fazer uma decisão* → ✅ *"**make** a decision"*, nunca
*"do a decision"* (o erro nº 1). (2) *Fazer pesquisa / a análise / o trabalho* → ✅ *"**do**
research / the analysis / the work"*, nunca *"make a research"*. (3) *Fazer um erro* → ✅
*"**make** a mistake"*; *fazer um bom trabalho* → ✅ *"**do** a good job"* — não troque os
dois. (4) *Fechar negócio* → *"**make** a deal"* × *fazer negócio (com alguém)* → *"**do**
business"*: ideias diferentes, verbos diferentes. Se ainda embolar o outro *fazer* que vira
*get* (mudar de estado), releia `lessons/2026-09-02-unit-052.md`.
